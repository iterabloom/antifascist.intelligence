7.1.4. Addressing Workforce Disruption
Superintelligent AI systems will inevitably lead to workforce disruption as tasks previously performed by humans are automated. To address these challenges and promote social equity, it is essential to devise policy interventions that mitigate the negative effects of displacement. In this section, we discuss specific measures that include retraining and upskilling programs, job guarantee (JG) programs, strengthening social safety nets, AI-driven job creation, lifelong learning programs, AI-human collaboration, and supporting small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs).

(1) Retraining and Upskilling Programs: Governments should fund and establish retraining and upskilling programs through public university systems to equip workers with the skills needed to thrive in an AI-driven economy. This approach allows individuals to adapt to new job requirements and the changing job market. For instance, Singapore's SkillsFuture initiative provides citizens with access to skill development opportunities, ensuring their competitiveness in a rapidly changing economy.

(2) Job Guarantee (JG) Programs: JG programs should be introduced, offering unemployed individuals a public sector job at a living wage. Through these programs, governments can create employment opportunities in sectors impacted by automation. Giving workers the chance to recalibrate their professional focus and adapt their existing experience to changing industries. JG programs can tackle pressing societal and environmental challenges by providing jobs in areas such as child care, elder care, education, infrastructure development, renewable energy, and community-based projects.

(3) Social Safety Nets: Strengthening social safety nets can protect individuals who cannot or prefer not to work when facing displacement. This expansion may include broadening access to unemployment benefits, healthcare, and affordable housing. An example is Finland's basic income experiment, providing a monthly stipend to unemployed individuals supporting their transition to new job opportunities. However, JG programs are morally prerequisite for basic income programs, as individuals should have the opportunity to contribute to society before being considered "unemployable."

(4) AI-Driven Job Creation: Policymakers should encourage the development of AI applications that generate new job opportunities and industries. For instance, AI advancements in healthcare could lead to a higher demand for healthcare professionals capable of harnessing technology for diagnostics, personalized medicine, and remote care.

(5) Lifelong Learning Programs: Governments should promote lifelong learning initiatives that support continuous skill development and career adaptability. These programs should be accessible to individuals across all career stages and socioeconomic backgrounds. For example, the European Union's Erasmus+ program enables individuals to gain international learning experiences and develop skills relevant to a rapidly changing job market.

(6) Encouraging AI-Human Collaboration: Policymakers and researchers should explore ways to foster AI-human collaboration, leveraging the unique strengths of both humans and AI systems. By creating environments where AI systems augment and support human decision-making and creativity, new job opportunities may emerge that require a combination of human and AI capabilities.

(7) Supporting Small and Medium-sized Enterprises (SMEs): Policymakers should consider providing SMEs access to AI resources and expertise to enable them to compete more effectively with dominant industry players. Encouraging a more diverse AI ecosystem can promote innovation, prevent monopolization, and create additional employment opportunities.
