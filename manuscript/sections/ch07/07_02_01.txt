7.2.1. National Policy Guidelines for AI Development, Employment, and Anti-Authoritarianism
To advance the development of altruistic and anti-authoritarian artificial intelligence systems, national policy guidelines should emphasize the importance of interdisciplinary collaboration, innovation, and addressing key ethical and safety concerns. The following recommendations outline crucial areas for policymakers to consider:

(1) Ethical and Value Alignment: National policies must prioritize the alignment of AI systems with human values and ethical principles. Support for research on value alignment, operationalizing ethical principles in AI systems, and funding a strong ethical culture within the AI research community is necessary. For instance, countries can follow the example of the European Union's High-Level Expert Group on Artificial Intelligence and its Ethics Guidelines for Trustworthy AI.

(2) Interdisciplinary Collaboration: National policies should fund collaboration between AI developers, ethicists, psychologists, neuroscientists, and other experts. This can be achieved by funding joint research projects, establishing interdisciplinary research centers, and incentivizing cross-disciplinary education and training programs. For example, a National AI Ethics Research Center, similar to Germany's German Research Center for Artificial Intelligence (DFKI), could be established in various countries to foster collaboration and research.

(3) AI Education and Workforce Development: National policies should address the need for AI literacy, technical skills development, and interdisciplinary ethics education and training programs. Governments can support these efforts through funding, curriculum development, and collaboration with educational institutions and industry partners. For instance, Singapore's AI Apprenticeship Programme (AIAP) focuses on building AI talent through hands-on training and mentorship.

(4) Public Engagement and Stakeholder Involvement: National policies should emphasize diverse stakeholder engagement in AI development, including the public, private sector, civil society, and academia. Support for public deliberation and participatory AI governance initiatives, such as citizen assemblies, public consultations, and partnerships with non-governmental organizations is vital. One example to follow is the United Kingdom's Centre for Data Ethics and Innovation (CDEI), which engages with various stakeholders in AI policy discussions.

(5) AI Safety and Risk Mitigation: National policies should prioritize AI safety research and risk mitigation strategies, such as value alignment, robustness, generalizability, and long-term safety considerations. Governments can achieve this through targeted research funding, safety guidelines and best practices development, and collaborating with international partners to establish global safety standards. The Partnership on AI, a global initiative involving governments, industry, and academia, serves as a platform for information sharing on AI safety.

(6) Legal and Regulatory Frameworks: National policies should establish clear legal and regulatory frameworks for AI development and employment, addressing issues such as data privacy, cybersecurity, accountability, and sentient AI rights. This can involve updating existing laws and regulations, as well as collaborating with international partners to harmonize legal frameworks and ethical guidelines. For example, the European Commission's proposed AI Regulation presents a comprehensive framework for AI governance that can be used as a reference for other countries.

(7) Encouraging Industry Best Practices and Self-regulation: National policies should encourage the AI industry to adopt best practices and self-regulate, while being mindful of potential corporate doublespeak and power imbalances. Governments could support the development of voluntary industry standards, codes of conduct, and certification programs, while closely monitoring compliance and maintaining oversight. The international AI initiative AI Global provides a valuable model for promoting industry best practices, responsible AI use, and self-regulation.
