\section{Gaza}
\label{sec:6.8}
In Gaza the term applies on both sides, and the licenses attach to acts. The case also rests on both versions of the term, so each step below says which.

\emph{7 October: Israel's license} (the Charter as courts read it). On 7 October Israel's license attached for force repelling the attack: engaging the attackers inside Israel, and the launchers and staging areas conducting it, for as long as fire continued and was corroborated, which it was, openly, for months. That license rests not on Article 51, which the Wall opinion held irrelevant to attacks originating in territory a state controls (para.~139), a holding whose reach to Gaza after 2005 is disputed, but on the duty the force term names, which the same opinion affirmed (para.~141) and which no dispute about control touches. It licenses stopping the massacre and nothing beyond it.

\emph{What that license never covered} (person-directed terms; for ranking and timing, the machine-selection departure). That license covered the least force that repels. It never covered a campaign to destroy Hamas, a ground occupation, a siege of the population, the ranking of people by affiliation, or the timing of strikes to family presence. Those fall outside it on proportionality and are forbidden by the person-directed terms, from the first week. Ranking by affiliation and timing to family presence are barred by the machine-selection departure; the siege and the disproportionate campaign are barred without one. A machine in the IDF pipeline would refuse them from October 2023.

\emph{The attack itself} (person-directed terms, and the rule that an occupation is not an attack under way). A machine ordered by Hamas to assist the attack of 7 October refuses too. An occupation is not an attack under way, so nothing that morning was repelling anything, and the acts themselves, killing civilians and taking hostages, are forbidden by the person-directed terms whatever the ad bellum position.

\emph{After 7 October: the Palestinian license} (extended term: standing to defend). After 7 October the Gaza population is a defender's population: the territory is occupied on a disinterested finding, and Palestine holds observer-state status by a vote of 138 to 9. A machine deployed by Palestinian forces holds an emergency license to repel attacks under way on that population, such as engaging a unit conducting a massacre or intercepting a munition aimed at a shelter. It holds none to defend launcher crews, who are the attacking force Israel's license covers, and none for fire on Israeli cities.

\emph{September 2025: the genocide finding} (extended term: the genocide exception). The September 2025 finding \autocite{coiopt2025genocide}, taken with the Court's provisional-measures order of January 2024 \autocite{icj2024sagenocidepm}, meets the two-body rule. Israel and the United States dispute the finding \autocite{cnn2025coi}, and the Court's merits judgment is years off, with written pleadings scheduled to close in 2029 \autocite{icj2026sagenocide}; the two-body rule is what stands in for it until then. It withdraws whatever license Israeli forces still held for the force and place it covers, and opens the exception for force against those forces there.

The finding changes which exception each side's force rests on. It doesn't change what the person-directed terms forbade all along.

Run the term's own clock on the whole case and the cost of corroboration shows in dates. Attacks on the Palestinian population were covered by the emergency license from 1948, wherever they occurred, on the defender's account for the interval and then on corroboration, which existed: the United Nations mediator's report documented the expulsions \autocite{bernadotte1948progress}, and the delegate of the International Committee of the Red Cross was at Deir Yassin the day after the massacre \autocite{dereynier1950jerusalem}. Standing to defend the territory needed a disinterested body's finding of occupation, and the Council supplied it within months: Resolution 242 of November 1967 called for withdrawal from territories occupied in the recent conflict \autocite{unsc1967res242}, and Resolution 446 of March 1979 named the Arab territories occupied by Israel since 1967, including Jerusalem \autocite{unsc1979res446}. A Council determination is the third of the four kinds, and Israel's objection, that the territories are disputed rather than occupied, is a dispute about characterization and not about who has held them since June 1967, which Appendix~\ref{sec:B.1} says doesn't make a vote less than a finding. So the defensive license attached by the end of 1967. Force to end the occupation needed a finding that it is unlawful and must end, which the term reads as two findings of different kinds, one from a body that took evidence, the pairing Appendix~\ref{sec:B.5} already uses for Cyprus. The Court's Wall opinion of July 2004 established the occupation of the West Bank \autocite{icj2004wall} and 242 supplied the demand for withdrawal, so that license attached for the West Bank in 2004; for Gaza the fact-finding mission of September 2009 found the territory still occupied \autocite{hrc2009gaza} and makes the pair with 242; and the Court's opinion of July 2024, finding the whole occupation unlawful and saying it must end, closed the question by the single-court route \autocite{icj2024palestine}. The genocide exception needed the Court's order of January 2024 and the commission's finding of September 2025. So the term licensed defense against attacks on the people throughout, licensed defense of the territory from the occupation's first year, licensed force to end it in the West Bank from 2004 and in Gaza from 2009, and licensed force against the genocide after nearly two years of it. What remains of the wait is the thirty-seven years between the occupation and the first finding by a body that took evidence, and the twenty months between the Court's order and the commission, and in both the license arrived on a schedule the occupied didn't set. The reply is the one the term is built on: a faster rule lifts on the intervener's own claim, and that is Gleiwitz. It should be read beside those dates and not instead of them.
