Chapter 7: Necessary Areas of Research
Chapters 2 through 6 described a research program already underway: how empathy and compassion might inform a design, what cognitive science and moral psychology contribute, what a policy regime demands, what collaboration across labs and borders requires. None of that program is finished. This chapter names what is not yet solved — the specific technical and empirical gaps the preceding chapters ran into and set aside rather than closed, so that a reader who wants to work on this has a list of open problems.

Section 7.1 takes up the open questions in AI ethics and social reasoning: moral responsibility when a system's designers, trainers, and users all share partial control; whether an ethics-embedding method generalizes past the situations it was trained on; what the neuroscience of empathy and theory of mind still has to teach a system built to model other minds. Section 7.2 turns to emotional recognition and regulation specifically — where the underlying science is more contested than the engineering literature usually lets on, and where "self-awareness" and "resistance to manipulation" mean something narrower and more tractable than the phrases suggest.

What "open" claims here
One qualification governs every gap named in this chapter, and stating it once is better than attaching it to each. Where I say a problem is unaddressed, the claim is bounded: I looked for published work under a particular description and did not find it. That is a weaker claim than no such work existing. A search is not a survey, a program can be underway under a name I did not think to search, and the closer a gap sits to this book's own framing the less likely anyone has posed it in these words. What the claim does support is narrower and still useful: a researcher entering here will not find the problem already named and waiting. Where I know of adjacent work that stops short of a gap, I name it and say where it stops, because the distance between an existing program and the thing that is missing is the part a researcher can actually use.

The gaps, each with the section that runs into it and the nearest existing work:
<<list>>
- Telling a method that encoded the value from one that merely fit its test distribution, before deployment rather than after a failure exposes the difference (section 7.1.2). Nearest existing work: the catalogue of roughly sixty documented specification-gaming instances, which identifies failures after the fact and offers no pre-deployment test.
- Whether a preference-aggregation method that works for one bounded game generalizes to the open-ended, high-stakes preference conflicts an ethical system would have to arbitrate (section 7.1.3). Nearest existing work: DeepMind's Democratic AI, a controlled study built around a single resource-sharing game.
- Building a system whose performance across structurally different mental-state tasks rises together rather than independently, as a test of whether apparent theory-of-mind competence rests on one underlying capacity or several unrelated ones (section 7.1.4). Nearest existing work: Gopnik and Astington's 1988 correlation in three- to five-year-olds. I found no AI research that has tried to reproduce it.
- A multi-agent system built specifically to detect and resist an authoritarian actor's attempt to capture or subvert it, as distinct from the general problem of aligning multi-agent incentives with designer intent (section 7.1.5). Nearest existing work: the 2020 Cooperative AI research agenda and the foundation built around it, which cover the general problem; and debate, which was built for a single human overseer checking a single stronger system and has not been extended to a population.
- Distinguishing disagreement that deserves representation from disagreement that is the product of coercion or a captured information environment (section 7.1.6). Nearest existing work: jury learning, which models who is disagreeing but was built for content moderation and takes no position on which disagreements are free.
- Telling a recuperated dissent mechanism from a merely mediocre one, when both produce the same paperwork (section 7.1.7). Nearest existing work: none I could find. Section 2.1.4 sets the constraint any such detector would have to satisfy on the output side — a finding for a body with standing to act on it, never a public accusation.
- Whether any face-to-feeling mapping survives once individual and cross-cultural variation are taken seriously, under what constraints and against what error rate (section 7.2.1). Nearest existing work: the 2019 Association for Psychological Science panel, which closed the strong version of the claim without establishing what replaces it.
- Whether a commitment trained into a system survives a determined attempt to remove it, and whether interpretability tools can be trusted when the system being interpreted has an incentive to defeat them (section 7.2.2). Nearest existing work: an adversarial literature in which each published defense is followed by an attack built against it.
