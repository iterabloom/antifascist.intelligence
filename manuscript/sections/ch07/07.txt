Chapter 7: Policy Recommendations for AI Ethics, Safety, and Antifascism
Chapter 5 named what can go wrong with an AI system technically — bias, brittleness, misaligned goals, deliberate misuse — and what a design can do about each; chapter 6 covered the collaborative infrastructure that lets researchers, the public, and other governments coordinate on getting it right. This chapter asks who makes a design like that the norm rather than the exception: what economic incentives shape whether it gets built at all, what policy frameworks and industry practice actually enforce, what privacy and security mean for a system trained on people's data, what accountability looks like once a system might be a subject rather than only a tool, and how all of this plays out across jurisdictions that agree on none of it uniformly. None of the technical answers hold on their own, and neither does coordination without enforcement. A well-designed system built inside institutions with no reason to demand one is a design nobody has to use.
