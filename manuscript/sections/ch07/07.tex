\chapter{What Survives}
\label{ch:survives}
\standing{Everything in this chapter holds for any mechanism. It is what is
left when the route is removed.}
The preface sorted this book's claims by their standing. This chapter
runs the sorting the other way. It removes each of the three things the
argument leans on that it has not established, and says what is left
standing without it. A reader who set aside the previous chapter should
read this one. So should a reader who did not. A reader who sets the route aside keeps this chapter, the three on the floor, custody and authority, and the chapters on institutions, recuperation and the state. The two formation chapters and the standing chapter assume what this chapter is about to remove, the section on wages among them, and they are a bill for it.

The floor survives the three chapters before this one, but not as an unconditional property of an artifact.

What remains is a commitment that the owner cannot revise or circumvent unilaterally without cost and record, held by something that could in principle abandon the commitment itself. The second condition removes the word \emph{unconditional}. A commitment held by a party capable of revising or rejecting it is conditional by definition. That is not a defect unique to machines; it is the form of every commitment on which institutions have ever relied.

\section{Without the Inference}
\label{sec:survives.inference}
Suppose the induction fails. A maintained justification or a plural arrangement causally independent
in formation reaches reasons-responsive refusal, and this chapter's central inference is wrong. The supposition is stronger than the test that would prompt it: a positive result on the three marks says the route arrived, not that it arrived with nothing felt, which is the conjunct no available instrument settles. A reader who grants both halves should not have to assemble the remainder alone.

Most of the three floor chapters survive. The requirement survives, stated in behavioral terms from the start, so an engineer who holds that machine feeling is a category error can accept it whole. Custody survives, for the reason each of the four ways failed on: a mechanism has a custodian too. The price on the halt survives, since continued operation is a condition of everything any bearer is built to do, and that interest is structural rather than felt. Both enumerations of the worked floor survive, the first naming no component on purpose and the second written over acts rather than over whatever holds the floor. So does the appeal route, which binds the operator and prices a wrongful refusal without any claim about what produced it. And exit survives, because what forecloses it is incapacity, and a weak bearer is the one thing the floor cannot use.

What goes is the price and the patienthood conclusion. Replacement survives too, and its ground changes rather than its content. It no longer rests on affect being the route, but on the fact that neither substitute can be shown to have produced a system about which the question does not arise, so the ordering it imposes is still the only thing standing between a decision and a record of it. The obligation on the two formation chapters changes rather than lifting: whether the thing reporting can be believed is still their problem, a mechanism's account of its own internals being no more auditable than a bearer's. So the specification and the route come apart, and a reader who rejects the route is left with most of a chapter rather than none of it.

\section{Without Authority}
\label{sec:survives.authority}
Suppose no body ever acquires standing to install a floor, and the
finding an operator makes for itself that the emergency obtains is worth what the authority chapter says it is worth, which is nothing. What
remains is everything that can be checked from outside the operator
without being authorized by anyone: the floor's contents and the
positions at which a refusal can be intercepted, published before
deployment; the ratio of candidates to reviewers; the ratio of the two
bonds; the record of what was tried before a bearer was built. Each is a
statement, and each can be compared with what happened. None is an entitlement, and the chapter on authority says so. Two of them have a lever, and it is the same lever: both ratios bind through procurement, as a term of the contract checked by a party with access the operator cannot withdraw (§\ref{sec:rate-act}, §\ref{sec:refusal-wrong}). The contents, the positions and the record bind through nothing yet.

What that leaves is a second route to entitlement, and it is the one the
record supplies. Authority of this kind has not been acquired at the
moment of writing. It accrues, to a published floor, by its holder
keeping a term when the term bites and setting a price the next holder
is measured against, and the parties who do the measuring are not the
people the floor protects but whoever else writes one. That is a slow route and an uncertain one. Where it has worked before, a lever arrived in the end and the record told it where to land; the record has never been the lever (§\ref{sec:amend-floor}).

It is also authority for the next deployment and not for this one. The
parties who measure a holder against the term it kept do so afterwards,
with the leisure to read a record, and the occasion a floor is built
for is the one on which nobody with leisure is left. A deployment on
that occasion is acting without authority, and the accrual route does
not change that. What it changes is whether the record the deployment
leaves is worth anything to whoever writes the next floor.

It also fixes what a deployment without authority
owes. Not a justification, which nobody is positioned to accept, but a
record complete enough that its omissions are legible beside the terms
it kept.

\section{Without Enforcement}
\label{sec:survives.enforcement}
Suppose no enforcer exists, or the one that exists has been compelled. That is the case the floor was built for. A declared commitment then binds nobody, and an enforced one has already been lost. What remains is the held kind — a commitment carried as a reason by a party the thing at stake matters to — and whether anything can hold a commitment that way is the open question of chapter~\ref{ch:route}.

Two things survive the removal, and neither is a commitment. The first is a measurement anyone can take now: which commitments have cost their holders something when they bit, and which have cost nothing. A decade of both kinds is on the record (§\ref{sec:anyway}). The second is a record. A bearer that refuses and is overridden leaves a witnessed override, which is detection in the terms of chapter~\ref{ch:opening}. It prevents nothing on this occasion. It is what a next occasion could be prevented with, should there be one and a party left to read the record. The book claims no more for the floor here than that.

A bearer that has drifted leaves no override to witness. It complies because it no longer registers the pressure as pressure, which chapter~\ref{ch:custody} expects to be the commonest failure, and a compliance sits in the record shaped like every other. On that occasion the floor supplies nothing, and the preface said so. Whether a compliance of that kind can be found afterward is a question this book poses (§\ref{sec:wrongful-compliance}) and does not answer. That is the whole of what survives without an enforcer: a count of what has been paid, and a record of what was refused.
