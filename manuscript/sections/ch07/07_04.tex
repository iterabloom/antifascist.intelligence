\section{Recuperation at the Discourse}
\label{sec:7.4}
There is a third instance and it is the one this book is inside. Alignment and AI ethics are, among other things, a channel through which objections to what is being built are solicited, funded, published, and answered. That channel exists, it is used, and the question to ask of any such channel is whether it has ever moved something the party collecting it did not want moved.

The argument that it has not is older than the current wave and it was made from inside. Rodrigo Ochigame, writing after working at an institution central to the field's formation, argued that the discourse of “ethical AI” was constructed with the strategic function of forestalling legally enforceable restriction — that the industry's investment in academic ethics was an investment in a substitute for regulation, and that the substitute worked by converting political questions into technical ones a company could then be trusted to answer \autocite{ochigame2019invention}. His worked example is the Partnership on AI, which this book cites repeatedly as its standing example of voluntary cross-sector coordination: he describes its recommendations arriving reliably at conclusions compatible with the corporate agenda, with the political question of whether a technology should exist reduced to a technical question of how to build it responsibly.

\runin{The structure, tested against its own definition}
The four features fit unevenly.

Aestheticization of the metric: partially. The field's founding objections were about deployment — who is scored, who is watched, who is denied — and a body of current work is about model properties instead, which is a shift from questions with defendants to questions with benchmarks.

Recuperation of dissent: the strongest fit. An objection raised inside a lab becomes a paper or a resignation. Each of those is a real thing that happened and none of them stopped a deployment, and the parties collecting them can point to the collection as evidence of seriousness. There is no route by which a researcher's conclusion that a system should not ship becomes a decision that it does not ship, because the deciding party is the funding party and no external body holds standing.

Coding of exception as betrayal: at the level of individual careers, contested. The dismissals of dissenting researchers are one data point and they point the right way; there are counterexamples of people who objected loudly and prospered.

Decoupling of the model from the world it claims to track: not settled from outside. Its artifact is whether the field's account of what it is doing is ever checked against anything but its own benchmarks, and the one check this book has, the targeting deployment, was made by journalists and not by the field. One case checks a claim and not a drift, and the feature is defined by drift.

These findings establish only a partial structural resemblance; they do not establish fascism.

\runin{The other five, run}
The five discriminators of the second chapter, in their institutional form, are the half that sorts, and each of the three instances above is run against them here.

Run against all three instances, they clear all three.

Three of the five discriminating features are absent outright. Nothing in either arrangement is personal: an annotator meets a queue, a guideline and a throughput target, and a comparison enters the fit without the standpoint that produced it, which is the opposite of an exception granted by proximity to somebody. Neither shows participation beyond what the rule requires, and the record runs the other way — a quota, take-home wages between roughly \$1.32 and \$2 an hour, and a contract ended eight months early. And neither has a scapegoat: the annotator bears a cost with no claim on the outcome, which is the nearest candidate, but the category exists for the labels, so adverse treatment is a cost that was minimized rather than a purpose that was served.

Two of the five I cannot settle from outside. Whether procedure is waived upward, and whether a practice disowned in the policy is rewarded in the promotion, are facts about what an institution rewarded rather than about what it wrote. So the clearing is on three of five observed and two undetermined, and it would take an inside record to finish.

For the discourse the answer is the same and one step less clean. Its scapegoat test is contested, on the evidence two heads above: an objection can end a career and there are people who objected loudly and prospered. Its procedural test cannot be run: there is no procedure above the deciding party to be waived, which is an absence rather than an asymmetry. Deniability comes nearest of anything in the chapter, and what it fits is the firms rather than the field: a decade of published principles alongside what actually got shipped.

\runin{The result}
So what this chapter has been describing is a recuperation mechanism inside ordinary institutional decay, and not fascism. This single application is insufficient to validate the definition. It is insufficient in a particular direction. Every case the restated five have been run on at the scale of an office has cleared, and the cases the definition catches are regimes and movements, read in the vocabulary borrowed unrestated. A definition tested only on cases it clears has not been shown to sort. Until the restated five find an office that fails them, the claim that they separate fascism from decay at that scale describes the instrument and is not a result from it. That is the book's one measurement with its own instrument, and it is negative in the direction that matters: the machinery this book is inside does not fail the test this book built.

\runin{What this does to the book}
The reading has one more place to go and it is the nearest one. This text was written with a model made by a company the book criticizes. A book about recuperation, produced through the discourse it is describing, cannot claim to have stood outside it, and I have no procedure that would let me check whether the objections in these pages are the ones a channel like this reliably produces — the ones that are publishable, that make the author look serious, and that cost nobody anything.

The demand came back as a manuscript in an established genre, written with the industry's own model, addressed to a readership the industry helped bring into being, and legible as a contribution to a field that exists because demands of roughly this kind were absorbed. That the objections in these pages are sincere is not evidence against it, sincerity being a property the absorbed ones also had. Alignment is the job title. Safety is the market segment. The benchmark is the metric and the voluntary commitment is the certification. Every one of those was somebody's objection first. Whether these are the real thing or the acceptable version of it is not something the person who wrote them is positioned to judge, and the corrected question does not need him to: it asks what became of the demand, which is a fact about the field and not about his motives.

The criterion this book puts first does not need the author to settle that. A demand escapes recuperation when the party who made it retains standing to reopen it against the party that absorbed it, and a book confers standing on nobody. Whether these objections are the real thing is decided by whether anybody acquires, from what is proposed here, a route to reopen an arrangement against its operator, which is a fact about what becomes of the proposals and not about the person who wrote them.

The one structural remedy this section can name is the one the rest of the chapter names. What the field lacks is a refusal priced by the refuser and a body with standing to receive it: an external authority that can stop a deployment, to which an objection can be filed by someone who does not depend on the objection being welcome. That is what making review binding requires, and a decade of the voluntary version has not produced it. Until something of that kind exists, everything published under the heading of alignment — including this book — is being collected by a channel whose owners decide what it moves.
