7.1.7. What an Antifascist Detector Would Actually Have to Detect
Section 2.1.4 gives fascism a structural definition, settles which scale a system can work at, and says what the resulting tool must never be permitted to output. Chapters 3, 4 and 5 then assume, in several places, that a system can be built to recognize authoritarian tactics and flag democratic backsliding early enough to matter. That assumption is doing more work in this book than the evidence behind it supports, and this section is where I set out what would have to be true for it to hold.

<<h>>Why the general framing yields no targets<</h>>
Most of the difficulty is not technical. A specification that says a system should oppose concentrated, unaccountable power cannot be turned into a classifier because it names a property of an entire political arrangement. Nothing observable in a document, a transaction, or a feed corresponds to it. The structural signature in section 2.1.4 is a real improvement, since each of its four features points at an artifact somebody could go and look at. But look closely at what those artifacts are. Whether a dissent mechanism has ever changed an outcome requires knowing the counterfactual. Whether a metric admits an unmeasured remainder requires knowing what the remainder contains. Whether the exception-filer was punished requires access to what happened to a particular person inside an institution that has every reason not to disclose it. These are not features you extract; they are findings you reach, usually after somebody has spent months inside the organization.

<<h>>What would actually have to be measured<</h>>
A detector worth building would need at least three things this book cannot currently tell an engineer how to obtain.

It would need longitudinal access rather than a snapshot. Every one of the four features is defined by a change over time — dissent metabolized, a metric substituted, an exception recoded, a model drifting from what it tracks. A single observation of an institution cannot distinguish any of them from an institution that has always been mediocre. Section 6.6's account of the slope, not the level, is the right instrument here, and its data requirements are severe: the same measurement, taken the same way, over years, by someone the institution cannot fire.

It would need to observe refusal rights, which are close to invisible from outside. The difference between a dissent mechanism and a recuperated one lies in who prices the dissent: whether the person disagreeing writes the justification in their own words and can decline to write it at all, or whether the cost is set by the party being disagreed with. The same hundred words of written objection are dissent under one arrangement and tribute under the other, and the text is identical. There is no feature of the document that separates them.

It would need a base rate, and none exists. Nobody has assembled a labelled corpus of institutions annotated for the four features by people who agree on what they mean, and it is not obvious the annotation task is even well posed. Section 7.1.6's argument for learning from disagreement applies here with unusual force: on this question, disagreement between annotators is likely to be the most informative signal available, and treating it as noise would discard exactly what a researcher needs.

<<h>>The discrimination problem, which is the hard one<</h>>
Suppose all of that were solved. What remains is the problem section 2.1.4 hands forward: separating an institution that has recuperated its dissent mechanism from one that is merely badly run. Both produce a channel that exists, is used, and changes nothing. Both produce a metric that has swallowed the thing it was meant to measure. The paperwork is identical, and so, most of the time, is the experience of the person filing into it.

I do not think the difference is always there to be found. Some of it is a matter of intent, which records do not carry. Some of it is a matter of degree, and the degree is what the measurement was supposed to establish in the first place. Where the difference does surface, it surfaces over time and under pressure: in what an institution does when the channel finally carries something expensive. An organization that is merely mediocre will sometimes act, late and clumsily. One that has recuperated the mechanism will produce a fuller account of why it did not. A longitudinal instrument could in principle catch that. No snapshot ever will.

This is also where the temptation to ship the tool anyway is strongest, and it needs resisting for a specific reason. A system that cannot yet make this discrimination, deployed as though it can, does not emit a noisy signal that improves with more data. It emits a confident one about whichever institutions happen to be legible to it, and the legible ones are those that keep good records. The correlation runs in exactly the wrong direction: the organizations easiest to audit are the ones with the least to hide, and they are what the tool would learn to talk about.

<<h>>What I am claiming and what I am not<</h>>
I am not claiming the project is impossible. The narrow case is bounded: whether a particular exception mechanism inside a particular organization is being recuperated can be asked of records that organization already keeps, and a system helping a regulator or an ombudsman ask it consistently across many institutions would be genuinely useful without ever pronouncing on a regime. Section 2.1.4's answer to the scale question is what makes that the whole job, and not a first approximation of some larger one.

What I am claiming is that this book asserts a capability it has not specified, and that the specification is where the work now is. A researcher who wanted to do it would start with the narrow case, with longitudinal data, with the slope rather than the level, and with the output constraint section 2.1.4 sets: a finding for a body with standing to act on it, never a public accusation. The gap between that program and the early-warning system section 4.1.4 gestures at is the honest measure of how far this is from done.
