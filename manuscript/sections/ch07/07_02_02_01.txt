7.2.2.1. The Dangers of Corporate Doublespeak in AI Ethics
Corporate doublespeak, a practice involving the use of deliberately ambiguous, evasive, or inflated language in an attempt to present a misleading impression of probity or propriety, represents a significant obstacle to the development of truly ethical artificial intelligence systems. The main concern lies in the potential for various corporations to espouse commitments to ethically-produced AI as a means of positive public engagement, while simultaneously engaging in practices that contradict this carefully-crafted image.

1) Erosion of Ethical Standards
Companies may champion lofty ethical values in their public statements or corporate policies, but lack demonstrable action to back up these claims, thereby eroding the meaning of these standards. Google's announcement of its AI Principles, in which the tech giant pledged to abstain from harmful uses of AI and surveillance technologies, seemed at odds with its involvement in Project Maven, a Pentagon contract involving the utilization of AI to analyze military drone footage. Similarly, OpenAI, originally avowing transparency through publishing its research in peer-reviewed scientific journals, abruptly reversed this policy once it identified a potential for profit, undermining not only its own credibility but the idea of accountability in AI research.

2) Undermining Public Trust
Public trust in technology is contingent upon honesty and transparency from corporations. When corporations fail to uphold their public commitments, such as the case with Google and the controversial dismissal of AI ethicists Timnit Gebru and Margaret Mitchell, it increases disillusionment and skepticism among the public and stakeholders, thereby undermining confidence in the organization's commitment to ethical AI practices.

3) Lack of External Oversight
Without external oversight, corporate doublespeak thrives unchecked. Companies may espouse alignment with ethical AI principles without fear of retribution or accountability. For example, Facebook's "Oversight Board" for content moderation has faced criticism due to its direct funding from Facebook, questioning the genuine oversight it can provide. There is a dire need for impartial, independent third-party validation of corporate ethical claims.

4) Dilution of AI Regulations
With corporations utilizing doublespeak, there exists the potential danger of them detracting from the vital process of creating robust AI regulations. Using their influence, corporations can manipulate legal ambiguities and provide skewed information that serves their interests, as seen in lobbying efforts by tech companies to soften regulations on facial recognition in the European Union.

5) Misrepresentation of AI Capabilities
Corporate doublespeak can contribute to a distorted view of AI capabilities, which can lead to inappropriate reliance on the technology. For instance, Clearview AI propagated unfounded claims about the "100 percent accuracy" of its facial recognition technology. Such misrepresentations cause not only inappropriate application of a flawed AI system, but encroachments on privacy and exacerbation of discriminatory biases.

Addressing these dangers necessitates transparent, enforceable regulations that hold corporations accountable for their claims about ethical AI development. Independent external monitoring, effective validation processes, and the promotion of interdisciplinary collaborations can all contribute to curbing doublespeak, ensuring that commitments to AI ethics are not just hollow words but translate into concrete action.
