7.2.3. Public-Private Partnerships for AI Research and Innovation
Public-private partnerships pool government and private-sector resources, knowledge, and expertise in ways neither side manages alone. Two existing examples show what that looks like applied to AI research specifically — and what adapting either one toward altruistic, antifascist goals would require.

<<list>>
1. The National Science Foundation's National AI Research Institutes, launched in 2020 and expanded to more than twenty institutes the following year, pair federal funding with industry co-funders — Amazon, Google, and Intel among them — and address themes from AI safety to human-AI interaction to climate applications [[cite:C0407]]. A program structured the same way but aimed specifically at altruistic and antifascist AI research, rather than folding that goal into a broader institute's mandate, is a model worth adapting directly.

2. The International Telecommunication Union's AI for Good initiative, a United Nations agency program launched in 2017, brings together governments, academic institutions, and companies — IBM, Microsoft, and NVIDIA have all participated through it — around AI applications for poverty, health, education, and climate [[cite:C0408]]. Extending that kind of platform to carry an explicit brief on democratic values and resistance to authoritarian misuse is the adaptation altruistic and antifascist AI goals would need.
<</list>>

Neither partnership was designed around antifascist AI specifically, and that gap is the point: a public-private partnership is only as good as the mandate written into it, and the self-regulation risk section 7.2.2 examines — a partner treating participation as public relations rather than practice — applies here too.
