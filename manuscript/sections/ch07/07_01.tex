\section{Recuperation at the Label}
\label{sec:7.1}
Every claim a model makes about what is harmful, what is acceptable, and what should be refused is downstream of a set of labels, and the labels are downstream of a labor arrangement. Section~\ref{sec:6.1.1} gives the case and the working conditions: to build the filter that keeps a deployed model from reproducing the worst of its training data, an outsourcing firm's workers read and categorized descriptions of child sexual abuse, torture, bestiality, murder and suicide for take-home wages between roughly \$1.32 and \$2 an hour, described lasting psychological injury afterward, and found the counseling they were entitled to rare and useless against production quotas \autocite{perrigo2023openai}.

That is a labor point and it is a sufficient one on its own. The second thing that follows is epistemic, and it tends to be skipped.

\runin{The category is the part that cannot be disputed}
An annotator working a queue can apply a category, and can apply it well or badly. What the arrangement gives them no way to do is say that the category is wrong — that the taxonomy fails to carve the material, that this item belongs to a kind the scheme does not contain, that the guideline produces the wrong answer here for a reason the guideline cannot express. There is no field for that. There is no time for that: the quota is set against classification throughput, and an annotator who stops to contest a scheme is an annotator falling behind.

So the disagreement that would be most informative is the one the pipeline is least able to receive. A misapplied label is a data-quality problem, detectable by inter-annotator agreement and fixable by better instructions. A wrong taxonomy is invisible to inter-annotator agreement, because high agreement is exactly what a shared bad scheme produces. The metric that governs the process cannot see the failure the process is most likely to have.

That is two of the four features in section~\ref{sec:2.1.2}'s structural definition of fascism, arriving together in an ordinary production workflow. The first is recuperation: a channel that collects judgment at scale and is built so that judgment about the channel itself moves nothing. The second is the aestheticization of the metric — agreement standing in for correctness, the substitution experienced as rigor, and whatever falls outside the measure going unnamed rather than refuted.

\runin{Who is excluded, and from what}
It matters that the exclusion is structural and not attitudinal. The people doing this work are, for much of the material, the best-placed judges in the whole pipeline: they have seen more of it than anyone who wrote the guideline, and they are the only party with direct evidence of where the scheme breaks. The arrangement routes their conclusions about individual items upward and their conclusions about the scheme nowhere. An annotation pipeline built this way produces exactly the ground truth it was built to produce, and the resulting model's values are a faithful rendering of a taxonomy nobody with evidence was able to contest.

There is a further asymmetry worth stating plainly. The annotator bears the cost of the work — the exposure, the injury — and has no stake in the outcome and no claim on it. Section~\ref{sec:9.3.5}'s distinction applies here as exactly as it does to an institution's ethics board. A worker who can flag an item but cannot decline a scheme, and who loses the contract by pressing the point, has a channel and no refusal right.

\runin{What this is not}
Two things this argument does not claim. It does not claim the labels are therefore worthless; a filter built this way does catch a great deal of what it was built to catch, and the alternative of not filtering is worse. And it does not claim that better-paid annotators would fix it. Pay is a real and separate obligation, and raising it changes the labor arrangement without adding the missing channel. What is missing is a route by which a judgment about the taxonomy reaches the people who own the taxonomy, with standing to force a response, held by parties who can use it without losing the work.

That is a design requirement on the pipeline, and it is checkable in the way that section asks for: has a guideline ever been changed because an annotator contested it, and what happened to the annotator who did.
