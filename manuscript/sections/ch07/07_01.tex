\section{The failure that doesn't announce itself}
\label{sec:7.1}
The instruments that answer a wrongful refusal don't cover the failure running the other way. The two failure routes are hidden on different axes (a wrongful compliance, unlike a refusal, doesn't flag itself). A wrongful compliance's harm is a harm of acting, measured on the temporal baseline, so it is legible in principle where the system completed the act, but nothing marks it. A wrongful refusal's harm is a harm of refusing, counterfactual, so it is only an estimate, but it arrives marked and with a complainant. They need different instruments. Every stage starts from a refusal having occurred. When the bearer complies where the floor should have prevented, there's a record and no disclosure, no objection (the person harmed usually isn't the requester and may not know the deployment exists), and no review (reviewing means re-adjudicating everything that looks like it). Three remedies follow: sampled re-adjudication by reviewers who didn't produce the record, notice to the people affected, and split log custody. The notice requirement: where a compliance names, scores, locates or classifies an identifiable person who didn't make the request, that person is notified, on the model of consumer credit's adverse-action notice, and has a right to respond and a right to contest. All three are unconditional, and none is exchanged for testimony: nobody has to give an account of their loss to be told what was done or to contest it. \emph{TransUnion v. Ramirez}, a Fair Credit Reporting Act case, held that a statutory violation without concrete harm gives no standing to sue in federal court \autocite{transunion2021}. That limits one thing, an individual suit for damages over a missed notice, which attaches where the compliance was acted on against the person: a denial, a detention, a place on a kill list. It doesn't limit notice, response or contest. Nor does it limit the standing to sue of organizations that represent a class of people affected. Where the person affected can't be identified (a target in a classified pipeline), individual notice doesn't bind, but notice to the class does, and that fact is published beside the list of positions where a refusal can be defeated.

Someone the floor protects, who thinks it's set wrongly or is protecting them in a way they'd decline, uses the same route. Notice tells them the floor acted on them, and contest reaches the floor court.
