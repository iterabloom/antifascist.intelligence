\section{What caregiving escapes}
\label{sec:23.2}
Caregiving survives each of those failures. Eisenberg's review found the same split as Batson's escape experiments, between sympathy, which goes with helping, and personal distress, which sometimes goes with less of it \autocite{eisenberg1990empathy}. And people given two weeks of compassion training spent more of their own money to redress a stranger's unfair share in an economic game than people given a different training \autocite{weng2013compassion}, which shows the disposition can be strengthened in people who already have it; it says nothing about producing it where there was none.

What caregiving doesn't escape is documented in people, and each failure has a machine version that needs no malfunction.
