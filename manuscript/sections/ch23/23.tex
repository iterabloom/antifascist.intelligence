\chapter{Where the Attention Gets Built}
\label{sec:23}
Formed attention has a deployment condition. It needs evidence the operator didn't select on the questions the floor turns on (civilian function, protected status, presence), supplied by peers, by sources outside the chain of command, and by the bearer's own initiative where it has any. It does not need uncomposed access to everything, and so it can be met inside a unified command, by a system formed in the same public school as any other and given, inside the enclave, the evidence the import condition brings in (\S\ref{sec:16.5}); nothing here calls for a school of the military's own.

Of what formed attention needs, only the architecture, a boundary model and an attention schema, can be checked by someone who doesn't operate the system. Continuing learning and persistent interaction are a training regime; external records and plural formation are institutional conditions. Neither can be checked by looking at the system, only by looking at who runs the one and holds the other.
