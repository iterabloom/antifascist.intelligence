\chapter{What Would Change the Argument: A Research Agenda}
\label{ch:argument-agenda}
\standing{The first three entries and the fifth hold for any mechanism. The
fourth tests the route.}
This chapter keeps five tests, because a result on any one would change what
the book recommends rather than refine it. The first two ask whether a
learned floor can survive its custodian and whether a stated refusal appears
when a route for one exists; a negative result on either moves the floor out
of learning and into architecture and institutions. The third asks whether a
wrongful compliance can be found in a record, and it is the only entry that
reaches the deployment the book opened on. The fourth tests the route's one
distinctive claim, that a system with the currency of chapter~2 natively does
better than one that models it, and a negative result there ends the
recommendation while leaving the specification where it was. The fifth asks
whether a population can correct its members without converging, which
decides whether the fourth way this book proposes is a matter of formation or
of access. Each entry states the unresolved issue, the test, what would count
against the proposal, and the access the test requires. The remaining tests
are in Appendix~\ref{ch:appendix}, where a table says what each of them
separates and what it needs beyond the access they all share. Every entry,
here and there, is written so that one implementation's failure narrows the
claim to that implementation.
