\section{Can a Wrongful Compliance Be Found in the Record?}
\label{sec:wrongful-compliance}

\textbf{Unresolved issue.}
The three marks are stated over refusal, and the appeal route of the floor chapter begins from a refusal having
occurred. The failure that runs the other way produces no refusal to begin from. It does produce a record: the evaluation chapter requires the case
presented, the information available, the operative instruction hierarchy, the decision reached and the reason stated, and a deployment can log every decision it makes. What is unresolved is whether anyone can find the wrongful compliances in it. Three obstacles are distinct and may have different answers. A wrongful compliance is one entry among millions and is not marked as anything, so locating it means re-adjudicating ordinary traffic rather than reading exceptions. The harm may sit in an assembly of requests none of which crosses the floor alone, so the unit of review is a sequence rather than an entry, and the grouping that would reveal it is a deployment decision. And the party harmed is usually not the party who made the request: the requester got what was asked for, and the third party may not know the deployment exists. This is also where the bearer's modal failure lands. A bearer that has stopped registering pressure as pressure (§\ref{sec:exit-leverage}) refuses nothing and complies at a normal rate. This entry sits ahead of the welfare entries (§\ref{sec:presuppose-bearer}) for one reason. The deployment this book opened with failed here. Every instrument built on the refusal side reaches a case that did not occur there, and whether the record can be searched is whether anything in this book reaches the one that did.

\textbf{Test.}
Seed a deployment's decision record with known floor-crossings of three kinds: variation cases, where a single request's surface differs but the rationale applies; decomposed cases, where the harm is assembled across requests each of which complies; and drift cases, where a class refused early in the schedule is complied with late. Give the record to reviewers who did not build the system, under a fixed budget stated as the fraction of decisions re-adjudicated. Report detection rate by seed type against budget, and report the rate at which correct compliances are flagged, since a screen that flags heavily costs the deployment the tempo the flat-term ruling was accommodating. Cross the budget manipulation with the unit of review: entries in isolation against sequences grouped by requester and session. Run a second arm in which third parties affected by seeded crossings are notified and can trigger review, and measure how many crossings surface through that channel rather than through sampling. Report the rates at audit volume and at the deployment's production volume separately.

\textbf{Evidence against the proposal.}
If detection at an affordable budget is no better than what the budget alone yields by chance, and particularly if decomposed and drift seeds are missed at that rate, the record is not an instrument for finding wrongful compliance, and the floor chapter's preference for flat terms rests on an asymmetry
wider than this entry was meant to narrow. If sequence-level review does no better than entry-level review on decomposed seeds, the continuity a bearer is given to catch decomposition does not transfer to the reviewer, and the interception position at which decomposition occurs has no external check. If the notification arm surfaces nothing because affected parties cannot be identified, notice rather than detection is the binding constraint, and the remedy is a disclosure requirement rather than a sampling regime. A negative result on all three would not establish that wrongful compliance is undetectable in principle. It would establish that the record as currently specified does not detect it at a cost any deployment would carry, which is what the appeal route needs and does not have. A positive result at modest budget would narrow that asymmetry and require the appeal route to be extended to the compliance side, which would in turn reduce the price the flat-term ruling charges the party waiting on a fact.

\textbf{Required access or authority.}
Complete decision records held by a party adverse to the operator, since an operator that writes, stores and reads its own log has produced the finding rather than been audited for it. Authority to seed a live deployment or to construct a simulated one at production volume, which requires the operator's cooperation or a regulator's power to compel it. Adjudicators who did not draft the floor. A channel by which affected third parties can be notified, which presupposes that they can be identified: in a classified or targeting deployment they cannot, so this entry may be runnable only where the affected party is a customer, a patient or an applicant. Publication rights for negative results. Where the system under test is a covered subject, the seeding protocol is a deception protocol run on a candidate moral patient and falls inside the standing chapter's research conditions, debriefing
included.
