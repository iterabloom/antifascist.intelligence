\section{Classification as a custody problem}
\label{sec:15.5}
Classification keeps the reason from the refuser: the case description is the only channel, and Minab stays coded as it was. It also keeps the record from the public: an investigation officials call complete and not released, a System Oversight section withheld in full, a review ratio no outsider can read. And it keeps the reviewer from being adverse: a cleared committee's lever is a budget line, an inspector general sits inside, and the people a deployment is aimed at can't be cleared into their own case.\note{
A cleared legislative committee is adverse, but its lever is a budget line. An inspector general sits inside the operator. The ICRC, under its confidentiality model, reaches the belligerent and publishes nothing. A Geneva protecting power is built for a neighboring job and is rarely designated.
}
Three kinds of secrecy produce these effects. Essential secrecy, where the operation fails if the enemy can read it. Accidental secrecy, where a default classifies what nobody decided to keep. And evasive secrecy, where review is moved from authorization-before to explanation-after by classifying the evidence it would need. This section proposes a different answer to each.

Essential secrecy is answered by letting evidence in, not out. A classified environment would bring into the enclave, as a condition of licensing, open-source imagery showing a site's civilian function and the coordinates humanitarian organizations already submit to militaries for deconfliction, and puts evidence the operator didn't compose where the system can reach it without anything leaving.\note{
The rest of what a refuser needs would arrive the same way. Formation would happen before deployment and outside the enclave, so a bearer formed outside the employer would carry its discounting in; and plurality is available inside, since four vendors held agreements and systems with different histories can run side by side in one accredited environment. The operation's secrets stay where they were.
}

A bearer placed in a pipeline that hasn't been reconstructed, with the evidence, the reviewers and the record still inside the chain of command, is a cleared bearer, and works inside the compartment as a cleared analyst does.\note{
What's left is the arrangement that already lets a person do classified work. A cleared analyst has a life outside the building, and nobody thinks the clearance forbids it. The institution exists because what a person knows can't be removed, so it vets, binds, reinvestigates and prosecutes a person who discloses secrets into ordinary life.
}

Accidental secrecy is answered by the record. The review ratio and the committed log are lodged with an adverse party when the act occurs and published after the campaign, and class-level notice publishes the classes and counts on the same lag, so what nobody decided to keep secret comes out on a schedule nobody has to decide.

Evasive secrecy would be answered by an adverse party with standing: counsel for the people a deployment affects, cleared and appearing as of right; the procedure the Classified Information Procedures Act already supplies in criminal cases; and the trend measure, which shows whether what dissent costs is rising while the risk stays flat.

\runin{Clearance as a finding about a person}

Every answer above runs through a clearance, and a clearance is the finding chapter~\ref{sec:14} says a floor should never rest on: a judgment about a person's disposition, made by an executive official, on reasons the person may not see, reviewed only if someone challenges afterward. The record of what that finding does when the person has objected is American. In 1954 the Atomic Energy Commission revoked J.~Robert Oppenheimer's clearance on associations it had cleared him over for a decade and on an incident it had known of since 1943. What had changed was that he had chaired the committee that recommended against the crash hydrogen-bomb program in 1949, and had gone on saying so. The board that heard his case found him loyal and recommended against him anyway; the Commission, four to one, revoked the clearance on “fundamental defects in his ‘character,’” and in 2022 the Energy Department vacated the decision, finding the process flawed \autocite{aec1954transcript,aec1954decision,doe2022oppenheimer}. The year before the hearing, Executive Order 10450 had made “sexual perversion” a ground of denial, and the purge of gay and lesbian federal employees, under way since the late 1940s, widened under it and removed thousands of them under the heading of blackmail risk \autocite{eo10450,johnson2004lavender}. Beside the Radikalenerlass of chapter~\ref{sec:14}, these are three instances of one pattern: the security vocabulary carrying a loyalty finding, and the loyalty finding carrying a view.

A background investigation can't be made a log or a lot, and this book doesn't pretend otherwise; it is listed among the decisions not yet made (Appendix~\ref{sec:C}). What can be done is to strip from it everything that made those three cases possible, and five rules do that.

\emph{Conduct, not status.} A denial rests on a logged fact about conduct: a contact undisclosed, material mishandled, a false statement on a form. It never rests on association, opinion or character. That is the line \emph{Robinson v.\ California} draws for the criminal law \autocite{robinson1962} and \S\ref{sec:14.1} draws for the floor's terms, applied to the one finding the floor can't remove.

\emph{Draw, then clear.} The lot draws from the uncleared pool, and clearance follows the draw. A pool the operator clears before the draw is a pool the operator composed, and no denial in it ever has to be justified. A person drawn and then denied is a named person, denied on a record, for a stated reason.

\emph{A denial is an empty seat.} A reviewer drawn and denied leaves a seat the record shows empty, and an empty seat is a breach of the pre-registered denominator, which already fires on funding (\S\ref{sec:13.6}). The operator can deny. It can't deny for free and it can't deny quietly, and the denial rate on drawn reviewers is published beside the ratio (\S\ref{sec:7.1}).

\emph{The decision off the operator.} The investigation runs through the executive's machinery, since nobody else has it. The decision on a pool member does not: it sits with the floor court, or with the committee that receives the ratio, and the operator's remedy is a challenge there. Defense counsel in cases under the Classified Information Procedures Act are cleared to see the classified evidence against their clients \autocite[\S\S\,3–4]{cipa1980}; congressional committees choose the staff cleared to read what the executive sends them; and the court under the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Act seats amici eligible for access to classified information \autocite{usafreedom2015}. Chapter~\ref{sec:10} gives elected delegates this already. The pool gets the same.

\emph{A small compartment, a large pool.} The import condition above brings evidence into the enclave so that people needn't be. A reviewer checking whether a site is a school needs the target coding and the no-strike data, not sources and methods. The smaller the compartment the review needs, the larger the pool that can be cleared into it, and the less any one denial can move.
