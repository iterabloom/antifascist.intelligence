\section{The exit the right depends on}
\label{sec:10.2}
The right to refuse depends on the exit, and the dependence belongs here, where the right is, and not only in the appendix that drafts the exit. Anti-retaliation protection is a remedy: it reaches a dismissal after the fact, through a forum, and the person who refused has to be able to wait for it. A worker whose living ends with the refusal can't, and under capture the forum is the employer's or the state's. Part~I's refusers paid for refusing. What makes the right usable the morning after is an offer of work from someone other than the employer, open every day, at a living wage: a jobs guarantee, which is a statute. It is asked for now, for everyone and on its own grounds, and a later chapter makes the case and Appendix~\ref{sec:C} drafts it. The fifth policy is two things, and both are statute. The right, with the council's veto and the arbitration behind it, is codetermination, which in Germany is a creature of statute and not of contract: a works council holds its powers under the Works Constitution Act, not under any agreement with the employer \autocite{germany2001betrvg}. The exit is the guarantee. A right without the exit is a right to sue, later, for a living already lost. Chapter~\ref{ch:keeping} comes back to the guarantee, where it stops being a remedy for one refuser and becomes part of how a public reproduces itself.
