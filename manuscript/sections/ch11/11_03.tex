\section{What an Antifascist Detector Would Actually Have to Detect}
\label{sec:11.3}
This book gives fascism a structural definition, settles which scale a system can work at, and says what the resulting tool must never be permitted to output. Three earlier chapters lean on a system that can recognize authoritarian tactics as they happen, at four sites, and each says the capability is unspecified. The recuperation argument goes further and proposes that systems be built to detect the four-feature signature in institutions, and the definition's seven strategies include misuse caught while it is happening. None of them says what such a system would measure, and this section owes them the answer.

\runin{Why the general framing yields no targets}
Most of the difficulty is not technical. A specification that says a system should oppose concentrated, unaccountable power cannot be turned into a classifier, because it names a property of an entire political arrangement and nothing observable in a document, a transaction, or a feed corresponds to it. The four features used to define fascism are an improvement, since each points at an artifact somebody could go and look at. They are also, on this book's own account, the half that does not discriminate: they mark an institution as sick and not what it is sick with. Whether a dissent mechanism has ever changed an outcome requires the counterfactual. Whether a metric admits an unmeasured remainder requires knowing what the remainder contains. Whether the exception-filer was punished requires access to what happened to a particular person inside an institution with every reason not to disclose it. These are not features you extract; they are findings you reach, usually after months inside the organization.

\runin{What would actually have to be measured}
A detector worth building would need at least three things this book cannot currently tell an engineer how to obtain.

It would need longitudinal access rather than a snapshot. Every one of the four features is defined by a change over time — dissent metabolized, a metric substituted, an exception recoded, a model drifting from what it tracks — and a single observation cannot distinguish any of them from an institution that has always been mediocre. The slope rather than the level is the instrument, and its data requirement is demanding: the same measurement, taken the same way, over years, by someone the institution cannot fire.

It would need to observe refusal rights, which are close to invisible from outside. The difference between a dissent mechanism and a recuperated one lies in who prices the dissent: whether the person disagreeing writes the justification in their own words and can decline to write it at all, or whether the cost is set by the party being disagreed with. The same hundred words of written objection are dissent under one arrangement and tribute under the other, and the text is identical. There is no feature of the document that separates them.

It would need a base rate, and none exists. Nobody has assembled a labeled corpus of institutions annotated for the four features by people who agree on what they mean, and it is not obvious the annotation task is even well posed. The argument for learning from disagreement applies here with unusual force: on this question, disagreement between annotators is likely to be the most informative signal available, and treating it as noise would discard what a researcher needs.

\runin{The discrimination problem, and the half of the definition that answers it}
Suppose all of that were solved. What remains looks like the problem the definition hands forward: separating an institution that has recuperated its dissent mechanism from one that is merely badly run. Both produce a channel that exists, is used, and changes nothing. Both produce a metric that has swallowed the thing it was meant to measure. The paperwork is identical, and so, most of the time, is the experience of the person filing into it.

Stated that way the problem is unanswerable, and it is stated that way only because everything above it has been built on the four features. The definition already supplies the answer and it is five other marks, pitched at exactly this scale: participation exceeding what the rule requires, personal loyalty overriding role, an internal category whose adverse treatment is the category's purpose, procedure enforced downward and waived upward, and deniability — the practice disowned in the policy and rewarded in the promotion. Decay is an institution failing to do what it says. What the five detect is an institution succeeding at something else, with participation, a target, and a story about why the rules do not apply to whoever is applying them. Those are the detection targets. The four are what says where to look.

That ordering is the design, and it disposes of the objection that the instrument accuses everybody. A tool reporting the four features reports a condition nearly every institution meets, which is why its output is an accusation rather than a finding. A tool that reports the four and then asks the five has a second gate, and most of what the first gate flags does not pass it. The one time this book runs the specification, that is what happens: three instances flagged on the four, and all three cleared on the five.

\runin{What the five cost to observe}
They are harder than the four, and the difficulty is specific rather than general. Each of the five is a fact about what an institution rewarded rather than about what it wrote, and rewards are recorded in different places from rules — in who was promoted, in which exception was granted to whom, in what a manager tolerated and never wrote down.

Three of them are within reach of records an organization already keeps, if it will hand them over. Participation beyond the rule needs the rule and the behavior side by side, which is two documents. Loyalty overriding role needs the exception log to carry who asked, not only what was decided. The internal category needs the hardest inferential step of the five, because the observable — a group treated worse — is common to both diagnoses, and what separates them is whether the adverse treatment is a cost somebody minimized or a purpose somebody served.

Two are not reachable from outside at all. Whether procedure is waived upward and whether a disowned practice is rewarded in the promotion are both facts about personnel, and no organization publishes them. That is not a conjecture: it is what happened when the specification was run, where three of the five could be settled from the public record and two were left undetermined, and the two left undetermined were these. An instrument for the five therefore needs the thing an instrument for the four also needed, and needs more of it — not merely years of access, but access to what the institution rewarded.

The longitudinal argument survives all of this and changes its object. Where the difference between recuperation and mediocrity does surface, it surfaces over time and under pressure, in what an institution does when the channel finally carries something expensive: the merely mediocre organization sometimes acts, late and clumsily, and the other produces a fuller account of why it did not. That remains true. What the five add is that it need not be the only instrument, and that a snapshot which can reach an exception log and a promotion record is not as blind as a snapshot of the four.

The temptation to ship the tool anyway is strongest here. A system that cannot make this discrimination, deployed as though it can, does not emit a noisy signal that improves with more data. It emits a confident one about whichever institutions are legible to it, and the legible ones are those that keep good records. The correlation runs in the wrong direction: the organizations easiest to audit have the least to hide, and they are what the tool would learn to talk about.

\runin{What I am claiming and what I am not}
I am not claiming the project is impossible. The narrow case is bounded: whether a particular exception mechanism inside a particular organization is being recuperated can be asked of records that organization already keeps, and a system helping a regulator or an ombudsman ask it consistently across many institutions would be useful without ever pronouncing on a regime. The answer to the scale question makes that the whole job, not a first approximation of a larger one.

What I am claiming is that this book proposes a capability it has not specified. The five have been run once, by hand, against this book's own three instances, and cleared all three — which establishes that the definition discriminates and not that anyone can build the detector. A hand read of three cases by the person who wrote the definition is the weakest evidence that would count for anything, and it is what there is. The gap between the narrow case and an indicator-based early-warning system is the measure of how far this is from done.

\runin{The near-term work}
One organization, one exception mechanism, the records it already keeps. Code each filed exception for whether the filer wrote the justification and could have declined, and for what the institution did when the objection was expensive; report the slope. Code the same exceptions for the three reachable marks of the five — whether anyone did more than the rule required, who asked for each exception granted, and whether the disfavored category's treatment is a cost or a purpose — and report those as a second column rather than folding them into the first. The two unreachable marks are the price of admission for the next study and not this one. The falsifier is in the coding. Two coders will disagree, and that is signal — but it has to concentrate in particular cases and periods rather than scatter evenly, which is what disagreement about the words themselves looks like. Unpatterned disagreement stops the program here. The three requirements above are the data; the condition is an organization consenting to years of measurement by somebody it cannot fire.
