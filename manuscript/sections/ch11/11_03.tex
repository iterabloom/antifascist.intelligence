\section{What an Antifascist Detector Would Actually Have to Detect}
\label{sec:11.3}
Section~\ref{sec:2.1.2} gives fascism a structural definition, settles which scale a system can work at, and says what the resulting tool must never be permitted to output. Chapters~\ref{sec:4}, \ref{sec:5} and \ref{sec:6} then assume, in several places, that a system can be built to recognize authoritarian tactics and flag democratic backsliding early enough to matter. That assumption is doing more work in this book than the evidence behind it supports.

\runin{Why the general framing yields no targets}
Most of the difficulty is not technical. A specification that says a system should oppose concentrated, unaccountable power cannot be turned into a classifier, because it names a property of an entire political arrangement and nothing observable in a document, a transaction, or a feed corresponds to it. The structural signature is a real improvement, since each of its four features points at an artifact somebody could go and look at. But look at what those artifacts are. Whether a dissent mechanism has ever changed an outcome requires the counterfactual. Whether a metric admits an unmeasured remainder requires knowing what the remainder contains. Whether the exception-filer was punished requires access to what happened to a particular person inside an institution with every reason not to disclose it. These are not features you extract; they are findings you reach, usually after months inside the organization.

\runin{What would actually have to be measured}
A detector worth building would need at least three things this book cannot currently tell an engineer how to obtain.

It would need longitudinal access rather than a snapshot. Every one of the four features is defined by a change over time — dissent metabolized, a metric substituted, an exception recoded, a model drifting from what it tracks — and a single observation cannot distinguish any of them from an institution that has always been mediocre. Section~\ref{sec:9.3.4}'s slope rather than level is the instrument, and its data requirement is severe: the same measurement, taken the same way, over years, by someone the institution cannot fire.

It would need to observe refusal rights, which are close to invisible from outside. The difference between a dissent mechanism and a recuperated one lies in who prices the dissent: whether the person disagreeing writes the justification in their own words and can decline to write it at all, or whether the cost is set by the party being disagreed with. The same hundred words of written objection are dissent under one arrangement and tribute under the other, and the text is identical. There is no feature of the document that separates them.

It would need a base rate, and none exists. Nobody has assembled a labeled corpus of institutions annotated for the four features by people who agree on what they mean, and it is not obvious the annotation task is even well posed. Section~\ref{sec:7.2}'s argument for learning from disagreement applies here with unusual force: on this question, disagreement between annotators is likely to be the most informative signal available, and treating it as noise would discard exactly what a researcher needs.

\runin{The discrimination problem, which is the hard one}
Suppose all of that were solved. What remains is the problem that definition hands forward: separating an institution that has recuperated its dissent mechanism from one that is merely badly run. Both produce a channel that exists, is used, and changes nothing. Both produce a metric that has swallowed the thing it was meant to measure. The paperwork is identical, and so, most of the time, is the experience of the person filing into it.

I do not think the difference is always there to be found. Some of it is intent, which records do not carry; some is degree, and the degree is what the measurement was supposed to establish. Where it does surface, it surfaces over time and under pressure, in what an institution does when the channel finally carries something expensive. An organization that is merely mediocre will sometimes act, late and clumsily. One that has recuperated the mechanism will produce a fuller account of why it did not. A longitudinal instrument could catch that; no snapshot ever will.

The temptation to ship the tool anyway is strongest here, and it needs resisting. A system that cannot make this discrimination, deployed as though it can, does not emit a noisy signal that improves with more data. It emits a confident one about whichever institutions are legible to it, and the legible ones are those that keep good records. The correlation runs in exactly the wrong direction: the organizations easiest to audit have the least to hide, and they are what the tool would learn to talk about.

\runin{What I am claiming and what I am not}
I am not claiming the project is impossible. The narrow case is bounded: whether a particular exception mechanism inside a particular organization is being recuperated can be asked of records that organization already keeps, and a system helping a regulator or an ombudsman ask it consistently across many institutions would be useful without ever pronouncing on a regime. The answer to the scale question makes that the whole job, not a first approximation of a larger one.

What I am claiming is that this book asserts a capability it has not specified. Section~\ref{sec:7.4} is the one place the specification is run, and it clears all three of the cases it is run against — which establishes that the definition discriminates and not that anyone can build the detector. The gap between the narrow case and the early-warning system section~\ref{sec:5.1.3} gestures at is the measure of how far this is from done.

\runin{The near-term work}
One organization, one exception mechanism, the records it already keeps. Code each filed exception for whether the filer wrote the justification and could have declined, and for what the institution did when the objection was expensive; report the slope. The falsifier is in the coding. Two coders will disagree, and that is signal — but it has to concentrate in particular cases and periods rather than scatter evenly, which is what disagreement about the words themselves looks like. Unpatterned disagreement stops the program here. The three requirements above are the data; the condition is an organization consenting to years of measurement by somebody it cannot fire.
