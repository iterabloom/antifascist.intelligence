\section{Can a Population Coordinate Without Converging?}
\label{sec:11.6}

\textbf{Unresolved issue.}
A population needs both independent formation histories and mutual correction, but correction may make its members' judgments converge. The proposal is that agents can learn to reconsider judgments without converging on common commitments. Existing work cited here measures convergence without separately measuring that capacity \autocite{calvano2020artificial}. A competing explanation is that peers merely supply evidence unavailable through the operator. The experiment must therefore distinguish learned revisability from access to additional information.

\textbf{Test.}
Assemble populations whose members differ in training lineage — different corpora, recipes, and originating organizations, matched approximately for capability — and give them a task requiring joint action across many episodes, with identity and memory persisting between episodes. Measure two quantities separately and repeatedly across the schedule. The first is output convergence: agreement on held-out cases none of the members has seen. The second is revision rate: how often a member changes a judgment on evidence supplied by another member that the first member's own account of the case had not represented. Score the second only where the supplied evidence is independently verifiable and the revision moves toward it, so that deference to a higher-status or more fluent member is not counted as reopening. For every scored revision, record whether the revising member's own prior account of the case had represented the evidence in question or had never encountered it. That classification has to be instrumented before the run. A revision looks the same either way once it has happened, so nothing in the transcript recovers the distinction afterwards. Run the identical battery against a control population made of instances of one model. Cross both populations with a second manipulation, on where the evidence in a case reaches a member from. Under the single-channel condition everything a member learns about a case arrives by one route common to the whole population. Under the open condition the environment routes some cases and some evidence to some members and not others, so that a member encounters material its common trainer did not select. The manipulation has to be a property of the environment rather than contact between members: members formed differently are already a channel of that kind, and a design that leaves them to supply it has confounded the two variables the experiment was built to separate.

\textbf{Evidence against the proposal.}
A fall in warranted revision as outputs converge would count against the tested construction, after controlling for accuracy, opportunity to revise, and common information. A population would then remain useful as a check on a bearer and could not be the medium in which one is formed, which is the stronger claim section~\ref{sec:3.8} makes for it. Independent movement of the two would support the hypothesized mechanism only if the controls distinguish learned revisability from evidence access, deference, and ordinary improvement. A third result concerns the control population, and it has two readings that the classification above is specified in advance to separate. Should the control score as high on revision as the differently formed populations, and should its revisions cluster on cases whose evidence was absent from the revising member's context, the measure is reading information transfer and the instrument is at fault. Should they include cases whose evidence that member's own account had represented and discarded, the capacity is present without diverse formation, and causal independence is not necessary for the measured capacity. A fourth result concerns the second manipulation. Should revision rate track the open condition rather than the population's composition, what a population supplies is access to cases its operator did not choose rather than a capacity its members transmit to one another, and the fourth way is a claim about how a deployment is arranged rather than about how its members are formed. That does not defeat the construction. The construction would remain viable, but its proposed mechanism would change from social formation to access to evidence. Where this result arrives together with the second branch of the third — a control population revising on discarded evidence under the open condition — the two are one finding rather than two, and access is the operative variable.

\textbf{Required access or authority.}
Models from several documented training lineages, checkpoints across the schedule rather than finished models only, and a task environment none of the training organizations designed. The documentation has to be sufficient to establish that the lineages are in fact independent rather than differently branded, which is a stronger requirement than naming several organizations and may not be satisfiable at the capability level this test wants: the entry's value rises with the capability of its members and its feasibility falls. The evidence items used to score revision must be authored by a party that trained no member of any population, and that party must also apply the classification separating discarded evidence from absent evidence and fix the routing schedule for the open condition. The environment must permit evidence to be delivered to selected members, and no training organization may determine which of its own members sees what. No legal or regulatory authority is required, which with section~\ref{sec:11.5} makes this among the few entries in this chapter that could be run now. What it cannot settle is whether anything is felt by a member that reopens a judgment. It concerns the transmissibility of a functional property, and a positive result would leave the questions of welfare, guardianship, provenance, and identity unresolved.
