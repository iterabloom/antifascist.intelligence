\section{Outcomes under the three adversaries}
\label{sec:11.7}
Run under the three adversaries of chapter~\ref{sec:4}, the statute prevents under an executive willing to carry it out, binds on paper and raises costs under a captured operator, and under total capture is a record of who was asked to end the agency and who signed (\S\ref{sec:11.4.4}). Two instruments behave in ways particular to this pipeline: the vendors' terms and notice.

The vendors first. The removal terms held by one vendor prevent nothing, since the lead goes to another contractor. Held by every vendor, they prevent the removals the terms name until the agency runs a system without them, at the price of rebuilding what was built on the floored one. A vendor that licenses a model into the pipeline without the schedule's terms, or after an audit has shown the model doesn't hold them, is liable in damages to the people harmed, and its officers who knew are criminally liable, in the floor court, on a prosecution brought by counsel the court appoints.

Notice is conditional. In a targeting pipeline notice and standing can only remedy afterward (\S\ref{sec:8.2}). Here they can act before the removal, because the person is there to be told, but only where the law makes the operator wait for the answer. Without a stay that binds by operation of law, and counsel to use it, the 2025 record is the outcome. A man with a lawyer, a pending claim and a hearing date was flown out on the evening a federal judge ordered the planes returned. Under an operator willing to carry out the statute, notice prevents. Under a captured operator, it raises the cost of outrunning it and leaves a record of each time the operator did. Under total capture it leaves only the record, which is why the sixth term moves the record out of the operator's reach before the agency ends.
