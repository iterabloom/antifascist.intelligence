\chapter{What Would Change the Argument: A Research Agenda}
\label{sec:11}

This chapter retains only open questions that could materially revise or defeat the proposal developed in the preceding chapters. Each entry specifies four things: the unresolved issue, the test or institutional decision that could address it, the evidence that would count against the proposal, and the access or independent authority required to obtain that evidence.

Questions that cannot yet produce a definite experiment, decision, or possible falsification remain where they arise in the argument rather than being repeated here. The entries are ordered by their importance to the proposal, not by their tractability. They divide by standing as the book does. Sections~\ref{sec:11.1},
\ref{sec:11.2} and \ref{sec:11.4} through \ref{sec:11.10} test the
specification, custody, and the institutional argument, and hold for any
mechanism. Section~\ref{sec:11.3} tests the route, and its embodiment
entry is where the route's one distinctive claim is decided.
