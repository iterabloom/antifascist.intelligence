\chapter{Necessary Areas of Research}
\label{sec:11}
Chapters~\ref{sec:2} through \ref{sec:10} ran into problems they could not close, and set each one aside with a note. This chapter is those notes, collected and put in order. It does not survey what the field has left to do: every entry is a gap this book's own argument opens, and they are ranked by what that argument fails without.

Two gaps this book records have no section here. Telling disagreement that deserves representation from disagreement produced by coercion or a captured information environment belongs to section~\ref{sec:7.2}, where the argument needing it is made; whether a model trained on collapsed disagreement is thereby less able to sustain its own belongs to section~\ref{sec:7.3}, which says nothing has tested it.

The criterion produces an order almost nobody would choose on other grounds. The problems this book most needs solved are not the ones nearest to being solved, and the first two entries are the least tractable things here. A funder ranking by progress per dollar, or a lab ranking by what its people could start on Monday, would invert most of this order. It is an account of what the book is running on credit.

Each section closes by naming four things: the experiment that could begin now with means that already exist, the result that would falsify the approach, the data or access it would take, and, where it has one, the institutional condition without which it cannot run. Where there is no near-term experiment the section says so rather than inventing one, and two say so — the aggregation half of section~\ref{sec:11.5}, and section~\ref{sec:11.9} entirely. Section~\ref{sec:11.2}'s third question has no welfare experiment and reaches for one about provenance instead, and it is the second-ranked problem in the chapter.

One qualification governs every gap named here. Where I say a problem is unaddressed, the claim is bounded: I looked for published work under a particular description and did not find it, which is weaker than no such work existing. A search is not a survey, a program can be underway under a name I did not think to search, and the closer a gap sits to this book's own framing the less likely anyone has posed it in these words. What it does support is narrower and still useful: a researcher entering here will not find the problem already named and waiting. Each section names the nearest work I could find and says where it stops, because the distance between an existing program and the missing thing is what a researcher can use.

One finding comes out of the sections below rather than out of any one of them, and taking each gap separately is what hid it. Five of the nine close by naming an institutional condition, and the five are one condition wearing different clothes. Weights a vendor cannot withdraw when a result embarrasses it. A guardian who is not also the operator. Longitudinal access by somebody an institution cannot fire, reporting to a body with standing to receive the finding. Evaluation results published whether or not they flatter the method. Training checkpoints released rather than kept. Every one puts something in the hands of a party outside the operator, able to act on what it finds. Two of the remaining four close on a data requirement rather than on a party, and section~\ref{sec:11.9} is that condition itself. Section~\ref{sec:11.6} is the exception, closing with no institutional prerequisite at all, which is what makes it the one entry here that could start today.

What keeps a party outside is the question none of the five asks, and in the United States it was answered while this book was being finished. \emph{Trump v. Slaughter} removed, for most of the federal government, the protection from dismissal that made an outside party outside, and \emph{Trump v. Cook} kept it for one body on grounds no new body can have; section~\ref{sec:3.1} carries both. Independence decomposes into components — a director's tenure, a funding stream, a mandate — and an adversary takes the weakest: the consumer-finance bureau whose funding stream the Court had upheld in 2024 had that stream shut from inside in late 2025, by an acting director, removable at will since 2020, who declined to request the money under a reading of the statute that two courts then rejected \autocite{cfpb2025funding,seilalaw2020,cfsa2024}. A condition that names the outside party and not what keeps it there has named half of what it needs. So the binding constraint on the research program below is not a research problem. Most of this work is fundable today and cannot be run.
