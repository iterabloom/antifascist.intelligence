\section{Before the statute: the auto-proceed class}
\label{sec:11.5}
Until the statute passes, the pipeline runs. This section and the three after it describe what a floor can do meanwhile. Each measure is justified by what it does now for the people the pipeline acts on, and by what it adds to the record and the public the statute will need. Each lapses when the statute takes effect, and none is offered as a way to make the pipeline run better. A measure that would make the pipeline more defensible without making anyone safer fails both tests, and the chapter drops it.

The removal terms say a determination made at a rate no adjudicator could sustain didn't occur. The review ratio governs the auto-proceed class by a pre-registered partition, and here the partition has a direction. The floor makes acting on a person require more than declining to act, and in an abduction and forcible transfer pipeline the act is adverse: a charging document, a detention, a location, a removal. So a case may auto-proceed only toward liberty, such as release on recognizance, termination of proceedings, return of documents, or a grant or renewal of status. No adverse act is ever in an auto-proceed class. Before operating, the deployment pre-registers the liberty classes, the thresholds that assign cases to them, the evidence the sorting may rely on, and the expected share of each.

For adverse determinations it pre-registers something else: for each kind of determination, the minimum time below which the determination is taken not to have occurred. The record's recipient sets that minimum, not the agency, just as the recipient sets the review ratio's denominator. A quota of 700 cases a year is about three a working day. Whether three a day is a rate an adjudicator can sustain on an asylum claim is a question the recipient answers in advance, on a record. Otherwise the agency answers it afterward by meeting the number. A validation guide that turns a tattoo into points is a determination made in no time at all, and Table C.2's individual-determination entry says it didn't occur.

Then the determinations are sampled. Reviewers who didn't produce the record re-adjudicate a sample of adverse determinations, weighted toward those made below the minimum, against evidence the agency didn't compose, and the error rate is published. When a kind of determination's sampled error rate exceeds its published bound, that kind is suspended until the bound is met, and everyone it was applied to is notified. The liberty classes are sampled too, so that their bounds are honest. An error there releases someone who should have been held for a hearing, and the hearing can still be held. Unlike Maven's, this pipeline's errors can be found, because the people they fell on are alive, can be located and can say what happened. Sampling finds the errors the people couldn't report. The statute's third term is what lets them report the rest.
