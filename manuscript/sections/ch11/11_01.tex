\section{The pipeline}
\label{sec:11.1}
The American abduction and forcible transfer pipeline is assembled from contracts the way Maven was. Immigration and Customs Enforcement's case-management system, the Investigative Case Management system, is Palantir's, under a five-year contract signed in 2022. It filters people by fields that include resident and entry status, criminal affiliation, location and licence-plate data, country of origin, race, birthplace and place of employment \autocite{Koebler2025ICM}. In April 2025 ICE paid Palantir about \$30 million to extend it into the Immigration Lifecycle Operating System, ImmigrationOS. The contract promised to cut the time spent selecting and apprehending people according to enforcement priorities, to add near real-time visibility into self-deportation, and to manage the “end to end immigration lifecycle from identification to removal” \autocite{Ho2025ImmigrationOS,Hubbard2025ImmigrationOS}. A follow-on task order renewed it in September 2025 \autocite{Jamison2025ImmigrationOSTaskOrder}. A second Palantir tool, ELITE, puts potential deportation targets on a map, with a dossier on each person and a confidence score for the person's current address. Its addresses come from several sources, among them the Department of Health and Human Services \autocite{Cox2026ELITE}.

In the street, agents identify people with Mobile Fortify, a phone app that captures a face or contactless fingerprints and checks them in real time against the system CBP uses to photograph travellers at the border \autocite{Cox2025MobileFortify}.

The data come from agencies that collected them for other purposes. In January 2026 Medicaid officials shared data on millions of people with immigration officials, going beyond what a court had allowed, and ICE passed the data to Palantir \autocite{JoffeBlock2026MedicaidPalantir}. ICE also requested the addresses of 1.28 million taxpayers. The request produced 47,289 records before a court stopped it, and the stop was affirmed in September 2026 \autocite{Shaw2026IRSICE}.

Leads also come from contractors paid to find people. In late 2025 ICE contracted thirteen companies, in awards that could reach \$1.2 billion over two years, to locate people from case data and send back photographs confirming where they live or work \autocite{Biddle2025BountyHunters,Hubbard2026SkipTracing}. Selection on speech has its own tools. In March 2025 the State Department launched an AI-assisted effort, “Catch and Revoke,” to review the social media of student visa holders for apparent support of Hamas \autocite{Caputo2025CatchRevoke}. In September 2025 a federal court found that the Secretaries of State and Homeland Security had “acted in concert to misuse the sweeping powers of their respective offices to target noncitizen pro-Palestinians for deportation primarily on account of their First Amendment protected political speech” \autocite{aaup2025}.

The pipeline's last step can be a foreign government. The flights of March 2025 ended in CECOT under the paid arrangement described at the opening of this chapter \autocite{Renteria2025ElSalvador}. In June 2025 the Supreme Court let removals to third countries proceed without the written notice and meaningful chance to raise a torture claim that a district court had required \autocite{dvd2025}.

Two statements of the ambition are on the record. At the 2025 Border Security Expo, ICE's acting director said the agency needed “to get better at treating this like a business” and wanted a deportation process “like (Amazon) Prime, but with human beings” \autocite{MacDonaldEvoy2025Prime}. In 2018 the Justice Department made 700 completed cases a year a condition of a satisfactory rating for immigration judges \autocite{eoir2018metrics}.

What a case description looks like here, and who writes it, is on the record in a way Maven's never was. The charging document, the Notice to Appear, has to state the acts alleged, the charges and the statutory provisions \autocite{usc1229}, and any of a long list of immigration officers may issue one \autocite{cfr239_1}. The government once conceded that almost every notice it issued over three years omitted the hearing's time and date \autocite{pereira2018}. The account of the person comes from the arresting officer's narrative on Form I-213. Agents routinely complete it after interviewing someone, it is presumed reliable unless the person shows otherwise, and its author need not appear to be cross-examined \autocite{espinoza1995,lopezmendoza1984}. The Alien Enemies Act removals had a case description of their own: a validation guide that scored people in categories including tattoos and dress. Anyone scoring eight points or more was validated as a member of the gang, and a score of six or seven went to a supervisor and a legal adviser \autocite{MartinezBeltran2025Validation}. The dossiers and confidence scores of the newer tools have no named author at all; they are assembled from database fields. The validation guide had authors, a points table and a threshold, which made it a score with a rubric attached. In the Maven run the case description was a target coding nobody outside could see. Here it is an officer's narrative, a set of fields, a score and a points table, all of which exist as documents a person could be shown.
