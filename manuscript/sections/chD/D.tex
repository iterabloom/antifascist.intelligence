\chapter{The Court Program}
\label{sec:D}
One holding stands between the record and the commander at the top. \emph{Trump v. United States} makes a President presumptively immune for official acts, commanding the armed forces among them, and bars testimony probing immune conduct from a jury \autocite{trump2024immunity}. Every instrument I propose takes effect through a statute, procurement, appropriation or coalition conditions, and none waits on changing that, or on the court below, since until it exists an ordinary Article III court hears floor claims. What changing it would take is a program of SCOTUS appointments run at historic speed. Although a smidge quicker than some folks may be used to, this program keeps the checking intact at any pace: the schedule is published in advance, the pool is published before the draw, every nominee gets a confirmation, and the lot is run in public.

\emph{Size by schedule:} once there is a trifecta and the filibuster is gone, appointments to the Court come on a fixed schedule, ninety-nine new seats in a single year, because size is the one step the Court cannot review. The first appointees' active terms are staggered by lot, as the first Senate's were, so that about six fall due each year, and the schedule is six a year after that. \emph{Two courts, two panels and a division:} an inferior Article III floor court, its judges drawn at random from the district and circuit courts for staggered terms, hears claims under the floor statute at first instance, holds classified records under the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court's procedures and grants interim relief, with appeal as of right to a floor panel of the Supreme Court drawn by lot, while placement disputes under the jobs guarantee run through an administrative tier and an Article III guarantee court of appeals to a separate guarantee panel, and appeals in immigration cases, claims under the abolition statute's remedy term among them (chapter~\ref{sec:11}), go to an immigration division of nine seats created for them. \emph{One court, reviewable in full:} a panel or the division decides for the Court unless the full Court, on the vote of two-thirds of its members, grants review, so that the Court remains what the 2021 Presidential Commission on the Supreme Court said a divided court must be, a body that operates “in some meaningful sense as a single ‘Court’” \autocite[89]{scotuscommission2021}. A large high court divided this way is ordinary elsewhere: Egypt's Court of Cassation has over 450 justices in thirty-three divisions, and Italy's about 370 judges in thirteen, with a united division that settles conflicts between the others \autocite{fjc2026egypt,fjc2026italy}. \emph{An adversary as of right:} counsel for the people a deployment affects appear in the floor court with clearance and standing as of right and receive the records lodged with an adverse party. \emph{Move a class of cases, never leave it with none:} Congress may move a class of cases from one Article III forum to another and may never leave a class with no Article III forum at all. \emph{The statute, then the amendment:} once the Court has changed, a statute removes the immunity, and an amendment reverses the holding \autocite{biden2024amendment} and entrenches the schedule, eighteen-year active terms, the panels, the division and the channeling rule, coming last because it is the one step no later Congress or Court can undo.

Nine of the ninety-nine seats form the immigration division. Congress can create them, fund them and fix their jurisdiction; it can't say who fills them. A President can. She can nominate by lot from a published pool of counsel who have represented noncitizens against the government in federal court, and say so before the first draw. That answers a selection pipeline shaped in private (chapter~\ref{sec:19}) with one shaped in public. It still chooses by side, it binds only the President who adopts it, and every later vacancy goes to whoever then holds the office, so the division is as good as its first nominees for a generation and as good as its later ones after that.
