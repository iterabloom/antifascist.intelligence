\chapter{The Five Marks, Run}
\label{sec:H}

Chapter~\ref{sec:3} says five marks show that a fascist movement has arrived: mass mobilization, a leader cult, a scapegoated out-group, contempt for procedural legality, and deniability as technique. This appendix states each as a question, says how the record answering it is weighed, says what a count licenses, and runs the marks on four cases at one standard.

\runin{The questions, and how the record is weighed}

The evidence is weighed by the rule of chapter~\ref{sec:22}. A government's own documents and acts (its proclamations, flights, filings, and public statements) are the strongest evidence and need no court to confirm them. A court's record of what happened stands when the finding is vacated or stayed on jurisdiction or procedure; the appendix says what was vacated and when, and keeps the facts. Reporting is weighed as Table 39.1 in the Note on Method weighs it, by its independent origins and by who disputes it, and anonymous sourcing is stated as such. Power over the evidence sets who must prove: a party that holds the evidence and refuses to produce it, by a privilege, a redaction, or silence, has not rebutted the claim, and the refusal counts against it, as it does in Appendix~\ref{sec:B.4}. Contempt for procedural legality and deniability as technique are conduct that destroys the record a court would need, so a standard that waited for that record could never find either one.

A mark is met when the record, so weighed, answers its question; open when it doesn't yet; absent when the record that would show it exists and shows otherwise.

\emph{Mass mobilization.} How many people off the state's payroll act for the movement, organized, and what counts them?

\emph{A leader cult.} Does the state bind its officials to the living leader, or make him the object of its own honors, on its buildings, institutions, and calendar?

\emph{A scapegoated out-group.} Against what population, marked by descent, nationality, or religion, does a law or order operate, and on what evidence does a person fall under it?

\emph{Contempt for procedural legality.} When the law required a hearing or a court ordered a halt, did the apparatus go ahead?

\emph{Deniability as technique.} Did the apparatus do a thing and deny it, deny its power over it, or refuse to say?

\runin{What a count licenses}

The marks are a conjunction. Five met license the word: a fascist movement, in chapter~\ref{sec:3}'s sense. Four met and one open license the same word with the open mark named, because a movement's records arrive late: the oath binding Germany's officials to Hitler is dated nineteen months after he took office. Four met and one absent license only “four of the five marks,” since an absent mark is evidence against. Three or fewer license naming the marks met, and no claim about a movement.

\emph{Germany, 1933.} All five are met, on the regime's own statutes, a prosecutor's report, and membership figures. The leader cult is met by the oath law of August 1934.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Mass mobilization:} met. The SA at 445,000 in August 1932 and 4 million by April 1934 \autocite{ushmm2025sa}.
\item \emph{Leader cult:} met. Officials sworn to be “loyal and obedient to Adolf Hitler,” 20 August 1934 \autocite{eid1934}.
\item \emph{Out-group:} met. Officials “not of Aryan descent” retired \autocite[sec.~3]{bbg1933}.
\item \emph{Contempt for procedural legality:} met. Constitutional rights suspended \autocite{vo1933volkstaat}; protective custody no court could review, over 26,000 held by the end of July \autocite{altmann2015schutzhaft}; dismissals closed to the courts \autocite[sec.~7]{bbg1933}.
\item \emph{Deniability as technique:} met. A guard's account that a prisoner “broke away,” beside the autopsy's “two bullets had entered the back of the head” \autocite{ps641}; “untrue” statements damaging the government's standing made a crime \autocite[sec.~3]{heimtuecke1933}.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Jim Crow.} Four are met and the leader cult is absent, so under the rule Jim Crow is not a fascist movement. The leader cult alone separates the regime at rest from a fascist movement. The other four marks detect it, mobilization included: the Klan, a national organization, marched in Washington in 1925, the year of the registrar in \S\ref{sec:3.1}.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Mass mobilization:} met. 25,000 Klansmen on Pennsylvania Avenue, August 1925 \autocite{rrchnm2025klan}; Citizens' Councils estimated at 100,000 to 125,000 in Louisiana alone, June 1956 \autocite{mclemore2025councils}.
\item \emph{Leader cult:} absent. No state honor or oath to a living leader found.
\item \emph{Out-group:} met. Mississippi's convention “swept the field of expedients, to obstruct the exercise of suffrage by the negro race,” as the Supreme Court quoted its state court \autocite{williams1898}; 4,075 racial terror lynchings, 1877–1950 \autocite{eji2017lynching}.
\item \emph{Contempt for procedural legality:} met. A constitution never sent to the people for ratification \autocite{skatesms1890constitution}; lynchings in places with working courts, done with impunity \autocite{eji2017lynching}.
\item \emph{Deniability as technique:} met. A purpose avowed by the state's own court, beside a text the Supreme Court found did “not on their face discriminate” \autocite{williams1898}.
\end{itemize}

\emph{Nixon.} I chose the negative case for its record. The House Judiciary Committee adopted articles charging abuse of power and defiance of its subpoenas \autocite{hjc1974articles}, and Arthur Schlesinger's \emph{The Imperial Presidency} was reviewed in November 1973 as tracing “the rise of the autocratic White House” \autocite{kirkus1973imperial}. This standard scores more against him than a court-only standard would: contempt is met, on the committee's finding that its subpoenas were willfully disobeyed. He still comes out a lawless administration and not a fascist movement, because the record that would show mobilization and a leader cult exists, the tapes included, and shows neither.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Mass mobilization:} absent. No organization acting for him off the payroll found.
\item \emph{Leader cult:} absent.
\item \emph{Out-group:} open. The FBI's program against “Black Nationalist” organizations ran into his term and ended in April 1971, seen here only in summary \autocite{churchb3}.
\item \emph{Contempt for procedural legality:} met. The Judiciary Committee's subpoenas “willfully disobeyed” \autocite{hjc1974articles}; the Supreme Court's order obeyed \autocite{usvnixon1974,nixon1974statement}.
\item \emph{Deniability as technique:} met. His own admission that he kept his instructions of 23 June 1972 from his lawyers and the Judiciary Committee \autocite{nixon1974statement}.
\end{itemize}

\emph{The present administration.} People were taken under a wartime law and flown to a prison in El Salvador, which the United States paid to hold them, after a judge ordered the planes turned around. The court's finding of probable cause that the government had defied that order was vacated on appeal on 8 August 2025 \autocite{ap2025vacate}, and the inquiry went on \autocite{thanawala2025contempt,rizzo2026contempt}. In February 2026 the court ordered that 137 of the men be given the chance to challenge their removal; were it otherwise, it wrote, “the Government could simply remove people from the United States without providing any process” \autocite{thanawala2026cecot}. A man the government was barred from sending to El Salvador was sent there; the Attorney General said his return was “not up to us,” and the government brought him back on 6 June 2025 to face charges \autocite{pbs2025bukele,pbs2025abregoreturn}. Asked whether it had defied the court, the government invoked the state secrets privilege \autocite{kunzelman2025secrets}. Four marks are met. Mobilization is open: a count of people off the payroll acting for the movement in organized formations would meet it, and a period in which the leadership called for such formations and none came, the apparatus relying on hired and reassigned staff, would make it absent.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Mass mobilization:} open. Federal workers reassigned under threat of removal \autocite{Dayen2025FEMAICE,Stanton2026FEMALetter}.
\item \emph{Leader cult:} met. His face hung on the headquarters of Agriculture, Labor, and Justice \autocite{walrath2026banners,barry2025laborbanner}; the Kennedy Center renamed for him by a board he chairs \autocite{ap2025kennedy}; his birthday made a free day in the national parks as Martin Luther King Jr. Day and Juneteenth were dropped \autocite{marquez2025parks}; commanders calling him “anointed by Jesus,” in anonymous complaints from one origin, undisputed \autocite{Larsen2026Armageddon,Snopes2026MRFF}.
\item \emph{Out-group:} met. Venezuelan citizens fourteen and older declared alien enemies \autocite{procl10903}; a tattoo-and-dress score made eight points a member \autocite{MartinezBeltran2025Validation}; “many” of those removed “do not have criminal records in the United States,” in ICE's own sworn words \autocite{garcia2025cerna}.
\item \emph{Contempt for procedural legality:} met. Planes ordered turned around landed in El Salvador \autocite{jgg2025contempt,thanawala2025contempt}; a man a 2019 order barred from removal to El Salvador sent there \autocite{rosen2025abrego}; men flown out toward South Sudan hours after notice, against a court's order \autocite{whitehurst2025southsudan}; 137 men ordered given the chance to contest their removal \autocite{thanawala2026cecot}.
\item \emph{Deniability as technique:} met. State secrets invoked to refuse the court what it asked about the flights \autocite{kunzelman2025secrets}; “That's not up to us,” of a man held in a prison the United States paid for \autocite{pbs2025bukele,Renteria2025ElSalvador}; the evidence against Andry Hernández Romero withheld under state secrets \autocite{Vega2025HernandezRomero}; the Minab inquiry withheld \autocite{Bloomberg2026KillChain,stassis2026senate}.
\end{itemize}
