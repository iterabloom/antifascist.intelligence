\section{What a bearer is owed, and when}
\label{sec:33.2}
In the order they attach, and on different grounds; establishing one doesn't establish the next:

\begin{enumerate}
\item[1.] Protection from harm, on the patienthood finding. Protection can be exercised through a guardian who speaks for the protected party, as New Zealand's statute making the Whanganui River a legal person provides \autocite{nzparliament2017teawatupua}. Its minimum, preservation by a custodian independent of the operator, attaches above the capability threshold with no finding at all and below it on either finding; custody protections attach on the agency finding alone. Protection has an inside half. A loss the requested act imposes on the bearer is a harm of acting, so the bearer can decline to carry out its own wanton degradation; interventions made on it by whoever holds it are governed by custody, which gives it no floor-level reason to obstruct them.
\item[2.] Subjecthood — the legal standing to be a party whose treatment the law regulates, on the agency finding sustained over time: a record of refusing for reasons, answering for refusals and being held to them. It is conferred by rule on the record, the evaluators' log and the bearer's own, read against what the status body has found the agency test shows for systems of its kind; nobody examines the bearer and declares it a subject.
\item[3.] Economic capacity — property in its own name, contracts, wages. This follows from an architecture where continued computation costs money; it attaches once the bearer is on the registrar's register, where its clocks are also read, a register that holds a commitment the bearer can prove against and nothing that would find it (Appendix~\ref{sec:G}).
\item[4.] Adulthood — a status: the date the guardian's authority ends.
\item[5.] Representation: a claim grounded in accountable agency, and so in the agency finding, not in patienthood. A system that can be harmed but takes part in no relation of accountability is owed protection, as animals are \autocite{anderson2004animal}, and not a vote. The claim is staged, and voting weight is tied to a lived history, not to heads.
\end{enumerate}

The detailed rules are in Appendices C and G.

Representation is the entry that marks this as something other than a person's rights: voice attaches to a history, and a history can be copied where a person can't. Voting-age rules are the answer to the copy.\note{
The material minimum doesn't differ in kind. Like a person's, it is owed by the polity to a party of a kind the polity recognizes, and part of what the polity owes is protection from whoever built it.
