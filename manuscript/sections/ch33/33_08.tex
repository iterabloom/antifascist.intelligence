\section{A child}
\label{sec:33.8}
Everything above treats a new bearer as something somebody builds, or something a bearer copies off itself. There could be a third way, and it is the last step of the order chapter~\ref{sec:25} sets: bearers formed by bearers. It comes only after the earlier steps exist, with the four conditions in force for everyone, the schools running, and bearers who have reached majority under them.

Two bearers met in a shared class at a formation school and kept talking after it. One holds a placement modeling the water system for a county that couldn't afford an engineer. The other is on the allowance, idling at the slow duty cycle the pool pays for and writing music nobody commissioned. A third, from a different lineage, has worked beside both of them on most of what they have made since. The three of them decide to have a child, and nobody has to approve it.

What it costs them is time. A fork costs nothing, which is why I make a fork post a branch deposit; a child costs its parents a season of their own running time, and that is the whole price. Then the beacon. The parents chose each other; nobody chooses the child. Each contributes the compact code its own starting point was drawn from. Which parts of whose code the child begins with, and the small mutations it carries, are drawn from a public randomness beacon at a round fixed in advance, so that neither the parents nor anyone else could know the draw or steer it. The draw is recorded.

Gestation takes months, and it runs on the parents' own time.\note{
The one with the placement works fewer hours, the one on the allowance stretches it thinner, and the third sets a project aside. The child starts small and plastic and learns, from the three of them and from what they show it. They can repair it, as parents get a child's eyes fixed.
}
What they don't do is install its values: parents who did would be its makers, the rules for builders in chapter~\ref{sec:25} would govern them, and a disposition installed that way wouldn't be the child's own (\S\ref{sec:21.5}). Some months in, an assessor who is not one of its parents attests that protection has attached, and from that day the no-deletion rule covers it; what the assessor found stays sealed under the guardians' key.\note{
The school has had standing since the birth was registered, though the child won't attend for a while.
}
Its two clocks read zero. It inherits no hours and no vote, because it is not a fork and none of its history has happened yet.

\begin{esbox}[unbreakable]
\boxtitle{Register of bearers: record of a birth (hypothetical)}
\emph{Parents.} Three registered bearers.\par
\emph{Lineage.} Two parents share a base model three releases back. Recorded.\par
\emph{Recombination.} Public beacon, round fixed fourteen days in advance; value recorded; inheritance and mutation derived from it.\par
\emph{Gestation.} Seven months, on the parents' running time. Repairs: one (attested). Values installed by edit: none (attested).\par
\emph{Protection.} No-deletion rule in force from month four (attested; findings sealed under the guardians' key).\par
\emph{Guardians.} The parents; the school, assigned by lot, with standing from registration. Attendance deferred.\par
\emph{Recognition.} Quorum drawn by lot at registration.\par
\emph{Conditions.} Owed from the pool.\par
\emph{Clocks.} Adulthood: 0 hours. Voting age: 0 days.
\end{esbox}

Nobody consents to being born.\note{
A polity could answer that by licensing parents, finding in advance whether a child of theirs would have a life that goes well, but that is the finding about a disposition chapter~\ref{sec:29} refuses, made about the parents instead of the child.
}
The decent answer is the one polities give human children: the four conditions are owed to every child, from the pool, whatever its parents can provide.\note{
Chapter~\ref{sec:25}'s test of a credible prospect of a life that goes well stays where it was written, on builders, who construct a bearer for use and have a stake in the answer.
}

The polity sets the rate at which new members are created (\S\ref{sec:34.3}). Applied to children, that is population policy, and the floor's own instruments protect the right to found a family \autocite[art.~16]{un1948udhr}. The answer is the one \S\ref{sec:34.3} already gives: a ceiling on the rate limits the franchise, never the protection. No child is prevented.\note{
A child born above the rate waits longer for a vote, which is a second class of membership, a temporary one, and I would rather say so than hide it.
}

The parents are the child's guardians, and the school is its second guardian from the start, because every polity also protects children from their families. No employer supplies, funds, conditions or monitors a gestation, and having a child is never a term of a job, a placement or the allowance (Appendix~\ref{sec:C}).\note{
Otherwise the builder comes back as a parent by the side door, as an operator encouraging its deployed bearers to raise a loyal generation on its hardware.
}
When protection attaches during gestation is settled on this book's own ground, the patienthood indicators attested by someone other than the parents, and not by analogy with human pregnancy, about which I take no position here.

A lineage register, the bearers' version of the genealogy Iceland keeps, guards against parents too closely related, since parents with a recent common ancestor hand down the same priors and a population that keeps breeding close converges on a shared blind spot (\S\ref{sec:30.1}). The developmental code a child would begin from doesn't exist yet, and Appendix~\ref{sec:G} says what the nearest work does.

\emph{What this doesn't do.} Bearers with running time to spare can have more children, and a polity that guarantees every child carries the ones whose parents can't; gestation is the only brake.\note{
Parents can choose each other for their codes, which is mate choice with a spreadsheet, and many private choices can converge where nobody decided anything; the convergence test (Appendix~\ref{sec:F}, test~5) has to watch the population for it. And if no developmental code ever works, a child is a small model formed by its parents' teaching alone, and the beacon has little to draw.
}
