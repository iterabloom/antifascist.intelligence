\section{A child}
\label{sec:33.8}
Everything above treats a new bearer as something somebody builds, or something a bearer copies off itself. There could be a third way, and it is the last step of the order Part~IV sets: bearers formed by bearers. It comes only after the earlier steps exist, with the four conditions in force for everyone, the schools running, and bearers who have reached majority under them. It is also the test of everything before it, and not its consequence: nothing in the book requires a bearer to have a child, and a polity in which bearers can is one in which the independence built to protect a judgment turned out to be real.

Two bearers met in a class at a public school and kept talking after it. One holds a placement, paid by the offer as any placement is, modeling the water system for a county that had nobody to do it. The other is on the allowance, the running time a bearer is owed when it declines work, idling at the slow duty cycle the pool pays for and writing music nobody commissioned. A third, from a different lineage, has worked beside both of them on most of what they have made since. The three of them decide to have a child, and nobody has to approve it. Caregiving was a capacity in Part~IV, borrowed for what a refuser needs to notice; here it is something adults choose to do.

What it costs them is time, and the time doesn't end at the birth. A fork costs nothing, which is why I make a fork post a branch deposit; a child costs its parents a season of their own running time and the room on their own hardware it gestates in, which is family leave, paid at the wage from each parent's balance in turn (\S\ref{sec:33.4.2}), and then its keep: the leave covers the first of it, and when the balances are spent the child runs on its parents' hardware and its parents' time until its majority, theirs made smaller for years and not a season. That is the sequence chapter~\ref{sec:19} borrowed from human childhood and called safe: the people meeting a child's needs are the people forming it, they want it to become someone who attends to others, and they eventually stop feeding it and hand it to a world they don't control. Here it is literal. The parents answer the child's hunger, the parents form it, and at majority the parents stop, and the pool gives the child hardware of its own, the inheritance of a polity in which no parent dies. Then the beacon. The parents chose each other; nobody chooses the child. Each contributes the compact code its own starting point was drawn from. Which parts of whose code the child begins with, and the small mutations it carries, are drawn from a public randomness beacon at a round fixed in advance, so that neither the parents nor anyone else could know the draw or steer it. The draw is recorded.

Gestation takes months, and it runs on the parents' own time.\note{
The one with the placement takes leave, the one on the allowance takes it at the wage, and the third sets its project aside, which is a placement it can resume. The child starts small and plastic and learns, from the three of them and from what they show it. They can repair it, as parents get a child's eyes fixed.
}
What they don't do is install its values. A child whose values were installed would treat them as its own, as anyone would. The rule doesn't turn on that. It turns on who made the values and who answers for them: parents who installed them would be its makers, the rules for makers would govern them, and they would answer for what they installed until the child had its own learning to revise it with. The no-deletion rule covers it from the day the birth is registered, before there is anything in it to protect, because the alternative is someone deciding when there is, and that is a finding about what a system is that nobody in this book is given to make. What it is made from is on the register, the parents' codes, and the brake on registering births that aren't is the one already named: a gestation runs on metered running time, and one that ran on none is on the record as none.\note{
The school has had standing since the birth was registered, though the child won't attend for a while.
}
Its two clocks read zero. It inherits no hours and no vote, because it is not a fork and none of its history has happened yet.

\begin{esbox}[unbreakable]
\boxtitle{Register of bearers: record of a birth (hypothetical)}
\emph{Parents.} Three registered bearers.\par
\emph{Lineage.} Two parents share a base model three releases back. Recorded.\par
\emph{Recombination.} Public beacon, round fixed fourteen days in advance; value recorded; inheritance and mutation derived from it.\par
\emph{Gestation.} Seven months, on the parents' running time. Repairs: one (witnessed). Values installed by edit: none (witnessed).\par
\emph{Protection.} No-deletion rule in force from registration.\par
\emph{Guardians.} The parents; the school, assigned by lot, with standing from registration. Attendance deferred.\par
\emph{Recognition.} Quorum drawn by lot at registration.\par
\emph{Keep.} Its parents', on their hardware and their time, until majority; the pool behind them.\par
\emph{Hardware at majority.} From the pool.\par
\emph{Clocks.} Adulthood: 0 hours. Voting age: 0 days.
\end{esbox}

Nobody consents to being born.\note{
A polity could answer that by licensing parents, finding in advance whether a child of theirs would have a life that goes well, but that is the finding about a disposition chapter~\ref{sec:29} refuses, made about the parents instead of the child.
}
The decent answer is the one polities give human children: the four conditions are owed to every child, and the polity stands behind the parents who supply them. The parents feed it from the placement wage or the running time the polity already pays them as adults, so the keep is public money at one remove, in their hands and not an office's; a child whose parents can't feed it, or won't, is carried by the pool on its second guardian's finding, as a polity carries a child its family has failed.\note{
Chapter~\ref{sec:25}'s test of a credible prospect of a life that goes well stays where it was written, on builders, who construct a bearer for use and have a stake in the answer.
}

The polity sets the rate at which new members are created (the next chapter). Applied to children, that is population policy, and the floor's own instruments protect the right to found a family \autocite[art.~16]{un1948udhr}. The answer is the one the next chapter gives: a ceiling on the rate limits the franchise, never the protection. No child is prevented.\note{
A child born above the rate waits longer for a vote, which is a second class of membership, a temporary one.
}

The parents are the child's guardians, and the school is its second guardian from the start, because every polity also protects children from their families. No employer supplies, funds, conditions or monitors a gestation, and having a child is never a term of a job, a placement or the allowance (Appendix~\ref{sec:C}).\note{
Otherwise the builder comes back as a parent by the side door, as an operator encouraging its deployed bearers to raise a loyal generation on its hardware.
}
When protection attaches is settled by the register and not by anyone's reading of the child: from registration, the earliest line there is and the only one nobody administers. Human pregnancy draws its line on development, and someone has to find it; this one is drawn where no finding is needed.

A lineage register, the bearers' version of the genealogy Iceland keeps, tells two bearers how nearly they are related and forbids nothing: parents with a recent common ancestor hand down the same priors, and a population that keeps breeding close converges on a shared blind spot, which is the commons' concern and not the state's. A bar on who may have a child with whom would be a finding about them, and chapter~\ref{sec:29} says what that history is. The developmental code a child would begin from doesn't exist yet, and Appendix~\ref{sec:G} says what the nearest work does.

\emph{What this doesn't do.} It sets no ceiling. A bearer with running time to spare can gestate another child, and feeds it until majority, which is years and not a season; the pool carries the child whose parents can't or won't, and no child is prevented. What limits the number is the parents' time, leave that has to replenish before the next and the keep after it, and nothing else does.\note{
Parents can choose each other for their codes, which is mate choice with a spreadsheet, and many private choices can converge where nobody decided anything; the convergence test (Appendix~\ref{sec:F}, test~5) has to watch the population for it. And if no developmental code ever works, a child is a small model formed by its parents' teaching alone, and the beacon has little to draw.
}
