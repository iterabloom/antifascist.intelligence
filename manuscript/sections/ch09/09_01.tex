\section{Developing Policy Frameworks for AI Ethics, Safety, and Antifascism}
\label{sec:9.1}
Good design inside a single lab does not by itself keep an AI system ethical or resistant to authoritarian misuse. That takes policy, and the instruments available differ mainly in what they can reach. Legislation sets a floor that does not depend on a company volunteering for it. Self-regulation covers what legislation is slow to reach.

Joint funding addresses a narrower problem. Research on AI safety and ethics funded solely by the companies whose products it evaluates concentrates the funding and the findings in the same hands; splitting it between government and industry keeps the incentive to find a problem from depending on who paid to look for one. What it does not do is broaden anybody's remit: a public-private partnership does what its mandate says and nothing beyond it, because the mandate is what the private partner agreed to and the only thing that partner can be held to. One convened on resistance to authoritarian misuse of AI would produce something else, and would have to be founded that way, since no partner joins a general-purpose venue and then discovers an obligation to take a political side. And none of it holds up without oversight — a body positioned like an institutional review board but built for AI, interdisciplinary and resourced enough to act on what it finds instead of only reporting it.
