\section{Developing Policy Frameworks for AI Ethics, Safety, and Antifascism}
\label{sec:9.1}
Good design inside a single lab does not by itself keep an AI system ethical or resistant to authoritarian misuse. That takes policy, and the instruments available differ mainly in what they can reach. Legislation sets a floor that does not depend on a company volunteering for it. Self-regulation covers what legislation is slow to reach, and what a voluntary commitment is worth is taken up below through the industry coalition that is this book's standing example of one.

Joint funding addresses a narrower problem. Research on AI safety and ethics funded solely by the companies whose products it evaluates concentrates the funding and the findings in the same hands; splitting it between government and industry keeps the incentive to find a problem from depending on who paid to look for one. And none of it holds up without oversight — a body positioned like an institutional review board but built for AI, interdisciplinary and resourced enough to act on what it finds instead of only reporting it.
