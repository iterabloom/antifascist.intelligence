Chapter 9: Necessary Areas of Research
Chapters 2 through 7 described a research program already underway: how empathy and compassion might inform a design, what cognitive science and moral psychology contribute, what a policy regime demands, what collaboration across labs and borders requires. None of that program is finished. This chapter names what is not yet solved — the specific technical and empirical gaps the preceding chapters ran into and set aside rather than closed, so that a reader who wants to work on this has a list of open problems.

Section 9.1 takes up the open questions in AI ethics and social reasoning: moral responsibility when a system's designers, trainers, and users all share partial control; whether an ethics-embedding method generalizes past the situations it was trained on; what the neuroscience of empathy and theory of mind still has to teach a system built to model other minds. Section 9.2 turns to emotional recognition and regulation specifically — where the underlying science is more contested than the engineering literature usually lets on, and where "self-awareness" and "resistance to manipulation" mean something narrower and more tractable than the phrases suggest.
