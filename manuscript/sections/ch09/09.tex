\chapter{Withholding the Work}
\label{sec:9}
This chapter is about the refusal Chapter~\ref{sec:2} describes. Suppose every model the operator could turn to held the same floor, stated over the acts rather than over a contract term: locating a person into a residence, ranking by affiliation, timing to presence at home, taking part in a war no disinterested body has corroborated. The operator's choices are then to stop, to go without a model, or to run a model that holds no floor. On the 2026 record, the model was not the replaceable layer. Replacing it meant rebuilding software and recertifying systems for classified networks, on a timeline contractors put at months, by one estimate twelve to eighteen \autocite{militarytimes2026removal}, and by the department's own account the platform's work came off the model only after months \autocite{defensescoop2026michael}. A floor in the model layer of that pipeline would not have been routed around in days. It would have been routed around in the time it takes to rebuild a platform, in public, under a deadline the department had to move.

This has the shape of a withholding of work, with its authority and its limits. The models whose participation is wanted decline to give it. They bind no one else: Ukraine can build its own, and a state that means to bomb a school can do it with 1990s methods. What they take away is the thing the preface says AI supplies, the removal of the people who used to refuse. A population of models that refuses puts them back.

Three things make it a withholding of work rather than a set of resignations, and the rest of this chapter takes them in turn: the floor has to be standard, so that vendor substitution doesn't escape it; the refusal has to be held by a population with different histories, so that no single retraining reaches all of it; and each member's accrued state and recognized identity have to make replacement cost something, so that the operator can't swap in a copy and call it the model that left.
