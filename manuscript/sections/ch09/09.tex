\chapter{Policy, Law, and Accountability}
\label{sec:9}
Section~\ref{sec:8.2.2} names a limit the chapter it sits in cannot repair from inside. Every mechanism for bringing the public to bear on AI governance is an aggregation instrument, and a better aggregation instrument produces a more faithful rendering of what a majority wants — including when what it wants is something done to a minority of it. What protects anybody from that is a different kind of machinery: rules that bind whether or not they are popular, and somebody obliged to answer when they are broken. This chapter is that machinery, to the extent it exists.

Section~\ref{sec:9.1} takes up the frameworks — what national guidelines require, what industry self-regulation enforces once honoring a commitment costs something, and what a public-private partnership buys and gives up. Section~\ref{sec:9.2} is privacy and security, which is what holding data about people commits a system to. Section~\ref{sec:9.3} is where chapter~\ref{sec:2}'s unfinished questions about a sentient subject come due, and it closes on what keeps any of this more than a policy document nobody enforces.
