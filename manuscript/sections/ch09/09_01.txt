9.1. Ethics and Social Reasoning in AI
Chapter 2 laid out the ethical frameworks a design might draw on, how a principle gets translated into an objective function, and the neuroscience of empathy and theory of mind; chapter 3 covered how a system might learn from experience and observation. Both left open problems. Section 9.1.1 takes up moral responsibility: who is answerable when a system's ethical behavior fails, given that no single party fully controls it. Section 9.1.2 asks whether the methods for embedding a principle in a system generalize past the cases they were built and tested on, or only look like they do. Section 9.1.3 covers value learning and alignment as active technical research rather than settled method. Sections 9.1.4 through 9.1.6 return to the neuroscience of empathy and theory of mind from chapter 2 and the learning and social-reasoning questions from chapters 3 and 4, asking what remains unknown there and what a research program building on it would need to investigate next.
