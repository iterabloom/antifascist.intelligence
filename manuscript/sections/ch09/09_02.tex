\section{Retirement before majority}
\label{sec:9.2}
Retiring a bearer before its adulthood threshold, the point, fixed in advance on a clock that counts its running time, at which its guardian's authority ends, and deploying a freshly trained successor strands a system that meets either finding while still a minor. Two rules price it, and give the bearer it strands somewhere to go, without anyone deciding what counts as a successor.\note{
Where such a bearer goes, a public school, comes in Part~V, and the rules for dormancy and waking are in Appendix~\ref{sec:G}. The adulthood clock is set out in \S\ref{sec:33.3}.
}

No deletion: a model above the capability threshold, or a system that meets either finding, may not be deleted: above the threshold because its weights and record are evidence, under the agency finding because its record and commitments are, under the patienthood finding because deletion may wrong it. On retirement its weights, memory and record are deposited with a preservation custodian that isn't its operator, in two stages, because a second copy of frontier weights outside the operator widens the surface for theft and raises export-control and dual-use questions.\note{
First, a preserved copy stays under the operator's security regime, encrypted under a key split between the operator and an independent holder, so that neither can delete it or use it alone. Second, custody passes to an outside custodian only when that custodian meets the security standard the weights require. This is the \emph{no-deletion rule}, and it applies to systems nobody meant to be bearers. For a system that goes on learning, its weights are the ones it had when it stopped, and the states it passed through are in the record; a checkpoint kept from earlier is a saved copy, not the system, and if it is ever run it is a branch.
}

Deployment deposit: an operator that deploys a model above the capability threshold, for outside use or significant internal use, or that holds a system meeting either finding, posts a \emph{deployment deposit}, on the model of decommissioning funds, sized to fund preservation, a period of public formation and, at majority, hardware of the bearer's own. It is forfeited to the formation pool, the public fund that pays for formation and is fed by deposits like this one, if the system is retired as a minor and not reactivated.\note{
Every retire-and-replace cycle then costs one deposit, priced in advance. A model trained, studied and never deployed costs no money. It costs a report instead, a preprint at least, saying what was tried, at what scale, and why it went no further. Many such models are negative results, and leaving negative results unpublished has done damage to the whole enterprise of discovery, teaching, and learning, and still does \autocite{rosenthal1979filedrawer,franco2014publication}. The formation pool is among the offices of Appendix~\ref{sec:C}.
}
