\section{Privacy, Security, and Antifascism in AI Systems}
\label{sec:9.2}
Privacy and security are not separable from antifascist design; they are two of the pieces it is built from. An AI system that leaks the data it touches, or that a hostile actor can quietly subvert, is available for exactly the surveillance and control this book argues against, whatever values sit in its training.

The EU's General Data Protection Regulation is the clearest existing legal precedent for treating data protection as a requirement rather than a design preference \autocite{eu2016gdpr}. The harder point about it is the one section~\ref{sec:10.8} makes in general form: a law of this kind is built to govern what a private company may do with data, not to block a government's own authoritarian uses of AI, and nothing forces the two categories to overlap. Extending comparable protections to close that gap is the unfinished part of the precedent.

A privacy protection a bad actor can simply break through was never a protection, so the trade-off between privacy and the data a system needs in order to learn is only half the problem; hardening the system itself is the other.
