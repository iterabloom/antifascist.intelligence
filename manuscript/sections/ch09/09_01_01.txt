9.1.1. Advancing Theories of AI Ethics, Moral Responsibility, and Resistance to Authoritarianism 
- Investigating the implications of various ethical theories for AI alignment: In order to align AI systems with human values, it is important to explore how different ethical frameworks, such as utilitarianism, deontology, and virtue ethics, can contribute to AI development. For example, utilitarianism emphasizes maximizing overall well-being, which could be applied in AI systems through reinforcement learning approaches aimed at maximizing socially beneficial outcomes. Deontology focuses on the importance of rule-based decision-making, which may inspire AI systems to abide by moral principles and respect human rights. Finally, virtue ethics highlights moral virtues and character, which could encourage AI systems to be diligent, empathetic, and morally sensitive in their interactions with human users.

- Analyzing moral relativism and AI value alignment in diverse cultural and social contexts: AI systems are increasingly operating in diverse cultural and social environments, which may lead to conflicts or disagreements in values and moral priorities. Addressing moral relativism in AI ethics involves recognizing and respecting cultural variations while also identifying universal human values that can serve as a common ground for ethical AI behavior. For instance, while specific moral norms might vary across cultures, a common thread might be the emphasis on respecting individual autonomy, promoting social welfare, and adhering to principles of fairness and justice. Understanding and integrating these values in AI systems will allow for greater flexibility in terms of cultural adaptation while still maintaining ethical integrity.

- Identifying and counteracting the potential use of AI for authoritarian purposes: Given the potential for AI technologies to be co-opted by authoritarian regimes for surveillance and social control, it is crucial to develop AI systems that are resistant to such exploitation. Exploring anti-authoritarian AI ethics involves examining how AI systems can be designed to prioritize democratic values, human rights, and social justice while actively resisting oppressive and manipulative applications. This might involve the development of mechanisms to ensure transparency, accountability, and decentralization in AI decision-making, as well as safeguarding user privacy and autonomy.

- Assessing moral responsibility and AI agency: As AI systems become more advanced and autonomous, questions surrounding the moral responsibility and agency of these systems arise. Investigating the boundaries of moral agency for AI involves determining under what circumstances AI systems can be held morally responsible for their actions, and how to distribute responsibility between AI systems and their human designers, users, and regulators. Addressing this challenge will involve both theoretical and practical approaches, such as developing new ethical frameworks for AI moral agency and establishing legal and regulatory systems that can account for the ethical implications of AI behavior.
