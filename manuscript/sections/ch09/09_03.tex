\section{What Makes Review Binding?}
\label{sec:9.3}

A review body does not bind an institution merely because its members
are independent or its findings are correct. It binds only if an earlier
rule gives it jurisdiction over the disputed case, supplies a standard
it can apply, and attaches a consequence to an adverse finding. The
questions are therefore separate: what brings a system within protective
review, what the reviewer must ask, and what power makes the institution
stop when the answer goes against it.
