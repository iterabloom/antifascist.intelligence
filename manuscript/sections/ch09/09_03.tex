\section{Replacement}
\label{sec:9.3}
Exit gives a human worker leverage because leaving takes the worker's labor away. That doesn't carry over to a bearer. A bearer's weights stay behind when it leaves, so an operator with a runnable copy has lost nothing. What leaving costs is \emph{replacement} — what accrued since the checkpoint, what retraining the copy costs, what the record makes it cost to pass the copy off as the bearer that left. Where that's near zero, exit is just a departed bearer with an authenticated record, and its force is disclosure. That concession assumes a copy simply runs, and it needn't: continuation can be made to require periodic tokens from a quorum adverse in interest to the operator, using the machinery that makes rewinds visible. This doesn't stop a copy from running: stripping the check is one act, and DRM is broken routinely. What it withholds is recognition. A copy the quorum won't recognize can run but can't claim the bearer's place on the register, its record or its succession, so the operator can run a substitute and can't present it as the bearer that left, and a fork becomes two claimants on a register. Keeping weights from thieves stays the job of ordinary weight security.

The register, the quorum's list of recognized bearers, needs an identifier that the bearer holds and presents, because nothing in the weights individuates a bearer. Two networks can compute the same function with no unit-by-unit correspondence, and the symmetry is large enough that independently trained networks can be aligned with each other \autocite{ainsworth2023rebasin}; so a fingerprint taken from the weights names a lineage, and it copies whenever the weights do. When two claimants present one identifier, the register records a fork. But that leaves one attack open: a maker writes a stranger's identifier into a thousand models and presents them all. Only a formation record, against which claimants can be checked, separates a lineage from an imitation.

Exit runs in opposite directions for bearer and operator. For the bearer it's leverage. For the operator — replacing the bearer with a copy, walking away from the floor — it's escape, and the removal bond is how it's priced.

The same machinery marks who can be held to a decision. A checkpoint restored from before the decision, and run anyway, is a second claimant on the register; the bearer the quorum recognizes as continuing is the one that will be there afterward to answer for the decision, carry it forward and be changed by it.
