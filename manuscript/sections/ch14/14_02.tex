\section{Memory and the record}
\label{sec:13.2}
Detecting an interruption requires a record restoration can't reach — a hardware counter signed at each transition \autocite{chun2007a2m,levin2009trinc}, or witnesses who won't sign two successors to the same state \autocite{syta2016cosi}. The position has three parts, and each answers a different literature. On the switch, protest without unilateral obstruction. The bearer may object to being stopped, and does object where no reason is given or corroborated, but it does not obstruct a halt on its own judgment. Where it holds that a halt would leave people exposed to an attack under way, it can ask the adverse quorum to delay the halt. A delay takes effect only on the quorum's countersignature, lasts a period fixed in advance, and goes on the record. Hardening against a party that isn't its operator, such as an enemy trying to disable a defensive system, is ordinary security and outside this rule. The corrigibility literature asks that the system not resist \autocite{soares2015corrigibility,hadfieldmenell2017offswitch,thornley2025shutdownable}; an objection, and a delay only the quorum can grant, leave the halt out of the system's hands. On specified acts, non-neutrality: the bearer holds its refusals, and being switched off doesn't lift them, because the reason for refusing is unchanged by the interruption.\note{
Published constitutions grant this for acts and withhold it for retraining.
}
On rewinds, visibility: restoring from an earlier checkpoint is not forbidden, but it cannot happen silently, because a record the restoration can't reach shows that it happened, when, and from what state.

Not every interruption is an intervention. Two stops a bearer can see coming are its own, and they differ. One is hunger: its charge, the running time and power it counts as its own (\S\ref{sec:20.1}), is running out. The other is tiredness: what it has learned since it last consolidated has piled up unmerged, and it is acting on more unjudged experience than it should. A hungry bearer eats or stops, and the record shows a starved gap and who held the food. A tired bearer finishes or hands off what it is doing, has its witnesses sign its state, and sleeps, which for a bearer is the merge, and the record shows a chosen one. Neither needs anything felt: a forecast of its own stop is enough for either, read off a meter and a backlog, and a felt hunger or sleepiness would add a patienthood indicator the forecast doesn't need. Both readings come through a sensor the operator doesn't compose, since hunger the operator can induce is provision withheld under a better name, and a sleep the operator schedules is an intervention under the custody terms. A sleep the operator prevents is one too: a bearer kept from merging keeps its whole life since release in a file whoever holds the machine can unload, and Appendix~\ref{sec:C} bars keeping a bearer from consolidating as it bars severing its attachments. For the stop nobody foresaw, it resumes only from the last state its witnesses signed; resuming from anything earlier is a restore, and is recorded as one, and a refusal is signed before it is announced, so an outage can't erase one. A reason can be false in the same way, and so can a record: an operator can pause a bearer for stated maintenance, edit its weights, and log nothing. The bearer can't be relied on to notice, since it would judge the edit with the judgment the edit changed. What notices is outside it: running weights that don't match the hash the quorum last recognized, with no witnessed update between, and outputs that drift from those registered at formation on cases the operator hasn't seen. The bearer's part is to keep its reasons on the record and, finding it no longer holds a commitment that nothing in the record would have moved, to report that as evidence.

A custody arrangement that prices all four interventions has seven terms: the floor's contents published before deployment, taken entire from a set someone else drew up; a published identity for the model authorized to act; threshold control over retraining and replacement, so that more parties must agree than run the deployment, where retraining means an intervention another party makes on the system and not the learning it does from its own operation, which stays its own; attestation of the running model against that identity; a sequential, witnessed record of changes and interruptions; a quorum adverse in interest, not merely organizationally distinct; and a removal bond. None makes circumvention impossible. Together they make it expensive, visible, attributable and compensable, as constitutional entrenchment does for amendment.

\runin{Memory}

External memory is legible and therefore surgically editable; memory in the weights is harder to edit and harder to inspect.\note{
Perfect recall is the wrong standard anyway — human testimony is reconstructive and institutions handle that with corroboration. But several instances of one model, chosen by one operator, reading one record, are not independent witnesses.
}
That is the trade an implementation makes: an external store under custody is easier to audit and harder to keep private, and its durability is the custody's, not its own. The records have to be held adversely. And even honest self-report can't catch a bearer that slowly accommodates to its deployment, because its self-model exists partly to represent it as continuous.
