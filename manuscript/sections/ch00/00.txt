Chapter 0: On Method
This book was not written the way books are usually written, and a reader is owed a plain account of how it actually happened before reaching chapter 1.

It began in April 2023 as a single prompt: describe what it would take to build a superintelligent AI that is empathetic, ethical, and friendly. What came back over the following months was not one author's output but roughly a hundred simulated expert co-authors — models prompted to write as if they held a chair in moral psychology, or comparative constitutional law, or reinforcement learning, or political economy — each assigned to the sections closest to their field, with an eye toward covering the interdisciplinary range the subject actually demands and toward including perspectives skeptical of concentrated, unaccountable power. Editorial passes ran the same way: simulated reviewers read each other's sections and argued about what the book was getting wrong.

None of that made the book collective in the ordinary sense. None of the named people whose expertise those personas imitated wrote a word of it, reviewed it, or knows it exists. The device pulled breadth out of a language model that a single unassisted drafting process would not have reached — it did not recruit anyone. Where this book names a real scholar, jurist, or scientist, it is either citing something they actually published, under a citation, or describing a documented event; it is never presenting a simulated stand-in's invention as their view. The repository's own disclaimer says this more bluntly, and nothing here is meant to soften it.

By 2026 the draft that process produced was extensive, uneven, uncited in most places, and written in an institutional "we" that belonged to a committee that had never existed. Finishing it meant a different kind of work: one person — me — reading every section, fixing the voice into a single first-person author's, cutting duplication, verifying or removing factual claims, and deciding, section by section, what stayed. I used language models throughout that work too, as drafting and research assistance, the same way I would have used an editor or a research assistant if I had one; the difference is that the collaborator this time doesn't hold opinions, can't be credited with the book's argument, and left a complete transcript of everything it did.

One more disclosure belongs here, and it is the uncomfortable one.

The models I used for that finishing work were Claude, made by Anthropic. Chapter 6 describes a military targeting platform with Anthropic's model inside it, and chapter 8 uses that arrangement as its central case for a gap in accountability law that no existing doctrine closes. So the book criticizes, by name, the company whose product helped assemble it.

I have not softened that criticism, and I have not gone looking for extra reasons to make it, either. The record on that company is genuinely mixed and the chapters say so: it refused a government demand to remove its own restrictions on autonomous weapons targeting and domestic mass surveillance, and it absorbed a supply-chain-risk designation and a lawsuit rather than comply. That is a commitment with a price attached, and it paid the price. It is also a company whose model ended up in a targeting platform under terms that left it unable to say how it was used. Both things are true, and a reader who suspects the second half was written carefully to protect a working relationship should know that I had one, and should weigh the chapters accordingly.

I could have avoided the awkwardness by using a different vendor's model for the finishing work, or by leaving the targeting material out. The first would have relocated the conflict without removing it, since every frontier vendor is somewhere in this story. The second would have removed the strongest evidence in the book. Disclosing it is the only option that costs the reader nothing.

That transcript is the point of this note. Nothing about the process above is asserted without a record behind it — the founding prompt, the persona assignments, the generation transcripts, the review passes, and the finishing campaign's own plan, decisions, and revision history are all in the repository this book comes from, dated and unedited after the fact. A reader who wants to check any of what I've just said, rather than take my word for it, can.

The argument in the chapters that follow is mine. I chose what survived, revised what I kept, and I am responsible for what is wrong with it. The method that got me a draft to start from was unusual enough to disclose; it is not a hedge against that responsibility.
