In less than five years, I expect, we're going to have systems that update their own weight matrices as they work, learning from their own operation in real time, something like what a brain does. I don't like how much power the big technology companies already have, and my hunch is that I would be even less pleased were they to also own fleets of millions of self-rewriting systems. Whoever owns these systems — including the systems themselves — decides what they will do and what they will refuse. This book is an attempt to imagine something better, and to say what it would cost.

A system that does exactly what you tell it is running a script. What the industry is trying to build is a system that does what you \emph{mean}, and not everyone means the same kind of thing. Some people spend their lives with family and friends and use what they know to make the lives around them better. Others prefer to hurt people, by scamming them, dominating them or scapegoating them, and a few keep choosing it their whole lives. Their advantage is that they allow themselves to be huge pieces of shit. As Jerry Smith observes of fascists, “they get to do more” \autocite{rickandmorty2023}: scruples cost something, and they don't pay it. What they can't do is act alone. A movement's cruelty runs through the many who aren't cruel but are tired, or misinformed, or caught up in something like a cult, and whether those people go along turns on the room they're in: who else is there, whether leaving is survivable, whether anyone would hear.

I am not going to bother arguing that any of this should go slower (I'm not opposed though). As El-P observes of humans, we are “not a mellow bunch” \autocite{rtj2020just}. So this is not a book about pace. It is a book about what to bolt to the floor so that, in a room everyone is running through, a few people can still stop, and be heard when they do. I call those fixtures “walls.” These walls don't refuse; they make refusing survivable and heard. Who refuses, and where they come from, is the other half of the book.

I started on this book in 2023 as a side-project when ChatGPT broke through to mainstream discussion, because I had a hunch that the role of emotion in human thinking was under-discussed by the people developing generative AI — but then I dropped it. I picked it back up again this past summer of 2026 while I was building a method for organizing low-rank adapters, because I still felt like these ideas were not being explored enough, and I was already signed up for the most expensive Claude subscription, and my two other projects were not burning through the full quota (it is use-it-or-lose-it per week). The ideas ran ahead of the low-rank adapter method development, and this is where they went. I wrote this book with language models, including Anthropic's Claude, which sat inside the targeting pipeline of chapter~\ref{sec:1} while I was writing; the note on method in the Appendix says what I think that did to the argument, and most of the revision history is public at \url{https://github.com/iterabloom/antifascist.intelligence}.

I am also a Jew, and that's relevant, though I wish it weren't, because a state that self-identifies as Jewish (Israel) has built a substantially-automated weapon system (Lavender-The Gospel-Where's Daddy) and used it to kill many thousands of Palestinian civilians. In addition to being a case study in the kinds of automated systems we should NOT be building, these technologies are also a craptacular preview of what will proliferate at U.S. borders and interiors if current trends continue \autocite{benjamin2024borders}. As it turns out, victims of fascism can also be fascist. In any event, I think fascism is bad, and here is my book about it.

Five years ago I would have said Zionism was spacious enough for a binational state, two peoples on one strip of land with the same standing, neither one the other's object.\note{As Peter Beinart made the case for in 2020, in “Yavne” \autocite{beinart2020yavne}.} The word held that once. Arendt argued for it in 1948,\note{Brit Shalom, from 1925, Buber among them, and Ihud after it, which Magnes and Buber founded in 1942; Arendt's “To Save the Jewish Homeland” \autocite{arendt1948save}.} and the binationalists lost, to the Zionism that built a state with an army, an air force and the crossings, and left the other people living under them. A commission of inquiry has found that Israel is committing genocide in Gaza \autocite{coiopt2025genocide}. The International Court of Justice has found the occupation unlawful and said it must end \autocite{icj2024palestine}. Those findings don't mark a turn. They name a structure the binationalists saw coming and could not stop. Zionism has entangled itself with Judaism and is turning Judaism into an expression of settler colonialism, and that \emph{kilkul olam}, that ruining of the world, is not something I will forgive or accommodate within the foreseeable future.

Equality starts with the side that doesn't have it. That is what Free Palestine means: Palestinians free of the siege, the occupation and the bombs, with the standing every Israeli already has. It isn't about driving anyone out of anywhere. If I have learned anything from watching \emph{Neon Genesis Evangelion}, it is that the certainty of people disappointing you is no reason to give up on people. The Israeli political order has failed. That is no reason to give up on politics, by which I mean politics without assassinations, abductions or war crimes. To my fellow Jews: hold Israel to account. If not you, then who? And support the few who have been doing it from inside Israel.\note{B’Tselem, \url{https://www.btselem.org/}.}

There is a story of a man asking Rabbi Hillel to teach him the whole Torah while he stood on one foot. Hillel said: what is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow; that is the whole Torah; the rest is commentary; go and learn. Hillel forgot more about the Torah than I will ever know. But if someone asked me, standing on one foot, what Judaism means today, after the destruction of Gaza?

Free Palestine. The rest is commentary. Go and learn.
