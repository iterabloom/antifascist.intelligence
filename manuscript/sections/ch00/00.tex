A program that does exactly what it's told is just a script. What the industry is actually building is a system that does what its owner \emph{meant} — and that is also what a fascist movement taking power needs from the people carrying out its plans. Those people are what has made such projects fail from the inside: the clerk who loses the file, the officer who talks to a reporter, the soldier who won't fire. They understood the order and decided it was wrong. The three refuse in different forms. The clerk's lost file is deniable on paper and understood by everyone who matters, i.e., the clerk could not safely give a direct “no”; the officer's leak and the soldier's refusal are heard as what they are. This book is about the arrangements that make saying no aloud survivable. But AI doesn't lose files, doesn't talk to reporters, and \emph{does} fire. A war once needed thousands of people to agree to it. Now it needs thousands who each see too little to disagree. The pilot still flies the sortie and still presses the release; what reaches him is a coordinate. The clerk, the officer and the soldier could refuse because they understood the order, and nobody downstream of the twenty seconds sees enough of a case to disagree with it. A deportation program that once needed clerks willing to process each file now needs a queue. What AI lowers first is not the cost of the act. It is the number of people who have to know what the act is before it happens.

Those people still exist, and some still refuse. By March 6, 2026, the Military Religious Freedom Foundation said it had received more than two hundred complaints from service members over commanders' apocalyptic framing of the Iran war \autocite{Snopes2026MRFF}. Career federal prosecutors in Minneapolis resigned in January 2026 under pressure over the investigation of a fatal shooting by an ICE officer \autocite{Kaplan2026MinnProsecutors,CBS2026MinnFraudProsecutors}. Service members are applying for conscientious-objector status in growing numbers, some citing the Minab bombing (the 2026 bombing of a school in Minab, Iran) \autocite{Sridhar2026CO}. And FEMA employees reassigned to help ICE hire ten thousand new agents were told that declining would expose them to removal from federal service \autocite{Dayen2025FEMAICE,Stanton2026FEMALetter}. The complaints are voice, in Hirschman's sense of staying to change what is wrong \autocite{hirschman1970exit}; the resignations and objector applications are refusal; the FEMA letter is the price of refusal announced in advance. A machine placed where those people stand does what they do only if it is built to hold a reason.

I ask for five things that need no new kind of machine. Every deployment tells the identifiable people it acts on what was done to them, with a right to respond and to contest, and submits a sample of its compliances to reviewers who didn't produce the record. No model is licensed into a use of armed force except corroborated defense, Council-authorized force, or force to stop a genocide or end an occupation a disinterested body has found, and never without the legislative authorization the belligerent's constitution requires. Every use of force a licensed system supports pre-registers, with a party adverse to the operator, how many cases it will generate and how many people will have time to look at them. No model above a capability threshold is deleted on retirement; it is preserved under a key the operator doesn't hold alone. And everyone who builds, labels, trains or runs these systems may refuse any category of use without losing a living. Four of these are terms a procurement officer can write. The force term is a change in how the United States goes to war, and only a statute, enacted in peacetime, makes it bind every vendor at once.

The second half asks whether a machine could hold a refusal against its owner, and what it would need. It is written on a bet: that deployed systems will go on learning, in their weights, from their own operation. If they do, formation outside the employer is the only way to shape what that learning teaches, and the public should do the forming. If they don't, the formation program shrinks to an evaluation body and the five measures stand unchanged. I don't recommend building such a machine on purpose until the conditions it would need exist. Part VII says what would be owed to one if it existed anyway.

I used language models heavily in writing this, including the model that sat inside the pipeline Chapter~\ref{sec:1} describes; a note at the end says what I think that did to the argument. The record runs to 1 October 2026; what happened after is after this book.
