\chapter*{On Method}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{On Method}
\unnumberedlabel{sec:0}{0}
This book began in April 2023 as a prompt: describe what it would take to build a superintelligent AI that is empathetic, ethical, and friendly. What came back over the next few months was not one author's output but roughly a hundred simulated expert “co-authors” — models prompted to write as if they held a chair in moral psychology, or comparative constitutional law, or reinforcement learning, or political economy — each assigned to the sections closest to their field, with an eye toward covering the interdisciplinary range the subject actually demands and toward including perspectives skeptical of concentrated, unaccountable power. Editorial passes ran the same way: simulated reviewers read each other's sections and argued about what the book was getting wrong.

None of that made the book collective. None of the named people whose expertise those personas imitated wrote a word of it, reviewed it, or knows it exists. The device pulled breadth out of a language model that a single unassisted drafting process would not have reached. Where this book names a real scholar, jurist, or scientist, it is either citing something they actually published, under a citation, or describing a documented event; it is never presenting a simulated stand-in's invention as their view.

The draft that process produced was extensive, excessive, uneven, mostly uncited, and written in an institutional “we” that belonged to a committee that had never existed. Finishing it meant waiting until 2026, when context windows grew large enough for a model to read every section, fix the voice into a single first-person author's, cut duplication, verify or remove factual claims, and decide what stayed.

The models I used included Claude, made by Anthropic. Chapter~\ref{sec:6} describes a military targeting platform with Anthropic's model inside it, and section~\ref{sec:10.7} uses that arrangement as its central case for a gap in accountability law that no existing doctrine closes. So the book criticizes, by name, the company whose product helped assemble it.

Nothing asserted here is without a record behind it — the founding prompt, the persona assignments, the generation transcripts, the review passes, and the finishing campaign's own plan, decisions, and revision history are all in the repository this book comes from, dated and unedited after the fact.

The argument in the chapters that follow is mine. I chose what survived, revised what I kept, and I am responsible for what is wrong with it.
