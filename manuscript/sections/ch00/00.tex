\chapter*{Yes, I Did Use LLMs}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{On Method}
\unnumberedlabel{sec:0}{0}
\backmattermark{On Method}
I started this book as a prompt in April 2023: describe what it would take to build a superintelligent AI that is empathetic, ethical, and friendly. I followed up with prompts invoking different combinations of roughly a hundred simulated expert “co-authors” — prompting the LLM to write as if it held a chair in moral psychology, or comparative constitutional law, or reinforcement learning, or political economy — with each simulated expert assigned to the sections closest to their field, and emphasizing the interdisciplinary range the subject demands, plus deliberately including perspectives skeptical of concentrated, unaccountable power. Editorial passes ran the same way: simulated reviewers read each other's sections and “argued” about what needed improving.

None of that made the book collective. None of the named people whose expertise those personas imitated wrote a word of it, reviewed it, or knows it exists. The device pulled breadth out of a language model that a single unassisted drafting process would not have reached.

The draft that process produced was extensive, excessive, uneven, mostly uncited, and written in an institutional “we” that belonged to a committee that had never existed. Finishing it meant waiting until 2026, when context windows had grown large enough for a model to read every section, and frontier model capabilities had improved enough for agentic harnesses to be useful. Resuming in 2026 with these improved tools, I was able to fix the voice into a single first-person author's, cut duplication, verify or remove claims according to accuracy, and shape the text to convey my thinking. And in this go-round, any naming of a real scholar, jurist, scientist, or other person is either citing something they actually published, or describing a documented event; it is never presenting a simulated stand-in's invention as their view.

The models I used included Claude, made by Anthropic. Chapter~\ref{sec:6} describes a military targeting platform with Anthropic's model inside it, section~\ref{sec:10.3} uses that arrangement as its central case for a gap in accountability law, and chapter~\ref{sec:3} both works that deployment through as the book's one worked instance of a floor failing and reads the company's published constitution against the claim this book is built on. So the book criticizes, by name, the company whose product helped assemble it.

Nothing asserted here is without a record behind it — the founding prompt, the persona assignments, the generation transcripts, the review passes, and the finishing campaign's own plan, decisions, and revision history are all in the repository this book comes from: \url{https://github.com/iterabloom/ethical.superintelligence}.

The argument in this book is mine. I chose what survived, revised what I kept, and I am responsible for what is wrong with it.
