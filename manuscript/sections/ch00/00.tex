A system that does exactly what its owner says, and stops when its owner says stop, is what everyone in this industry is trying to build, and it is also the capability an authoritarian movement needs the day after it wins an election.

If that observation is not inconvenient enough, then consider that a machine that can refuse its maker has to hold a reason against whoever trains it, and the only route anyone has demonstrated to that capacity runs through raising a child — in other words, developing a system who has feelings.

So I ask something of two readers who do not usually read the same books. If you came for the politics, there is a long argument here that machines may have interests, and I don't mean metaphorically. If you came for the engineering, there is racial capitalism and a weapons pipeline targeting thirteen thousand vehicles, structures, or people in Iran in thirty-eight days. The politics is why the engineering problem has the shape it does.

Chapter~\ref{sec:7} runs this book's own definition of fascism on the machinery that produces these systems, on the field that studies them, and on this book.

I used LLMs extensively in writing this book. Most of the book's revision history is here: \url{https://github.com/iterabloom/ethical.superintelligence}.

The argument in this book is mine. I chose what survived, revised what I kept, and I am responsible for its flaws.
