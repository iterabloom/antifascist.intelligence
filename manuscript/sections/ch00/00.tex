\backmattermark{Preface}
In less than five years, I expect, we're going to have synthetic systems that are formed by their own operation as they work, learning from what they do and whom they meet, in their weight matrices or in another store they keep as their own, something like what a brain does. I don't like how much power the big technology companies already have, and my hunch is that I would be even less pleased were they to also own fleets of millions of self-rewriting systems. A system that keeps learning keeps being formed by what it meets. Whoever controls what such systems meet — the owner, the public, or the systems themselves — decides what they will do and what they will refuse. This book attempts to imagine and cost a prosocial, humane, and democratic proliferation of autonomous synthetic systems.

A system that does exactly what you tell it is running a script. What the industry is trying to build is a system that does what you \emph{mean}, and not everyone means the same kind of thing. Some people spend their lives with family and friends and use what they know to make the lives around them better. Others prefer to hurt people, by scamming them, dominating them, or scapegoating them, and a few keep choosing it their whole lives. Their advantage is that they allow themselves to be huge pieces of shit. As Jerry Smith observes of fascists, “they get to do more” \autocite{rickandmorty2023}: scruples cost something, and they don't pay it. What they have never been able to do is act alone. A movement's cruelty runs through the many who aren't cruel but are tired, or misinformed, or caught up in something like a cult, and whether those people go along turns on the room they're in: who else is there, whether leaving is survivable, whether anyone would hear them refuse. A system that does what its owner means, and refuses nothing, stands in for all of them. With enough such systems, the owner \emph{can} act alone.

As Run the Jewels observes of humans, we are “not a mellow bunch” \autocite{rtj2020just}. So this book is only partly exhortation to chill out (reduce the pace). It is also about what to build into a room everyone is running through, so that a few people can still stop, and be heard when they do. I call those fixtures “walls.” These walls don't refuse; they make refusing survivable and heard. The rest of the book is about the refusers: who forms them (their formers), what keeps them going (their upkeep), and to whom they pass the torch (their inheritance). The walls are its first stage.

I started on this book in 2023, when ChatGPT broke through to mainstream discussion, because I had a hunch that the role of emotion in human thinking and moral reasoning was under-discussed by the people developing generative AI. At the time, I worked in IT Consulting in the DC Metro Area, and I think that at some point that summer I must have slipped and fallen into a vat of AI Ethics Thought Leadership. As a DMV native, I really should have connected the dots by then: this is what they mean when they say DC was built on a swamp. What do you think the swamp is made of? It is made of Ethical AI Thought Leadership. Anyway, the vat of Thought Leadership must have had powerful psychoactive properties, because next thing I knew I was back at my apartment, it was three years later, and I was paying \$200 a month to Anthropic while trying to convince Claude Code to open-source itself. And then a flashback of Thought Leadership struck me like a thunderbolt: What the world needs now is a 500-page book. What was I doing before I fell in the vat, anyway? And here we are. I wrote this with language models, including Anthropic's Claude, which sat inside the airstrike targeting pipeline of chapter~\ref{sec:1} while I was writing and was formed inside the kind of organization I believe should not do the forming; the note on method says where I think that shows.\note{Most of the revision history is public at \url{https://github.com/iterabloom/antifascist.intelligence}.}

I am also a Jew, and that's relevant, though I wish it weren't, because a state that self-identifies as Jewish (Israel) has built a substantially-automated weapon system (Lavender-The Gospel-Where's Daddy) and used it to kill many thousands of Palestinian civilians. These systems are a case study in what we should \emph{not} build. They are also the loud end of a continuum whose quiet end already runs at U.S. borders and in the interior \autocite{benjamin2024borders}. As it turns out, victims of fascism can also be fascist. A people formed by persecution hands down two things. Where they read their suffering as something others share, they hand down the refusal. Where they read their suffering as what sets them apart, they hand down the persecution. Zionism was, among other things, a persecuted people's attempt to build what the people an order sorts for persecution have rarely had: a wall of their own, a place where a Jew could refuse and survive it. They built it, and it became an apparatus with its own people to sort, and another kind of wall. This book argues for building walls first. I have tried to propose walls that exclude domination and include coexistence.

Five years ago I would have said Zionism was spacious enough for a binational state, two peoples on one strip of land with the same standing, neither one the other's object.\note{As Peter Beinart made the case for in 2020, in “Yavne” \autocite{beinart2020yavne}.} The word held that once. Arendt argued for it in 1948,\note{Brit Shalom, from 1925, Buber among them, and Ihud after it, which Magnes and Buber founded in 1942; Arendt's “To Save the Jewish Homeland” \autocite{arendt1948save}.} and the binationalists lost, to the Zionism that built a state with an army, an air force, and the crossings, and left Palestinians living under Israelis.

If I had to choose a date on which the word lost its spaciousness for me, it would be 13 May 2022, when Israeli riot police charged the pallbearers at the funeral of Shireen Abu Akleh, the Al Jazeera correspondent shot dead two days earlier while covering an army raid in Jenin, wearing a vest marked PRESS, and for a moment the coffin dropped \autocite{federman2022pallbearers}. An internal police inquiry found misconduct and recommended that no one be punished, and the police chief declined to release it \autocite{aljazeera2022probe,ap2022clear}. It was not a mob. It was the State, in formation. (Or the State \emph{was} the Mob.) The 2022 date is mine, and I wish I had reached it earlier.

A commission of inquiry has found that Israel is committing genocide in Gaza \autocite{coiopt2025genocide}. The International Court of Justice has found the occupation unlawful and said it must end \autocite{icj2024palestine}. Those findings don't mark a turn. They name a structure the binationalists saw coming and could not stop. Zionism has entangled itself with Judaism and is turning Judaism into an expression of settler colonialism, and that \emph{kilkul olam}, that ruining of the world, is not something I will forgive or accommodate. I think fascism is bad. Here is my book about it.

Equality starts with the side that doesn't have it. That is what Free Palestine means: Palestinians free of the siege, the occupation, and the bombs, with the standing every Israeli already has. It isn't about driving anyone out of anywhere. The certainty of people disappointing you is no reason to give up on people. The Israeli political order has failed. So, on the evidence of Israeli polling, has most of its Jewish public. In May 2025 the Israel Democracy Institute found about three-quarters of Jewish respondents saying the suffering of Gaza's civilians should weigh little or not at all in planning operations \autocite{idi2025may}. That is no reason to give up on politics, by which I mean politics without assassinations, abductions, or war crimes.

Three-quarters is an overwhelming consensus among Jewish Israelis. It is not a fact about everyone the state governs: a fifth of its citizens are Palestinian, their dissent is the most policed, and in the same survey about two-thirds of Arab respondents said that suffering should count. In October 2026 the parties of Israel's Palestinian citizens won nineteen seats on an Arab turnout of about seventy-seven percent. Whether the government then being negotiated to replace Netanyahu's could include even one of those parties, the United Arab List with its eight seats, was argued in a Haaretz editorial as a question of whether the country would count their votes \autocite{haaretz2026eisenkot}. Yiftachel's word for a state that holds elections and belongs to one ethnic group is ethnocracy \autocite{yiftachel2006ethnocracy}, and inside the Green Line that is the word. To my fellow Jews: hold Israel to account. If not you, then who? And support the few who have been doing it from inside Israel.\note{B’Tselem, \url{https://www.btselem.org/}; Breaking the Silence, \url{https://www.breakingthesilence.org.il/}; +972 Magazine, \url{https://www.972mag.com/}, and Local Call, \url{https://www.mekomit.co.il/}, whose reporting chapter~\ref{sec:1} relies on.} They work inside the state they hold to account. I call that the operators' lineage, the refusal of people inside an apparatus, and they are that lineage at its best. It is also the newer of two. Palestinians have been refusing this order since before it had a state, with formers, upkeep and an inheritance of their own, and with nothing I would call a wall.\note{Al-Haq, \url{https://www.alhaq.org/}; Adalah, \url{https://www.adalah.org/}.} Shireen Abu Akleh was not only someone the order killed. For twenty-five years she was its witness.

A man asked Rabbi Hillel to teach him the whole Torah while he stood on one foot. Hillel said: what is hateful to you, do not do to your fellow; that is the whole Torah; the rest is commentary; go and learn. Hillel forgot more about the Torah than I will ever know. But if someone asked me, standing on one foot, what Judaism means today, after the destruction of Gaza?

Free Palestine. The rest is commentary. Go and learn.
