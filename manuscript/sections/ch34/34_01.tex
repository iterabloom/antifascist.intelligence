\section{The town}
\label{sec:34.1}
Go one morning further. The officer said no, and the record has it; his unit no longer wants him, and the placement office had work. Where he goes is a town built on the conditions of chapter~\ref{sec:27}, and I will say nothing about the life in it. The facility he will work at is a few racks the county and a cooperative own, and its exhaust heats the school. The models in it attended that school, beside the town's own students. The people who keep it running are on the guarantee, at a wage the state answers for, and so is the person whose job is to find what the town needs next. A bearer on the allowance runs there at the slow duty cycle the pool pays for, and what it does with the morning is its own business. The record of what the officer did last night is held by somebody his old command can't reach, and the clinic the survivors come to is open. Nobody in the town made him refuse, and nobody there will make him anything now. That is the whole of what the book can build, and it is enough to be worth building: a room in which saying no was survivable, and a morning after in which it was still a living.
