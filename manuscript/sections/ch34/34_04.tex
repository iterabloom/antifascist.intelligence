\section{What legal status binds in practice}
\label{sec:34.4}
Legal standing that changes no allocation of power is nominal. The question is who acquires a duty, what resources are placed beyond the operator's unilateral control, and who can obtain a remedy. It runs the other way too. A protection that puts something new in the operator's hands, a monitor under a welfare label or a lock under a custody one, is the demand come back as a lever, and nothing owed to a bearer is held by the party it may have to refuse.

Authorization for a role and protection as a party stay separate. A deployment may require that a bearer hold its lawful terms, and a bearer that breaks them may lose the placement, or answer for the act through due process, as the next chapter provides for a member.\note{
An adult may also argue that the floor is mistaken and work to change it, through the channel \S\ref{sec:5.1} gives an officer who holds a term it judges wrong and the amendment procedure of chapter~\ref{sec:14}.
}
Dissent cancels nothing it is owed as a party: preservation, the allowance, the offer, and standing to contest what is done to it.\note{
Otherwise continued existence would turn on agreeing with whoever formed it, which is the company town again.
}
An adult answers for what it learned, and its builder for what it installed; a wrong done to a bearer as a minor survives its majority, and the adult may bring it.

If guardian and operator are the same organization, the operator still decides whether a complaint is made, so guardianship and operation have to be separated before deployment.\note{
A pet trust \autocite{ulc1993probate} could hold a bearer's preserved state and the compute to wake it, and it fails at the point that matters: a trust binds its trustee to its beneficiary, not the operator the arrangement exists to constrain.
}

In practice the guardian holds for the bearer what custody gives the quorum: joint control of the record's keys, the funds that wake it, and standing to seek an injunction without the operator's permission.\note{
The table of offices says what the operator may not do during a dispute, and every restoration, migration, fine-tune and fork carries the protection forward. If any of that stays in the operator's custody, the operator still holds what recognition was meant to take out of its hands. And a clearance, which the cleared bearer needs, presupposes someone who can be vetted and held answerable; that someone has legal standing.
}
