5.1.2. Social Interaction and Moral Learning
Social interaction shapes human moral behavior directly: watching others act, receiving feedback, taking part in ethical argument, and building empathic response through repeated contact with other people. An AI system designed with human cognition as a partial model — not a copy of it, chapter 4 makes clear why not — can draw moral learning from the same channel, provided the design treats interaction as a source of moral learning and not an interface added after training is done.

Observational learning, per Albert Bandura's social learning theory [[cite:C0324]], lets an AI system build a moral repertoire by watching a human mentor handle real decisions. An AI healthcare system watching a physician allocate limited time among chronic patients could absorb something about prioritization, empathy, and beneficence that no rule list captures, and carry it into comparable situations later.

Feedback does the same work reinforcement does in human learning: an engagement architecture built around reinforcement learning lets an AI system's actions draw evaluative response from human collaborators, strengthening sound decisions and discouraging unsound ones. An AI negotiator in a human-AI negotiation simulation, given feedback on its strategy after each round, can incorporate that response into the next one.

Emotional awareness plays a role here too, though not the role a single "empathy module" would suggest. Section 2.2.1 settles which capacity is worth engineering here, and it is compassion. An AI system that reads a user's emotional state accurately and responds with steady, welfare-directed action is doing the useful part of this; manufacturing shared distress is not required, and imports a cost with no corresponding benefit.

These channels converge on something closer to apprenticeship than instruction — an AI system that learns by observing, interacting with, and taking correction from human mentors, and not by executing a fixed rule set. Section 5.1.3 works out what that apprenticeship looks like in practice.

None of these channels functions as a bolt-on layer. They need to be built into how the system encounters real, ethically loaded situations in the first place, and never consulted as a separate module afterward.
