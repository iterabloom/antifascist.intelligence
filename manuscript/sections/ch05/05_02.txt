5.2. Emotions and Intuitions in Moral Decision-Making
Chapter 2 covered how an AI system might recognize, interpret, and respond to emotion in general — the science of what an emotion is (2.3.1), the engineering of detecting one (2.3.2), and the specific case of empathy and compassion (2.2.1). This section asks a narrower question: what emotion does specifically inside moral judgment. Guilt and shame track a violation of a standard already accepted; empathy supplies information a purely consequentialist calculation would miss; and the fast, intuitive response that dual-process theories describe — taken up at length in section 5.3 — is itself partly an emotional signal, arriving before deliberation has a chance to run. The four sections below take up moral emotions specifically (5.2.1), how a system's capacity for them might be assessed (5.2.2), how it might recognize and respond to them in others (5.2.3), and what role introspection plays in keeping that capacity honest (5.2.4).
