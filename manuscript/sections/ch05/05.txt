Chapter 5: Overcoming Challenges, Risks, and Authoritarianism in AI Development
<<quote>>
			We [humans] cannot process so much information... you still cannot
			produce enough targets per day.

		Brigadier General Y.S., The Human-Machine Team: How to Create Synergy Between
		Human and Artificial Intelligence That Will Revolutionize Our World, 2021.
		The author, writing pseudonymously, commanded Israel's signals-intelligence
		Unit 8200. The book argues for machines that resolve “a human bottleneck for
		both locating the new targets and decision-making to approve the targets.”
<</quote>>
A system can have a moral sense, built the way chapter 4 describes, and still fail badly — because it was trained on skewed data, because it breaks in situations its training never covered, because its own incentives point somewhere other than where its designers intended, or because someone with power over it decides to point it at people rather than for them. This chapter takes up those four failure modes in turn: bias baked into data and algorithms, brittleness outside the conditions a system was built for, the harder problem of a system whose goals have quietly come apart from what it was meant to want, and the oldest failure mode of all, a tool built well turned toward control instead of help.

The epigraph is there because it is a design document rather than a critique. It states, without embarrassment, that human deliberation is a bottleneck and that the point of the machine is to remove it. Every argument in this book about meaningful human control is an argument with that sentence, and section 5.4.1 shows what the sentence looks like once it has been implemented.
