Chapter 5: Overcoming Challenges, Risks, and Authoritarianism in AI Development
A system can have a genuine moral sense, built the way chapter 4 describes, and still fail badly — because it was trained on skewed data, because it breaks in situations its training never covered, because its own incentives point somewhere other than where its designers intended, or because someone with power over it decides to point it at people rather than for them. This chapter takes up those four failure modes in turn: bias baked into data and algorithms, brittleness outside the conditions a system was built for, the harder problem of a system whose goals have quietly come apart from what it was meant to want, and the oldest failure mode of all, a tool built well turned toward control instead of help.
