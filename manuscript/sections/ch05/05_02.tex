\section{Emotions and Intuitions in Moral Decision-Making}
\label{sec:5.2}
Chapter~\ref{sec:2} covered how an AI system might recognize, interpret, and respond to emotion in general. This section asks the narrower question of what emotion does inside moral judgment specifically, which is chapter~\ref{sec:3}'s question in a smaller frame: the floor needs a violation to register as one before any penalty is attached to it, and the moral emotions are where that registering has been studied. Guilt and shame track a violation of a standard already accepted; empathy supplies information a purely consequentialist calculation would miss; and the fast, intuitive response that dual-process theories describe is itself partly an emotional signal, arriving before deliberation has a chance to run.
