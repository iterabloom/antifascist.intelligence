\section{Emotions and Intuitions in Moral Decision-Making}
\label{sec:5.2}
Chapter~\ref{sec:2} covered how an AI system might recognize, interpret, and respond to emotion in general — the science of what an emotion is (section~\ref{sec:2.3.1}), the engineering of detecting one (section~\ref{sec:2.3.2}), and the specific case of empathy and compassion (section~\ref{sec:2.2.1}). This section asks a narrower question: what emotion does specifically inside moral judgment. Guilt and shame track a violation of a standard already accepted; empathy supplies information a purely consequentialist calculation would miss; and the fast, intuitive response that dual-process theories describe — taken up at length in section~\ref{sec:5.3} — is itself partly an emotional signal, arriving before deliberation has a chance to run. The four sections below take up moral emotions specifically, how a system's capacity for them might be assessed, how it might recognize and respond to them in others, and what role introspection plays in keeping that capacity honest.
