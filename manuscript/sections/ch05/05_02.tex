\section{Emotions and Intuitions in Moral Decision-Making}
\label{sec:5.2}
How an AI system might recognize, interpret, and respond to emotion in general has already been covered; this section asks what emotion does inside moral judgment specifically. The floor needs a violation to register as one before any penalty is attached to it, and the moral emotions are where that registering has been studied. Guilt and shame track a violation of a standard already accepted; empathy supplies information a purely consequentialist calculation would miss; and the fast, intuitive response that dual-process theories describe is itself partly an emotional signal, arriving before deliberation has a chance to run.
