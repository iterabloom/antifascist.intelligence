\section{Nobody Raises a Machine Alone}
\label{sec:5.4}
Urie Bronfenbrenner's ecological systems theory holds that a person develops inside several nested environments at once, from the immediate family outward to culture and historical time, each one shaping the individual while being reshaped by it in turn \autocite{bronfenbrenner1979ecology}. He pictured them as concentric rings, and the same structure applies: an AI system's alignment with humane values is not set by its training data alone but by the interlocking systems around that training — the people who build it, the institutions they work inside, the culture and regulatory climate those institutions answer to, and the historical moment all of this falls in. The rings bear on the floor's question because a commitment is not formed in a vacuum. What a bearer ends up holding is set as much by the institution that trains it as by the training, and every one of those rings belongs to somebody the floor may have to hold against.
