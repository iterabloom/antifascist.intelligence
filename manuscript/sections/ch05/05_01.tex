\section{What Formation Would Have to Add}
\label{sec:5.1}

Performance is an output that satisfies a standard. Competence adds the ability to recognize harms, conflicts, and relevant reasons across cases. Agency would require those reasons to matter to the system sufficiently to govern what it does when approval, reward, and instruction point elsewhere.

The familiar distinction among rules, norms, and principles is useful only as a taxonomy of what an evaluation must separate \autocite{piaget1932moral,kohlberg1969stage}. A system following a rule can match an explicit condition. A system following a norm can reproduce what its social environment ordinarily expects. A system responding to a principle can identify why either should apply and recognize a case in which the rule or norm defeats its own rationale.

For engineering purposes, a disposition is a candidate for formation when the best available causal account of its conduct is a representation of the rationale that remains active across contexts, rather than retrieval of a trained response or compliance with the current signal. The relevant tests are novel pressure, correct defeat, and priced cost.

No mark is sufficient alone. Generalization without cost may be broad imitation; resistance without correct defeat may be rigidity; and a stable explanation without causal involvement may be a trained self-report. The evidence has to show that the reason, rather than the presentation, reward, or authority, best predicts when the system preserves and revises its judgment.

Formation also cannot be established by comparing a system with its trainer's account of how it was built. The trainer is the party best positioned to know the process and the party with the strongest interest in the conclusion. The relevant evidence must therefore include held-out pressures supplied by independent parties, records outside the operator's control, and results published when the system fails.

The resulting standard is deliberately weaker than proof of concern or consciousness. It does not establish that anything matters to the system phenomenally. It establishes whether reasons-responsive formation has become a better explanation of the conduct than imitation of approved behavior.
