\section{A clean pass: Ukraine}
\label{sec:5.3}
Ukraine is licensed on the adoptable term, and passes both tiers of the departed term as well, as it has since 2014. In March 2014 the General Assembly affirmed Ukraine's territorial integrity, 100 to 11 with 58 abstaining \autocite{unga2014res68262}; under the Assembly's Rule 86 abstentions are not votes cast, so that is 100 of 111. For Donbas, the OSCE monitoring mission supplied a running record from 2014, and the European Court of Human Rights found, in \emph{Ukraine and the Netherlands v. Russia}, that Russia had effective control of the separatist areas from 11 May 2014 \autocite{ecthr2022ukraine}, and found violations on the merits in 2025 \autocite{ecthr2025ukraine}. Russia's covert war was the case-description attack in its purest form: it denied being at war at all, so a machine in a Russian pipeline was told it was assisting volunteers. In 2022 the record only got stronger. Russian forces were on Ukraine's territory, which Russia didn't dispute, since it claimed the invasion as self-defense under Article 51 \autocite{russia2022s154}, resting that claim on collective defense of entities it had recognized days earlier, on a genocide no disinterested body found, and on NATO as an anticipated threat, all three of which the term rejects. In March 2022 the General Assembly deplored the aggression 141 to 5 \autocite{unga2022es111}. The Court's order that month that Russia suspend its military operations points the same way, but as a provisional measure it isn't the finding \autocite{icj2022ukraine}. The American airstrikes on Iran in 2026 pass neither.
