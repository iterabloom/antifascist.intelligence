\subsection{The Rings Outside the Lab}
\label{sec:5.4.2}
The immediate environment shapes a system through people rather than data. Developers and early users are mentors whether or not anyone calls them that, and what they treat as an acceptable answer is what the system learns to produce. Breadth in that circle is not a fairness gesture: a system that has only been argued with by one kind of person has no way to notice what it is missing when a different kind arrives.

One ring out, the question is consistency across the interfaces — between engineers, ethicists, legal staff, policy teams, and the people a system is used on. The mechanisms that do the work there are reciprocal cross-training, external participation in design decisions rather than in review, requirements written into the specification instead of appended as principles, and an internal culture where moral objections are raised before deployment rather than discovered afterward. Section~\ref{sec:7.4} is the argument for why that last one is harder than it sounds.

Further out sit forces no team negotiates with. A market-financed healthcare system rewards tools for profitable conditions and well-insured populations and gives nobody a reason to build for preventive or mental health care where the payer is unclear; a publicly financed one rewards tools that serve the whole covered population, because there is no more profitable subset to redirect toward. Neither incentive is chosen by the engineers, and the same logic reaches everywhere a system is built.

The outermost ring is time. A system built to be ethical at release is answering a question that keeps moving: norms shift, capability grows and has to be re-secured at each new level, and the legal environment is a moving target rather than a fixed constraint. The European Union's General Data Protection Regulation is the pattern rather than the instrument — a rule whose reach exceeded its jurisdiction, because a system built to comply for European users tends to get built that way for everyone \autocite{eu2016gdpr} — and the next binding shift will not be that one.

A development effort that never asks what its own outer rings reward has not stayed neutral on the question. It has let the answer be settled somewhere it cannot see.
