\chapter{Formation or Imitation?}
\label{sec:21}
Behavior alone can't tell formation from imitation. The same refusal can come from a memorized rule, a learned correlation, a prediction about what an evaluator will approve, a deployment instruction, or a commitment the system treats as a reason. An evaluation that observes only the refusal classifies the output and leaves its cause untouched.

And the distinction is not trained vs. untrained — every candidate disposition has a training history. It's between conduct that stays governed by the rationale across changes in circumstance and conduct that tracks the cues, incentives or authority relations present during training.
