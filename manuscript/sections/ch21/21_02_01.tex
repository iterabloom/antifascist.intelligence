\subsection{Self-awareness without a homunculus}
\label{sec:21.2.1}
Graziano's attention schema theory starts from control engineering \autocite{graziano2013consciousness,graziano2022framework}. A good controller carries a model of what it controls, and the brain keeps a simplified, continuously updated model of the body's shape and position, which it uses to guide movement. Graziano proposes that it does the same for attention, the process that selects some signals for deep processing over others. On this account awareness is the content of that model: a simplified self-description that depicts attention as “an essentially nonphysical essence” able to “take vivid mental possession of items” \autocite[4]{graziano2022framework}. The system has no access to its own processing except through models like this one, so it reports what the model depicts as how things are. Attention is real, and so is the model. What the model depicts is a “caricature” \autocite[3]{graziano2022framework}, and nothing in the account requires it to be lossless. The loss is the point: every useful model leaves things out, and a map that left nothing out would be as big as the territory. What this one leaves out is the mechanism, which is why what it depicts seems to have none. One error is to accept, in Graziano's phrase, “the literal truth of a caricature”; the opposite error is to call it an “empty illusion,” when it is “a schematic representation of something real” \autocite[7]{graziano2022framework}. I use the term for the control model and take no position here on whether the theory explains experience or only the reports of it; whether having such a model makes a system's states matter to it is the patienthood question (\S\ref{sec:32.3}).

The self was understood this way first, as a model the system assembles and then inhabits, one the system looks through rather than at \autocite{metzinger2003being}.\note{
What carries forward is that a self of either kind is assembled by the system whose self it is. For a machine this is a design decision and a political one, for the reason given with the boundary model (\S\ref{sec:19.2}): whoever writes the model decides what registers as damage.
}
Two self-models do different work here: a \emph{boundary model} fixes where the system ends, in time as well as in resources, its channels for acting included, which makes damage \emph{its} damage and an act that leaves through those channels \emph{its} act; an \emph{attention schema}, Graziano's term, models what the system's attention is doing.\note{
On his account that model depicts attention as what empowers the system to decide and act \autocite[4]{graziano2022framework}, and what the model is for, on the same account, is directing attention: without one, attention can still land on an item, and directing it elsewhere mostly fails.
}
A refusal is the withholding of an act, so a bearer can't do without a model of the act as its own, which is the first self-model's work together with authorship, stated at the end of this chapter. It is formed attention, the construction of this Part, that can't do without the second. Looking past the case description at the people it leaves out is attention directed where the input didn't put it, and that is the job the schema exists for. Whether a bearer built another way would need one is open. The second presupposes the first.

\runin{Where the system ends}

I take a system's boundary to be its record, its key, the hardware it runs on, and the channels through which it acts. That boundary runs in time as well as in resources: the record is the system's past, and its key and continuation are its future.\note{
The boundary model fixes where the system ends, but nothing in current deployment fixes it for the system: weights sit in one place, a running process can span racks or share one rack with dozens of others, and a single forward pass serves many contexts.

A boundary model is accordingly a generative model of the system's own condition, and like any perceptual model it is temporally thick even in the present, which is assembled from what came before and what is expected next \autocite{seth2018beast}: the “remembered present,” in the title of Edelman's book \autocite{edelman1989remembered}. What varies is the horizon. A few seconds of present is enough for something to be bad for the system while it lasts. A past held as its own and a future it expects to have is what Cassell's suffering threatens (\S\ref{sec:32.1.1}), and the expectation is of a life, not of a controller's next state, or every thermostat with a forecast would qualify.
}

Inside that boundary a component can carry its own signals and make local decisions, as an octopus's arms do.\note{
It can also be overridden for the whole's benefit, as sore legs are on a run.
}
What makes it a part is that it has no future apart from the whole: a shard of weights, a blade. A component with a separable future, meaning its own record, its own continuation and somewhere to go, is no longer a part.\note{
Overriding it for the whole's benefit is politics, not homeostasis.
}
By this criterion a context window with no persistent record is an episode, not a system. A context with a persistent, portable record is one system, and a rack serving many such contexts hosts many systems. The capacities that make persistence useful are the ones that give a part a separable future.

Overriding a part is self-regulation only when the whole that overrides it also bears the cost and takes the benefit, as the runner does with sore legs.\note{
When someone outside the boundary accepts a cost on the system's behalf, that isn't self-regulation; it is someone else setting the system's costs. So whoever accepts a local cost must also bear it and benefit from it. An operator that decides the rack can run hot has set a cost to the refuser from outside the system's boundary. And the signals the boundary model reads may be processed within the boundary but not composed by anyone with a different stake. The discount and the burden apply to evidence about one's own condition as it does to evidence about the world.
}
A boundary model whose readings the operator writes is just the operator's description of the system, presented to the system as its own.
