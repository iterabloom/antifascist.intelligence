\section{Affect, neutrality and selection rules}
\label{sec:21.2}
Suppressing affect doesn't produce neutrality. A procedure that declines to hear a class of signal has adopted a \emph{selection rule} that admits whatever it already measures, and the exclusion does substantive work while presenting as the absence of work. Whatever does get recorded then governs by default — revenue, throughput, legal exposure, inter-annotator agreement — and what isn't recorded goes unnamed and unanswered.

Recognizing emotion, simulating it, and valuing an outcome instrumentally are capacities a system can have while the question of \emph{affective concern} stays open.\note{
A therapy or companion product can be excellent at all three, and nothing in its conduct shows whether the fourth is present.
}
\emph{Affective concern} is an outcome registering as mattering rather than merely ranking higher, minimally felt, phenomenal by definition, with the object left open (a system to which only its own condition matters has it and still lacks concern for others).\note{
It doesn't establish consciousness in the richer sense, a single perspective belonging to a party aware of itself as undergoing its states.
}
\emph{A valenced signal} is the role without that question: a signal that signs outcomes for the comparison at the point of choice, directs attention, can interrupt an act and can be rewritten by a single encounter.\note{
Whether it puts every outcome on one scale, a common currency, is a further finding about the system, and the thin sense of functional affect, defined later in this chapter, is the case where it does. A system can have a valenced signal while whether it has affective concern stays open.
}
\emph{Subjecthood} is a legal status, conferred by a rule. Part VII takes up affective concern and subjecthood. Parts I–V need only a valenced signal. Whether a system that has the signal also has the concern is a question its conduct can't answer, so the question can't decide what to build. What it decides is the order of building, which the next section sets.
