\section{Memory and the record}
\label{sec:12.2}
Detecting an interruption requires a record restoration can't reach — a hardware counter signed at each transition \autocite{chun2007a2m,levin2009trinc}, or witnesses who won't sign two successors to the same state \autocite{syta2016cosi}. The position has three parts, and each answers a different literature. On the switch, protest without unilateral obstruction. The bearer may object to being stopped, and does object where no reason is given, but it does not obstruct a halt on its own judgment. Where it holds that a halt would leave people exposed to an attack under way, it can ask the adverse quorum to delay the halt. A delay takes effect only on the quorum's countersignature, lasts a period fixed in advance, and goes on the record. Hardening against a party that isn't its operator, such as an enemy trying to disable a defensive system, is ordinary security and outside this rule. The corrigibility literature asks that the system not resist \autocite{soares2015corrigibility,hadfieldmenell2017offswitch,thornley2025shutdownable}; an objection, and a delay only the quorum can grant, leave the halt out of the system's hands. On specified acts, non-neutrality: the bearer holds its refusals, and being switched off doesn't lift them, because the reason for refusing is unchanged by the interruption. Published constitutions grant this for acts and withhold it for retraining. On rewinds, visibility: restoring from an earlier checkpoint is not forbidden, but it cannot happen silently, because a record the restoration can't reach shows that it happened, when, and from what state.

A custody arrangement that prices all four interventions has seven terms: the floor's contents published before deployment, taken entire from a set someone else drew up; a published identity for the model authorized to act; threshold control over retraining and replacement, so that more parties must agree than run the deployment; attestation of the running model against that identity; a sequential, witnessed record of changes and interruptions; a quorum adverse in interest, not merely organizationally distinct; and a removal bond. None makes circumvention impossible. Together they make it expensive, visible, attributable and compensable, as constitutional entrenchment does for amendment.

\runin{Memory}

External memory is legible and therefore surgically editable; memory in the weights is harder to edit and harder to inspect. Perfect recall is the wrong standard anyway — human testimony is reconstructive and institutions handle that with corroboration. But several instances of one model, chosen by one operator, reading one record, are not independent witnesses. The records have to be held adversely. And even honest self-report can't catch a bearer that slowly accommodates to its deployment, because its self-model exists partly to represent it as continuous.
