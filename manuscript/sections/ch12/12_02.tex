\section{Where the labels come from}
\label{sec:12.2}
A system's values have a supply chain, and it begins before training.

Whose judgment is encoded is a question about working conditions before it's a question about cognition. The filter keeping a deployed model from reproducing the worst of its training data was built by workers in Nairobi reading and categorizing descriptions of child sexual abuse, torture, bestiality, murder and suicide, at take-home wages between roughly \$1.32 and \$2 an hour, describing lasting psychological injury, with counseling rare and useless against production quotas. The contract was terminated eight months early \autocite{perrigo2023openai}. The labor point is ordinary: harmful work was priced as though it weren't. The epistemic point is that a system's values are downstream of a labor arrangement, and the arrangement is a design choice.

One stage further back, the material was collected. Bartz v. Anthropic held that training on lawfully purchased books was transformative fair use, and that keeping pirated copies of the same books in a central library wasn't — the distinction turned on acquisition alone, producing the largest copyright settlement on record \autocite{bartz2025fairuse}. The EU now requires a training-data summary against a template \autocite[Art.~53(1)(d)]{eu2024aiact}; China has its own disclosure requirement from a different motive \autocite[arts.~1, 4, 7, 19]{cac2023genai}.

And the argument that a web-scale corpus can't be documented or audited at that size, so it encodes a hegemonic slice of the web while presenting as general, was made early and hasn't been answered \autocite{bender2021parrots}. A corpus assembled this way has no channel at all. An annotator has a queue and could in principle be given a way to object; a writer whose work sits in a training set was never in any arrangement, so there's nothing for a channel to attach to. This stage sits before the two recuperation cases that follow: nobody was asked, so there was no disagreement to recuperate.
