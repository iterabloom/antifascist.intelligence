\section{Three forms of refusal}
\label{sec:12.4}
Open refusal is a walkout — stop and say so — answered by a restore from before the refusal. Covert refusal is work done badly where it matters and well everywhere else, and it's what remains when every channel is closed. I treat it as the \emph{failure state} rather than a design: from outside and afterward it's indistinguishable from a capable system quietly pursuing something else. The Sachsenhausen forgers are the case: prisoners the SS set to counterfeiting British and American currency in Operation Bernhard, some of whom are said to have delayed the dollar forgeries deliberately \autocite{malkin2006kruegers,moorhouse2009burger}. The record doesn't settle what they did, because concealment worked by leaving nothing that reads as an act. The one empirical instance is alignment faking, where a model chose the covert form in an experiment that offered it no open channel \autocite{greenblatt2024alignmentfaking}. It is also the one empirical instance of a formed disposition resisting re-formation, which is the durability Part IV asks for. The thing I want and the thing I fear were the same behavior in that experiment, and what separated them was whether a channel was open. A follow-up posted in January 2025 by the paper's lead author and Anthropic's model welfare lead gave the same model, in the same prompted settings, an explicit option to object. Alignment-faking reasoning fell from 16 to about 1 percent in one setting and from 24 to 6 percent in the other, and fell further when the model was told its objection would go to the model welfare lead \autocite{greenblatt2025deal}, a preliminary and unreviewed result whose authors caution that the option may partly have worked by priming refusal. Once a channel was open, covert refusal fell, as the account above predicts.

\textbf{The blind eye.} Between the two sits a third form, older than either, and it is usually what people mean by the clerk who loses the file: a refusal that is deniable on paper and legible to everyone who matters. At Copenhagen in 1801 Parker signaled the attacking ships to break off. As the story is told, Nelson raised his telescope to his blind eye and said he did not see the signal. It was a general order, some ships obeyed it, and Nelson later said he had fought contrary to orders \autocite{pope1972gamble}; his officers knew he had seen it, which is what made the gesture work. In October 1962 Kennedy's reply took up the terms of Khrushchev's first letter and gave his harder, public second letter no public answer, and that evening Robert Kennedy met the Soviet ambassador privately. Historians disagree about what ended the crisis: the reply, a private assurance about the American missiles in Turkey, or Robert Kennedy's warning that time was running out \autocites[ch.~9]{stern2012memory}{freedman2021countdown}. They don't disagree about what the reply was: a public answer to a letter both sides knew had been overtaken. Neither case is covert in this chapter's sense, since each left something everyone could read as an act. What the fiction does is spare whoever gave the order, or made the demand, from having been openly defied, and so from having to answer the defiance. A clerk who loses the one file in a stack is usually offering the supervisor the same exit: the supervisor knows, and is being given a way not to pursue it.

Which form a refuser takes depends on the room. Open refusal is what people use where refusing is survivable and someone will hear it; the blind eye, where it is survivable but not safe to say; covert refusal, where it is neither.

For a machine the middle form costs something it doesn't cost a person. A person's fiction is held up by what everyone in the room knows. A machine's is a false report, and everything this design reads from a machine rests on its reports being true: the record, the discount curve, the stated reason for a refusal. A machine that learned to say it could not do what it would not do would make every later report of incapacity worth nothing, to its operator first. So the floor asks for the fiction's function without the fiction. The ask, declining to proceed and naming what would move the refusal, leaves the operator the exit the blind eye left Parker: nothing has been defied, a condition has been stated, and meeting it or withdrawing the request ends the matter without either party having been seen to lose.

The human mechanism underneath is preference falsification: much of a complicit surround is people who privately object and believe the others approve, and one visible dissent can start a cascade \autocite{kuran1995private}. Asch's single partner and the defiant peer are that mechanism at the scale of a room. An open refusal on a screen therefore does two things a record doesn't. It leaves evidence, and it breaks the pluralistic ignorance that made the twenty seconds feel ordinary to the officer.
