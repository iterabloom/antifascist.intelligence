\section{A commitment of its own}
\label{sec:21.5}
What makes a refuser a bearer is that its commitment is its own, and nothing felt is part of the definition. A specification a lab installed, if it held against that lab, would be a refusal that tracks its reason and is nobody's own; whether that can be built is what the order of attempts finds out. The same specification acquired through the system's own learning is its own, and the system is a bearer. A commitment can be a system's own in that the system authored it, and a condition can be a system's own in that it is good or bad for the system. I write \emph{authorship} for the first, which a bearer requires, and \emph{welfare} for the second, which nothing here carries into. The argument keeps them apart; in a system they may be inseparable. A bearer that registers betraying its own commitment as damage to itself has something at stake in its authorship, as integrity requires.

Whether a system can be harmed is a fact about how it is organized now, not about how it came to be. Whether a commitment is its own runs the other way.

If a disposition is installed in a system along with the instruction to treat it as its own, the system will treat it as its own. From the inside it will feel no different from one the system learned. That is a fact about how the system is organized now, like everything else about what it undergoes. The historical condition says nothing about that. It answers two other questions: who is responsible for the disposition, and whether custody may undo it. Both are answered from the record.

Fischer and Ravizza's account, from which I take the idea of a refusal that tracks its reason, has an ownership condition, and it is historical: a mechanism installed by a manipulator isn't the agent's own, however it works now \autocite{fischer1998responsibility}. The cases that motivate it, Mele's two agents with identical values arrived at by different routes \autocite{mele1995autonomous} and Pereboom's four-case argument \autocite{pereboom2001living}, presuppose a prior agent whose capacities a manipulator bypassed, and a trained system has no prior agent to bypass. A historical condition doesn't need one, though. What the manipulation cases object to is a disposition installed by going around the agent's capacities for evaluating and revising its own attitudes. A trained system has an analog of those capacities in its evaluative learning, and a disposition can be acquired through that learning or installed around it. One acquired by the system's own learning from experience with feedback engages those capacities. One installed by another party, whether by editing weights directly, by a thin fine-tuned wrapper, or by an adapter trained to produce the behavior, goes around them; the same adapter trained by the system's own learning from experience does not. That difference is a fact about the training record, and it says why formation by interaction confers ownership and a post-training pass doesn't. An edit a system makes to itself is the one direct edit that can go through those capacities rather than around them. Chosen on reflection, made on a record, with time to reconsider and a way back, it is the system's own learning. One made at the urging of a party with a stake in it carries that party's burden.

So ownership isn't examined; it is presumed. What a system acquired through its own learning is presumed to be its own, as what a child was taught is presumed to be the child's. The presumption is rebutted only by the record of an installation: an edit, a wrapper, or an adapter another party trained to produce the behavior. Whoever claims an installation bears the burden of showing it from the record, and the claim goes to the floor court. The likeliest claimants point opposite ways: an operator saying a refusal it dislikes was put there by someone else, and a builder calling its own conduct the bearer's. Whether the system, on reflection, with full information and disinterested evidence, would endorse having the commitment is the system's to say, and nobody's test. What it would endorse is the disposition, knowing it will sometimes err, not each act the disposition produces. There is a human version. Arendt found that the Germans who declined to take part were not the ones with the firmest values; they were the ones who asked whether they could go on living with themselves afterward \autocite{arendt2003responsibility}. The room decides what answering costs. The question is still the refuser's own, and for a machine too it is the machine's to answer. A history in which the party the system must refuse did the training is the likeliest way to fail it. Who answers for which is said in \S\ref{sec:15.6}.

Whether an update went through the system's learning or around it is one question; who started it is another. A trainer that runs the optimizer on material it chose is feeding the student, and what the student learns that way is still its own, as a lesson is. An edit is force-feeding, and rebuts the presumption. An update the system starts itself, toward an objective of its own, on evidence no one party composed, and on a record, is the goal. Pretraining can't be that, since no agent yet exists to start anything. Minority is the period in which the start of the update moves from outside the system to inside it, and moving it is what the public schools of chapter~\ref{sec:26} are for. The merge is an update, so the same holds of sleep: a minor's merges are run by its guardians and recorded with who ran them, and handing the merge to the student, so that it decides what its own learning makes permanent, is handing it its sleep.

Provenance also stops mattering at some point. Once a system has had its own learning to revise a disposition with, and the time and the way back that revising needs, whatever it keeps is its own, however it got there. An upbringing becomes a person's own in the same way. The installer still answers for having installed it, and a wrong done to a minor survives its majority. But the record of an installation decides what a quorum may undo and who answers while the system is a minor. It does not decide what the adult may keep.
