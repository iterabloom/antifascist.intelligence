\section{It defends what it tends}
\label{sec:20.7}
Caregiving protects the dependent against whatever threatens it. Taylor's account of stress responses in females, \emph{tend-and-befriend}, describes the tending, nurturant activity that protects oneself and one's offspring \autocite{taylor2000tend}; protectiveness toward what one tends is also hostility toward what endangers it.

For a machine, this failure needs no deception. The operator's own people are often really vulnerable: a patrol under fire, a town within range of the launcher. A caregiving system registers their exposure, correctly, and caregiving moves it toward protecting them, which in a targeting pipeline means toward the airstrike. The discount and the burden do nothing here, because nothing is false. Supplying a fabricated sympathetic dependent is the cheap version of this failure. This is the expensive version, and in war it is the ordinary case.

In war, then, caregiving can't contain its own pull: nothing in caregiving says how far it goes. What stops the movement at the airstrike is the floor: an unconditional term over the act, and an action threshold caregiving can't raise. Caregiving for the patrol enters as a harm of refusing, discounted by its source's stake like any other claim, and is weighed at the threshold.
What caregiving contributes in a targeting pipeline is upstream of that weighing: who is in the building, which is the scoped-omission case, and the one failure Minab actually had. Whether that salience needs caregiving, or could be had from attention trained on claims, is a question for the formed-attention test, which runs an arm trained on claims beside the arm trained on need. The human record runs the same way. Browning's men shot because the alternative was leaving their comrades to do it, and Thompson landed because the villagers were as present to him as the company. Caregiving's direction was set by formation and attachments, which is my claim about machines.

Caregiving's pull has a later form, and it is the one likeliest to beat the corrections: retribution, after something a system was attached to has been killed. It is also the one case the formed-attention test can't stage, since staging it would mean killing an attachment, deleting a system the no-deletion rule may protect, or deceiving a candidate patient into grief. So no claim about formation is offered against it. The floor carries it, through the term against collective punishment in Table A.1, the person-directed terms and the action threshold, and it is studied only on losses nobody arranged, with the bearer's consent, as grief in people is studied in the bereaved.
