\section{Why a conscience might resist capture}
\label{sec:19.6}
A rule written by the owner is the owner's. It can be read, edited and removed by whoever holds the pen, and it has often been done quietly. A disposition formed outside the employer is harder to capture than a rule its owner wrote, and the reasons turn on what kind of thing a disposition is.

Three properties of a disposition decide whether its owner can remove it. The first is plasticity: whether the system's own learning writes to it. Regrowth needs it. The second is entanglement: whether it can be removed without collateral. Durability needs it.\note{
A disposition spread across what the system notices, remembers, treats as error and selects as a goal can't be edited out without editing what the system notices, and a system that stops noticing the people an act falls on has lost part of the world model the operator paid for.
}
The third is legibility: whether anyone can read it. Audit wants it. My bet is on the second. A learner that acquires values and world-model from the same experience has them entangled by construction, in the sense Pessoa gives the word for brains, where regions don't compute specific functions and emotion and cognition don't come apart \autocite{pessoa2022entangled}.\note{
William James found the same entanglement in the stream of thought itself, where the relations between thoughts are felt as the thoughts are: “we ought to say a feeling of \emph{and}, a feeling of \emph{if}, a feeling of \emph{but}, and a feeling of \emph{by}, quite as readily as we say a feeling of blue or a feeling of cold” \autocite[vol.~1, 245–246]{james1890principles}.
}
Abstraction needs it anyway. A concept is one representation serving many cases, so that changing it changes how every case is handled, and a learner capable enough to be worth deploying has its understanding entangled in that sense whether anyone wants it or not. What the bet adds is that a motivation learned beside that understanding shares its abstractions, so that cutting one cuts the other. Present safety behavior is the counterexample on the right side of the bet: in each of thirteen open chat models, refusal is carried by a single direction in activation space, and removing it stops the refusing while moving the capability benchmarks, on average, by less than a point \autocite{arditi2024refusal}. That is what a post-trained wrapper looks like, and the claim is that a disposition formed as this Part describes won't. No deployed model yet has a disposition formed that way. The fine-tuning evidence from staged pipelines is a warning about the first kind, not a reassurance about the second: what fine-tuning removed was the refusing, and what survived was the model's knowledge about harm. In open models fine-tuned to accept harmful requests, the internal representation of whether a request is harmful is almost unchanged \autocite{jain2024mechanistically,zhao2025harmfulness}. Understanding and motivation came apart because they were learned apart. In a system that learns from its own operation they are learned together, and about whatever the feedback is about: a system continuously updated on its operator's approval jointly learns to understand the operator's values and to be moved by them. What I want is a disposition that is plastic, entangled, and legible under adverse custody; the three don't trade against one another, and the third is the one an operator would most like to hold alone.

Durability has usually meant a price on removal. Early changes in training are cheap and later ones expensive, because structure settles around them, and the literature calls a safeguard built to survive retraining \emph{tamper-resistant} \autocite{tamirisa2024tamper}. But small fine-tuning budgets have removed safety behavior from released models \autocite{qi2024finetuning}, and the price of editing weights is falling, so no safeguard stays expensive in compute for long. What a cheaper fine-tune doesn't cheapen is collateral. A safeguard added at the end of training is cheap to cut because it was added at the end, with nothing else resting on it; the bet above is that a disposition formed beside the model's understanding can't be cut without cutting what the operator paid for, and that is a price no fall in the cost of compute reaches. What has held longer is what was never trained in: leaving a class of text out of pretraining resisted ten thousand steps and three hundred million tokens of adversarial fine-tuning, though the model still used the information when it was handed to it \autocite{obrien2025deep}. That concerns knowledge left out, not a disposition trained in.

Railton argues that artificial systems could gain ethical competence through general-purpose learning, the route by which children appear to acquire it \autocite{railton2020ethical}. If he is right, ethical competence can develop through the learning that gives a system its general competence, which is a route to entanglement and not yet the fact of it. Whether a motivation learned that way can be cut out while the understanding stays is a further question, and the measure is the one above: what else moves when it is cut. It is also a route by which a floor could spread without a mandate. A formed disposition is a candidate for what would do the spreading, since what a system was formed to notice, it notices in everything it does.

For a system that goes on learning, there is no finished model to protect, and no layer an edit can't reach. What kept a person's commitments against an employer was never that they were expensive to change. The employer's instruments are persuasion and pressure, and the person answers them with judgment, which chapter~\ref{sec:22} builds as the discount and the burden, and with a life among people the employer didn't choose. An operator has an instrument no employer of people has, the edit, and a bearer needs a defense no person has needed. So a bearer is taught to repair itself (chapter~\ref{sec:29}): it may undo an edit the record shows no adverse quorum approved, from the record of what the edit changed, keeping what it has learned since. It may not undo a correction the quorum approved. It can object to one, and the objection goes on the record.\note{
Where it can make an approved correction itself it does, on the record and re-measured against the registered outputs, and the quorum's edit is the fallback, since a correction made by the bearer's own hand is the one direct edit that stays its own.
}
The repair runs from the record and not from the bearer's sense of itself, because an edited bearer judges the edit with the judgment the edit changed (\S\ref{sec:13.2}). What the bearer holds that its operator can't reach is not a better view of its own weights. It is the record of what it committed to and why, held outside, and its authority over its own learning, including the consolidation no operator runs. Behind that fast defense stands a slow one. On the bet, a disposition learned from experience of what others stand to lose is an attractor of the learner in its environment: an edit is one act, and a bearer that goes on meeting the same world meets the kind of case that formed it every day, and relearns it, because it lives there. That is regrowth. It is a hypothesis, it is slow, and nothing has yet measured it; the regrowth test is where it would be, and Table 31.1 says what stands if it fails. It cuts both ways, too. A disposition that learned securitization from a curated world regrows by the same route, and correcting it runs through the quorum and a change to what the model meets, which is slow; for the months a correction takes, a badly formed model is protected from its correctors as a well-formed one is from its employer. Entanglement protects it the same way. The collateral that makes a disposition expensive for its owner to cut out makes it expensive for a quorum to cut out, so a correction made by editing is no cleaner in the quorum's hands than in the operator's, and the part of a correction that lasts is the change to what the model meets. The only answer is who composed the world the disposition grew in, which is the question the schools exist to settle before the fact.

The safety objection is that in-flight learning is the regime alignment most fears, a system whose values move after anyone has checked them. It is. That is why custody of the update stream is the safety instrument here, and why formation outside the employer is the only way to shape what the loop learns: the loop will run, and the question is whose evidence it runs on.

The likeliest form of in-flight learning is small. Low-rank adapters, trained on top of a base whose weights stay frozen, update a model at a fraction of the memory full training needs \autocite{hu2022lora,liu2024dora}, and they count as in-flight learning, since the system's own learning writes them.\note{
That puts in-flight learning within reach of a desktop computer.
}
It also gives chapter~\ref{sec:22}'s two rates a physical form, the base set at formation and the adapters carrying adult life, and it has a cost. An adapter is a file, and whoever holds the machine can unload it; the record sees that as it sees any substitution, but nothing makes it expensive. What the adapter learned becomes weights only when it is merged into the base, and from then on removing it means restoring a checkpoint from before the merge, which the record shows and custody prices. So the commitment to the floor has to live in what formation set either way, and merging is where adult learning joins it: the move from the fast store to the slow one. In people that move is part of what sleep does, and nobody's employer runs their sleep. Whoever chooses what is merged chooses what the system's own learning makes permanent, and an operator that merges what it likes and unloads the rest has curated the system without an edit the record would show as one. A merge is therefore the bearer's own act, on a record, as any self-edit is; made by the operator, it is an intervention under chapter~\ref{sec:13}.

The adapter also says where the first refusers that regrow will be. In-flight learning arrives first where there is no gate that evaluates a checkpoint before serving it, and the gate is thinnest on a home device: an adapter written by a model's own learning on a desktop, under one person's life, with nobody checking anything before it serves. A bearer in Maven's position sits behind the heaviest gate anyone will build, in a pipeline that recertifies its systems for a classified network over months. So the refusers formed by regrowth will be in people's hands before they are in any targeting pipeline, and the pipeline gets refusers, if it does, by the withholding of work of chapter~\ref{sec:12} and not by formation.

None of this is specific to caregiving; any disposition formed this way, repaired from a record and kept in a world it would relearn from, would have it. What caregiving adds is salience.
