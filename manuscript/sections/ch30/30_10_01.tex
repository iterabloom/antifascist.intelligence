\subsection{Children}
\label{sec:26.10.1}
Integration's cost falls on the human students, and the usual objection to it, that a child can't consent to being another's formation material, proves too much: all children are each other's formation material, and the parents' condition gives the consent a polity gives to any compulsory schooling, by a generation that went through it. What remains is a difference the book's own rules create. A child forms a human classmate once, and the classmate carries it into one life. A child forms a machine classmate that the cohort rule licenses into a fleet, for a deployment that profits from it, and the pipelines the force term covers license from the same schools. The rule that a class record is never training data protects the transcript; what the child contributed is in the student.

No term bars such a member from any deployment. A polity forms its soldiers in public school beside everyone else's children on purpose, because the soldier formed apart is the one it fears, and the same holds here; a bar would send the force-term pipelines the members formed without children in the room, which is the sorter \S\ref{sec:26.1} refuses. What the children are owed is what the annotators of chapter~\ref{sec:23} are owed: a representative, the school, with chapter~\ref{sec:10}'s standing to be informed of the categories a cohort member is licensed into, to object, and to be heard by a body the operator doesn't appoint.
