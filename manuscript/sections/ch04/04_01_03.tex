\subsection{Human-Like Learning and Generalization in AI Systems}
\label{sec:4.1.3}
Few-shot learning aims at models that classify or predict accurately from a handful of examples rather than from a dataset built for the case in front of them, and large pretrained language models do this at system scale, performing a task from a few examples in the prompt with no parameter update at all \autocite{brown2020fewshot}. The technique inventory changes yearly; the problem underneath it does not, and it is an evaluation problem rather than a modeling one. A handful of examples is easy to overfit, moral dilemmas do not arrive in clean representative sets, and telling whether a system's few-shot ethical judgment generalized or merely matched surface features of the examples it saw needs people trained in ethics to say what the system was supposed to have learned. The same difficulty returns for transfer, where a floor carried into a setting it was not built for cannot tell that it has stopped applying.

A system built for empathy and altruism can also be built in developmental order rather than assembled at once.
