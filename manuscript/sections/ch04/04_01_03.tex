\subsection{Human-Like Learning and Generalization in AI Systems}
\label{sec:4.1.3}
Few-shot learning aims at models that classify or predict accurately from a handful of examples rather than from a dataset built for the case in front of them — for an ethical system, judgment that extends to a new scenario the way a person's values apply to situations they have never seen. Large pretrained language models do this at system scale, performing a task from a few examples in the prompt with no parameter update at all \autocite{brown2020fewshot}. The technique inventory changes yearly; the problem underneath it does not, and it is an evaluation problem rather than a modeling one.

A handful of examples is easy to overfit, moral dilemmas do not arrive in clean representative sets, and telling whether a system's few-shot ethical judgment generalized — or merely matched surface features of the examples it saw — is hard. Building that test is not a machine-learning task alone; it needs people trained in ethics to say what the system was supposed to have learned. The same difficulty comes up for transfer, where a floor carried into a setting it was not built for cannot tell that it has stopped applying.

A system built for empathy and altruism can also be built in developmental order rather than assembled at once, and section~\ref{sec:5.1.1} sets out what the developmental stage theories will and will not carry.
