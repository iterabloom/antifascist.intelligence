4.6.3.1.2. Strategies for Developing AI Systems Committed to Democratic Principles
Creating AI systems that are fundamentally committed to democratic principles calls for robust strategies that cater to various aspects of AI development - value alignment, interdisciplinary collaboration, government oversight, public education, and international cooperation. These facets, when meticulously put together, can help spur the development of AI systems that not only resist authoritarian tendencies but actively bolster democratic values.

1) Democratic Value Alignment: The moral compass of AI systems should be attuned to fundamental democratic principles, including freedom of expression, privacy, fairness, and inclusivity. Utilizing techniques such as fairness-aware machine learning and debiasing algorithms, engineers should be continually vigilant in straightening any biases that may skew the AI's judgement. By recurrently vetting data or models throughout the development phase for sources of discrimination, AI can be honed to uphold democratic values. 

2) Interdisciplinary Stakeholder Collaboration: A successful AI ecosystem thrives in diversity. Engaging stakeholders from various walks of life - public, business, academia, and civil society - can infuse AI decision-making with a spectrum of insights and perspectives. Interdisciplinary research, backed by substantial funding, can aid in comprehending societal needs and values more holistically. This, in turn, can help in architecting AI systems that staunchly defend human rights and democratic values. Models for such collaborative platforms already exist, such as the Partnership on AI, which promotes a multi-stakeholder dialogue to foster ethical AI practices.

3) Government Regulation and Public Oversight: Governments play a crucial role in framing robust regulatory frameworks that underline transparency, accountability, and fairness for AI technologies. Such legal safeguards must include regular scrutiny of AI applications, compelling organizations to adhere to ethical AI practices, data privacy, and algorithms' accountability and explainability.

4) AI Literacy: Educating the public, policymakers, and all AI stakeholders about potential biases and the ethical implications of AI can empower society to make informed choices regarding AI technologies. Funding and promoting interdisciplinary ethics education and training programs for AI developers can ensure that they are equipped to navigate the ethical waters of AI development.

5) International Cooperation: Encouraging cross-border dialogues and collaborations on ethical AI research and policies can help establish international norms and standards that safeguard against authoritarian misuse of AI. Such global cooperation can create a united front against any threats to democratic principles in AI developments.

Practical examples of AI systems embodying these principles include:

- AI-assisted Fact-checking: AI systems, like Full Fact, that debunk false narratives or misinformation, protecting public opinion and democratic processes from manipulation.

- AI for Fair Elections: AI technologies that monitor political campaigns, scrutinizing funding sources, detecting voting patterns, and identifying unfair campaign tactics, can create a more transparent and fair electoral process.

- Privacy-enhancing Technologies: AI systems, like Apple's differential privacy implementation, shielding individual privacy and thwarting mass surveillance.

- Tackling Online Hate Speech: AI entities, like Google's Perspective API, that spot and inhibit hate speech, online harassment, and cyberbullying, contribute to safer internet domains and foster democratic discourse.

With the creation and utilization of AI systems engineered around democratic principles and values, it's possible to construct a technologically advanced society that empowers its citizens, protects human rights, and upholds democratic norms.
