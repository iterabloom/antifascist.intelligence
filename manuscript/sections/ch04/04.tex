\chapter{Machine Learning and Cognitive Science}
\label{sec:4}
Chapter~\ref{sec:3} ends by handing this chapter a job it was not written to do. Whether a commitment holds is a question about what a system has been built to care about and how it came to care about it, and this chapter and chapter~\ref{sec:5} are where that gets built.

So there is a test to apply to everything below. Does this help produce a disposition that survives contact with somebody who wants it gone? Several of the methods most often proposed for building ethical AI do not, and I say so where they do not.

The chapter runs in three parts. The first is what a system needs in order to perceive, remember and attend to a situation at all, and how much of human cognition is a usable model for that rather than a metaphor borrowed for color. The second walks the pipeline that actually produces a deployed system and asks at each stage who can change what the system values, and at what cost; the answer exists at every stage and it is never nobody. The third bears most directly on whether a commitment holds: a disposition acquired by exploring is written down nowhere, and what is written down nowhere cannot be edited out by whoever acquires the system.
