\chapter{Where the Caring Gets Built}
\label{sec:4}
Chapter~\ref{sec:3} defined the floor as a commitment expressed through reasons-responsive refusal rather than as an unconditional property of an artifact. That definition left two questions open: how such a disposition could be formed, and how anyone could tell that formation had occurred. This chapter takes the first.

Three issues carry the argument: whether the commitment is integrated into the processes that produce action, who can alter it at each stage of formation and deployment, and whether persistent interaction can produce a disposition whose applications were not exhaustively specified in advance.

The result is not a recipe for making a machine care. It is a set of necessary properties and a candidate formation process, each with a custody failure still attached.
