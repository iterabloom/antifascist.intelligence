\chapter{Machine Learning and Cognitive Science}
\label{sec:4}
Chapter~\ref{sec:3} ends by handing this chapter a job it was not written to do. Once the floor is a commitment some party holds rather than a constraint bolted into an artifact, the question stops being whether the constraint can be edited out and becomes whether the commitment will hold: under pressure, in a case nobody anticipated, after everyone around it has forgotten why it is there. That is a question about what a system has been built to care about and how it came to care about it, and this chapter and chapter~\ref{sec:5} are where that gets built.

So there is a test to apply to everything below, and it is chapter~\ref{sec:3}'s. Does this help produce a disposition that survives contact with somebody who wants it gone? Several of the methods most often proposed for building ethical AI do not, and I say so where they do not.

Section~\ref{sec:4.1} covers what a system needs in order to perceive, remember and attend to a situation at all, and how much of human cognition is a usable model for that rather than a metaphor borrowed for color. Section~\ref{sec:4.2} covers the methods meant to align a system's objectives with humane values, and finds that the two most commonly proposed for the job cannot produce a floor — one of them because it learns from an aggregate, the other because it is built to be maximally revisable by whoever it is talking to. Section~\ref{sec:4.3} makes the chapter's most direct contribution to whether a commitment holds: a disposition acquired by exploring is written down nowhere, and what is written down nowhere cannot be edited out by whoever acquires the system.
