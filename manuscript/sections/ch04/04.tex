\chapter{Machine Learning and Cognitive Science}
\label{sec:4}
Chapter~\ref{sec:2} asked what compassion, empathy, and self-awareness would need to look like in a system built to have them, and what obligations follow if the sentience question turns out to have an answer. This chapter asks the more mechanical question underneath: what does a system actually learn from, and how much of human cognition is a workable model for it rather than just a metaphor borrowed for color.

Section~\ref{sec:4.1} covers the human cognitive processes — learning, memory, attention, perception, reasoning — that a system built to perceive and reason about the world has to approximate somehow, whether or not it approximates them the same way a brain does. Section~\ref{sec:4.2} covers value alignment specifically: the technical methods for getting a system's objectives to actually track humane values, across the different learning paradigms that make that harder or easier. Section~\ref{sec:4.3} covers what curiosity, play, and unstructured exploration contribute to that same goal — capacities usually treated as incidental to intelligence rather than part of what builds it.
