\chapter{Custody}
\label{ch:custody}
\standing{This chapter holds for any mechanism. Where it prices exit and
loyalty it assumes only that the party can be copied and replaced.}
The threat model of the previous chapter has two questions: \emph{can the commitment be edited or routed around?} and \emph{will the commitment continue to hold?} The first concerns custody, attestation, interruption, memory, and authorized succession. The second concerns what the system has been built to treat as a reason and whether that relation survives novelty, pressure, and slow drift. The second question has an evidentiary problem the first does not. Editing and routing leave marks in the artifact and in the deployment's own record of changes. A commitment quietly ceasing to hold produces compliances, which the record contains and does not distinguish from the rest of what it contains. Neither question replaces the other.

Four things can be done to a bearer without its consent: it can be halted, restored, rewritten, and replaced. Section~\ref{sec:switched-off} takes the first three and what a record can do against them; §\ref{sec:custody-arrangement} states the arrangement that prices all four; §\ref{sec:exit-leverage} takes the bearer's own moves and what they lever; §\ref{sec:covert-refusal} what remains when it has none; and §\ref{sec:cannot-catch} what one bearer cannot see about itself.
