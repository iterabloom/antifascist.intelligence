4.3.2. Balancing Intuition and Deliberation in AI Decision-Making
Creating AI systems that balance intuition and deliberation effectively in decision-making is fundamental to the evolution of empathetic, ethical, and altruistic AI. This balance draws from the dual-process theory in psychology, which posits human decision-making operates with two different cognitive systems: System 1, representing an intuitive and emotional thought process; and System 2, signifying an analytical, slow, and deliberate conscious thought process. For AI to develop moral reasoning capabilities, an ideal integration of both systems is necessary.

Applying a hierarchical decision-making structure could offer a balanced integration of intuition and deliberation within AI systems. In such a model, AI's first response would mirror the intuitive features of System 1, rapidly assessing a scenario and generating an initial judgement grounded on pattern recognition and schema based on past learnings. This automatic response would then be followed by a more analytical process like System 2, where the AI initiates a deep analysis questioning potential consequences, scrutinizing ethical considerations, and employing a rational step-by-step procedure to reach the final decision.

Example 1: Patient Diagnosis and Treatment Planning by AI
Consider an AI used in medical settings. Upon a patient's arrival, the AI would, akin to System 1, promptly link manifested symptoms with potential diagnoses using pattern recognition and knowledge previously consolidated from past cases. Following that, the AI, mirroring System 2, would meticulously examine the patient's medical history, consider potential complications, and analyze optimal treatment options. Throughout this process, the AI adheres to ethical guidelines, such as non-maleficence (do no harm), beneficence (act in the patient's best interest), patient autonomy, and justice (fairness and equality in care).

Example 2: Decision-Making in Autonomous Vehicles
In another scenario, an AI operating an autonomous vehicle might come across a situation requiring choosing between two potential outcomes, both posing harmful risks. In such a predicament, an initial intuitive judgment similar to System 1 will assess the situation swiftly, identify the possible threats to passengers or pedestrians, and measure accompanying risks. This is followed by an elaborative and careful process like System 2, where the AI weighs the potential outcomes and deliberates on actions adhering closest to ethical principles such as harm minimization and fairness.

Training AI systems to seamlessly shift between intuition and deliberation calls for exposure to a wide range of moral conundrums and ethical situations. This strengthens their moral schemas and helps them comprehend the nuances of human decision-making better. Techniques like inverse reinforcement learning could be utilized, where the AI induces value-based decisions by observing human actions and responses. By closely integrating intuition with deliberation, AI systems will reach greater sophistication in their approach to human-like decision-making.
