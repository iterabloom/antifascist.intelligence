\section{You Can't Hold a Line You Didn't Notice}
\label{sec:4.1}
A system that is going to hold anything under pressure has to notice the pressure. It has to register what is in front of it, hold why a constraint exists long enough to see that the reason has died, and keep its attention on the part of a situation that a well-designed process would rather it skipped. Those are perception, memory and attention. Each has a failure mode a system cannot detect in itself, and a floor that has failed gives no sign that it has. A system built for empathy and altruism can also be built in developmental order rather than assembled at once, which is chapter~\ref{sec:5}'s subject and not this one's.
