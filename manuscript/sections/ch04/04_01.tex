\section{What a floor can do}
\label{sec:4.1}
A floor stands between a pipeline and a person, and it can prevent, arrest, detect, or remedy something the pipeline might do, is doing, or did to that person.

\begingroup\small\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.12}
\begin{longtable}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{\dimexpr 0.259\linewidth-2\tabcolsep\relax}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{\dimexpr 0.741\linewidth-2\tabcolsep\relax}@{}}
\hline
\textbf{Outcome} & \textbf{What happens to the act} \\
\hline
\endhead
Prevent & The refusal happens and reaches the person: the act doesn't occur. \\ \hline
Arrest & The act is stopped before completion. \\ \hline
Detect & The act happened, and left a record someone outside can read. \\ \hline
Remedy & The person affected gets disclosure, a finding, or compensation. \\ \hline
\end{longtable}
\endgroup

The table also sorts what the five policies do when nobody in the room will refuse. The first two rows need a refuser: take out of the room the one person who would have said no, and prevention and arrest leave with them. The last two rows don't. A pre-registered ratio still shows how many cases there were and how many people had time to look at them, which is a machine deciding and a person signing, on the record. Notice still publishes which classes of people were acted on and in what numbers, and the organizations that speak for a class still have standing. Preservation still holds the weights, and the logs with them, under a key the operator doesn't hold alone. An institution with no conscience in the room keeps the evidence for the conscience that comes later: a commission, a court, the people left to tell the story. That much the policies do without a refuser. The first row they can't.
