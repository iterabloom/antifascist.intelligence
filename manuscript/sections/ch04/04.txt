Chapter 4: Moral Psychology and AI
Chapter 3 asked what a system would need to perceive, remember, and attend to the world at all. This chapter asks what it would take for that system to act well in it — not by following a rule, but by developing something closer to a moral sense: intuitions that fire fast and reasoning that checks them, built the way a human's moral sense is built, through practice, correction, and the pull of other people's expectations. The chapter follows that development from its social origins, through the friction between gut reaction and deliberate judgment, out to the wider ecosystem of institutions and incentives it operates inside, and down to the motivation and play that make learning a moral sense possible in the first place, rather than just having one installed.
