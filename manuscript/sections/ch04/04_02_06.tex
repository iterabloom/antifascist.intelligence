\subsection{Scalable Oversight}
\label{sec:4.2.6}
Every stage above needs somebody competent to judge the output being scored. Scalable oversight is the name for what happens when the system's competence exceeds any available judge's, and it is unsolved. Debate has two instances of a system argue opposing sides in front of a human judge, on the wager that a true claim is easier to defend under scrutiny even when the judge cannot check either side directly \autocite{irving2018ai}. Weak-to-strong generalization trains a stronger model on labels from a deliberately weaker one and asks whether the stronger model's own latent competence lets it exceed its supervisor rather than imitate the supervisor's mistakes \autocite{burns2023weaktostrong}. Section~\ref{sec:11.5} has the status of both, which is that neither has been demonstrated at the scale it is built for, because that scale does not yet exist to test against.

What belongs here is what the whole program is for. Every proposal in it is a way of preserving one party's ability to check a system that has outgrown them. That is the control relation section~\ref{sec:3.5} analyses, engineered to survive a capability gap, and it inherits the analysis whole. A scalable-oversight scheme that worked perfectly would deliver a system reliably steerable by whoever holds it at a capability level where nobody else could tell whether the steering was sound — which is the property section~\ref{sec:3.5} identifies as the one that prevents a system from holding anything against the party doing the steering.

The same research reads the other way round, and this is the part worth carrying forward. Nothing in debate or in weak-to-strong generalization specifies that the judge is the operator. An auditor, a regulator, or a research team with access could occupy the same position, and then the identical technique is what makes an outside party able to check a system it did not build. Chapter~\ref{sec:11} arrives at this from a different direction and finds it the binding constraint on its whole research program: the instruments are dual, and what determines which way they point is who holds them, which is not a parameter anybody sets in a training run.
