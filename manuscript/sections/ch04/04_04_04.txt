4.4.4. Macrosystem Influences on AI Development and Employment
Macrosystems, aptly explained as the overarching cultural, societal, and economic contexts, play an integral role in shaping the development and utilization of AI technologies. Acknowledging these influences is pivotal to the creation of AI systems that uphold ideals of altruism and resist authoritarian tendencies.

Engaging with two distinctive healthcare systems, the market-driven, competitive framework of the United States, and the equity-focused, centralized system of Norway, we can uncover how macrosystemic influences directly affect AI development.

In the American healthcare setting, underpinned by neoliberal values, profit-driven objectives tend to guide AI development. This results in a disproportionate focus towards generating technologies that cater to wealthier segments of society or high-profit medical conditions, leaving vital yet less profitable sectors like preventative care or mental health under-resourced. Additionally, the fragmented US healthcare system may lead to AI solutions being tailored towards specific provider networks or insurance groups, complicating interoperability and increasing disparities in healthcare access.

Alternatively, the Norwegian healthcare system, rooted in principles of universal access and equity, promotes AI development aimed at elevating patient outcomes and enhancing system-wide efficacy. These imperatives encourage the production of comprehensive AI solutions addressing a broad spectrum of health issues while benefiting the entire patient population. Moreover, the centralized nature of Norway's healthcare system fosters seamless integration and data sharing across the system, resulting in coherent and synergistic AI applications.

Extending beyond healthcare, these macrosystem dynamics pervade various sectors and countries. Factors such as public policy, cultural values and economic conditions hold considerable sway over the direction and trajectory of AI research and applications. In regions with a strong focus on public education investment, AI applications are likely to prioritize lifelong learning and accessibility. Conversely, areas marred by insufficient worker rights protections may grapple with the task of aligning efficiency improvements driven by AI with labor rights and job security concerns.

In fostering altruistic and anti-authoritarian AI systems amidst diverse macrosystem contexts, we propose several strategies:

(1) Promote and fund interdisciplinary research and dialogue to scrutinize the intersection of cultural, socio-political, and economic contexts with AI development and deployment.

(2) Advocate for regulatory measures and public policies that prioritize ethical and anti-authoritarian AI development, address potential risks, and foster collaboration among stakeholders.

(3) Perform comparative analyses of different AI systems in diverse macrosystem contexts to compile best practices and innovations that advance altruism, counter authoritarianism, and promote shared ethical principles.

(4) Support cross-border and cross-cultural knowledge exchange to encourage the creation and dissemination of AI applications that protect democratic values and resist authoritarian inclinations.

The context of adaptive macrosystem environments profoundly shapes the trajectory of AI development. A nuanced understanding of these influences is critical in ensuring the creation of AI systems that espouse ideals of altruism, foster equity, oppose authoritarian tendencies, and ultimately, enhance societal well-being and uphold democratic principles throughout their deployment.
