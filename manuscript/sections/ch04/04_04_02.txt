4.4.2. Microsystem Interactions and AI Moral Learning
An AI system's microsystem is the immediate environment it is built and trained in — the research lab, the company, the academic department — where it first encounters the researchers and engineers who write its code, the datasets that carry social norms into its training, and the early users who give it feedback.

At this level, an AI system's moral learning is shaped less by any single dataset than by direct interaction with the people around it: the developers who train it, the users who query it, and the wider public whose expectations eventually reach it secondhand. Two things do most of that shaping.

<<list>>
- Human mentorship: developers, researchers, and engineers act as an AI system's mentors whether they intend to or not, the way a child absorbs ethical behavior from parents and teachers. An autonomous vehicle's training, for instance, runs through sustained contact with its designers, safety engineers, and the driving public — contact that is where judgment about a crash scenario actually gets built, not just specified after the fact.
- Exposure to diversity: a system trained on a narrow slice of human perspective learns a narrow slice of human ethics. Datasets and stakeholder groups that span cultures, languages, and moral frameworks give a system the material to navigate an ambiguous case rather than defaulting to whatever perspective dominated its training data. A conversational AI system that has only ever talked to one demographic will not recognize what it is missing when a different one arrives.
<</list>>

A microsystem built this way — mentored deliberately, exposed broadly — is what gives an AI system the raw material for something like altruism, rather than a narrow reflection of whoever happened to build it and easily redirected by whoever controls it next.
