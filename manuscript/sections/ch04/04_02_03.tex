\subsection{Supervised Learning for Ethical Decision-Making}
\label{sec:4.2.3}
Supervised learning fits a model to labeled examples so that it can classify cases it has not seen. Pointed at ethics, that means a corpus of decisions marked ethical or unethical — medical cases scored against autonomy, beneficence and justice; human-rights situations annotated by experts; MIT's Moral Machine project, which collected tens of millions of human judgments about who an autonomous vehicle should spare \autocite{awad2018moral}.

The method works, and what it produces is an aggregate. That is the whole of the problem. Every label came from somebody, the model recovers the central tendency of what those people said, and a system trained this way will reproduce a moral consensus rather than stand against one. Chapter~\ref{sec:3}'s opening argument is that no procedure over what people want yields a floor, because the floor is precisely what has to survive the answer coming back wrong — and a labeled corpus is what people want, written down and counted. Sections~\ref{sec:8.2.2} and \ref{sec:10.10} make the same point about deliberation and about majority rule.

The Moral Machine data makes the limit concrete rather than theoretical. Its headline finding was systematic cross-cultural variation: different populations weighed age, numbers and the legal status of the crossing differently, and the differences were structured rather than noise. Fit a model to the aggregate and it encodes a compromise nobody holds. Fit it per-region and it encodes each region's answer, including the answers a floor exists to refuse.

The unsupervised version escapes none of this. Clustering and dimensionality reduction over a body of moral judgment surface which dilemmas group together and which distinctions do most of the separating, and generative models can build cases no annotator wrote. All of it is a way of finding what is in a corpus, which puts it under section~\ref{sec:6.1.1}'s warning rather than outside it: structure recovered from text is the structure of the text.

So the honest place for the method is above the floor and not under it. Labeled cases are how a system learns the enormous amount of ordinary moral competence that is genuinely a matter of what people think — what counts as rude, what a reasonable delay is, when an explanation is owed. What they cannot supply is the part that has to hold when the labels change.
