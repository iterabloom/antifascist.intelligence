8.2.3. Fostering Ethical AI Development and Anti-Authoritarian Sentience in International Contexts
In order to promote the development of ethical AI systems with anti-authoritarian sentience on an international level, it is crucial to involve and engage various stakeholders, ranging from governments to international organizations, research institutions, and the private sector. By establishing an ecosystem in which diverse perspectives and expertise can contribute to the shaping of AI ethics and applications, we have the potential to foster collaboration and shared commitment to a future that upholds democratic values and combats authoritarian ideologies.

Some key steps to achieve this objective are outlined below:

(1) International Guidelines and Ethical Frameworks: International organizations such as the United Nations (UN), the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD), and the World Economic Forum (WEF) can play an important role in developing and promoting global ethical guidelines and frameworks for AI development. These guidelines can serve as a common foundation for countries and organizations to ensure that AI systems are designed with transparency, accountability, and respect for human rights at their core.

For example, the European Union has recently introduced a proposal for AI regulations that put forward an "AI Act" aimed at ensuring that AI systems are used only in ways that are lawful, ethical, and respect human rights. This could serve as an inspiration for other countries and international organizations to harmonize their own AI regulatory frameworks.

(2) Sharing Best Practices and Lessons Learned: These international organizations can also serve as platforms for exchanging best practices, experiences, and innovative approaches to ethical AI development that fosters anti-authoritarian sentiments. By facilitating dialogue and collaboration across borders, these organizations can help create a global AI community united in the pursuit of ethical, altruistic, and anti-authoritarian AI applications.

(3) Capacity-Building Programs: In order to build expertise in ethical AI development and its applications worldwide, international organizations should initiate and support capacity-building programs for researchers, policy makers, and AI developers. These programs can help provide the necessary knowledge and skills needed to tackle the ethical challenges associated with AI and contribute to the development of anti-authoritarian AI systems.

For instance, UNESCO's "AI Ethics" capacity-building program aims to equip stakeholders in AI policy and deployment with the necessary ethical background in AI technologies. Such initiatives can be expanded and replicated across regions to promote a global understanding of ethical AI and train the next generation of AI developers with the right set of values.

(4) Multilateral Research Initiatives: Establishing joint research initiatives among different countries and institutions can fund collaborative research on AI ethics, especially concerning anti-authoritarianism. These collaborations should focus on understanding the underpinnings of authoritarian tendencies and how AI systems can be designed to be resistant or even actively counteract them.

For example, the Partnership on AI, founded as a cooperative effort among various tech giants, academic institutions, and non-profit organizations, aims to address global challenges related to AI, including upholding democratic values and combatting authoritarian tendencies.

Fostering ethical AI development and anti-authoritarian sentience in international contexts requires the dedicated efforts of numerous stakeholders, supported by international organizations and frameworks. By creating a global community committed to ethical AI and sharing best practices in this domain, we have the best chance of achieving the development and deployment of AI that upholds democratic values and resists authoritarian ideologies.
