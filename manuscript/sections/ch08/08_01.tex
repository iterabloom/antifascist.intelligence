\section{Interdisciplinary Collaboration and Knowledge Exchange}
\label{sec:8.1}
Building benevolent AI takes more than one field. Machine learning sets what a system can do; cognitive science and moral psychology describe what actually tracking human welfare would require of it; ethics asks whether the result is good. None of these fields gets the system right by itself, and how they combine matters as much as what each contributes on its own.

The mechanisms that combine them are easy to name and easy to overrate. Conferences convene, centers house, journals publish, and an inventory of them would imply that what is scarce here is venues. Venues are abundant. What is scarce is a structure that makes collaboration expensive to walk away from. A grant that requires a machine-learning lab and an ethics or psychology department to work the same problem rather than adjacent ones puts people who would not otherwise share a budget, a dataset, or a deadline into the same project, and keeps them there after the novelty wears off. A conference ends on Friday.
