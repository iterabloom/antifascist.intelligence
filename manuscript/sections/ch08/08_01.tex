\section{Everybody Knows a Different Piece}
\label{sec:8.1}
Building benevolent AI takes more than one field. Machine learning sets what a system can do; cognitive science and moral psychology describe what tracking human welfare would require of it; ethics asks whether the result is good.

Conferences convene, centers house, journals publish, and an inventory of them would imply that what is scarce here is venues. Venues are abundant. What is scarce is a structure that makes collaboration expensive to walk away from. A grant that requires a machine-learning lab and an ethics or psychology department to work the same problem rather than adjacent ones keeps people in the same project after the novelty wears off. A conference ends on Friday.

Which structures actually held can be ranked by something better than plausibility, because enough time has passed to look.

Almost everything founded on standing money between 2016 and 2019 is still running — two venues with no announced end date, an endowed university institute, the Berkeley center pairing AI researchers with cognitive scientists and philosophers inside one institution's budget, and the AAAI/ACM conference on AI, ethics and society at a ninth annual edition \autocite{aiethicslab2017,miri2018alignmentforum,stanford2019hai,berkeley2016chai,aaai2026aies}. Surviving is not the same as continuing the work: the institute that launched one of those venues moved its own priority in 2024 from technical alignment research to policy and public communication, so the venue has outlasted the program that motivated it \autocite{miri2024strategy}. Against that, the European Commission's SHERPA project put eleven organizations across six countries to work on a grant that ran four years, and the consortium ended with it, on schedule \autocite{sherpa2018shaping}. Project-term money buys a network with an expiry date attached.

The pattern in the failures is sharper than the pattern in the survivals. What proved most disposable was not the arm's-length academic work but the kind sited closest to a shipping product: an in-house ethics and society team of roughly thirty people in 2020 was cut to seven in a 2022 reorganization and to zero the following year, in the same wave of layoffs that accompanied a strategic reorientation around a model vendor \autocite{techcrunch2023microsoft}; a platform's machine-learning ethics team, the group that had found racial bias in its employer's own image-cropping algorithm, did not survive the first round of layoffs after an acquisition \autocite{gizmodo2022twitter}. Siting the work where the decisions are made is what makes it useful and is also what puts it inside the budget that gets cut first. That is an argument for external standing rather than against proximity.
