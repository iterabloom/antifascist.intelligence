\section{The Political Economy of AI and Power}
\label{sec:political-economy}
Concentrated power comes with access to capital. Concentrating power is the default outcome of AI development, because absent any pushback, capital does an excellent job of recruiting the inputs needed for a capable AI system: compute, data, and talent. Resistance to market power is among the antifascist design goals named at the start of this book.

Financial incentive mostly determines what AI development produces. The largest firms commit enormous resources to AI research aimed at operational efficiency, deeper user engagement, and market dominance, and that spending drives technical progress \autocite{maslej2025index}. What it does not automatically produce is a system whose outputs serve the people using it rather than the shareholders funding it: a recommendation system tuned to maximize time-on-platform has no built-in reason to prefer accurate content over inflammatory content \autocite{germano2026ranking}.

None of that is a fixed property of the technology. It is a property of who is paying, which is why the source of research funding is a policy instrument and not an accounting detail.
