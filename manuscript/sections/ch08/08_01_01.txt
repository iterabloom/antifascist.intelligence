8.1.1. Whether the Dialogue Outlasts the Book
Chapter 6 named the mechanisms this kind of research depends on: funded joint programs, standing institutes, recurring conferences, open forums, cross-border tooling. What that catalog does not answer is how much of it survives past the specific funding cycle or corporate mood that built it.

Some of it plainly has. The AI Ethics Lab and the AI Alignment Forum, convened in 2017 and 2018 respectively, are still running with no announced end date (section 6.1.3). The Center for Human-Compatible AI at Berkeley, Stanford's Institute for Human-Centered AI, and the joint MIT-Harvard Ethics and Governance of AI Initiative are endowed university centers, not grants with an expiration clause (sections 6.1.1-6.1.2). The AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society has recurred every year since it was first held in 2018 (section 6.1.1). That is infrastructure built to outlast whoever wrote the first proposal.

Some of it plainly has not. The SHERPA project, a substantial four-year, eleven-partner effort on AI's ethical and human-rights dimensions, ran on a fixed Horizon 2020 grant and ended on schedule in 2022 with no announced successor (section 6.1.2). And the specific kind of dialogue that is easiest to praise in the abstract — ethicists and engineers working inside the same organization on the same product decisions, not at arm's length in a university — has proven the most disposable part of the whole system. Microsoft's Ethics and Society team, tasked with keeping the company's stated AI principles connected to actual product decisions, shrank from roughly thirty people in 2020 to seven in an October 2022 reorganization, then to zero in March 2023 — cut, that time, in the same wave of layoffs Microsoft had announced that January as it reoriented itself around OpenAI's technology [[cite:C0601]]. Twitter's Machine Learning Ethics, Transparency and Accountability team, formed in 2021 and the group that had found racial bias in the platform's own image-cropping algorithm that same year, did not survive Elon Musk's first round of layoffs after he acquired the company in November 2022 [[cite:C0602]].

Neither fact makes the case for interdisciplinary dialogue wrong. It makes the case for asking, before treating a research program's existence as settled, which kind of institution is running it — and for noticing that the dialogue closest to an actual deployed system is currently the least protected kind there is.
