\subsection{Money}
\label{sec:8.4.3}
The only price a state operator can pay for removing a floor is a condition on its appropriation. The removal pipeline has been funded so that the condition seldom bites:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{2025 reconciliation.} The act gave ICE \$45 billion for detention capacity and nearly \$30 billion more for enforcement, both available through fiscal 2029 \autocite{obbba2025}.
\item \textbf{Fiscal 2026.} ICE received no full-year appropriation. It ran on continuing resolutions until DHS appropriations lapsed on 14 February 2026, and the reconciliation money stayed available throughout \autocite{gao2026detention}.
\item \textbf{Secure America Act, June 2026.} Roughly \$70 billion more for ICE and CBP through fiscal 2029, again by reconciliation. Unlike the earlier acts, it funds enforcement functions generally, without naming detention at all \autocite{secureamerica2026,gao2026detention}.
\end{itemize}

Annual appropriation, the instrument Boland used and Chapter~\ref{sec:4} and \S\ref{sec:10.6} relied on, now reaches little of the money. Multi-year lump sums without line items also give a funds limitation less to attach to. Amnesty's letter asks Congress to rescind ICE's “current funding” \autocite{AmnestyUSA2026Letter}. Most of that funding is now multi-year balances, which the Abolish ICE Act's rescission reaches and next year's appropriations bill does not.

Appropriations can also run the other way, and the record shows how. From fiscal 2010 through fiscal 2016, DHS appropriations acts provided that ICE's funding “shall maintain a level of not less than” 33,400, later 34,000, detention beds \autocite{dhsapprop2010,Geng2018BedQuota}. The text required beds, not detainees, but ICE tracked its compliance by average daily population \autocite{Geng2018BedQuota}. A capacity floor therefore worked as an occupancy target: a rate the funder fixed in advance and the operator met by finding people. It is Chapter~\ref{sec:4}'s review ratio turned inside out, and an instance of the metric replacing the thing measured (\S\ref{sec:12.1}).

The same 2010 act carried the other kind of condition too. None of the funds under its ICE salaries heading could continue a 287(g) delegation if the DHS Inspector General found that the delegation's terms had been violated \autocite{dhsapprop2010}. A finding by someone other than the operator ended the operator's ability to continue, which is the shape of a review that binds: a finding by someone else that changes the operator's ability to continue, without its consent.
