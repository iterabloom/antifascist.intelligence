\section{Where the labels come from}
\label{sec:23.3}
Custody of the methods is one question. The material the methods run on is another: a system's values have a supply chain, and it begins before training.

Whose judgment is encoded is a question about working conditions before it's a question about cognition. The filter that keeps one lab's model from reproducing the worst of what it was trained on was built in Nairobi, by workers paid between roughly \$1.32 and \$2 an hour to read and categorize descriptions of child sexual abuse, torture and murder, on production quotas \autocite{perrigo2023openai}. Workers like them organized. In May 2023 content moderators in Nairobi, working for several platforms through contractors, formed a union, and moderators had already sued their contractor and its client in Kenyan courts over their dismissal and their conditions \autocite{madung2023union,trf2023moderators}. Everything this chapter proposes for annotators, a right to refuse a category and standing to contest a scheme, is law catching up to something those workers did first, which is the order in which codetermination has always arrived (chapter~\ref{sec:10}). The labor point isn't ordinary. The work that scars went to Nairobi for the reason the plantation ledger sorted by price: there is a division of who does the damaging work, it follows the colonial one, and the price at which the work is done there is the product of that history, not a market error. A channel for the Nairobi worker regulates the terms of the division. What ends it is that nobody is made cheap enough to do the work at that price, which is a wage and not a channel, and the moderators' union asked for pay, hazard pay and mental-health care before anyone offered it a channel \autocite{trf2023moderators}. The epistemic point is that a system's values are downstream of a labor arrangement, and the arrangement is a design choice.

One stage further back, the material was collected, and each jurisdiction's rule about collecting it protects something other than the people whose words were taken. \emph{Bartz v.\ Anthropic} held that training on lawfully purchased books was transformative fair use, and that keeping pirated copies of the same books in a central library wasn't: the distinction turned on acquisition alone, producing the largest copyright settlement on record \autocite{bartz2025fairuse}. That is copyright doing what it was built to do, which is to protect a market. A writer whose book was bought is owed its price, once; a writer whose book was pirated is owed damages for the piracy, not for the training. A liability measured in billions is also a price only the largest firms can pay, so a ruling that punished one lab's piracy set a cost of entry for every smaller one. Washington's motive is on the record. In May 2025 the Copyright Office released a report concluding that “making commercial use of vast troves of copyrighted works to produce expressive content that competes with them in existing markets, especially where this is accomplished through illegal access, goes beyond established fair use boundaries” \autocite[107]{copyright2025part3}; the next day the administration fired the Register of Copyrights, without giving a reason \autocite{nawotka2025fired}. In July the President said that paying for “every single article, book or whatever you've studied” was “not doable”, comparing training to a person reading a book, and added, “China's not doing it” \autocite{crouse2025notdoable}. That frees culture for the firms and leaves the people who make it with nothing, while the firms close what they make from it behind paid access. The EU requires a public training-data summary against a template \autocite[Art.~53(1)(d)]{eu2024aiact}, which serves rightsholders enforcing the opt-out its copyright law gives them. China requires providers to train on data from “lawful sources” and to explain those sources to the regulator on inspection, under rules whose first duty is to “uphold the Core Socialist Values” and to generate nothing “inciting subversion” of state power \autocite[arts.~1, 4, 7, 19]{cac2023genai}; disclosure there serves the Party's control of what a model may say.
None of those rules separates a writer's living from control over how a work is used. A public culture would: work free to read and learn from, by people and by machines, and the people who make it paid by something other than the policing of copies. A polity can pay them as it pays the jobs guarantee, by appropriation. What limits that is the real resources on any given day, the computing, energy, materials and people's time there are to go around, not dollars, which are numbers in a spreadsheet that a government issuing its own currency can always write. The levy on compute (chapter~\ref{sec:32}) exists to free some of those resources, by making the training and inference that would otherwise claim them dearer. The institution that would pay them is in \S\ref{sec:28.5}.

And the argument that a web-scale corpus can't be documented or audited at that size, so it encodes a hegemonic slice of the web while presenting as general, was made early and hasn't been answered \autocite{bender2021parrots}. A corpus assembled this way has no channel at all. An annotator has a queue and could in principle be given a way to object; a writer whose work sits in a training set was never in any arrangement, so there's nothing for a channel to attach to. This stage sits before the two recuperation cases that follow: the corpus was never a channel, so there was no disagreement to recuperate.
