\section{Affect, neutrality and selection rules}
\label{sec:16.2}
Suppressing affect doesn't produce neutrality. A procedure that declines to hear a class of signal has adopted a \emph{selection rule} that admits whatever it already measures, and the exclusion does substantive work while presenting as the absence of work. Whatever does get recorded then governs by default — revenue, throughput, legal exposure, inter-annotator agreement — and what isn't recorded goes unnamed and unanswered.

Recognizing emotion, simulating it, and valuing an outcome instrumentally are capacities a system can have while the question of \emph{affective concern}, an outcome registering as mattering rather than merely ranking higher, stays open. A therapy or companion product can be excellent at all three, and nothing in its conduct shows whether the fourth is present.
