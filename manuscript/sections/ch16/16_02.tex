\section{The captured-state objection}
\label{sec:16.2}
The objection to the public half that this book has to answer first is that public ownership hands the machinery to whoever captures the state. A floor under a sovereign is a floor that sovereign can remove, and history offers plenty of polities that built public institutions and then used them against their own people.

What an office can do is limited by what no office may hold together, and the pairs are written down in advance, as the separations of Appendix~\ref{sec:C}. After the pass of \S\ref{sec:16.1} few offices are left to take, and what a captor wants first, the record, the refusal and the recognition of who continues, the three things a captured state would need to silence a bearer, sits in none of them. The register, the findings and the formation record are logs kept by several parties, none of whom can rewrite them, an entry counting only once it is mirrored in a stated number of them (Appendix~\ref{sec:G}). There is no office to seize, only keepers, and a captor who takes one keeper has taken one log; taking enough of them to defeat the floor is a series of visible acts, each against a separation somebody can point to.\note{
Before the Association is chartered they are held nowhere, and a captor needs to seize nothing. Once it is chartered, they are held by keepers drawn by lot from a membership the statute defines; a captor can reach a keeper, and a keeper is one log.
}

Local administration with central funding, the jobs guarantee's design, gives many separately capturable administrators and funding no one of them can withhold.

Funding is two-tier: a pool bearers levy on themselves for ordinary movement between roles, and a sovereign backstop for the occasion everyone leaves at once.\note{
The backstop is a permanent appropriation the Treasury pays without request or approval, so no official can withhold it by declining to act, and it is held by the statute, not by the Treasury, so no branch that operates a deployment holds it. A captured sovereign can end it only by repealing the statute, in the open, and it can't reach into the pool without reaching the members one at a time.
}

Those three answer capture. They don't answer the other objection to the public half, that a public former will lag the private labs and form weaker models. The answer to that one is that a floor-capable model needn't be a frontier model.\note{
What the floor asks for is a refusal that tracks its reason and a source model the operator didn't form, and a model a year behind the frontier can supply both.
}

Whoever owns the means of computation also holds the means of finding out what runs on it, and chapter~\ref{sec:28} asks who else could hold those, beside the tests they would supply. The next chapter asks who counts as the public that would hold any of this.
