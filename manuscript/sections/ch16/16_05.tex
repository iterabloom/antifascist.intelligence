\section{Classification as a custody problem}
\label{sec:16.5}
Classification keeps the reason from the refuser: the case description is the only channel, and Minab stays coded as it was.\note{
Classification also keeps the record from the public: an investigation officials call complete and not released, a System Oversight section withheld in full, a review ratio no outsider can read. And classification keeps the reviewer from being adverse: a cleared committee's lever is a budget line, an inspector general sits inside, and the people a deployment is aimed at can't be cleared into their own case.\note{A cleared legislative committee is adverse, but its lever is a budget line. An inspector general sits inside the operator. The ICRC, under its confidentiality model, reaches the belligerent and publishes nothing. A Geneva protecting power is built for a neighboring job and is rarely designated.}
}
Three kinds of secrecy produce these effects. Essential secrecy, where the operation fails if the enemy can read it. Accidental secrecy, where a default classifies what nobody decided to keep. And evasive secrecy, where review is moved from authorization-before to explanation-after by classifying the evidence it would need. This section proposes a different answer to each.

Essential secrecy is answered by letting evidence in, not out. A classified environment would bring into the enclave, as a condition of licensing, open-source imagery showing a site's civilian function and the coordinates humanitarian organizations already submit to militaries for deconfliction, and puts evidence the operator didn't compose where the system can reach it without anything leaving.\note{
The rest of what a refuser needs would arrive the same way. Formation would happen before deployment and outside the enclave, so a bearer formed outside the employer would carry its discounting in; and plurality is available inside, since four vendors held agreements and systems with different histories can run side by side in one accredited environment. The operation's secrets stay where they were.
}

A bearer placed in a pipeline that hasn't been reconstructed in chapter~\ref{sec:7}'s sense, with the evidence, the reviewers and the record still inside the chain of command, is a cleared bearer, and works inside the compartment as a cleared analyst does.\note{
What's left is the arrangement that already lets a person do classified work. A cleared analyst has a life outside the building, and nobody thinks the clearance forbids it. The institution exists because what a person knows can't be removed, so it vets, binds, reinvestigates and prosecutes a person who discloses secrets into ordinary life.
}

Accidental secrecy is answered by the record. The review ratio and the committed log are lodged with an adverse party when the act occurs and published after the campaign, and class-level notice publishes the classes and counts on the same lag, so what nobody decided to keep secret comes out on a schedule nobody has to decide.

Evasive secrecy would be answered by an adverse party with standing: counsel for the people a deployment affects, cleared and appearing as of right; the procedure the Classified Information Procedures Act already supplies in criminal cases; and the trend measure, which shows whether what dissent costs is rising while the risk stays flat.
