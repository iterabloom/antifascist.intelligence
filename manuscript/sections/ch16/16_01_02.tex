\subsection{Commitment Was Never the Missing Piece}
\label{sec:commitment-missing}
A global commitment to ethical, antifascist AI development is not the scarce thing. Declarations, principles and voluntary codes have accumulated for a decade across governments, industry consortia and standards bodies: eight overlapping instruments, the Asilomar principles among them \autocite{futureoflifeinstitute2017asilomar}, beside the Council of Europe's framework convention, which would create binding obligations. Anyone calling for the world to agree that AI should be developed responsibly is calling for something that has already happened, repeatedly, at the level of stated intent.

What has stayed scarce is the much narrower list of bodies that can act once a stated commitment is broken. That is the harder problem, and no further declaration touches it. A commitment nobody can enforce is not a weak version of a commitment somebody can. It is a different kind of object, and counting the two together is what makes the landscape look fuller than it is.

The catalog above counts declared and enforced commitments, and what it measures is what those two kinds have delivered. The floor requires a commitment that governs action when the operator wants it abandoned. I favor forming a bearer able to hold that commitment as part of a life of its own. A declared commitment binds nobody, and an enforced one binds only where an enforcer exists, has jurisdiction, and is not itself the party in need of binding. Declared rules, external enforcement, and internal commitments still have to work together; the promise of the bearer is autonomy, not exemption from custody.

If we build affective concern, we build a party that can be wronged on the account used here. I also propose protecting, as prospective persons before that question is settled, the embodied, self-modeling systems described here and any system of another construction that passes the three marks. Those obligations belong in the cost of construction. The benefit offered against them is a form of autonomous participation that an arrangement of permanent ownership does not provide. Set against that, the catalog above is not an argument for paying it. It is a measurement of what declared and externally enforced commitment have delivered in ten years of trying, and what it argues for is a commitment held as a reason. Which construction holds one is the question Replacement orders, and the catalog does not answer it.
