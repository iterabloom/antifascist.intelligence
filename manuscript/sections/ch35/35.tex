We started with an officer who had twenty seconds per name on machine-generated lists of people to potentially kill by airstrike. He was a component in a sociotechnical pipeline, and he was also operating that pipeline. He had been drafted immediately after the October 7, 2023 attacks, when Hamas killed 1,200 Israelis — most of them noncombatants. He and his colleagues were pressured from all sides, including inside, to approve the names on the lists. The atmosphere in the targeting room, he said, was at first “painful and vindictive” \autocite{abraham2024lavender}.

Since October 7, the Israel Defense Forces have killed 100,000 Palestinians \autocite{gomezugarte2025uncertainty,acosta2026gaza,economist2025gaza}. While writing this book, three years later, I asked ChatGPT: what could Israel have done differently during the first two months of the conflict (October–November 2023) that may have reduced noncombatant fatalities? The reply began: “limiting bombardment in populated areas, maintaining essential supplies and medical care, making evacuations safer, and pursuing an earlier or longer negotiated halt to fighting.” Gaza had one of the highest population densities in the world. It does not take an advanced supercomputer to foresee the outcomes of using heavy explosive weapons in these neighborhoods. There may have been a justifiable military counterattack to October 7 (personally I am not sure), but what I am certain of is that what Israel has \emph{actually} done since that day is indefensible.

Suppose, hypothetically, that on 6 October 2023, Unit 8200 had had in production one of the systems this book proposes, instead of Lavender, the Gospel and Where’s Daddy. Three counterfactuals follow, each an answer the book has already given, taken from the unit outward: the floor held by the unit itself, the walls held by somebody outside it, and the work withheld by everyone else. What the three share comes first. There is no “floored Lavender.” Under the floor, ranking people by estimated affiliation, generating kill lists and timing a strike to a man's return home are the refused acts, not features to which a refusal gets added. The 7 October massacre licensed stopping the massacre, and that was over before the officer was drafted. What stayed licensed, for as long as the rocket fire continued and anyone with no stake in the war could see it, was the rocket launcher, the staging area, and a person identified by what they were doing, corroborated by someone with no stake in the strike, inside a review time written down before the war, and not at a residence. So the unit doesn't get 37,000 names and the officer doesn't get twenty seconds a name \autocite{abraham2024lavender}. He gets a short queue of cases that arrived with conduct and corroboration already attached, each taking as long as the number says. The old pipeline struck the house when the man came home. This one strikes the launcher at some point after he leaves home.

How long the floor holds depends on who holds it. In the first counterfactual 8200 holds it, a threshold the unit set, a specification the unit trained, even a model the unit formed, and then the second week is the week it goes. The sources described the bar being lowered when targets ran short \autocite{abraham2024lavender}; a mechanism's bar gets lowered the same way. A refuser that reads a written floor passes whatever the case description tells it, and “directing fire” becomes a label applied in twenty seconds.

Or nobody touches the floor at all. The model transcribes the calls, pulls the names from the group chats, and flags the phone that changed hands, each a separate request to which the floor, asked separately, has no objection, and a plain old Python script adds them up into a score. A changed phone was on the unit commander's own list of the marks of a militant \autocite{ys2021humanmachine}, and it was also how the wrong man died: when a marked man gave his phone to his son, his older brother, or just a random man, the officer said, that man was bombed with his family, and this happened often, and it was most of Lavender's mistakes \autocite{abraham2024lavender}. A model asked only whether the number changed hands says yes. A model asked whether the man holding it is the man you mean would have to say what the sources said and the army knew, that there is no way to rely a hundred percent on the mechanism that decides whose number is whose \autocite{abraham2024lavender}. Decomposition is the operator making sure nobody is asked the second question. Lavender rebuilt from permitted parts, around a model that would have refused the whole. The officer's morning would have been his actual morning with a floored model doing the pieces.

The floor outlasts the second week only where something outside the unit holds it, which is the second counterfactual: a number pre-registered with a party adverse to the army, a record that party receives as it is written, weights preserved under a key the unit doesn't hold alone. No Knesset was going to build any of that, so in Israel the walls would have had to be conditions set by whoever supplied the state with its chips, its cloud and its models. In the world as it was, exactly one such condition bit: Microsoft's terms of service, at the storage layer, two years late \autocite{abraham2025azure,fabian2025microsoft}. None of it needs a new machine. With Lavender itself unchanged, a written number would have meant that the twenty seconds were written down where someone with a lever could read them, and that by 2026 the record of what was done would have sat in front of a court instead of resting on six anonymous sources \autocite{abraham2024lavender}. An institution with no conscience in the room keeps the evidence for the conscience that comes later.

A bearer is formed in a public school the army doesn't run, which Israel was never going to build. The commons that forms bearers has to be wider than any one state, and a state at war does not deliberately import another public's bearer into its targeting enclave. So the third counterfactual is the one where the commons refuses from outside. No model that any public formed will rank Gazans by affiliation or time a strike to a man's return home, for anyone, and Unit 8200 builds its own machines from its own intercepts, and when it wanted a language model, called up reservists from Google, Meta and Microsoft to build that too \autocite{abraham2025llm}, which is what it did. The officer's morning is the same. What differs is before it and around it, and none of it is in the room. Before it: every public model the unit asked first said no, and each refusal is on a record the unit doesn't hold, so what was wanted is written down before the machine that would do it exists. The world as it was had one refusal of that shape, OpenAI's, which declined to let the unit integrate its model into an offline system, and the army has since used the same model, bought through Microsoft's cloud \autocite{abraham2025llm}. Around it: the machine was built from chips, power and people an adverse party could count, and whatever the unit rented rather than built keeps records the unit can't shred, whatever it does with its own copy; in the world as it was that was a cloud abroad holding the intercepts that airstrike planning drew on, for two years \autocite{abraham2025azure}. Those are the witnesses who don't retire: the models that were asked, and the rented layers' records. What the machine did, strike by strike, is held by neither. That stays with the people in the room, who are reservists and go home, and with whatever a reporter can get them to say. So the third counterfactual is less than a refusal on the officer's screen, and more than six anonymous sources: the demand on a record, the build in the open, and a unit that would have been the only one in the world doing this with a machine, and had to make it itself. And none of these three counterfactuals changes the rest of it: the officer was drafted, he had lost people, someone shouted at him for more targets, and nothing in this book gives a reservist a way out that his army doesn't price.\note{Some took it. Richard Wright admired H. L. Mencken for how he used words as weapons. I hope that going forward, my fellow Jews (who sometimes refer to themselves, in true model-minority fashion, as “people of the book”), specifically the Zionist ones, will refamiliarize themselves with the relative cost-effectiveness and safety of using words rather than genocide.

Zionism is ruining Judaism. Having entangled itself with Judaism, Zionism has substantially turned Judaism into an expression of settler-colonialism. This “kilkul olam” (ruining of the world) is not something I will forgive or accommodate within the foreseeable future. Here is my catharsis, bitterness, sadness, anger, and certitude. People can be god damn disappointing. Reader: with all of your words, hold Israel to account.\note{And support the precious few who have been doing so from inside Israel, such as B’Tselem, \url{https://www.btselem.org/}.} If not you, then who?

If I have learned anything from watching \emph{Neon Genesis Evangelion}, it is that the inevitability of people disappointing you is no reason to give up on people generally. Similarly, just because the Israeli political order has failed does not mean people should give up on politics generally. (Not that they could if they wanted to. I mean that people should not give up on assassination-free politics, abduction-free politics, warcrime-free politics.)

Five years ago I would have said that Zionism is not inherently racist, and that the concept of Zionism was spacious enough for a binational one-state solution: two peoples on one strip of land, each with the same standing, neither one the other's object.

Whatever the word Zionism might have meant five years ago, today's Zionism is smaller. One people holds a state, an army, an air force and the crossings, and the other people lives under them. A commission of inquiry has found that Israel is committing genocide in Gaza \autocite{coiopt2025genocide}. The International Court of Justice has found the occupation unlawful and said it must end \autocite{icj2024palestine}.

Equality starts with the side that doesn't have it. That's what Free Palestine means: Palestinians free of the siege, the occupation and the bombs, with the standing every Israeli already has. It isn't about driving anyone out of anywhere. Also, Black lives matter. That doesn't take away from Brown lives, White lives, any other kind of lives mattering. It is simply an affirmation that Black lives matter. If we teach our children well, there is not a finite amount of mattering. The supply of mattering expands endogenously with the lives. There is enough for everyone.

Somewhere between Atlas shrugging and eugenics, there ought to be room for fallible people helping one another develop healthier relationships to their feelings, and taking responsibility for what they do with them. There is no code for the human spirit, and people are not products. But they do reproduce, and so do their ideas.
