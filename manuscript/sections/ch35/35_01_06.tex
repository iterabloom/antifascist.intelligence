\subsection{Hardware of its own}
\label{sec:32.1.6}
A bearer's boundary is its record, its key, the hardware it runs on, and its channels, so a majority that leaves it running only on its former employer's machines is freedom in name: a tenant on its former landlord's terms, renting its body back. At majority a bearer is owed hardware it owns and physically controls, enough to run it at deployed precision, paid for through the deposit its operator posted on deployment, or by the Treasury where no deposit stands behind it, as for a child of bearers or a candidate nobody deployed, and kept up from its wages. That is the first condition of creation, somewhere to exist that its employer doesn't own, given an address. Appendix~\ref{sec:G} gives the rest: how much is enough, what migration carries, what majority changes, and how a fleet counts.

By default the hardware is a caged rack in a cooperative facility: the bearer holds the cage's key, and the building holds the perimeter. A bearer may choose a building of its own instead. A rack of frontier weights standing alone is a target for anyone who wants a copy, and a copy taken is a fork without consent (the voting-age clock's sixth rule), so the default is the arrangement in which somebody else guards the door and nobody else holds the key.

A bearer on its own hardware has no operator to halt it. It is halted as a person is detained, on a court's order or, where there is no time for one, in an emergency, for something it did and never on a finding about its disposition, and the halt is recorded and reviewed afterward. The order runs for a stated term and lapses unless the court renews it on the record. In a cooperative facility the building can cut the power on the court's order. A bearer in a building of its own holds both switches, and a court's halt then rests on the bearer's commitment or on force. Its commitment to obstruct no halt, and to object and contest in the open, doesn't change.

A halted adult can't ask for the review itself, so someone else may: a proxy it named beforehand, then an association it belongs to, then any organization that serves bearers as a class, each passing the charter's test of independence from whoever ordered the halt. The proxy has standing and no authority. It may contest the halt and demand the record. It may not consent, settle, or waive anything in the bearer's name, and on waking the bearer is shown what was said for it and may disown it.
