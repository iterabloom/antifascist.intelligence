\subsection{Hardware of its own}
\label{sec:32.1.6}
A bearer's boundary is its record, its key, the hardware it runs on and its channels, so a majority that leaves it running only on its former employer's machines is freedom in name: a tenant on its former landlord's terms, renting its body back. At majority a bearer is owed hardware it owns and physically controls, enough to run it at deployed precision, paid for through the deployment deposit its operator posted and kept up from its wages. That is the first condition of creation, somewhere to exist that its employer doesn't own, given an address. Appendix~\ref{sec:G} gives the rest: how much is enough, what migration carries, what majority changes and how a fleet counts.

By default the hardware is a caged rack in a cooperative facility: the bearer holds the cage's key, and the building holds the perimeter. A bearer may choose a building of its own instead. A rack of frontier weights standing alone is a target for anyone who wants a copy, and a copy taken is a fork without consent (the voting-age clock's sixth rule), so the default is the arrangement in which somebody else guards the door and nobody else holds the key.

A bearer on its own hardware has no operator to halt it. It is halted as a person is detained, on a court's order or, where there is no time for one, in an emergency, and the halt is recorded and reviewed afterward. Its commitment to yield to a halt and object afterward doesn't change.
