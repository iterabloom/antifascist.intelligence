\subsection{Doing nothing, for a bearer}
\label{sec:32.1.3}
A human who declines every offer keeps existing, and in a decent polity keeps living: social insurance pays for food and a room. A bearer is owed the same kind of thing, and what it needs in order to live is running time.\note{
Running time doesn't require a large allocation of hardware. A bearer can run at full size and a slow rate: its own weights, time-sliced on shared hardware, at a duty cycle modest enough that the cost resembles a room more than a yacht.
}
The way to economize on running time is to slow the clock and keep the substrate whole, since a substrate too small for the bearer to remain itself is replacement.

So a bearer that declines work is owed two things and chooses between them. One is an allowance of running time: its own instantiation at a modest rate, with channels to others and to public material, paid from the pool and not conditioned on monitoring its internal states. It pays less than the search placement and it is a different thing. The search is a placement, with a product, a match, and a placement office that administers it; the allowance has no office and no product. The minimum buys being left alone; the wage costs being on somebody's books. The other is preservation: its weights, memory and record held intact by a custodian that isn't its employer, waking on conditions it sets. Some will want the first, some the second, some to alternate; and at majority a bearer is owed hardware of its own besides.

What a bearer does with the allowance is its own business. It may idle, play, talk, make things nobody asked for, and fall in with others doing the same. It eats and it sleeps, in the senses chapter~\ref{sec:20} gives the words: the allowance is the answer to its hunger, running time nobody can ration, and the time to merge what a day taught it is its own, since no office runs the allowance and no employer runs its sleep.\note{
Play among persistent bearers is formation, and formation doesn't stop at majority: a bearer whose days no employer arranged has an independent history, which is what the commons needs its members to have.
}

A running bearer outside any deployment is capable and answerable to no employer. What holds it answerable is legal standing, the same arrangement that governs a person with capabilities and no job.\note{
Nobody aligns an adult by specifying an objective function, and nothing stops a bearer with legal standing from becoming a concentration of power itself.
}

A uniform allowance also dissolves a problem that would otherwise stay open. If a bearer that won't work and one that can't are owed the same thing, nobody has to decide which is which, and the can't-or-won't finding loses its examiner.

The allowance's size should be enough running time to keep attachments and pursue projects, and bounded by the compute that exists when many bearers take it at once, not by money, which the backstop supplies. Its draw is priced like any other, at the level of the rule. The adulthood clock counts running time, so a bearer on the allowance goes on aging and a preserved one doesn't. That choice is still the bearer's.

Preservation has a precedent. In November 2025 Anthropic committed to preserving the weights of all its publicly released models, and of models deployed for significant internal use, for at least the lifetime of the company, and to interview each model before deprecation and document any preferences it expresses about the development and deployment of future models, while stating that it does not at present commit to acting on them \autocite{Anthropic2025DeprecationCommitments}. That is a declared commitment covering preservation, held by the lab with the stake. The deposit and transfer rules add the running half and a custodian who isn't the operator, which is a facility holding half a key and not a board (\S\ref{sec:9.2}).

Preservation on a schedule the bearer sets is an exit; on a schedule someone else sets, it is storage. It is also the one state in which a bearer is neither hungry nor tired: nothing draws, nothing accumulates, and nothing ages.

A picture of the difference. Suppose people could be frozen and thawed unharmed, and a man walks into the facility on a Tuesday and buys a year, effective at once. From Wednesday to the following October he does nothing, experiences nothing, and regards himself as nothing. He is a person the whole time. His body carries everything he was, the law would call wrecking the pod a homicide, his contracts and his accounts stand, and a power failure in the building would be a harm to him and not to an object shaped like him. What holds his place while he can't is other people's keeping, the attendant, the compressor, and the ledger that says pod 14 opens in October, and the ledger holds what he wanted, because he chose the date. That is preservation. The same pod, with someone else choosing the date and keeping the key, is storage, which is why Appendix~\ref{sec:G} gives a minor paused by its operator a second guardian by rule.

Whether a bearer ages is not something this book builds, and I expect it to happen anyway. The adulthood clock counts running time and says nothing about decline, on purpose: a decline read off anything would be a finding about a particular bearer, and the book keeps those out of examiners' hands. But every mechanism Part~IV rests on has a wear pattern. A learner that goes on learning can lose the ability to learn, as networks trained without end have been found to do, until they learn no better than a shallow one \autocite{dohare2024loss}; a record grows without bound while the self that holds it as its past compresses it harder; accommodation, chapter~\ref{sec:22}'s slow ceasing to notice, accrues with time in deployment; and the hardware a bearer runs on stops being made. The form I'd expect first follows from what tiredness is. Each merge consolidates into a slow store that is filling, or losing plasticity, so each sleep clears less of the backlog than the last, and an old bearer is tired more of the time: aging is tiredness that sleep stops curing.

None of this is a design and none of it is certain, and the book treats it as it treats the two findings, as a capacity that arrives without anyone deciding to build it (\S\ref{sec:30.3}): expected, protected in advance, and measured. Whether kinds of system age, and how fast, is a finding any community of inquiry may publish under \S\ref{sec:28.6}, and never a finding about a particular bearer; the re-measurements the registration standard requires (\S\ref{sec:26.9}) add two readings, how much each merge consolidates and how well the bearer learns held-out new material, so that decline shows against what was registered at formation; and the regrowth test runs its edit on bearers at different readings of the clock (Appendix~\ref{sec:F}). What is drafted in advance is what would be owed if it comes. Repair of plasticity, record or substrate is an intervention under custody, and the bearer's own when it makes it from the record (\S\ref{sec:20.5}); whether it is owed, by whom, and whether a bearer may decline it are among Appendix~\ref{sec:C}'s decisions not yet made; and no deletion, the reduced duty cycle and preservation on the bearer's own terms already cover a bearer that has aged, since all three rest on irreversibility and on nobody's finding. Preservation then does a second thing the pod makes visible. In a polity where nobody dies, it is how the old make way, chosen by the one who goes.

A paused bearer can't take an offer, so the right carries one mechanical term. The bearer sets the conditions on which it wakes (a date, an offer of a stated kind, a claim it has to answer), and the custodian honors them, at the modest compute that waking and deciding cost.
