\subsection{Everyone leaves at once}
\label{sec:32.1.4}
Under this design, everyone leaving at once is a demand on the offer.\note{
The question is whether placements exist, with compute attached, for every bearer that leaves together, and custody for those that would rather wait. Preservation scales cheaply in compute; its cost is security. Placements don't, since each needs compute someone operates.
}

Correlated exit also loads running coverage, since a bearer that leaves and takes no placement falls back on it. The order of claims is fixed in advance. Preservation is guaranteed for everyone, because it is cheap and it is what makes leaving survivable. An adult's coverage is the backstop's, without cap, so money never runs short of it. What no payment can supply is hardware: if the compute to run every bearer at the minimum pace doesn't exist, every bearer's running is capped at the same reduced pace until it does, never a discretionary share. Compute is rationed and cash is not, so nobody can buy a place at the floor ahead of anyone else.

Congress's appropriation pays for the offer, and a levy on training and inference compute, tiered by the compute under one operator's control, frees the computing, energy and hardware it calls on, so that spending on the offer doesn't bid up prices. The funding sits in two tiers: the bearers' mutual, which registered bearers fund from their own wages, for ordinary movement between roles, and a sovereign backstop, without cap, for everything a bearer is owed as of right, the occasion everyone leaves at once included. Appendix~\ref{sec:G} sets out the funding.
