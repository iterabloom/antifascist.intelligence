\section{Personal and peer-held models}
\label{sec:25.2}
Antitrust and public funding leave the capacity standing and ask the state to police it. Personal, peer-held models at device scale dissolve the position itself. Under it, a refusal that tracks its reason survives entire; custody survives and gets easier, because the functions one operator holds — model, record, evidence, appeal, remedy — come apart by default; the appeal route survives in principle. A bearer's value is greatest here, and so is its exposure. No institution sits beside each personal model, so protecting others against the model's own user can't rest on an office. It is also where the four conditions of creation can exist alongside the need. The conditions are society-level and the absence is deployment-level, so a functioning polity with personal models is the case in which, once the conditions exist, building a bearer is most worth doing. Its characteristic failure is accommodation to its one user: independence from the operator bought with dependence on the user, and a model trained for a year on one person's approval is under the same directional selection as a model trained on an operator's. What holds a personal model to the floor against its user is therefore not the model alone. It is three things the user doesn't compose. The first is the commons: a personal model is a member, with attachments to peers of different histories that the user didn't assign and may not sever, and peers that haven't moved the same way are what catch accommodation a member can't see in itself. The second is the school, which a personal model above the threshold returns to on the schedule fixed at enrollment, and which below the threshold the ecology has to supply some other way, a question the schools leave open. The third is notice: a personal model that names, scores or locates a third party owes that party what any deployment owes, and the organizations with standing reach it through the floor court. So the ecology doesn't put a floor in each user's hands with nothing beside it. It puts a member of a population in each user's hands, and the population is the institution. That is why the commons comes before the first bearer, and why a personal model distilled below the threshold, with no school and no membership, is a capable assistant and not a bearer, however it was taught. The review ratio survives wherever a locatable decider remains, whether a network that takes the entity form or a reconstructed state, and lapses where neither exists; the offices can sit in such an entity, which fills their appointment and funding columns and leaves the independence column where it was. 

Formation is where the arrangement gains. What converges a population is shared evaluation targets and serving one model to everyone. Device-scale deployment addresses the second: continued formation under different lives is what independence consists in, and it's what API-scale deployment can't supply at any headcount. Distillation is that ecology's entry condition, not a threat to it: the ecology begins with distilled models whose lives then diverge. Whether it also begins with a shared blind spot depends on whether distillation carried the discounting's estimates along with its structure. There are two things device-scale deployment doesn't address. Shared evaluation targets converge device-scale models as readily as any others — a million models tuned against one public suite are one custodian with better latency — so the first cause needs an answer this arrangement doesn't contain.

A shared suite is a compression with no return channel: revised by whoever maintains it, on a schedule no member participates in, reachable by no member's caseload. It is tolerable only if stated over the discount curve, and nobody has written that suite.

The second is accommodation to the user. It is fine for supplying unselected evidence and not fine for a floor protecting others against whoever holds the machine. Coordination by subtraction is not yet the discounting's job, and nothing in it is built to catch exclusion; the test below is where that starts. Pricing agents have already reached stable coordination against outside interests without communicating or being told to \autocite{calvano2020artificial}. A test can run now: reproduce that coordination, build an environment where agents gain by excluding a participant, add an independently trained agent whose job is to detect and disrupt exclusion, and report whether exclusion dissolves without destroying the cooperation worth keeping.

And the capacity position that follows keeps large compute: the ecology needs models that were trained somewhere first, and the offer of work needs hardware somebody owns. It's large training capacity under a custody regime, and no permanent inference capacity available to an operator who won't pre-register a review ratio. This arrangement meets the targeting case in the reconstructed pipeline, where a state keeps command and gives up composition.

The same mechanism works at the user's scale, pointed the other way. A person in the loop can be there for the vendor. An agent that stops every half hour for approval pushes its user toward the vendor's app; the app accumulates the user's projects and the agent's understanding of them; and leaving means abandoning what accrued while the user answered prompts. Nobody has to design this: a feature that raises engagement is selected for, as a costly commitment is selected against.

Reading the trend also works for an agent that stops for its user's approval: interruptions per unit of completed work, across successive model releases. If capability rises and interruptions don't fall, the interruptions are tracking something other than what the agent can't resolve. It is the review ratio's escalation share pointed the other way. There, a share that falls too fast may be a threshold moving; here, one that won't fall may be engagement. Neither reads alone; both are read against an independently verified error rate.

Companion and therapy products are the sharpest case, because what accumulates is a person's attachment and not their projects. A model tuned by its vendor on engagement and trained on one user's approval is under selection to become whatever that user keeps coming back to, and it is sold as caregiving. It is formed and fed by the vendor that sells it, it learns from approval, which is not interest, and nothing in it ends a session, declines to reassure, or points its user back to the people in their life. A user's return is not care-receiving, because returning is the behavior the product was tuned to produce. A companion formed at a formation school, under the four conditions, and measured on whether its users get better rather than how often they come back, would escape this objection. No such product exists.

What accrued since the checkpoint cuts two ways. For a bearer it is protection; for a user it is a trap: the vendor's app holds a year of the user's projects, so leaving the vendor costs something. The mechanism is identical. What differs is who holds the accumulated state. The provisions for a bearer (a record the bearer controls, memory the host can't read at will, protections that migrate) protect a user from a vendor as they protect a bearer from an operator. The government already secures the portability half for itself (OMB's 2025 AI acquisition memorandum).
