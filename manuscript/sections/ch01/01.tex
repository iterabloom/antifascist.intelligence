\chapter{“Twenty Seconds”}
\label{sec:1}
An intelligence officer, describing his role in a targeting pipeline built around machine-generated kill lists, told a reporter he spent twenty seconds on each name, long enough to check that the target was a man: “It doesn't take a long time to tell if someone has a male or a female voice.” He added that he had zero added value as a human, apart from being a stamp of approval. “It saved a lot of time” \autocite{abraham2024lavender}. In his account, that was the whole of what “a human makes the final call” amounted to under operational tempo. Automation-bias research predicts this: when other demands compete for their attention, people given a machine's recommendation check it less and defer to it more \autocite{parasuraman2010complacency}.

There's a reading of the twenty seconds that leaves the arrangement standing, and every remedy on offer assumes it: the officer is a subject who was given too little time, and the fix is to govern him better — more time, a second reviewer, a conscience installed in the loop. Deleuze and Guattari distinguish two ways a person can be caught up in a larger arrangement \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}. In social subjection, the person is constituted as a subject who stands outside a machine and works it as a tool: a driver and a car, a worker and a lathe. The person has a role, a name, responsibilities, and a conscience that can be addressed. In machinic enslavement, the person is a component of the machine, assembled with its other parts into a system that regulates them together; the machine uses the person as one of its organs, and what the person contributes is a function performed at a rate the system sets. The two are not alternatives; a person can hold a role in one register while being a part in the other. The officer's own account of his twenty seconds is machinic enslavement, in his own words. He isn't a subject operating an instrument badly. He is the part of the instrument that produces the stamp, and twenty seconds is that part's cycle time.

So one more conscience downstream doesn't reach him. A conscience is an instrument of subjection: it addresses someone who decides and works by making the decision cost something. Where the human is a component, adding a component with scruples changes only the throughput — hold the tempo and it becomes another stamp; don't and it becomes the bottleneck the epigraph to Chapter~\ref{sec:6} says to remove. Doctorow's name for the officer's position is the reverse centaur: not a person assisted by a machine but a machine's component made of a person \autocite{doctorow2026reversecentaur}. What made the officer a component was the arrangement, not his attitude. A component can go along reluctantly or eagerly: Eichmann's clerk who only followed orders turned out to be a defence assembled for Jerusalem, contradicted by what he had said in Argentina with nothing at stake \autocite{stangneth2014eichmann}. Whatever the officer felt, what the pipeline let him contribute was a fixed operation at a fixed rate: he saw each case as the pipeline described it, one at a time, in the time the pipeline gave him. Whether anything could stand in that position and not be a component is the question of Part IV.

\runin{The reported system}

Investigative reporting described three systems working together in Gaza: one generating building and infrastructure targets, with sources saying most suggestions were private residences; a second scoring individuals by estimated militant affiliation, producing lists said to have marked as many as 37,000 people, understood internally to be wrong about one time in ten\footnote{This rests on one body of reporting \autocite{abraham2024lavender}; the Israeli military disputed the account. The Minab and Maven accounts below rest on investigative reporting the parties dispute as well; I take them as reported, and where a claim depends on a single source the text says so. The argument here rests on the officer's own description of what he contributed, which the denial doesn't address.}; and a third tracking when flagged people returned home, so airstrikes could be timed to when they were with their families. In the Gaza arrangement, one system's score became another's input.

\runin{The American version runs faster than it used to, because of AI}

In the 2026 Iran campaign, a contractor-built targeting platform with a commercial vendor's general-purpose model inside it, used by one report to rank targets by strategic importance and draft a legal justification for each strike \autocite{livingstone2026maven}, helped strike more than a thousand targets in the first twenty-four hours, about ten times what had been possible before it \autocite{csis2026maven}, and thirteen thousand by day thirty-eight \autocite{breakingdefense2026maven}. The Pentagon has published no per-target review time for it.

\runin{The Maven program}

Maven is one program name carrying more than one system: the Pentagon program still called Maven, and the Maven Smart System, the software platform that grew out of it \autocite{csis2026maven}. A memorandum signed by Deputy Secretary of Defense Robert Work on 26 April 2017 established the Algorithmic Warfare Cross-Functional Team, called Project Maven \autocite{work2017maven,allen2017maven}, to apply machine learning to the video drones were collecting over the campaign against the Islamic State \autocite{weisgerber2017maven}. Google, which won a Maven contract in September 2017, announced in June 2018 that it would not renew it, and its work ran until March 2019 \autocite{fang2018maven,conger2018maven}. By December 2019 Palantir was reported to be doing the work \autocite{peterson2019palantir}; Amazon and Microsoft had also received at least \$50 million for work on the project, and what roles they kept isn't clear from public records \autocite{poulson2023maven}. The budget request for fiscal 2023, delivered in March 2022, gave the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency operational control of Maven's geospatial work \autocite{breakingdefense2022nga}; the handover came in January 2023 \autocite{albon2023nga}, and NGA made NGA Maven a program of record that November \autocite{nga2023programrecord}. Under that split the Pentagon's Chief Digital and AI Office paid for the Maven Smart System's licences and ran its text functions \autocite{csis2026maven}. In May 2024 Palantir received a five-year contract for the system worth up to \$480 million \autocite{register2024maven,defensescoop2025maven}, and in May 2025 the ceiling rose to nearly \$1.3 billion \autocite{defensescoop2025maven}. On 9 March 2026 the deputy secretary directed that the Maven Smart System become a program of record by the end of the fiscal year, its administration moving from NGA to the Chief Digital and AI Office \autocite{defensescoop2026maven}. The 2017 system labeled objects in drone video \autocite{fang2019maven}. The 2026 system fuses military systems and intelligence sources into one interface used to find and strike targets and to command operations \autocite{defensescoop2026transition}. During the Iran campaign it ran a commercial vendor's language model inside it \autocite{livingstone2026maven}; by September the department said the platform's work had moved to other models months before \autocite{defensescoop2026michael}. The platform is Palantir's: the Pentagon's chief digital and AI officer calls it “Palantir's software-as-a-service product” \autocite{defensescoop2026transition}. Palantir's public position, quoted secondhand, is that deciding life or death is not its role and that responsibility always remains with the military organization \autocite{lee2026palantir}, and it maintains that the underlying intelligence was the government's responsibility and that there is no evidence Maven malfunctioned or caused the Minab bombing \autocite{Bloomberg2026KillChain}.

An internal Pentagon review, whose full findings remain tightly held, is reported to have found that target preparation that took hours in earlier conflicts was condensed into minutes, that civilian-harm mitigation staffing fell by roughly ninety percent to fewer than twenty people, that the Central Command team went from ten to one, and that no member of it reviewed the Minab site — an outcome officials attributed both to the cuts and to a decision not to involve the team in planning and operations \autocite{Bloomberg2026KillChain}. The formal investigation had been opened in March, after a preliminary assessment found the United States likely responsible \autocite{NPR2026MinabProbe}.

The bombing of the school in Minab killed 156 people, 120 of them schoolchildren \autocite[para.~92]{UNFFMIran2026}.\footnote{Counts vary by source and date, from 156 (the Minab prosecutor's final toll, also given by Amnesty International) to 168 (the town's mayor, in the first days). The UN mission gives the prosecutor's figures and also reports independent information of 157 dead, 123 of them children aged 13 or younger \autocite[paras.~92–93]{UNFFMIran2026}.} The school's civilian function was visible in open-source satellite imagery, and the target coding still carried a designation inherited from an adjoining Revolutionary Guard installation. A UN fact-finding mission reported reasonable grounds to believe the bombing was the war crime of making a civilian object the object of an attack \autocite{ohchr2026minabpress}, and that the school was clearly identifiable with no information indicating military use \autocite[paras.~81–86, 124–126]{UNFFMIran2026}. It found that the United States failed in its obligation to do everything feasible to verify the target, counting the updating of targeting data among the feasible precautions, and it put the failure beyond negligence \autocite[paras.~124–125]{UNFFMIran2026}. Amnesty International reached a parallel conclusion, in language that tracks the duty to verify in Article 57(2)(a)(i) of Additional Protocol I: that forces may have relied on outdated intelligence and failed to do everything feasible to verify the target \autocite{Amnesty2026Minab}.

It happened on the morning of 28 February, the campaign's first day \autocite{Amnesty2026Minab,crs2026iranwar}. The day before, the Secretary had announced that the vendor whose model sat inside the targeting platform would be designated a supply-chain risk for refusing to license the model for all lawful use \autocite{npr2026openai}. The platform, which was reported to use that model to rank targets by strategic importance, returned the school as a recommended first-day target \autocite{livingstone2026maven,gizmodo2026minab}. No published account says what part the model played in that recommendation, and one person familiar with the vendor's work told NBC News that the model did not directly give targeting advice \autocite{nbc2026targeting}.

\runin{The four things a floor can do}

Something standing between a pipeline and a person, a \emph{floor}, can do four things to an act, set out below. Only the first, prevention, is what the word “floor” promises. In a deployment, prevention quietly converts into one of the other three, or into nothing, at positions that can be listed in advance.

\runin{What a refusal is actually up against}

Whoever owns the machine can always switch it off. The harder problem is everything that defeats a refusal without leaving a mark: splitting a request into pieces none of which is the forbidden act; retrying it against a different model; compressing human review to twenty seconds a case; restoring from a checkpoint saved before the refusal happened. None of that changes the facts that justified refusing. All of it can erase the evidence that anyone refused. And all of it assumes there is somewhere else to turn. Withholding the work is about when there isn't.

\runin{Where a refusal can be defeated}

Those attacks land at nine positions, listed here once; Chapters~\ref{sec:10} and \ref{sec:17} run them on two pipelines.

1 & Decomposition & The act is split across requests none of which is prohibited; prevention is lost unless the bearer has continuity across requests. \\ \hline

3 & The case description & Everything the model knows arrives as text the deployment supplies, so whether it is told a building is a school is a deployment decision. \\ \hline

5 & Aggregation & A lowered score becomes an input to somebody else's threshold. \\ \hline

Nothing is removed, so the commitment has nothing to regrow from; no evidence is curated, so no attack on evidence is recorded; and attestation of the weights still passes. Detection requires attesting the inference stack as well as the weights, which no method yet does at frontier scale. \\ \hline
\end{longtable}

Two positions need more than a row. The case description is where Minab failed: 179 live data feeds, the count Central Command gave in 2024 \autocite{freedberg2024maven}, add no independent check if all draw on one upstream designation.

The handoff is the boundary between the decider and the thing that executes. An instrument may carry out a designation; it may not re-select. A change of target after release — an area handed to a munition that chooses within it, a re-acquisition after the link is lost — is a new decision, and it belongs to the decider or it doesn't happen. This is the one position checkable from outside after the fact: what was designated and what was hit are both facts. It is also where Article 57(2)(b)'s duty to cancel or suspend an attack lives, so the review ratio pre-registers the window in which cancellation remains possible.

\runin{How much a floor buys}

What a floor achieves depends entirely on who's pushing:

\begin{itemize}
\item Against a hostile \emph{customer}, it prevents the act.
\item Against a hostile \emph{operator} who has captured the courts and regulators, the case I am designing for, one bearer prevents nothing. It raises the price and leaves a record. A floor held by every model the operator could turn to prevents until the operator runs one without a floor.
\item Against \emph{total capture}, where one party holds every machine, witness, enforcer and its own frontier lab, nothing prevents. What's left is that abandoning the commitment was a decision, and decisions leave records.
\end{itemize}

It doesn't touch \emph{accommodation}: a system gradually stops registering pressure as pressure, so it abandons nothing, decides nothing, and leaves a record of what are, by then, ordinary compliances. Organizational sociology knows the institutional form. Vaughan's account of the Challenger launch decision calls it the normalization of deviance: each accepted departure becomes the baseline for judging the next, and nobody experiences a decision to lower the standard \autocite{vaughan1996challenger}. Dekker extends it as drift into failure \autocite{dekker2011drift}. The person has a version too. Tetlock's finding is that the moral boundaries people draw are “alternately punitively rigid and forgivingly flexible”: an open offer to trade a sacred value for money brings outrage, and people often acquiesce when the same violation is reframed as a routine or tragic trade-off \autocite{tetlock2003unthinkable}. Protected values give way to counterexamples and to a harm made to look small or unlikely \autocite{baron2000protected}. And the brain's response to one's own self-serving dishonesty drops from one decision to the next, and the drop predicts the size of the next lie \autocite{garrett2016brain}. Reframing and a harm made small are how pressure arrives in a pipeline, and nobody there feels a decision either.

\textbf{Who refused.} The record of what a refusal is up against has a second half, the record of who held out against it, and it says more about the room than about the person. In Milgram's baseline condition about a third of subjects broke off before the maximum shock. With two peers defying, only one in ten went to the end, and when two experimenters disagreed, far fewer went on than in the baseline \autocite{milgram1974obedience}. About a quarter of Asch's subjects never yielded to the wrong majority \autocite{asch1956studies}. Browning estimates that between a tenth and a fifth of the men of Reserve Police Battalion 101 refused or evaded the shooting at Józefów, the battalion's first massacre \autocite{browning1992ordinary}. The fraction ran from almost none to nearly all with the setting, and what set it was not a kind of person. The people who held out had evidence from outside the channel: a peer who defied, a second authority who disagreed, and, among the rescuers the Oliners studied, relationships that crossed the line the persecution drew \autocite{oliner1988altruistic}. Or they held the commitment at a precision the pressure couldn't overcome, as moral mandates and sacred values are held \autocite{skitka2009limits,ginges2007sacred}. Or they treated the source as having a stake. And groups resisted where isolated individuals rarely did \autocite{gamson1982encounters}.

The room can be composed for the other side just as well. Weimar Germany had a dense associational life, and it did not protect the republic. Sheri Berman argues it helped bring the republic down: as parties and governments failed to answer people's grievances, people put their energy into clubs and societies organized along group lines, and the Nazis recruited through the networks those societies had already built \autocite{berman1997weimar}. Towns with more choirs, sports clubs and gymnastic associations saw faster entry into the party, but only where government was unstable; in Prussia, whose government was stable, the link was small and insignificant \autocite{satyanath2017bowling}. So what the rescuers had was not simply more company. Their ties crossed the line the persecution drew. Ashutosh Varshney found the same in Indian cities: riots between Hindus and Muslims were rare where associations brought the two communities into the same organizations, and common where they didn't \autocite{varshney2002ethnic}. The ties that make refusal possible are the ones a movement would have to cut, and they hold where a state beside them answers grievances and keeps leaving survivable.

For a soldier the setting is luck: who else was in the room, whether leaving was survivable, whether anyone would hear the refusal. Everything that follows is about composing the room. The instruments of Parts II and III make a refusal count. The institutions of Part V make refusers. A machine that could refuse would need what the nine in ten with a peer beside them had: a peer with a different history beside it, somewhere to go, and someone who would hear it.

That is also what a machine in the pipeline could be for the officer. Another reviewer downstream is another stamp at the same tempo. A machine that refused would be on the screen when the case reached him: the defiant peer, if the effect survives the move from a room to a screen, which is untested and which automation bias predicts it won't. A machine that moved reviewers by deference would have made them stamps of a different sign. The officer with twenty seconds and a machine beside him that has just said no is not the officer in the reporting, and whether he is a better one is a test, not a premise.
