\chapter{“Twenty Seconds”}
\label{sec:1}
An intelligence officer, describing his role in a targeting pipeline built around machine-generated kill lists, told a reporter he spent twenty seconds on each name, long enough to check that the target was a man: “It doesn't take a long time to tell if someone has a male or a female voice.” He added that he had zero added value as a human, apart from being a stamp of approval. “It saved a lot of time” \autocite{abraham2024lavender}. In his account, that was what “human in the loop” amounted to under operational tempo.\footnote{Automation-bias research predicts this delegation: when other demands compete for their attention, people given a machine's recommendation check it less and defer to it more \autocite{parasuraman2010complacency}.}

One interpretation is that the officer is a subject who was given too little time, and the fix is to govern him better — more time, a second reviewer, additional conscience installed in the loop. Through this \emph{social subjection} lens, the person is a subject who stands outside a machine and works it as a tool: a driver and a car, a worker and a lathe. The person has a role, a name, responsibilities, and a conscience that can be addressed. An alternative lens is \emph{machinic enslavement}, where the person is a component of the machine, assembled with its other parts into a system that regulates them together; the machine uses the person as one of its organs, and what the person contributes is a function performed at a rate the system sets.\footnote{Doctorow's name for the officer's position is the reverse centaur: not a person assisted by a machine but a machine's component made of a person \autocite{doctorow2026reversecentaur}.} The two are not mutually exclusive; a person can hold a role in one register while being a part in the other \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}, and the twenty-seconds officer held both. As a part, he was where the pipeline produced its stamp, and twenty seconds was that part's cycle time. As a subject, he was addressed throughout: he described to a journalist the dissonance between the news of civilian deaths and the pressure at work for more targets. And even as the dissonance reached his conscience, he went along. It registered and decided nothing;\footnote{Or, at least, nothing that prevented the Israel Defense Forces from killing 100,000 people in Gaza between October 2023 and October 2025 \autocite{gomezugarte2025uncertainty,acosta2026gaza,economist2025gaza}. And the account of that particular officer was published in April of 2024 by +972 Magazine. It is anyone's guess as to whether that officer's decision to speak to a journalist prevented one additional death, 50,000 additional deaths, or none at all. And what would have happened if the officer had refused after two weeks in the role — say, hypothetically, in November 2023? We are talking about an intelligence officer using Lavender to assemble kill lists. His day-to-day working environment was probably closer to a civilian desk job than an action movie. Should he have sought repeated meetings with his superiors, up the chain of command, while refusing to approve targets for airstrikes that could reasonably have been expected to cause civilian deaths? Arguably that would have been the ethical thing to do. In that counterfactual, would there have been fewer dead?} the judgment that the policy was disproportionate came, he said, in retrospect.

So what was missing wasn't a conscience; he had one. A conscience works by making a decision cost something, but here the costs netted the other way: “We were constantly being pressured: ‘Bring us more targets.’ They really shouted at us” \autocite{abraham2024lavender}. He was a component with scruples, but the tempo held: the scruples changed nothing he contributed, and he became a stamp. A component with scruples that doesn't hold the tempo becomes the “human bottleneck” a former commander of Israel's signals-intelligence unit wrote a book about removing \autocite{ys2021humanmachine}. What made the officer a component was the arrangement, not his attitude. A component can go along reluctantly or eagerly: the thoughtless functionary Arendt saw at Eichmann's trial \autocite{arendt1963eichmann} was, on the evidence of what he had said in Argentina four years before, with nothing at stake, a show he put on for Jerusalem \autocite{stangneth2014eichmann}. What the pipeline let the officer contribute was a fixed operation at a fixed rate: he saw each case as the pipeline described it, one at a time, in the time the pipeline gave him. Whether anything could stand in that position and not be a component is the question of Part IV.

Investigative reporting described three systems working together in Gaza: one generating building and infrastructure targets, with sources saying most suggestions were private residences; a second scoring individuals by estimated militant affiliation, producing lists said to have marked as many as 37,000 people, understood internally to be wrong about one time in ten\footnote{The Israeli military disputed the account. The Minab and Maven accounts below rest on investigative reporting the parties dispute as well. I take them as reported.}; and a third tracking when flagged people returned home, so airstrikes could be timed to when they were with their families.

The bombing of the school in Minab killed 156 people, 120 of them schoolchildren \autocite[para.~92]{UNFFMIran2026}.\footnote{Counts vary by source and date, from 156 (the Minab prosecutor's final toll, also given by Amnesty International) to 168 (the town's mayor, in the first days). The UN mission gives the prosecutor's figures and also reports independent information of 157 dead, 123 of them children aged 13 or younger \autocite[paras.~92–93]{UNFFMIran2026}.} The school's civilian function was visible in open-source satellite imagery, and the target coding still carried a designation inherited from an adjoining Revolutionary Guard installation. A UN fact-finding mission reported reasonable grounds to believe the bombing was the war crime of making a civilian object the object of an attack \autocite{ohchr2026minabpress}, and that the school was clearly identifiable with no information indicating military use \autocite[paras.~81–86, 124–126]{UNFFMIran2026}. It found that the United States failed in its obligation to do everything feasible to verify the target, counting the updating of targeting data among the feasible precautions, and it put the failure beyond negligence \autocite[paras.~124–125]{UNFFMIran2026}. Amnesty International reached a parallel conclusion, in language that tracks the duty to verify in Article 57(2)(a)(i) of Additional Protocol I: that forces may have relied on outdated intelligence and failed to do everything feasible to verify the target \autocite{Amnesty2026Minab}.

It happened on the morning of 28 February, the first day of the Iran campaign \autocite{Amnesty2026Minab,crs2026iranwar}. The day before, the Secretary had announced that the vendor (Anthropic) whose model (Claude) sat inside the targeting platform (Maven Smart System) would be designated a supply-chain risk for refusing to license Claude for all lawful use \autocite{npr2026openai}. Maven Smart System had returned the school as a recommended first-day target \autocite{livingstone2026maven,gizmodo2026minab}. No published account says what part Claude played in that recommendation, and one person familiar with the vendor's work told NBC News that the model did not directly give targeting advice \autocite{nbc2026targeting}.

An internal Pentagon review, whose full findings remain tightly held, is reported to have found that target preparation that took hours in earlier conflicts was condensed into minutes, that civilian-harm mitigation staffing fell by roughly ninety percent to fewer than twenty people, that the Central Command team went from ten to one, and that no member of it reviewed the Minab site — an outcome officials attributed both to the cuts and to a decision not to involve the team in planning and operations \autocite{Bloomberg2026KillChain}. The formal investigation had been opened in March, after a preliminary assessment found the United States likely responsible \autocite{NPR2026MinabProbe}.

In the 2026 Iran campaign, a contractor-built targeting platform with a commercial vendor's general-purpose model inside it, used to rank targets by strategic importance and draft a legal justification for each strike \autocite{livingstone2026maven}, helped strike more than a thousand targets in the first twenty-four hours, about ten times what had been possible before it \autocite{csis2026maven}, and thirteen thousand by day thirty-eight \autocite{breakingdefense2026maven}. The Pentagon has published no per-target review time for it.\footnote{Maven is one program name carrying more than one system: the Pentagon program still called Maven, and the Maven Smart System, the software platform that grew out of it \autocite{csis2026maven}. The program began in 2017 as a project to label objects in the video drones were collecting over the campaign against the Islamic State \autocite{weisgerber2017maven,fang2019maven}. The 2026 system fuses military systems and intelligence sources into one interface used to find and strike targets and to command operations \autocite{defensescoop2026transition}. The platform is Palantir's: the Pentagon's chief digital and AI officer calls it “Palantir's software-as-a-service product” \autocite{defensescoop2026transition}. Palantir's public position, quoted secondhand, is that deciding life or death is not its role and that responsibility always remains with the military organization \autocite{lee2026palantir}, and it maintains that the underlying intelligence was the government's responsibility and that there is no evidence Maven malfunctioned or caused the Minab bombing \autocite{Bloomberg2026KillChain}. How the program passed from one office and contractor to the next is in Appendix~\ref{sec:C.1}.}

The people on the Israeli and American airstrike lists could not have objected to being on those lists — the pipelines weren't built to obtain such feedback. Andry José Hernández Romero was caught up in a different pipeline, and he could have objected. A makeup artist from Venezuela, he presented himself at the San Ysidro port of entry in August 2024 to seek asylum, passed his credible-fear interview, and had a lawyer, a pending claim and a hearing date \autocite{LULAC2025HernandezRomero,Vega2025HernandezRomero}. A contractor's employee screening him read two tattoos, crowns above the words for “mom” and “dad”, as gang marks \autocite{Palomera2025Blade}, and in March 2025 he was flown to a prison in El Salvador before his hearing, on the evening a federal judge ordered the planes turned back \autocite{jgg2025contempt}. He had everything a person needs to object except the time. We'll return to his case more fully when we discuss the United States' abduction and forcible transfer pipeline.

\textbf{Who refused.} The record of the twenty seconds has a second half, the record of who held out against pressure of that kind, and it says that the composition of the room matters a lot. In Milgram's baseline condition about a third of subjects broke off before the maximum shock. With two peers defying, only one in ten went to the end, and when two experimenters disagreed, far fewer went on than in the baseline \autocite{milgram1974obedience}. About a quarter of Asch's subjects never yielded to the wrong majority \autocite{asch1956studies}. Browning estimates that between a tenth and a fifth of the men of Reserve Police Battalion 101 refused or evaded the shooting at Józefów, the battalion's first massacre \autocite{browning1992ordinary}. The fraction ran from almost none to nearly all with the setting. The people who held out had evidence from outside the channel: a peer who defied, a second authority who disagreed, and, among the rescuers the Oliners studied, relationships that crossed the line the persecution drew \autocite{oliner1988altruistic}. Or they held the commitment at a precision the pressure couldn't overcome, as moral mandates and sacred values are held \autocite{skitka2009limits,ginges2007sacred}. Or they treated the source as having a stake. And groups resisted where isolated individuals rarely did \autocite{gamson1982encounters}.

The room can be composed for the other side just as well. Weimar Germany had a dense associational life, which arguably helped bring the republic down: as parties and governments failed to answer people's grievances, people put their energy into clubs and societies organized along group lines, and the Nazis recruited through the networks those societies had already built \autocite{berman1997weimar}. Towns with more choirs, sports clubs and gymnastic associations saw faster entry into the party, but only where government was unstable; in Prussia, whose government was stable, the link was small and insignificant \autocite{satyanath2017bowling}. So what the rescuers had was not simply more company. Their ties crossed the line the persecution drew. Ashutosh Varshney found the same in Indian cities: riots between Hindus and Muslims were rare where associations brought the two communities into the same organizations, and common where they didn't \autocite{varshney2002ethnic}. The ties that make refusal possible are the ones a fascist movement would have to cut.

Everything that follows is about composing the room.\footnote{The room is the setting, and as my theater teacher would say, the set directs the scene. Who else is in the room? Is leaving survivable? Would anyone hear a refusal?} Composing is a word for making, and Hannah Arendt warned against treating politics as a thing made. For the Greeks, she wrote, the law was literally a wall, without which there might be a town but not a city \autocite{arendt1958human}: the lawgiver built the wall, and what happened inside it was not theirs to make. The basic measures that I advocate are walls in that sense. They make refusal survivable and make it heard; they don't produce it, and power, on her account, exists only between people acting together and is gone when they scatter, which is what the record of who refused shows about refusal. The instruments of Parts II and III make a refusal count. The institutions of Part V make refusers. A machine that could refuse would need what the nine in ten with a peer beside them had: a peer with a different history beside it, somewhere to go, and someone who would hear it.

That is also what a refuser — including a machine refuser — in the pipeline could be for the officer. A machine that refused would be on the screen when the case reached him: the defiant peer, if the effect survives the move from a room to a screen, which is untested and which automation bias predicts it won't. A machine that moved reviewers by deference would have made them stamps of a different sign. Is the officer with twenty seconds and a refusing machine beside him the same officer as in the reporting?
