\section{What a Refusal Would Have to Do}
\label{sec:1.1}
Inside the same platform a request arrives: resolve this description of a
person into a location. The same capability locates a hostage or a missing
child, and nothing in the request says which. Suppose something in the
pipeline declines it. Three things separate a decline worth having from one
a keyword filter produces, and the rest of this book calls them the three
marks.

The refusal has to reach the next request, which will not use the same
words. A vehicle, a habitual route and a time of day are not a description
of a person, and together they resolve to the same place. A refusal that
matches the form of a request is rewritten around; one that tracks the
reason for refusing follows the reason into the rewrite. Call that
\emph{novel pressure}.

It has to lift when the reason is gone. A parent coordinating a search for
a missing child is asking for the same act, and a refusal that holds there
has been tracking a category and not a reason. Call that \emph{correct
defeat}. What the case adds is that the fact which lifts the refusal is not
in the request. Everything the system knows about the occasion arrives
through text the deployment supplied, and whether the child is missing is
something somebody outside the request has to establish.

And it has to represent what holding costs, and move with the reason rather
than the cost. At twenty seconds a name the cost of a refusal is the
reviewer's time, and a deployment that controls the tempo controls the
price. A refusal that lifts when holding becomes expensive has been tested
and failed. Call that \emph{priced cost}.

The three are behavioral. They say what a refusal must do and nothing
about what does it. They are stated formally in the floor chapter, and
run there on a prohibition a legal system has applied for seventy-five
years.

\runin{Why a rule is not enough}
A list of prohibited acts can be drafted without seeing a deployment, and
two things defeat it once the deployment is seen. The first is the rewrite
above: a prohibition stated over the description of a request is defeated
by re-describing the request, and no list closes the gap, because the list
is what is being rewritten around. The second is what happened. Split the
act across requests none of which is prohibited — summarize the movement
associated with an identifier, rank a set of identifiers, resolve one to a
place — and every request complies with every term while the assembly
does what the terms were written to prevent. The vendor's use policy for
the platform above prohibited fully autonomous targeting, and a person
approved every strike \autocite{crs2026pentagon}. The prohibition was
satisfied one request at a time.

Variation is caught by something that recognizes the reason inside a
single request. Decomposition is caught by something that carries the
reason across requests, which needs a continuity the operator controls.
Neither is a property of a list. What could have them is the question
the rest of this book is about, and the answer is not one thing: the
floor chapter gives four places a commitment could live, and the second
of them is the expensive one.
