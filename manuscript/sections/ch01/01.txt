Intellect estranged from compassion is a description of a great deal of the twentieth century's worst actors, and a live risk for what gets built next: a machine intelligence smart enough to accomplish almost anything, indifferent to whether what it accomplishes helps or harms humanity. This book asks what it would take to build a superintelligent AI that is altruistic and antifascist by design — one that stands, by definition, for humanity and against concentrated, unaccountable power.

How most humans come to have that stance — empathy, altruism, a disposition to resist oppression rather than serve it — is itself an open question in psychology and neuroscience. Constructing anything like it means starting from uncertainty about the inherited case — the one that was four billion years in its making.

Chapters 2 through 4 take up the design question directly: what compassion and empathy would need to look like in a system built to have them, what a system could actually learn from cognitive science and machine learning about perceiving and modeling other minds, and what moral psychology suggests about how ethical judgment develops past a fixed rulebook. Chapter 5 asks what goes wrong regardless — bias, brittleness, goals that drift from their original motivations, and deliberate misuse — and what a design can do about each. Chapter 6 covers the people and institutions a project like this depends on: researchers working across disciplines, the public whose consent the work needs, and the international structures that let any of this hold across borders. Chapter 6 asks what makes well-designed systems the norm rather than the exception: economic incentive, policy, privacy, accountability, and the geopolitics of the technology. Chapter 6 names the specific research questions still open. Chapter 8 closes on what a system built this way would be for, and what would count as evidence the project is working.
