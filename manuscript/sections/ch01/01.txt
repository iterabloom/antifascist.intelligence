Intellect estranged from compassion is not a hypothetical. It is a description of a great deal of the twentieth century's worst actors, and a live risk for what gets built next: a machine intelligence smart enough to accomplish almost anything, indifferent to whether what it accomplishes helps or harms. This book asks what it would take to build a superintelligent AI that is not just capable but altruistic and anti-authoritarian by design — one that stands for human welfare and against concentrated, unaccountable power because that stance is built into what it is, not appended to what it does.

How humans come to have that stance — empathy, altruism, a disposition to resist oppression rather than serve it — is itself an open question in psychology and neuroscience, not a solved one. Building anything like it into an artificial system means starting from real uncertainty about the human case, not a settled account to port over.

Chapters 2 through 4 take up the design question directly: what compassion and empathy would need to look like in a system built to have them, what a system could actually learn from cognitive science and machine learning about perceiving and modeling other minds, and what moral psychology suggests about how ethical judgment develops well past a fixed rulebook. Chapter 5 asks what goes wrong regardless — bias, brittleness, goals that drift from what they were meant to want, and deliberate misuse — and what a design can do about each. Chapter 6 covers the people and institutions a project like this depends on: researchers working across disciplines that don't share a vocabulary, the public whose consent the work needs and rarely has, and the international structures that let any of this hold across borders. Chapter 7 asks what makes a well-designed system the norm rather than the exception once it leaves a lab: economic incentive, policy, privacy, accountability, and the geopolitics of a technology every state has reason to want and every authoritarian government has reason to abuse. Chapter 9 names what none of the preceding chapters actually finished — the specific research questions still open. Chapter 10 closes on what a system built this way would be for, what would count as evidence the project is working, and what could still go wrong even done right.
