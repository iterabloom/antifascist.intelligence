2.1.4. Antifascist Ethics for AI Alignment
Antifascist ethics opposes concentrating power and authority in AI systems and counters the potential misuse of AI technology by authoritarian actors. The word is chosen deliberately: fascism is the narrower and more charged term, and I use it because it is more honest about the politics in which this book is being written — but the target is authoritarian power in every form, and a system built on these principles is anti-authoritarian in full, not only antifascist. In AI alignment terms, that means building systems that enhance democratic values, civil rights, and inclusive solidarity, and that refuse to facilitate autocratic practices or prop up an oppressive regime. Seven strategies do that work:
<<list>>
1. Democratic decision-making: build AI systems whose decisions respect and promote democratic values. An AI system used in media and journalism, for instance, should weight balanced coverage, unbiased reporting, and transparent sourcing, cutting the odds it ends up spreading disinformation or propaganda — natural language processing that flags potential bias in a news article and surfaces alternative sources is one way to do it.

2. Protecting privacy and autonomy: design AI systems that respect individual privacy and autonomy rather than enabling mass surveillance or manipulating individual choices — federated learning and differential privacy, applied to systems handling sensitive healthcare, financial, or communications data, are two ways to do it (section 5.4.2 goes further into both as technical countermeasures against authoritarian misuse). A privacy-preserving diagnostic tool, for instance, can keep a patient's data encrypted on their own device rather than sending it to an external server for anything beyond the diagnosis itself.

3. Equitable distribution of power and resources: build AI systems that keep the distribution of power and resources fair, transparent, and accountable. An AI decision-making system used in social services or law enforcement should work against power concentration, bias, and discrimination rather than reinforce them — an algorithm allocating scholarships, for instance, distributing them by need and correcting for the systemic bias already in the data it was trained on.

4. Transparency and accountability: an AI system needs an explanation of its own algorithm and outcomes that a person can actually read, and an independent body to audit whether it holds to antifascist principles in practice rather than on paper.

5. Resistance to market power: decentralized AI architectures resist the monopolistic and monopsonistic concentration that a handful of corporate entities would otherwise have over the field. Multi-agent systems and edge computing, publicly funded and released under a strong copyleft license, let AI agents function independently and make decisions collaboratively without a profit-motivated gatekeeper positioned to shut down whatever threatens its position.

6. Monitoring and counteracting misuse: build the mechanisms to detect misuse, unintended consequences, and violations of antifascist principles as they happen — AI-powered censorship, digital surveillance, an oppressive use of facial recognition — and the redress and corrective action to follow, in something closer to real time than an after-the-fact report.

7. AI systems that support dialogue and collaboration: design AI systems that promote open discussion and creative problem-solving among a diverse set of stakeholders, built around active listening and mutual understanding rather than around manufacturing a feeling of empathy in either party. A translation tool or a content-moderation tool that reduces the practical barriers to communication between people from different backgrounds is doing this kind of work already.
<</list>>
