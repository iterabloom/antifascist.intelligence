\subsection{Balancing Scientific Progress and Ethical Constraints}
\label{sec:2.4.3}
Scientific progress and ethical constraint pull hardest against each other exactly where the distinctions in section~\ref{sec:2.4.1} matter most: a candidate sentient system is also the most scientifically valuable one to study, and the study most likely to harm it if the answer to whether it can suffer turns out to be yes. The principles in section~\ref{sec:2.4.2} hold here without restatement. What changes at the point of balancing is a practical question — whether a specific study's expected knowledge is worth its expected risk to a system that might be able to suffer, decided case by case rather than by formula, and revisited as a project's own understanding of its subject changes.

The negotiation itself — who sits on it, what it can veto, what it must disclose — is where the antifascist principle earns its place in AI research practice.
