\subsection{Navigating AI Values, Priorities, and Moral Dilemmas}
\label{sec:2.1.3}
Values assigned to a system will conflict with each other, and the interesting question is not that they conflict but which conflicts a designer is allowed to resolve by choosing.

Most are. A consequentialist weighting and a deontological duty pull apart on a particular case, and settling that case is ordinary design work: rank the values explicitly, keep a record of how comparable cases were resolved, and audit afterward for the gap between the principles a system was meant to implement and what it actually did.

One conflict is not like the others, and it is the one usually filed under cultural difference. the UN's 1948 Universal Declaration of Human Rights, adopted by the General Assembly with zero dissenting votes, is the universal floor. Forty-eight states voted for it and none against; the eight that abstained were the Soviet bloc, Saudi Arabia, and South Africa — which is to say that the objection came from precisely the governments whose treatment of their own populations the Declaration was written to name. That is not evidence of genuine cultural disagreement about human rights. It is evidence of who finds the floor inconvenient \autocite{un1948udhr}. What varies by community is how a right gets translated into local practice — a real design problem, worth cross-cultural input built into the design process and a system that weighs how a right is honored in context rather than assuming one fixed application works everywhere. A jurisdiction refusing to extend the right at all isn't a local variant to accommodate — it's a violation the system doesn't launder as diversity.

That distinction is the whole of what this section contributes to chapter~\ref{sec:3}. A system that treats every value conflict as a weighting problem has no way to represent the difference between a case where reasonable people disagree and a case where somebody wants a right withdrawn from somebody else. The first calls for the machinery above; the second is what the floor is for, and a system with only the machinery will resolve it by whichever weight is larger.
