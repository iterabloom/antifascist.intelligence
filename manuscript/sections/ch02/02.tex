\chapter{Yes, It Has to Have Feelings}
\label{sec:2}
My thesis is that a floor is only going to hold against the owner if the system that they own \emph{cares} about things and people. Affect is part of the machinery of valuation and not a coloring applied to it. Spinoza states the claim in a form that needs no defending as a metaphor: an affect is an increase or a decrease in a body's power of acting, together with the idea of that change \autocite[III Def.~3]{spinoza1985collected}. It is not a report about a valuation carried out somewhere else. It is the valuation, registered as a change in what the body can now do. A system's self-model is not an inner observer whose reports have to be trusted, but rather an abstraction that is built, checkable, and revisable. And in principle, whether a candidate system can be harmed is a separate question from whether it is conscious. I also describe what the standard ethical frameworks can and cannot carry, and what makes an ethics antifascist rather than merely responsive.
