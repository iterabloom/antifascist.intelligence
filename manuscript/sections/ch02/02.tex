\chapter{Ethics, Affect, and Machine Subjects}
\label{sec:2}
Chapter~\ref{sec:3} argues that a floor holding against the party in possession has to be held by something that cares what becomes of somebody, and that the only demonstrated way to care about anything is affective. Three results here are load-bearing. Affect is part of the machinery of valuation rather than a coloring applied to it. A system's self-model is a model — built, checkable, revisable — and not an inner observer whose reports have to be trusted. And whether a candidate system can be harmed is a separate and much harder question than whether it is conscious at all. The chapter also fixes terms the rest of the book argues in: what the standard ethical frameworks can and cannot carry, and what makes an ethics antifascist rather than merely responsive.
