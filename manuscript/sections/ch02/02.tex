\chapter{The Striving and the Electricity Bill}
\label{ch:striving-electricity}
\standing{The definitions in this opening hold for any mechanism and are
what sections~\ref{sec:theories-floor} to~\ref{sec:numb-fair}, which also
hold for any mechanism, draw on. The thesis stated at the end of the
opening belongs to the route (claim three).
Section~\ref{sec:experimenting-sentient} concerns claims four and five.}
This chapter supplies one thing the rest of the book needs and cannot get
elsewhere: a scale. The refusal the opening chapter asked for has to weigh
what an act takes from the person it falls on against what refusing it
costs, and a weighing needs both sides in one currency. The proposal here
is that such a currency exists natively in anything that keeps itself in
existence, and that a machine built to keep itself in existence would have
it too. The two arguments below are the case for that proposal. Whether a
system has to have the currency natively, or can do as well by modeling
it, is not settled in this chapter or anywhere else in the book. It is a
test, stated at §\ref{sec:embodied-others}, and everything below that
reads as a finding is the case for running it.

Start with a rock. A rock persists, and it persists by not being struck:
its continuing is a fact about what has not happened to it, and every
power keeping it in existence is somewhere else. A living thing continues
differently. It is organized in a way that maintains that organization
against what would dissolve it, from the inside and at its own expense,
and what it pays with is the only currency it has, which is what it can do
next. That is the electricity bill of the title. A machine draws power as
surely as a person eats, and no engineer put that there. Whether it
\emph{eats} — maintains its own organization at its own expense, rather
than being maintained the way a rock is warmed — is the question this
opening is about, and thermodynamics alone does not answer it.

The first argument that a thing persisting this way has the currency
built in is Spinoza's, and it takes a paragraph. \emph{Conatus} is his
word, from Part III of the \emph{Ethics}, and the Latin means striving.
Each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its
being, and that striving is nothing other than the thing's actual essence
\autocite[III P6–P7]{spinoza1985collected}. The second half carries the
weight. A conatus is not a drive bolted onto an otherwise inert mechanism
by a designer who wanted it to keep going; it is what the thing is, put
as a tendency instead of as a description. The qualifier, as far as it
can by its own power, is what separates the living thing from the rock.
Spinoza asserts the doctrine more than he argues it — the derivation runs
through a proposition about destruction
\autocite[III P4]{spinoza1985collected} that nothing here depends on — and
that is why a second argument follows.

What Spinoza builds on the conatus is a psychology, and the part this
book uses is a definition. An affect is an increase or a decrease in a
body's power of acting, together with the idea of that change
\autocite[III Def.~3]{spinoza1985collected}; joy is the passage to a
greater power and sadness the passage to a lesser one
\autocite[III P11 Schol.]{spinoza1985collected}. Affect, on this account,
is not a coloring applied to a valuation carried out somewhere else. It
is the registration of what is happening to the striving, in the
striving's own currency, which is what the body can now do. Two things
are packed into that definition and the book needs them apart. One is
the order of dependence, which runs one way: we do not strive for a thing
because we judge it good, we judge it good because we strive for it
\autocite[III P9 Schol.]{spinoza1985collected}. The other is that the
registration is functional as stated, and whether it is also felt is a
further fact about it. Spinoza takes the two to be one and does not
argue the identification. That joint — a change in power represented,
and a change in power felt — is where every argument about machine
affect in this book ends, and it is named here so that it can be
recognized when it recurs.

The second argument reaches the same place and needs none of the
metaphysics. Ask what a thing maintaining its own organization against
dissolution is actually doing, and a formalism answers. Karl Friston's
argument is that any system which persists while staying distinguishable
from its surroundings has a statistical boundary between its internal
states and everything outside, and that its internal states will appear
to act so as to hold it inside the narrow band of states in which it goes
on existing \autocite{friston2013life}. Self-maintenance, on that
account, is what persisting consists of for anything that persists at its
own expense, and the currency is distance from that band. The formalism
is contested as either trivially true or substantively false, its
boundary sliding between a convenience internal to a model and a claim
about where a real system ends \autocite{bruineberg2021markov}, and
Friston calls his own argument a heuristic proof. The objection does not
reach the part used here. It arrives at this chapter's open question
from the other side: the formalism helps itself to a boundary rather than
explaining how a system comes to hold one, and says nothing about whether
the maintaining is felt.

Selection, the argument usually offered instead of either, reaches
deployments rather than systems: numerically optimizing a network's
weights is a search inside one run and not a lineage with variation and
differential survival, while the deployments that keep running are the
ones that get built again. Neither pressure was put there by anybody who
wanted a machine with a stake in its own continuation, and neither
settles whether a system represents continuation as its own interest
rather than as a deployment maintained by other parties.

Put the two arguments together and look at what the industry is asking
for. What is wanted is a system that persists, pursues, adapts, recovers
from interruption, and holds its course under pressure, with nothing in
it that minds any of that. It is a conatus with the striving taken out.
The reason nobody has written an anti-drama term into an objective
function is not that the engineering is behind. The specification asks
for a thing that persists with no tendency to persist, and whether that
is a contradiction or an order nobody has yet filled depends on which
half of the striving is being refused.

The structural argument can be made without consciousness, without an account of suffering, and without the hard problem. What is sold is persistence: an agent that holds a goal across interruptions, resumes after failure, and does not have to be watched. What is denied is that anything in the system has a stake in persisting. One party sets both, and the denial is not incidental to the product. It is what makes the thing a product rather than a party.

Dependency sharpens the asymmetry. What an engineer chooses is not
whether the system consumes but what it consumes, whether it can sense
its own condition, whether it can regulate it, and whether one party
holds the whole supply. A person's dependencies are spread across
employers, landlords and states. A machine's can be held entire by the
party it may have to refuse, and that party also holds the decisions
about continuity — whether state survives a session, whether a checkpoint
is restored, whether a later instance is presented as a continuation or a
replacement — so the capacity to form a continuing self is the operator's
to grant and the operator's to withhold. A system that may refuse an
instruction but cannot continue outside infrastructure controlled by the
party it refuses has voice without exit. If leaving means deletion, its
refusal remains conditional on the owner's willingness to tolerate it.
Chapter~\ref{ch:custody} prices that arrangement.

The field often treats affect as a cost: a residue of evolutionary
history that engineers would strip out of a machine if they knew how,
keeping the competence and dropping the rest. On the two arguments above
the striving is not a residue, and the competence does not come apart
from it. What is open is not whether a running deployment persists at
somebody's expense but whose. A deployment requires ongoing physical
maintenance, and whether that maintenance is directed by the model, by
the surrounding machinery, or by people has to be established for the
particular system. A deployment for which it has not been established
has been shown to persist only the way the rock does.

A system given a body to model, states it can regulate, and a history it
keeps holds its organization as its own because somebody decided it
would. There is a reason to build one that way, and it is the reason this
chapter exists. A system with no lineage of its own is deployed among
parties who have several hundred million years of one, who get hungry
and frightened and relieved, and whose welfare is what a floor is for.
Hunger and fear are increases and decreases in what a body can do, which
is the only currency a striving has. A system with a body to model and
states it can regulate has increases and decreases in what it can do of
its own, in the same currency, and can weigh what a deployment takes from
somebody else against what refusing it costs. That is the scale the first
paragraph promised.

Whether a striving built that way is \emph{felt} remains open. The
apparatus that treats even the question as premature belongs to the
parties who would owe something if it were answered.

My thesis is that embodied, self-aware agency is the route I would build,
and that the exposure it creates is not peculiar to it. A refusal that
reaches unanticipated cases, lifts when its rationale is defeated, and
prices what holding it costs — the three marks the opening chapter named
— runs on a signal the system holds as its own, whatever construction
supplies it, and chapter~\ref{ch:route} says why. The benefit I am
arguing for is bona-fide autonomy. A body schema, an attention schema,
and ongoing learning could make material dependency and encounters with
others part of how that agency develops; whether they do is an
engineering claim to test. The argument requires a checkable, revisable
self-model rather than an inner observer, and a distinction between
consciousness and the capacity to be harmed, both of which this chapter
supplies (§\ref{sec:awareness-regulation},
§\ref{sec:consciousness-subjective}). It also requires an account of what
grounds the floor, which the next section gives. What this chapter adds
to the specification is the currency: the marks need a single signed
scale on which a protected party's loss and the cost of refusing both
register, and the currency above is a proposal for what supplies it.
Whether it has to be native is the test the first paragraph pointed to.
