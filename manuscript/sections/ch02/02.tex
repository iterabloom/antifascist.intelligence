\chapter{Yes, It Has to Have Feelings}
\label{sec:2}
My thesis is that a floor is only going to hold against the owner if the system that they own \emph{cares} about things and people. In this chapter, I argue that affect is part of the machinery of valuation [rather than a coloring applied to it] -- THIS MIGHT NEED REWRITE. A system's self-model is not an inner observer whose reports have to be trusted, but rather an abstraction that is built, checkable, and revisable. And in principle, whether a candidate system can be harmed is a separate question from whether it is conscious. I also describe what the standard ethical frameworks can and cannot carry, and what makes an ethics antifascist rather than merely responsive.
