\chapter{Ethics, Affect, and Machine Subjects}
\label{sec:2}
This book argues that a floor holding against the party in possession has to be held by a system that \emph{cares} what becomes of someone, and that the only demonstrated way to care about anything is affective. In this chapter, I argue that affect is part of the machinery of valuation rather than a coloring applied to it. A system's self-model is a model — built, checkable, revisable — and not an inner observer whose reports have to be trusted. And whether a candidate system can be harmed is a separate question from whether it is conscious. The chapter also fixes terms the rest of the book argues in: what the standard ethical frameworks can and cannot carry, and what makes an ethics antifascist rather than merely responsive.
