\chapter{Foundations of Compassion and Empathy in Friendly AI}
\label{sec:2}
This chapter asks what an AI system needs, psychologically, to be worth calling friendly. The answer it lands on is not the obvious one. Empathy — feeling what another feels — turns out to be a poor foundation: parochial, prone to burnout, biased toward whoever's distress is most vivid and nearest at hand. Compassion, the separable capacity to act on another's welfare without first manufacturing their suffering in oneself, is what does the load-bearing work.

Section~\ref{sec:2.1} sets the ethical frame those capacities have to serve: which ethical theories translate into machine-learning objectives, and specifically what makes an alignment target antifascist rather than merely obedient. Section~\ref{sec:2.2} works through empathy, social intelligence, and theory of mind, and is where the compassion argument gets made. Section~\ref{sec:2.3} takes up emotional intelligence and self-awareness, including why a system's self-model is a model — built, checkable, revisable — rather than an inner observer to be taken on faith. Section~\ref{sec:2.4} turns to the hardest question the chapter raises: if a system might be sentient, experimenting on it carries ethical weight, and knowing whether a candidate system can suffer turns out to be a harder question than knowing whether it is conscious at all.
