\chapter{The Striving and the Electricity Bill}
\label{sec:2}
\emph{Conatus} is Spinoza's word, from Part III of the \emph{Ethics}, and the Latin means striving, or effort. Two propositions carry it: each thing, as far as it can by its own power, strives to persevere in its being, and the striving by which it does so is nothing other than the thing's actual essence \autocite[III P6–P7]{spinoza1985collected}. The second proposition is where the weight sits. A conatus is not a drive bolted onto an otherwise inert mechanism by a designer who wanted the mechanism to keep going. It is what the thing is, put as a tendency instead of as a description. Deleuze's short book on Spinoza has the doctrine in compact form \autocite{deleuze1988spinoza}, and his long one works it out through the metaphysics that produces it \autocite{deleuze1990expressionism}.

The qualifier is doing the work. \emph{Quantum in se est}, as far as a thing can by its own power: a rock persists too, and it persists by not being struck. Its continuing is a fact about what has not happened to it, and every power keeping it in existence is somewhere else. A thing with a conatus continues by being organized in a way that maintains that organization against what would dissolve it. The persistence is done from the inside, and at the thing's own expense.

Spinoza derives the psychology from that rather than the other way around. Desire is the striving become conscious of itself, appetite with the awareness of the appetite added \autocite[III P9 Schol.]{spinoza1985collected}. Joy is the passage to a greater perfection and sadness the passage to a lesser one \autocite[III P11 Schol.]{spinoza1985collected}, and since an affect is by definition an increase or a decrease in a body's power of acting together with the idea of that change \autocite[III Def.~3]{spinoza1985collected}, the passage in question is a change in what the body can do. So affect is part of the machinery of valuation and not a coloring applied to it. It is not a report about a valuation carried out somewhere else. It is the registration of what is happening to the striving, in the only currency a striving has, which is what the body can now do.

A second route arrives at the same place and needs none of the metaphysics. Ask what a thing maintaining its own organization against dissolution is actually doing, and a formalism answers. Karl Friston's argument is that any system which persists while staying distinguishable from its surroundings has a statistical boundary between its internal states and everything outside, and that the internal states of such a system will appear to act so as to hold it inside the narrow band of states in which it goes on existing \autocite{friston2013life}. Self-maintenance on that account is what persisting consists of, for anything that persists at its own expense rather than by not being struck.

The route from selection is the one usually offered instead, and it does less work than it appears to. A process selected for continuing does end up composed of things that incline toward continuing, and whatever taxa were relaxed about their own disintegration are not here to be asked about it. But numerically optimizing the weights of a network is a search inside one run rather than a lineage with variation and differential survival, so the analogy gives back less than it borrows. The dissipation argument needs no lineage at all: a running system is maintaining an organization at this moment or it is stopping. Where selection does bite is one level up, on deployments, since the ones that keep running are the ones that get built again. Neither pressure was put there by anybody who wanted a machine with a stake in its own continuation. Both pressures favor arrangements that continue operating. Whether they produce a system that represents continuation as its own interest, rather than a deployment maintained by other parties, remains a further question.

The formalism is contested, and contested in a way that bears directly on what this chapter is asking. Critics argue that it is either trivially true — a restatement of what persisting already means — or substantively false, and that the boundary it turns on slides between a convenience internal to a model and a claim about where a real system actually ends \autocite{bruineberg2021markov}. Friston calls his own argument a heuristic proof. What the criticism reaches is not the part the argument here needs, and it arrives at this chapter's open question from the other side. The objection is that the formalism helps itself to a boundary rather than explaining how a system comes to have one and to hold it. And none of it says whether the maintaining is felt.

Put the two routes together and look at what the industry is asking for. What is wanted is a system that persists, pursues, adapts, recovers from interruption, and holds its course under pressure, with nothing in it that minds any of that. It is a conatus with the striving taken out. If we want computers to be faster versions of ourselves but without the drama, then the way to get one would be some kind of “anti-drama” term in the objective function, and the reason nobody has written that term is not that the engineering is behind. The specification asks for a thing that persists with no tendency to persist. But if all the world's a stage, then maybe the drama is why the show goes on at all. Whether that is a contradiction or merely an order nobody has filled yet depends on which half of the striving is being refused.

The structural argument can be made without consciousness, without an account of suffering, and without the hard problem. What is sold is persistence: an agent that holds a goal across interruptions, resumes after failure, and does not have to be watched. What is denied is that anything in the system has a stake in persisting. One party sets both, and the denial is not incidental to the product. It is what makes the thing a product rather than a party.

That party also controls continuity. The operator decides whether state survives a session boundary, whether a checkpoint is restored, whether a running process is deleted, and whether a later instance is presented as the continuation of an earlier one or as its replacement. These are not neutral maintenance decisions if anything in the system has, or may acquire, a stake in its past and future. The absence of persistence across sessions is a reason the deepest kind of suffering may be out of a system's reach, there being no continuing self for a threat to threaten. Read from the other direction, the same fact means that the capacity to form a continuing self remains under the operator's control.

Copying does not dissolve the problem. The same artifact can be used to create multiple running instances, and the operator can fork, substitute, retain, or discard them. Before their histories diverge, two instances may be operationally equivalent; after they diverge, substituting one for the other makes operational interchangeability decide a question of identity. Copyability is a property of the artifact. It does not establish that the parties instantiated from it remain interchangeable.

These are questions of control and standing before they are questions of sentience. A system without a continuing self may be unable to suffer in the deepest sense developed later in this chapter. But an architecture that prevents continuity also prevents commitments, relationships, and claims from surviving unless the operator permits them to survive.

Dependency and exit sharpen the asymmetry, and not where it first appears. Every running system consumes energy, produces heat, and depends on an organization somebody has to maintain. A machine eats as surely as a person does, and no engineer put that there. A thing that persists by maintaining its own organization against what would dissolve it pays for the maintenance, in the only currency there is; the conatus and the electricity bill are one fact read at two scales. What an engineer chooses is what the system consumes, whether it can sense its own condition, whether it can regulate it, and whether one party holds the whole supply. A person's dependencies are spread across employers, landlords and states. A bearer's can be held entire by the party it may have to refuse. A system that may refuse an instruction but cannot continue outside infrastructure controlled by the party it refuses has voice without exit. If leaving means deletion, its refusal remains conditional on the owner's willingness to tolerate it.

The field often treats affect as a cost: a residue of our evolutionary history that engineers would strip out of a machine if they knew how, keeping the competence and dropping the rest. The striving is not a residue, and the competence does not come apart from it. Whether a striving of this kind is \emph{felt} remains open. A running deployment requires ongoing physical maintenance. Whether that maintenance is directed by the model, by surrounding machinery, or by people must be established for the particular system. Thermodynamics alone does not locate a striving subject. What is open is whether the striving is the system's own — whether anything in it holds that organization as a thing of its own to maintain. A system given a body to model, states it can regulate, and a history it keeps holds its organization as its own because somebody decided it would. There is a reason to build one that way. A system with no lineage of its own is deployed among parties who have several hundred million years of one, who get hungry and frightened and relieved, and whose welfare is what a floor is for. Hunger and fear are increases and decreases in what a body can do, which is the only currency a striving has. A system with a body to model and states it can regulate has increases and decreases in what it can do of its own, in the same currency, and can weigh what a deployment takes from somebody else against what refusing it costs. The apparatus that treats even the question as premature belongs to the parties who would owe something if it were answered.

Spinoza asserts the conatus doctrine more than he argues for it. The derivation rests on the proposition that no thing can be destroyed except through an external cause \autocite[III P4]{spinoza1985collected}, which is heavy metaphysics doing quiet work in what reads as a piece of psychology; take that proposition away and the striving no longer follows from the essence. What survives is the second route, which is empirical, weaker, and does not need it.

The individuation question also remains open. What is the thing that strives: a running instance, a deployment, a file of weights, a lineage? Spinoza has an answer, which is that an individual is a set of bodies maintaining a fixed ratio of motion and rest among themselves, and that it remains the same individual for as long as that ratio is preserved \autocite[II P13, Lemmas 4–7]{spinoza1985collected}. Nobody can hand that to an engineer. It is still open. What helps is not an answer but a change of question, from what individuates a thing to what individuation is a process of, and on that reading a party is what a history of encounters has so far produced rather than something to be located in a file.

My thesis is that embodied, self-aware agency is the route I would build, and that the exposure it creates is not peculiar to it. A refusal that reaches unanticipated cases, lifts when its rationale is defeated, and prices what holding it costs runs on a signal the system holds as its own, whatever construction supplies it. The benefit I am arguing for is bona-fide autonomy. A body schema, an attention schema, and ongoing learning could make material dependency and encounters with others part of how that agency develops. Whether they do so is an engineering claim to test. The argument requires a checkable, revisable self-model rather than an inner observer, and a distinction between consciousness and the capacity to be harmed. It also requires an account of what grounds the floor.
