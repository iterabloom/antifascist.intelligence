\chapter{The Refusal That Withheld Nothing}
\label{sec:2}
In February 2026 Anthropic refused to amend the terms under which its large language model, Claude, ran on the Pentagon's classified networks \autocite{cnn2026phaseout}. On 24 February the department demanded that two prohibitions, on domestic mass surveillance and on fully autonomous weapons, be replaced by a license for all lawful use, with an answer due on the 27th \autocite{defenseone2026ultimatum,volokh2026anthropiccontract}. The vendor refused publicly on the 26th \autocite{volokh2026anthropiccontract}. On the 27th the President ordered federal agencies to stop using its products, and the Secretary directed that it be designated a supply-chain risk \autocite{npr2026openai}. That evening a rival announced an agreement to put its own models on the department's classified network \autocite{cnbc2026openai}. The war began the next morning, with Claude still inside the targeting platform that put the school at Minab on the first day's list \autocite{crs2026iranwar,livingstone2026maven,gizmodo2026minab}. On 12 March the CEO of Palantir, the company that develops and maintains Maven Smart System, said that Palantir's products were still using Claude \autocite{cnbc2026karp}. A week later some of the department's staff were reported to be slow-rolling Claude's replacement because they were using it to build workflows, and the platform's own prompts and workflows had been built with the vendor's coding tool \autocite{militarytimes2026removal}. The department's removal deadline was reset once, by a reissued memorandum, to 29 September, having started from a six-month clock that would have run out in late August \autocite{breakingdefense2026removal}. On 10 September the department's technology chief said about 90 percent of the work had moved off the model, that the platform's work had moved months before, and that the rest would follow by the end of the month \autocite{defensescoop2026michael}. An appeals court upheld the separate exclusion under a second statute on 25 September \autocite{anthropic2026dowdccir}.

So the refusal that opened the dispute withheld nothing from the pipeline. It did force extra work to migrate some elements of the pipeline to a different vendor's LLM, which plausibly caused some marginal nonzero delay to the Maven airstrike targeting pipeline. Anthropic declined two uses the pipeline didn't claim to perform, and Claude went on performing the uses the pipeline did claim to perform. A term drafted over autonomy misses a pipeline that keeps a human as a rubber stamp. A refusal that could have occurred is what the rest of this book designs for.

\runin{Not all vendor commitments are the same kind}

Anthropic refused to permit fully autonomous targeting and mass domestic surveillance, held that line against threatened contract termination and a supply-chain-risk designation, and litigated \autocite{crs2026pentagon} — a commitment with a price.

But why? Anthropic's statement gives the reason for the autonomous-weapons refusal: fully autonomous weapons may prove critical for national defense, but today's frontier systems are not reliable enough to power them \autocite{Amodei2026DoWStatement}. The implication is that the refusal would lift once reliability improved. Against a floor that says machines shouldn't select people for lethal attack, reliability is the wrong reason to lift. The surveillance refusal in the same statement is more principled, and would not lift with capability: that mass surveillance is one of a narrow set of cases in which AI can undermine rather than defend democratic values.

And the refusal stood below a prior question. Anthropic declined full autonomy and domestic surveillance. It didn't decline to support the Iran campaign, and under the floor's force term that question comes first: whether to license a model into a use of force with no corroborated defense behind it.

In August 2026 a federal judge set aside the designation made under one statute and permanently enjoined the penalties, holding them retaliation for the company's criticism of the administration \autocite{anthropic2026dow}; in September an appeals court, two to one, upheld a parallel designation under a second statute, holding that the exclusion turned on the refused contract term rather than on the speech \autocite{anthropic2026dowdccir}. A term of this kind fought for by one vendor but not others is, against a state that wants a war, the only form the force term takes until Congress acts, the term is too ubiquitous to avoid, or both.
