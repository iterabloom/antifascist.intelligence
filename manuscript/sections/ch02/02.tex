\chapter{Foundations of Compassion and Empathy in Friendly AI}
\label{sec:2}
Chapter~\ref{sec:3} argues that a floor holding against the party in possession has to be held by something that cares what becomes of somebody, and that the only demonstrated way to care about anything is affective. This chapter is where that argument gets its materials, and not everything in it carries them.

Four results are load-bearing. Empathy — feeling what another feels — is a poor foundation for a system meant to help: parochial, prone to burnout, biased toward whoever's distress is most vivid and nearest at hand. Compassion, the separable capacity to act on another's welfare without first manufacturing their suffering in oneself, is what does that work instead. Affect is part of the machinery of valuation rather than a coloring applied to it. A system's self-model is a model — built, checkable, revisable — and not an inner observer whose reports have to be trusted. And whether a candidate system can be harmed is a separate and much harder question than whether it is conscious at all.
