\chapter{Yes, It Has to Have Feelings}
\label{sec:2}
This book argues that a floor holding against the party in possession has to be held by a system that \emph{cares} what becomes of someone. In this chapter, I argue that affect is part of the machinery of valuation rather than a coloring applied to it. A system's self-model is not an inner observer whose reports have to be trusted, but rather an abstraction that is built, checkable, and revisable. And whether a candidate system can be harmed is a separate question from whether it is conscious. I also describe what the standard ethical frameworks can and cannot carry, and what makes an ethics antifascist rather than merely responsive.
