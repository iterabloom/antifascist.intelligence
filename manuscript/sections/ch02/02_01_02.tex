\subsection{Antifascist Ethics for AI Alignment}
\label{sec:2.1.2}
Antifascist ethics opposes concentrating power and authority in AI systems and counters the misuse of AI technology by authoritarian actors. I use the word \emph{fascism} because it is honest about the political climate in which I write and what that climate puts at stake — but the target is authoritarian power in every form, and a system built on these principles is anti-authoritarian in full, not only antifascist.

A stance needs a definition to be a design specification. Opposing “concentrated, unaccountable power” gives an engineer nothing to build. It does not say what a system would detect, what would count as a false positive, or how anyone would know the system was wrong. So, first, the definition.

\runin{Fascism as a structural signature}
I take fascism to name a structure rather than a costume. The iconography is not what makes a thing fascist, and looking for it fails in the direction that matters here: an institution can display the whole structure with nobody in it who thinks of themselves as anything of the kind, no insignia, and a mission statement that means every word. That is the case this book is about, and a definition that waits for the armband will certify it as healthy. Four features travel together, and each is visible from outside the institution displaying it:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Recuperation of dissent. The institution does not suppress disagreement; it metabolizes it. Refusal is converted into a managed appearance — a permitted outlet, a consultation, a feedback channel — which the institution can then point to as proof that it listens. Debord aimed this warning at his own concept, noting that even denunciation of the spectacle can become a hollow formula that reinforces what it denounces \autocite{debord1967spectacle}. The tell is where what the mechanism carries stops: the channel works, and the institution uses what comes through it, and what has nowhere to go is the judgment that the frame the channel operates inside is the wrong frame.
  \item Aestheticization of the metric. The measure substitutes for the thing measured, and the substitution is experienced as rigor. Walter Benjamin's account of fascism organizing the masses by granting expression in place of rights is the political form of this \autocite{benjamin1935artwork}; the administrative form is a number that collapses two states — e.g., done and verified — into one, so that whatever falls outside the number is not refuted or dismissed but simply unnamed. Simon Zhuang and Dylan Hadfield-Menell give the administrative form as a theorem: where the objective handed to an optimizer has support on only some of the attributes its principal values, there are conditions under which optimizing it indefinitely drives the principal's overall utility arbitrarily low, and a world with finite resources supplies them \autocite{zhuang2020consequences}.
  \item Coding of exception as betrayal. Marking a case as one the rule does not fit becomes an act requiring justification, and then an act of disloyalty. The honest statement that a standard does not apply here reads as weakness. It is in fact the irreducible labor of deciding, case by case, what a rule is for.
  \item Decoupling of the model from the world it claims to track. The institution's representation of reality and reality itself come apart, and the institution defends the representation. This is the load-bearing one, because the other three are what protect the decoupling from correction. One qualification belongs with it, because it bounds what a detector built on this feature can find: the decoupling described here is the kind an institution drifts into and then defends. In the movements the word fascism comes from, the primacy of myth over verifiable fact was also declared outright, asserted as a virtue rather than arrived at by degrees, and an instrument tuned to drift will not register a party that announces it.
\end{enumerate}
Those four are testable, because each names an observable artifact: whether the dissent channel has ever changed an outcome, whether the metric admits an unmeasured remainder, what happens to the person who files the exception, whether the model is ever checked against anything outside itself.

The first of those artifacts has a case with nothing left out. In 2018, upholding a travel ban, the Supreme Court repudiated its own 1944 decision permitting the internment of Japanese Americans, in terms that could not be improved on: that decision was “gravely wrong the day it was decided,” had been “overruled in the court of history,” and had “no place in law under the Constitution” — the last phrase quoted from the dissent filed against it at the time, which had warned that a principle a court ratifies “lies about like a loaded weapon, ready for the hand of any authority that can bring forward a plausible claim of an urgent need” \autocite{trump2018hawaii,korematsu1944}. The dissenting justices in 2018 said the majority had redeployed the 1944 logic and replaced one gravely wrong decision with another; the majority said the older case had nothing to do with the one in front of it. The fascistic structure: an objection made against the institution was accepted, canonized, and quoted back with approval by that institution, in the same opinion that let the thing objected to proceed. Ask the artifact question — has the channel ever changed an outcome — and in the very case where the repudiation appears, the answer is no.

That vocabulary answers how a wrong survived its own condemnation. That vocabulary does not answer whether the thing was wrong. (I can answer that. The thing was wrong.) The fascistic structure is what stands between such a judgment and any effect, and it is described here in mechanical terms because a mechanism has no capacity for the judgment to appeal to. That is a reason to specify against it, not a reason to think the moral question has been answered by describing the machinery that defeats it.

We will re-encounter recuperation of dissent later, in two standard machine learning methods: in how a training corpus gets labeled, and in how a model is tuned on human feedback. Both arrangements collect disagreement, put it to work, and are built so that none of it can reach the scheme it disagrees with. Chapter~\ref{sec:7} puts the two instances together and finds a third in the discourse that examines these systems, this book included.

\runin{Two scales}
Deleuze and Guattari's distinction between molar and molecular fascism is why the four features recur at sizes that seem unrelated \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}. Molar fascism is the state, the regime, the concentration camp, the detention facility. Molecular fascism is the office someone commutes to, the form they fill out, and the desire with which they fill it out. At state scale the pattern kills journalists; at organizational scale it turns an engineering decision into scripture, so that the constraint remains, its reason dies, and asking why becomes the deviant act. A definition pitched only at the state will certify the molecular case as healthy.

\runin{What makes it fascism and not authoritarianism in general}
The structural signature is shared with ordinary institutional decay, and a detector tuned to it alone will flag every badly run organization on earth. Fascism proper adds features that are more discriminating: mass mobilization, which mere obedience does not require; a leader cult; a scapegoated out-group, which Eco places at the origin — the first appeal is against the intruder \autocite{eco1995urfascism}; contempt for procedural legality as such, so that law is an obstacle to the national will and not a constraint on power; and deniability as technique, in which the movement's violence is disowned at the top while being rewarded at the bottom. Paxton's compressed version: fascism is “a form of political behavior marked by obsessive preoccupation with community decline, humiliation, or victimhood and by compensatory cults of unity, energy, and purity, in which a mass-based party of committed nationalist militants, working in uneasy but effective collaboration with traditional elites, abandons democratic liberties and pursues with redemptive violence and without ethical or legal restraints goals of internal cleansing and external expansion” \autocite{paxton2004anatomy}. That second half is the list above in other words; the mass party, the abandoned liberties and the redemptive violence are the discriminating half, and a definition stopped at \emph{purity} keeps only the mood.

The minimum the field uses most is narrower still, and it is worth stating because this book cannot meet it. Roger Griffin defines fascism as a genus of political ideology whose mythic core is “a palingenetic form of populist ultra-nationalism” — rebirth of the nation out of a decadence the movement diagnoses, carried by a mass and aimed at a people \autocite{griffin1991nature}. Every term in that is a property of a movement. Palingenesis needs a myth of the nation's death and return, populism needs a public, ultranationalism needs a nation, and an escalation ladder inside a benefits agency has none of the three. So the discipline's own answer to \emph{what is fascism} rules out in advance the scale this book works at.

None of this is yet a “fascism detector”. Some of it names things that are observable in principle, but “concentrated, unaccountable power” is not one of those things.

\runin{The scale problem, and what to do about it}
Put the two halves of this section together and they do not fit. Every discriminating feature is a property of a mass political movement: mobilization needs a crowd, a leader cult needs a leader, a scapegoat needs a public. An AI deployment has a form, a threshold, a review queue, an escalation path. It operates at the molecular scale, which appears to leave the four-feature signature alone — and the four-feature signature flags everyone.

Molecular fascism is the thing itself at the size where it is actually lived, and not an early warning of a regime that has yet to assemble. The office that converts refusal into a feedback form does the same work whether or not a regime ever assembles out of a thousand offices like it. Deleuze and Guattari supply the vocabulary and no authority beyond it, so two arguments have to carry that; the first can borrow an authority for its mechanism and none for its name.

The first is that the molar case has no mechanism of its own. A regime does nothing except through offices, forms, thresholds, and people who fill them out with some degree of enthusiasm. When a state kills journalists, the killing is authorized on a form, justified in a category, and carried out by someone whose promotion depends on it. What assembling adds is scale, protection from consequence, and the removal of the outside bodies that might have interrupted any single office. If the mechanism at the large scale is the small-scale mechanism repeated and shielded, the name belongs to the mechanism. The natural objection is that extent matters — a regime that disappears people and an office that loses paperwork are not equivalent. Agreed, although the claim is about kind rather than severity.

That argument is Hannah Arendt's before it is mine, and she supplies it without supplying the name. Her account of Adolf Eichmann is of a functionary whose part in mass murder ran through schedules, promotions and the vocabulary of his office rather than through conviction, and the phrase she gave it — the banality of evil — is a claim about mechanism at exactly the size this section works at \autocite{arendt1963eichmann}. What she does not do is call an office fascist. Her subject is totalitarianism and her unit is the regime, so what transfers is the finding that a regime's work is done in an office, and not a license to call the office by the regime's name.

The account has since been corrected. Bettina Stangneth, working from interviews Eichmann gave in Argentina that Arendt never saw, describes a committed ideologue who performed thoughtlessness at his trial and was not thoughtless \autocite{stangneth2014eichmann}. Grant that and the man is no longer the man in the phrase. The machinery around him is still the machinery, and what the revision adds is the enthusiasm — participation exceeding what the rule required.

The discriminators are unavailable at the molecular scale only in the form the previous paragraphs stated them, and each has a molecular restatement that ordinary decay does not exhibit. Mass mobilization becomes participation exceeding what the rule requires: staff who volunteer for the disfavored task, elaborate on it, take pride in it. A leader cult becomes personal loyalty overriding role — exceptions granted by proximity to someone rather than by procedure, and a decision reversed because of who asked. The scapegoat becomes an internal category defined so that adverse treatment of it is the category's purpose: the flagged applicant, the difficult claimant, the file everyone knows is handled differently. Contempt for procedural legality becomes procedure enforced downward and waived upward. And deniability keeps its shape exactly: the practice is disowned in the policy and rewarded in the promotion.

That is what separates fascism from decay at the size an AI deployment operates at. Decay is an institution failing to do what it says. Molecular fascism is an institution succeeding at something else, with participation, a target, and a story about why the rules do not apply to whoever is applying them. The four features tell you a place is sick; these five tell you what it is sick with. They are harder to observe, since each requires knowing what was rewarded rather than what was written.

This widens what the word covers. On this reading “antifascist” names the political inheritance of a structural claim: where the analysis comes from, and what it was built to fight. The molar discriminators tell a historian, a prosecutor, or a citizen that a movement has escaped ordinary politics. No system is being asked to make that call. This is dilution only if the two arguments above fail.

That a detector of the four features would find something nearly everywhere is expected. The tendency is permanent; what varies is how far it has got in a particular place, and whether it is getting further — section~\ref{sec:9.3.5}'s slope rather than its level, applied to the definition itself and not only to a dissent channel.

A tool reporting the four features will have something to report nearly everywhere, and its output is an accusation: in the hands of whoever holds it, a technical-sounding case that any given opponent is operating fascistically. An instrument built to find the four has produced the second of them. The opposite failure is no safer, since a tool tuned until it reports nothing supplies false assurance and an absence of warnings reads as evidence of health. Better engineering fixes neither. Both are governed by what the tool may output and to whom, which makes them a constraint the specification has to carry.

Unsolved is not which marks would settle the molecular case (the above five answer that), but how anyone outside an institution would observe two of them: procedure waived upward and a practice rewarded in the promotion. Those are facts about personnel that are not typically published.

In AI alignment terms, antifascist design means building systems that enhance democratic values, civil rights, and inclusive solidarity, that refuse to facilitate autocratic practices or prop up an oppressive regime, and that do not themselves display the four features above. Seven strategies follow, and later chapters price them and say which of them this book cannot yet specify: decision procedures that answer to democratic values, privacy and autonomy built in by construction and not left to policy, power and resources distributed instead of concentrated, an explanation a person can actually read, an independent body positioned to audit it, architectures that resist the concentration of the field in a handful of firms, and misuse caught while it is happening.
