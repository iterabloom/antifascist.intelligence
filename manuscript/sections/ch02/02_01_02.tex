\subsection{Antifascist Ethics for AI Alignment}
\label{sec:2.1.2}
Antifascist ethics opposes concentrating power and authority in AI systems and counters the misuse of AI technology by authoritarian actors. I use the word \emph{fascism} because it is honest about the political climate in which I write and what that climate puts at stake — but the target is authoritarian power in every form, and a system built on these principles is anti-authoritarian in full, not only antifascist.

Opposing “concentrated, unaccountable power” gives an engineer nothing to build. It does not say what anyone would look for, what would count as a false positive, or how anyone would know they had got it wrong. So, first, the definition of fascism.

\runin{Fascism as a structural signature}
I take fascism to name a structure rather than a costume. The iconography is not what makes a thing fascist: an institution can display the whole structure with nobody in it who thinks of themselves as anything of the kind, no insignia, and a mission statement that means every word. That is the case this book is about, and a definition that waits for the armband will certify it as healthy. Four features travel together, and each is visible from outside the institution displaying it:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Recuperation of dissent. The institution does not suppress disagreement; it metabolizes it. Refusal is converted into a managed appearance — a permitted outlet, a consultation, a feedback channel — which the institution can then point to as proof that it listens. Debord aimed this warning at his own concept, noting that even denunciation of the spectacle can become a hollow formula that reinforces what it denounces \autocite{debord1967spectacle}. The tell is where what the mechanism carries stops: the channel works, and the institution uses what comes through it, and what has nowhere to go is the judgment that the frame the channel operates inside is the wrong frame. Stated that way it invites the wrong artifact question. \emph{Has the channel ever changed an outcome} is passed daily by every institution this feature is about, because an institution of this kind is not in the business of refusing demands. It decodes the demand and adds an axiom, which is Deleuze and Guattari's account of how a capitalist axiomatic absorbs what is asked of it: the rule set is modified so that the demand can be met, and nothing that necessitated the demand is settled \autocite[Plateau~13]{deleuze1987plateaus}. The question that discriminates is \emph{what did the demand come back as}. A market segment, a metric, a certification, a job title — where a demand returns wearing one of those, an outcome changed and the demand was recuperated, and those are one event and not two.
  \item Aestheticization of the metric. The measure substitutes for the thing measured, and the substitution is experienced as rigor. Walter Benjamin's account of fascism organizing the masses by granting expression in place of rights is the political form of this \autocite{benjamin1935artwork}; the administrative form is a number that collapses two states — e.g., \textit{passes unit tests} and \textit{passes User Acceptance Tests} — into one, so whatever that number mischaracterizes is not refuted or dismissed but simply unnamed. The administrative claim is also a theorem. Simon Zhuang and Dylan Hadfield-Menell prove that where the objective handed to an optimizer has support on only some of the attributes its principal values, there are conditions under which optimizing it indefinitely drives the principal's overall utility arbitrarily low \autocite{zhuang2020consequences}. A world with finite resources supplies those conditions.
  \item Coding of exception as betrayal. Marking a case as one the rule does not fit becomes an act requiring justification, and then an act of disloyalty. The honest statement that a standard does not apply here reads as weakness. It is in fact the irreducible labor of deciding, case by case, what a rule is for.
  \item Acceleration past the rate at which anything can be checked, of which decoupling is the symptom. The institution's representation of reality and reality itself come apart, and the institution defends the representation. The coming-apart has a mechanism and the mechanism is tempo: a representation is corrected by being checked against the world, checking costs time, and an institution whose rate of action outruns the capacity to check it has decoupled by construction rather than by mistake. The other three features protect the gap once it is open; speed is what opens it. This is also the feature with a number attached, section~\ref{sec:5.1.3} making the ratio between the rate at which a system produces candidates and the people available to examine them an auditable property of a deployment. One qualification belongs with it, because it bounds what this feature can find: the acceleration described here is the kind an institution runs into and then defends. In the movements the word \textit{fascism} comes from, the primacy of myth over verifiable fact was also declared outright, asserted as a virtue rather than arrived at by degrees. An instrument watching a rate would not register a party that simply announces the myth.
\end{enumerate}
Those four are testable, because each names an observable artifact: what the demand came back as, whether the metric admits an unmeasured remainder, what happens to the person who files the exception, whether the rate of action has outrun the capacity to check it.

Here is an example of recuperation. In 2018, upholding a travel ban, the Supreme Court repudiated its own 1944 decision permitting the internment of Japanese Americans, in terms that could not be improved on: that decision was “gravely wrong the day it was decided,” had been “overruled in the court of history,” and had “no place in law under the Constitution” — the last phrase quoted from the dissent filed against it at the time, which had warned that a principle a court ratifies “lies about like a loaded weapon, ready for the hand of any authority that can bring forward a plausible claim of an urgent need” \autocite{trump2018hawaii,korematsu1944}. The dissenting justices in 2018 said the majority had redeployed the 1944 logic and replaced one gravely wrong decision with another; the majority said the older case had nothing to do with the one in front of it. The fascistic structure: an objection made against the institution was accepted, canonized, and quoted back with approval by that institution, in the same opinion that let the thing objected to proceed. Ask the artifact question and the case answers it precisely. The demand was that this never be done again. It came back as a formula of repudiation, quotable, and the Court quoted it while permitting the thing again. The objection had become citable, and being citable was the outcome that changed.

That vocabulary answers how a wrong survived its own condemnation, but it does not answer whether the thing was wrong. (I can answer that. The thing was wrong.) The fascistic structure is what stands between such a judgment and any effect. I describe the structure here in mechanical terms because you can write a specification against a mechanism.

We will re-encounter recuperation of dissent later, in two standard machine learning methods: in how a training corpus gets labeled, and in how a model is tuned on human feedback. Both arrangements collect disagreement, put it to work, and are built so that none of it can reach the scheme it disagrees with. Chapter~\ref{sec:7} puts the two instances together and finds a third in the discourse that examines these systems, this book included.

\runin{Two segmentarities}
Deleuze and Guattari's molar and molecular are not two sizes \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}. They are two kinds of segmentarity, and both of them run at every scale. Rigid segmentarity divides in advance: the category exists before the case, the binary is given, and what does not fit is made to fit. Supple segmentarity is what actually moves underneath — a threshold crossed one case at a time, a category widened by the people applying it, a practice that becomes standard without anyone having decided it. A state has both. So does an office, so does a review queue, and so does the person filling out the form. Reading the pair as large and small is the mistake that makes fascism look like something that happens at the top and reaches everywhere else later.

\runin{What makes it fascism and not authoritarianism in general}
The structural signature is shared with ordinary institutional decay, and read on its own it fits every badly run organization on earth. Fascism proper adds features that are more discriminating: mass mobilization, which mere obedience does not require; a leader cult; a scapegoated out-group, which Eco places at the origin — the first appeal is against the intruder \autocite{eco1995urfascism}; contempt for procedural legality as such, so that law is an obstacle to the national will and not a constraint on power; and deniability as technique, in which the movement's violence is disowned at the top while being rewarded at the bottom. Paxton's compressed version: fascism is “a form of political behavior marked by obsessive preoccupation with community decline, humiliation, or victimhood and by compensatory cults of unity, energy, and purity, in which a mass-based party of committed nationalist militants, working in uneasy but effective collaboration with traditional elites, abandons democratic liberties and pursues with redemptive violence and without ethical or legal restraints goals of internal cleansing and external expansion” \autocite{paxton2004anatomy}. That second half is the list above in other words; the mass party, the abandoned liberties and the redemptive violence are the discriminating half, and a definition stopped at \emph{purity} keeps only the mood.

The minimal definition in the fascism studies field is narrower than what I use in this book. Roger Griffin defines fascism as a genus of political ideology whose mythic core is “a palingenetic form of populist ultra-nationalism” — rebirth of the nation out of a decadence the movement diagnoses, carried by a mass and aimed at a people \autocite{griffin1991nature}. Every term in that is a property of a movement. Palingenesis needs a myth of the nation's death and return, populism needs a public, ultranationalism needs a nation, and an escalation ladder inside a benefits agency has none of the three.


\runin{Fascism is not totalitarianism}
The two words get used as though one were the stronger dose of the other, and the difference between them is the one this book runs on. Arendt's subject is the totalitarian state and her unit is the regime \autocite{arendt1963eichmann}. That state is conservative in the strict sense of the word. It wants the bureaucracy to hold, the categories to stay where they were put, the files accurate and the machinery running; its ideal is an apparatus that does not stop, and the horror of it is how well it works.

Fascism's distinguishing mark runs the other way, and Deleuze and Guattari name it a line of abolition \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}. Where a totalitarian state seals every exit, fascism is built on an exit it converts into pure destruction, and the destruction does not stop at the enemy it was raised against. The reading is Paul Virilio's before it is theirs: he called that state suicidal rather than totalitarian, which is the difference between a machine that has failed and a machine doing the thing it was accelerating toward \autocite{virilio1976insecurite}. A regime that orders the wrecking of its own country's infrastructure while it is losing the war is not a bureaucracy failing to hold. It is that line arriving where it was always going. Which is why tempo belongs in the definition rather than among the consequences: a totalitarian state can be slow and remain entirely itself, and a fascist one cannot. Acceleration is not what fascism does once it is established. It is the thing that distinguishes it from every other bad government.
\runin{Resonance}
Micro-fascisms are not miniature regimes and they do not wait for one. The office that converts refusal into a feedback form is doing its own work, on its own account, whether or not a regime ever assembles out of a thousand offices like it. That is Deleuze and Guattari's claim: these things exist first and everywhere — in the couple, the school, the neighborhood, the workplace — each one a small hardening that stands on its own. What a fascist state does is not invent them. It induces accord among them, so that a thousand separate hardenings line up and amplify one another instead of cancelling out. This is also the answer to the objection that the field's minimal definition is about movements and an escalation ladder in a benefits agency is not one. The ladder is not a small movement. It is the thing itself, in the place where it is lived, and a movement is what happens when enough of them are brought into tune. What tuning adds is not only reach but speed: hardenings that reinforce one another stop having to argue with each other, and an institution that has stopped arguing with itself can move as fast as its machinery allows.

The five discriminators above are stated in the vocabulary of a mass movement, and each has a restatement in the vocabulary of an office that ordinary decay does not exhibit. Mass mobilization becomes participation exceeding what the rule requires: staff who volunteer for the disfavored task, elaborate on it, take pride in it. A leader cult becomes personal loyalty overriding role — exceptions granted by proximity to someone rather than by procedure, and a decision reversed because of who asked. The scapegoat becomes an internal category defined so that adverse treatment of it is the category's purpose: the flagged applicant, the difficult claimant, the file everyone knows is handled differently. Contempt for procedural legality becomes procedure enforced downward and waived upward. And deniability keeps its shape: the practice is disowned in the policy and rewarded in the promotion.

That is what separates fascism from decay wherever the reading is done. Decay is an institution failing to do what it says. What the five find is an institution succeeding at something else, with participation, a target, and a story about why the rules do not apply to whoever is applying them. The four features tell you a place is sick; these five tell you what it is sick with. They are harder to observe, since each requires knowing what was rewarded rather than what was written.

These criteria widen what \textit{fascism} covers. On this reading, \textit{antifascist} names the political inheritance of a structural claim: where the analysis comes from, and what it was built to fight. In their mass-movement form the discriminators tell a historian, a prosecutor, or a citizen that a movement has escaped ordinary politics. In the form restated above they are not a “fascism detector” and are not a stage on the way to one. They are a way of reading an institution somebody can name, run by a person who has to say what they looked at. Some of these features are observable in principle, but “concentrated, unaccountable power” as a bulk property is not among them.

That the four features are found nearly everywhere is not a defect in them. It is the finding, and an instrument reporting otherwise would be the broken one. Micro-fascisms are already everywhere, doing their own work, and they were before any of this was built; what the reading is for is not their presence but their accord — whether something is bringing them into tune, and what. The tendency is permanent; what varies is how far it has got in a particular place, and whether it is getting further — section~\ref{sec:9.3.5}'s slope rather than its level, applied to the definition itself and not only to a dissent channel. The reading is an accusation waiting to happen: taken up by whoever wants it, it supplies a technical-sounding case that any given opponent is operating fascistically.

The opposite failure is no safer. A reading pressed until it finds nothing supplies false assurance, and an absence of findings reads as evidence of health. Neither is an engineering problem, and automating the reading would produce both at scale while committing the second feature on this list: a number standing in for the judgment, arrived at by someone looking for exactly that.

Two of the five cannot be observed from outside at all. Whether procedure is waived upward, and whether a practice disowned in the policy is rewarded in the promotion, are facts about what an institution rewarded rather than about what it wrote, and no organization publishes them. That is a limit on the definition and not a gap in it. Where this book runs the five, it runs three of them and reports the other two as undetermined, which is the honest form of the result and the form anyone outside an institution will get. The definition tells you what to look for and, for two of the five, tells you that you will not find it unless you are already inside.
