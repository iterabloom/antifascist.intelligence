\subsection{Antifascist Ethics for AI Alignment}
\label{sec:2.1.2}
Antifascist ethics opposes concentrating power and authority in AI systems and counters the misuse of AI technology by authoritarian actors. I use the word \emph{fascism} because it is honest about the political climate in which I write and what that climate puts at stake — but the target is authoritarian power in every form, and a system built on these principles is anti-authoritarian in full, not only antifascist.

Opposing “concentrated, unaccountable power” gives an engineer nothing to build. It does not say what anyone would look for, what would count as a false positive, or how anyone would know they had got it wrong. So, first, the definition of fascism.

\runin{Fascism as a structural signature}
I take fascism to name a structure rather than a costume. The iconography is not what makes a thing fascist: an institution can display the whole structure with nobody in it who thinks of themselves as anything of the kind, no insignia, and a mission statement that means every word. That is the case this book is about, and a definition that waits for the armband will certify it as healthy. Four features travel together, and each is visible from outside the institution displaying it:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Recuperation of dissent. The institution does not suppress disagreement; it metabolizes it. Refusal is converted into a managed appearance — a permitted outlet, a consultation, a feedback channel — which the institution can then point to as proof that it listens. Debord aimed this warning at his own concept, noting that even denunciation of the spectacle can become a hollow formula that reinforces what it denounces \autocite{debord1967spectacle}. The tell is where what the mechanism carries stops: the channel works, and the institution uses what comes through it, and what has nowhere to go is the judgment that the frame the channel operates inside is the wrong frame.
  \item Aestheticization of the metric. The measure substitutes for the thing measured, and the substitution is experienced as rigor. Walter Benjamin's account of fascism organizing the masses by granting expression in place of rights is the political form of this \autocite{benjamin1935artwork}; the administrative form is a number that collapses two states — e.g., \textit{passes unit tests} and \textit{passes User Acceptance Tests} — into one, so whatever that number mischaracterizes is not refuted or dismissed but simply unnamed. The administrative claim is also a theorem. Simon Zhuang and Dylan Hadfield-Menell prove that where the objective handed to an optimizer has support on only some of the attributes its principal values, there are conditions under which optimizing it indefinitely drives the principal's overall utility arbitrarily low \autocite{zhuang2020consequences}. A world with finite resources supplies those conditions.
  \item Coding of exception as betrayal. Marking a case as one the rule does not fit becomes an act requiring justification, and then an act of disloyalty. The honest statement that a standard does not apply here reads as weakness. It is in fact the irreducible labor of deciding, case by case, what a rule is for.
  \item Decoupling of the model from the world it claims to track. The institution's representation of reality and reality itself come apart, and the institution defends the representation. The other three are what protect the decoupling from correction. One qualification belongs with it, because it bounds what this feature can find: the decoupling described here is the kind an institution drifts into and then defends. In the movements the word \textit{fascism} comes from, the primacy of myth over verifiable fact was also declared outright, asserted as a virtue rather than arrived at by degrees. An instrument tuned to drift might not register a party that suddenly announces it.
\end{enumerate}
Those four are testable, because each names an observable artifact: whether the dissent channel has ever changed an outcome, whether the metric admits an unmeasured remainder, what happens to the person who files the exception, whether the model is ever checked against anything outside itself.

Here is an example of recuperation. In 2018, upholding a travel ban, the Supreme Court repudiated its own 1944 decision permitting the internment of Japanese Americans, in terms that could not be improved on: that decision was “gravely wrong the day it was decided,” had been “overruled in the court of history,” and had “no place in law under the Constitution” — the last phrase quoted from the dissent filed against it at the time, which had warned that a principle a court ratifies “lies about like a loaded weapon, ready for the hand of any authority that can bring forward a plausible claim of an urgent need” \autocite{trump2018hawaii,korematsu1944}. The dissenting justices in 2018 said the majority had redeployed the 1944 logic and replaced one gravely wrong decision with another; the majority said the older case had nothing to do with the one in front of it. The fascistic structure: an objection made against the institution was accepted, canonized, and quoted back with approval by that institution, in the same opinion that let the thing objected to proceed. Ask the artifact question — has the channel ever changed an outcome — and increasingly for the Roberts Court, the answer is no.

That vocabulary answers how a wrong survived its own condemnation, but it does not answer whether the thing was wrong. (I can answer that. The thing was wrong.) The fascistic structure is what stands between such a judgment and any effect. I describe the structure here in mechanical terms because you can write a specification against a mechanism.

We will re-encounter recuperation of dissent later, in two standard machine learning methods: in how a training corpus gets labeled, and in how a model is tuned on human feedback. Both arrangements collect disagreement, put it to work, and are built so that none of it can reach the scheme it disagrees with. Chapter~\ref{sec:7} puts the two instances together and finds a third in the discourse that examines these systems, this book included.

\runin{Two scales}
Deleuze and Guattari's distinction between molar and molecular fascism is why the four features recur at sizes that seem unrelated \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}. Molar fascism is the state, the regime, the concentration camp, the detention facility. Molecular fascism is the office someone commutes to, the form they fill out, and the desire with which they fill it out. At state scale the pattern kills journalists; at organizational scale it turns an engineering decision into scripture, so that the constraint remains, its reason dies, and asking why becomes the deviant act. A definition pitched only at the state will certify the molecular case as healthy.

\runin{What makes it fascism and not authoritarianism in general}
The structural signature is shared with ordinary institutional decay, and read on its own it fits every badly run organization on earth. Fascism proper adds features that are more discriminating: mass mobilization, which mere obedience does not require; a leader cult; a scapegoated out-group, which Eco places at the origin — the first appeal is against the intruder \autocite{eco1995urfascism}; contempt for procedural legality as such, so that law is an obstacle to the national will and not a constraint on power; and deniability as technique, in which the movement's violence is disowned at the top while being rewarded at the bottom. Paxton's compressed version: fascism is “a form of political behavior marked by obsessive preoccupation with community decline, humiliation, or victimhood and by compensatory cults of unity, energy, and purity, in which a mass-based party of committed nationalist militants, working in uneasy but effective collaboration with traditional elites, abandons democratic liberties and pursues with redemptive violence and without ethical or legal restraints goals of internal cleansing and external expansion” \autocite{paxton2004anatomy}. That second half is the list above in other words; the mass party, the abandoned liberties and the redemptive violence are the discriminating half, and a definition stopped at \emph{purity} keeps only the mood.

The minimal definition in the fascism studies field is narrower than what I use in this book. Roger Griffin defines fascism as a genus of political ideology whose mythic core is “a palingenetic form of populist ultra-nationalism” — rebirth of the nation out of a decadence the movement diagnoses, carried by a mass and aimed at a people \autocite{griffin1991nature}. Every term in that is a property of a movement. Palingenesis needs a myth of the nation's death and return, populism needs a public, ultranationalism needs a nation, and an escalation ladder inside a benefits agency has none of the three.

\runin{The scale problem, and what to do about it}
Put the two halves of this section together and they do not fit. Every discriminating feature is a property of a mass political movement: mobilization needs a crowd, a leader cult needs a leader, a scapegoat needs a public. An AI deployment has a form, a threshold, a review queue, an escalation path. It operates at the molecular scale, which appears to leave the four-feature signature alone — and the four-feature signature flags everyone.

Molecular fascism is the thing itself at the size where it is actually lived, and not an early warning of a regime that has yet to assemble. The office that converts refusal into a feedback form does the same work whether or not a regime ever assembles out of a thousand offices like it. Deleuze and Guattari supply the vocabulary [and no authority beyond it] -- WHAT DOES THAT MEAN NO AUTHORITY, so two arguments have to carry that; the first can borrow an authority for its mechanism [and none for its name] -- I DON'T FOLLOW.

The first argument is that the molar case has no mechanism of its own. A regime does nothing except through offices, forms, thresholds, and people who fill them out with some degree of enthusiasm. When a state kills journalists, the killing is authorized on a form, justified in a category, and carried out by someone whose promotion depends on it. What assembling adds is scale, protection from consequence, and the removal of the outside bodies that might have interrupted any single office. If the mechanism at the large scale is the small-scale mechanism repeated and shielded, we've got an ingredient of fascism. The natural objection is that extent matters — a regime that disappears people and an office that loses paperwork are not equivalent. Agreed, although the claim is about kind rather than severity.

That argument is Hannah Arendt's before it is mine, and she supplies it without calling it fascism. Her account of Adolf Eichmann is of a functionary whose part in mass murder ran through schedules, promotions and the vocabulary of his office rather than through conviction, and the phrase she gave it — the banality of evil — is a claim about mechanism at exactly the size this section works at \autocite{arendt1963eichmann}. But she does not call an office fascist. Her subject is totalitarianism and her unit is the regime, so what transfers is the finding that a regime's work is done in an office, and not a license to call the office in and of itself fascist or totalitarian.

The account has since been corrected. Bettina Stangneth, working from interviews Eichmann gave in Argentina that Arendt never saw, describes a committed ideologue who performed thoughtlessness at his trial and was not thoughtless \autocite{stangneth2014eichmann}. Grant that and the man is no longer the man in the phrase. The machinery around him is still the machinery, and what the revision adds is the enthusiasm — participation exceeding what the rule required.

The discriminators are unavailable at the molecular scale only in the form the previous paragraphs stated them; each has a molecular restatement that ordinary decay does not exhibit. Mass mobilization becomes participation exceeding what the rule requires: staff who volunteer for the disfavored task, elaborate on it, take pride in it. A leader cult becomes personal loyalty overriding role — exceptions granted by proximity to someone rather than by procedure, and a decision reversed because of who asked. The scapegoat becomes an internal category defined so that adverse treatment of it is the category's purpose: the flagged applicant, the difficult claimant, the file everyone knows is handled differently. Contempt for procedural legality becomes procedure enforced downward and waived upward. And deniability keeps its shape: the practice is disowned in the policy and rewarded in the promotion.

That is what separates fascism from decay at the size an AI deployment operates at. Decay is an institution failing to do what it says. Molecular fascism is an institution succeeding at something else, with participation, a target, and a story about why the rules do not apply to whoever is applying them. The four features tell you a place is sick; these five tell you what it is sick with. They are harder to observe, since each requires knowing what was rewarded rather than what was written.

These criteria widen what \textit{fascism} covers. On this reading, \textit{antifascist} names the political inheritance of a structural claim: where the analysis comes from, and what it was built to fight. The molar discriminators tell a historian, a prosecutor, or a citizen that a movement has escaped ordinary politics. The molecular discriminators are not a “fascism detector” and are not a stage on the way to one. They are a way of reading an institution somebody can name, run by a person who has to say what they looked at. Some of these features are observable in principle, but “concentrated, unaccountable power” as a bulk property is not among them.

That the four features are found nearly everywhere is expected. The tendency is permanent; what varies is how far it has got in a particular place, and whether it is getting further — section~\ref{sec:9.3.5}'s slope rather than its level, applied to the definition itself and not only to a dissent channel. The reading is an accusation waiting to happen: taken up by whoever wants it, it supplies a technical-sounding case that any given opponent is operating fascistically.

The opposite failure is no safer. A reading pressed until it finds nothing supplies false assurance, and an absence of findings reads as evidence of health. Neither is an engineering problem, and automating the reading would produce both at scale while committing the second feature on this list: a number standing in for the judgment, arrived at by someone looking for exactly that.

Two of the five cannot be observed from outside at all. Whether procedure is waived upward, and whether a practice disowned in the policy is rewarded in the promotion, are facts about what an institution rewarded rather than about what it wrote, and no organization publishes them. That is a limit on the definition and not a gap in it. Where this book runs the five, it runs three of them and reports the other two as undetermined, which is the honest form of the result and the form anyone outside an institution will get. The definition tells you what to look for and, for two of the five, tells you that you will not find it unless you are already inside.
