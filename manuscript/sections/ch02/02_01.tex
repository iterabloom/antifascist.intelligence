\section{Ethical Frameworks and AI Alignment}
\label{sec:2.1}
Which ethical theory an AI system is built on decides what it counts as a good outcome, and the theories on offer disagree with each other in ways that matter for what gets built. The survey below is groundwork rather than argument. Every framework in it is a way of deciding well, and chapter~\ref{sec:3} is about what a system must not do regardless of how the deciding comes out. The two are not rivals. The floor is a small number of things settled in advance, and the frameworks are how everything above it gets settled case by case — which is the shape section~\ref{sec:2.1.1} arrives at, and which nothing here delivers on its own.

Deontology is the awkward case and worth flagging before section~\ref{sec:2.1.1} sets it aside. It is the family that puts unconditional constraints first, which is what chapter~\ref{sec:3} ends up needing, and the standing objection to it — duties conflict, rules run out — is fatal to a complete moral theory and harmless against a floor that decides a deliberately small number of things. That objection is why the hybrid this section gestures at was never given content, and chapter~\ref{sec:3} is where the content goes.
