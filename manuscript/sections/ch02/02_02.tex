\section{Antifascist Ethics for AI Alignment}
\label{sec:antifascist-alignment}
Antifascist ethics opposes concentrating power and authority in AI systems and counters the use of the technology by authoritarian actors. I use the word \emph{fascism} because it is honest about the political climate in which I write and about what that climate puts at stake. The target is wider than the word: authoritarian power in every form, so a system built on these principles is anti-authoritarian in full and not only antifascist.

“Concentrated, unaccountable power” gives an engineer nothing to build. It does not say what anyone would look for, what would count as a false positive, or how anyone would know they had got it wrong. So the first job is a definition, and the word will carry two senses in this book, at two scales. At the scale of an office — a review queue, an annotation pipeline, a targeting cell — fascism names a structure: four operations by which an institution closes itself against what its own decisions left out. At the scale of a movement it names what the historians study, and five further features from that literature are what separate the structure from ordinary decay. The four come first because they can be checked from outside. The five are what a finding would need. The word in the title names the inheritance of that instrument, and not a finding it has returned.

\runin{Four operations on the remainder}
Every intelligence compresses; that is most of what deciding is. What varies is whether what the compression left out — the remainder, in the sense \S\ref{sec:safe-whom} gave the word — can come back and change the answer, or has been filed as noise by the thing it is evidence against. The iconography is not what makes an institution fascist. An institution can run all four operations below with nobody in it who thinks of themselves as anything of the kind, no insignia, and a mission statement that means every word, and a definition that waits for the armband certifies it as healthy. Each of the four is visible from outside the institution displaying it, and each ends in a question about an artifact.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Recuperation of dissent. The institution does not suppress disagreement; it metabolizes it. A demand is met by changing the rules so that it can be met, and the objector is given a channel the institution can then point to as proof that it listens. The question: what did the demand come back as, and who holds it now?
  \item Aestheticization of the metric. The measure stands in for the thing measured, and the substitution is experienced as rigor. Whatever the number mischaracterizes is not refuted; it goes unnamed. The question: does the metric admit an unmeasured remainder?
  \item Coding of exception as betrayal. Saying that the rule does not fit this case becomes an act requiring justification, and then an act of disloyalty. The question: what happens to the person who files the exception?
  \item Acceleration past the rate at which anything can be checked. A representation is corrected by being checked against the world; checking costs time; an institution whose rate of action outruns the capacity to check has come apart from reality by construction, and then defends the representation. The question: has the rate of action outrun the capacity to check it?
\end{enumerate}

Those four are testable because each names an artifact somebody can go and look at. They are one family rather than a sample because each is an operation on the remainder: recuperation builds a channel that carries everything except a judgment about the channel; the metric leaves what it mischaracterizes unnamed; exception-as-betrayal prices the person who names it anyway; acceleration removes the interval in which it could have returned. A fifth direction is not ruled out, and an institution plainly sick while clearing all four would be evidence of one. What the claim buys, if it holds, is a short instrument. Three of the four need more said.

Recuperation is the one most often mistaken for its opposite, because the obvious test for it fails. \emph{Has the channel ever changed an outcome} is passed daily by every institution this feature is about. An institution of this kind is not in the business of refusing demands. It decodes the demand and adds a rule, which is Deleuze and Guattari's account of how a capitalist axiomatic absorbs what is asked of it: the rule set is modified so that the demand can be met, and nothing that necessitated the demand is settled \autocite[Plateau~13]{deleuze1987plateaus}. Debord aimed the same warning at his own concept, noting that even denunciation of the spectacle can become a hollow formula that reinforces what it denounces \autocite{debord1967spectacle}. So the outcome does not sort, and what sorts is what the demand became. A market segment, a metric, a certification, a job title: where a demand returns wearing one of those and the party who made it has no standing to reopen it, an outcome changed and the demand was recuperated, and those are one event. A demand that returns as a rule the objector can invoke again, against the institution, enforced by somebody the institution does not employ, has come back in the same clothes, and that is a transfer of power. The Voting Rights Act's suspension of literacy tests is the instance this book relies on for the pass (\S\ref{sec:subjecthood-scientific}): a demand that returned as a statute, held by the people it was made for against every registrar it reached, and when part of it was taken back forty-eight years later it took a decision of the Supreme Court to do it \autocite{shelby2013}. That is what a transferred power looks like from the far side — something somebody had to take back. Recuperation is the case where nothing has to be taken back, because standing was never given.

The metric has a political form and a theorem. Walter Benjamin's account of fascism organizing the masses by granting them expression in place of rights is the political form \autocite{benjamin1935artwork}; the administrative form is a number that collapses two states — \emph{passes unit tests} and \emph{passes user acceptance tests}, say — into one. Simon Zhuang and Dylan Hadfield-Menell prove that where the objective handed to an optimizer has support on only some of the attributes its principal values, there are conditions under which optimizing it indefinitely drives the principal's overall utility arbitrarily low, and a world with finite resources supplies those conditions \autocite{zhuang2020consequences}. The metric and exception-as-betrayal work the same material from two sides: one leaves the remainder unnamed, the other prices the person who carries it in by hand. An institution running both has closed the measured route and made the human one expensive.

Acceleration is the feature with a number attached. The other three protect the gap once it is open; speed is what opens it, and the ratio between the rate at which a system produces candidates and the people available to examine them is an auditable property of a deployment (\S\ref{sec:rate-act}). Eco's cult of action for action's sake — action before reflection, with thinking treated as a weakness — is the attitudinal form \autocite{eco1995urfascism}; what changes here is that the attitude becomes a rate, and a rate is something somebody can measure. The feature has a limit. The acceleration described here is the kind an institution runs into and then defends. In the movements the word comes from, the primacy of myth over verifiable fact was declared outright, as a virtue, and an instrument watching a rate would not register a party that simply announces the myth.

\runin{An example}
In 2018, upholding a travel ban, the Supreme Court repudiated its own 1944 decision permitting the internment of Japanese Americans, in terms that could not be improved on: that decision was “gravely wrong the day it was decided,” had been “overruled in the court of history,” and had “no place in law under the Constitution” — the last phrase quoted from the dissent filed against it at the time, which had warned that a principle a court ratifies “lies about like a loaded weapon” for any authority with a plausible claim of urgent need \autocite{trump2018hawaii,korematsu1944}. The dissenters in 2018 said the majority had redeployed the 1944 logic; the majority said the older case had nothing to do with the one in front of it. Ask the artifact question. The demand was that this never be done again. What it came back as was a formula of repudiation — quotable, canonical, and available to the Court in the act of permitting the thing again. The ban stood. The objection had become citable, and nothing had to be taken back, because the objectors were never given anything to hold.

That vocabulary says how a wrong survived its own condemnation. It does not say whether the thing was wrong, which it was. What the structure does is stand between a judgment of that kind and any effect, and I describe it in mechanical terms because you can write a specification against a mechanism.

\runin{What the four find, and what sorts it}
On their own the four fit every badly run organization on earth, and some regimes this book does not call fascist. NKVD Order 00447 set regional numerical quotas against a four-month deadline and gave three-man boards several hundred cases in a sitting of about half a day, with the accused absent and undefended \autocite{werth2010nkvd00447}. An instrument reading rate of action against capacity to check flags that as hard as it flags the twenty seconds of the opening chapter. The flag is correct, and the inference somebody will draw from it is not: a hardening of that kind inside a state does not make the state a fascist one, and nothing at the scale of an office was ever going to sort one regime from another.

What separates fascism from the decay the four also find is five further features, and they come from the study of movements: mass mobilization, which obedience does not require; a leader cult; a scapegoated out-group, which Eco puts at the origin \autocite{eco1995urfascism}; contempt for procedural legality as such; and deniability as technique, the violence disowned at the top and rewarded at the bottom. Each has a form at the scale of an office that ordinary decay does not exhibit. Mobilization becomes participation exceeding what the rule requires: staff who volunteer for the disfavored task, elaborate on it, take pride in it. The leader cult becomes personal loyalty overriding role — exceptions granted by proximity to somebody, a decision reversed because of who asked. The scapegoat becomes an internal category defined so that adverse treatment of it is the category's purpose: the flagged applicant, the difficult claimant, the file everyone knows is handled differently. Contempt for procedure becomes procedure enforced downward and waived upward. Deniability keeps its shape: disowned in the policy, rewarded in the promotion. The four find a sick institution; the five find what it is sick with.

The first of the five has arrived with a name. Bettina Stangneth, working from some seventy hours of interviews Eichmann gave in Buenos Aires in 1957, unavailable to Arendt when she reported the trial, found that the clerk who only followed orders was a defense assembled for Jerusalem and contradicted by what the same man said in Argentina with nothing at stake \autocite{stangneth2014eichmann}. That is participation exceeding what the rule required, years after the rule had stopped requiring anything of him.

Now the limits, together, so that the instrument's reach is stated once. The five concern what an institution rewards rather than what it writes, and public records show that only in part. An outsider reaches whichever of them left a document — a wage, a quota, a contract ended early — and reads the rest by inference or not at all. A clearance on the five is therefore a statement about what was observable, and where this book runs them it says which it could reach and reports the rest as undetermined. Neither list sorts a genus. That is done at the scale of a movement, by properties with no office-scale form — Griffin's palingenetic ultranationalism, a myth of the nation's death and rebirth carried by a mass \autocite{griffin1991nature}, and Paxton's mass party of nationalist militants in uneasy collaboration with traditional elites \autocite{paxton2004anatomy} — and this book borrows them only to say what its instrument is not. Nor do the five distinguish among regimes: run on the Great Terror, most of them register \autocite{getty2002excesses}, and what clears it as a genus is the movement material just named, left in its original form. What does separate fascism from a totalitarian state, on Deleuze and Guattari's reading, is a line of abolition: where the totalitarian state seals every exit and wants the machinery to hold, fascism converts an exit into destruction that does not stop at the enemy it was raised against, or at itself \autocite[Plateau~9]{deleuze1987plateaus}. The reading is Virilio's before it is theirs; he called that state suicidal rather than totalitarian \autocite{virilio1976insecurite}. It is a separator at the scale of a regime and nothing at the scale of an office, it is kept apart from the four for that reason, and it is never run on a deployment. Last, the instrument has one result so far, and it is a result about the instrument: run on the machinery that produces these systems, in chapter~\ref{ch:recuperation-dissent}, the five clear every case. A definition tested only on cases it clears has not been shown to sort.

\runin{Resonance}
Micro-fascisms are not miniature regimes and they do not wait for one. That is Deleuze and Guattari's claim: these hardenings exist first and everywhere — in the couple, the school, the workplace, the review queue — each a small closure that stands on its own \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}. Their molar and molecular are not two sizes. They are two kinds of segmentarity, and both run at every scale: rigid segmentarity divides in advance, the category existing before the case, and supple segmentarity is what moves underneath it — a threshold crossed one case at a time, a practice that becomes standard without anyone deciding. A state has both. So does an office, and so does the person filling out the form. Reading the pair as large and small is the mistake that makes fascism look like something that happens at the top and reaches everywhere else later.

What a fascist state does is not invent the hardenings. It induces accord among them, so that a thousand separate closures line up and amplify one another instead of cancelling out. This answers the objection that the historians' definition is about movements and an escalation ladder in a benefits agency is not one. The ladder is not a small movement. It is the thing itself, in the place where it is lived, and a movement is what happens when enough of them are brought into tune. Tuning adds speed as well as reach: hardenings that reinforce one another stop having to argue with each other, and an institution that has stopped arguing with itself can move as fast as its machinery allows.

That is what separates fascism from decay wherever the reading is done. Decay is an institution failing to do what it says. What the five find is an institution succeeding at something else, with participation, a target, and a story about why the rules do not apply to whoever is applying them. The four name the hardenings; the reading asks what is bringing them into accord; and the reader has to show that in the institution at hand, which is harder, because it requires knowing what was rewarded rather than what was written.

\runin{What the instrument is, and what it is not}
On this reading \emph{antifascist} names the political inheritance of a structural claim: where the analysis comes from and what it was built to fight. It is not a property a system has, and the lists above are not a fascism detector or a stage on the way to one. They are a way of reading an institution somebody can name, run by a person who has to say what they looked at. That the hardenings are nearly everywhere is an inheritance from Deleuze and Guattari, not something this reading has established; a proposed tuner can turn out not to do the work attributed to it, and failing to show accord does not give an institution a clean bill of health.

The instrument fails in two directions, and a procedure guards against neither. It is an accusation waiting to happen: taken up by whoever wants it, it supplies a technical-sounding case that any opponent is operating fascistically. And a reading pressed until it finds nothing supplies false assurance, an absence of findings read as health. Automating the reading would produce both at scale, while committing the second feature on its own list — a number standing in for the judgment, arrived at by someone looking for exactly that.

What guards against both is not a procedure. Foucault, prefacing the first volume of the work the micro-fascisms come from, called it an introduction to the non-fascist life, and what he meant was an art of living: not a position somebody takes but something practiced, daily, on oneself \autocite{foucault1977preface}. The operational form of that, for a reading of this kind, is that it gets run on the party holding it — on the machinery that party is standing on — before it is aimed anywhere else. Chapter~\ref{ch:recuperation-dissent} is where this book does it.
