\section{Antifascist Ethics for AI Alignment}
\label{sec:2.2}
Antifascist ethics opposes concentrating power and authority in AI systems and counters the misuse of AI technology by authoritarian actors. I use the word \emph{fascism} because it is honest about the political climate in which I write and what that climate puts at stake — but the target is authoritarian power in every form, and a system built on these principles is anti-authoritarian in full, not only antifascist.

Opposing “concentrated, unaccountable power” gives an engineer nothing to build. It does not say what anyone would look for, what would count as a false positive, or how anyone would know they had got it wrong. So, first, the definition of fascism.

\runin{Fascism as a structural signature}
Every intelligence compresses, and what varies is whether what the compression left out can return and change the answer. Fascism, as this book uses the word, names a structure rather than a costume, and the structure is four operations by which an institution closes itself against that return. The iconography is not what makes a thing fascist: an institution can run all four with nobody in it who thinks of themselves as anything of the kind, no insignia, and a mission statement that means every word, and a definition that waits for the armband certifies it as healthy. The four travel together, each is visible from outside the institution displaying it, and each names what became of the remainder:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Recuperation of dissent. The institution does not suppress disagreement; it metabolizes it. Refusal is converted into a managed appearance — a permitted outlet, a consultation, a feedback channel — which the institution can then point to as proof that it listens. Debord aimed this warning at his own concept, noting that even denunciation of the spectacle can become a hollow formula that reinforces what it denounces \autocite{debord1967spectacle}. The tell is where what the mechanism carries stops: the channel works, and the institution uses what comes through it, and what has nowhere to go is the judgment that the frame the channel operates inside is the wrong frame. Stated that way it invites the wrong artifact question. \emph{Has the channel ever changed an outcome} is passed daily by every institution this feature is about, because an institution of this kind is not in the business of refusing demands. It decodes the demand and adds an axiom, which is Deleuze and Guattari's account of how a capitalist axiomatic absorbs what is asked of it: the rule set is modified so that the demand can be met, and nothing that necessitated the demand is settled \autocite[Plateau~13]{deleuze1987plateaus}. The question that discriminates is \emph{what did the demand come back as}, and then \emph{who holds it}. A market segment, a metric, a certification, a job title: where a demand returns wearing one of those and the party who made it has no standing to reopen it, an outcome changed and the demand was recuperated, and those are one event and not two. The artifact alone does not sort. A demand that returns as a rule the objector can invoke again, against the institution, enforced by somebody the institution does not employ, has come back in the same clothes and is a transfer of power. The Voting Rights Act's suspension of literacy tests is the instance this book relies on for the pass (§\ref{sec:9.3.1}): a demand that returned as a statute, held by the people it was made for against every registrar it reached, and the formula deciding where it applied was struck down forty-eight years later \autocite{shelby2013}, which is what a transferred power looks like from the far side — something somebody had to take back. Recuperation is the case where nothing has to be taken back, because standing was never given.
  \item Aestheticization of the metric. The measure substitutes for the thing measured, and the substitution is experienced as rigor. Walter Benjamin's account of fascism organizing the masses by granting expression in place of rights is the political form of this \autocite{benjamin1935artwork}; the administrative form is a number that collapses two states — e.g., \textit{passes unit tests} and \textit{passes User Acceptance Tests} — into one, so whatever that number mischaracterizes is not refuted or dismissed but simply unnamed. The administrative claim is also a theorem. Simon Zhuang and Dylan Hadfield-Menell prove that where the objective handed to an optimizer has support on only some of the attributes its principal values, there are conditions under which optimizing it indefinitely drives the principal's overall utility arbitrarily low \autocite{zhuang2020consequences}. A world with finite resources supplies those conditions.
  \item Coding of exception as betrayal. Marking a case as one the rule does not fit becomes an act requiring justification, and then an act of disloyalty. The honest statement that a standard does not apply here reads as weakness, when it is in fact the irreducible labor of deciding, case by case, what a rule is for. This feature and the one before it work the same material from two sides. A measure that admits no unmeasured remainder leaves whatever it dropped unnamed; coding exception as betrayal prices the person who names it anyway. The exception-filer is the party carrying the remainder in by hand, and an institution running both features has closed the measured route and made the human one expensive.
  \item Acceleration past the rate at which anything can be checked, of which decoupling is the symptom. The institution's representation of reality and reality itself come apart, and the institution defends the representation. The coming-apart has a mechanism and the mechanism is tempo: a representation is corrected by being checked against the world, checking costs time, and an institution whose rate of action outruns the capacity to check it has decoupled by construction rather than by mistake. The other three features protect the gap once it is open; speed is what opens it. This is also the feature with a number attached: the ratio between the rate at which a system produces candidates and the people available to examine them is an auditable property of a deployment. This feature has a limit: the acceleration described here is the kind an institution runs into and then defends. In the movements the word \textit{fascism} comes from, the primacy of myth over verifiable fact was also declared outright, asserted as a virtue rather than arrived at by degrees. An instrument watching a rate would not register a party that simply announces the myth.
\end{enumerate}

Those four are testable, because each names an observable artifact: what the demand came back as, whether the metric admits an unmeasured remainder, what happens to the person who files the exception, whether the rate of action has outrun the capacity to check it.

Testability explains why each one can be checked and not why there are four. An institution has an enormous number of properties, and a reading obliged to examine all of them would be no reading at all. The claim carried by the list is that micro-fascisms are not spread evenly across that space: they run along a few directions, and these four are those directions rather than four items drawn from a longer inventory. What makes them one family is that each is an operation on the remainder. Recuperation builds a channel that carries everything except a judgment about the channel. The metric leaves whatever it mischaracterizes unnamed. Exception-as-betrayal prices the person who names it anyway. And acceleration removes the interval in which a remainder could have returned at all, since a representation is corrected by being checked against the world and checking takes time.

Nothing here establishes that claim and I have no way to establish it. It is what the list asserts rather than what the list has shown, and a fifth direction is not ruled out — an institution plainly sick while clearing all four would be evidence of one. What the claim buys is the possibility of a short instrument. Were the tendency spread across every dimension an institution has, no four features would find it, and this list would be a sample rather than a set.

Here is an example of recuperation. In 2018, upholding a travel ban, the Supreme Court repudiated its own 1944 decision permitting the internment of Japanese Americans, in terms that could not be improved on: that decision was “gravely wrong the day it was decided,” had been “overruled in the court of history,” and had “no place in law under the Constitution” — the last phrase quoted from the dissent filed against it at the time, which had warned that a principle a court ratifies “lies about like a loaded weapon, ready for the hand of any authority that can bring forward a plausible claim of an urgent need” \autocite{trump2018hawaii,korematsu1944}. The dissenting justices in 2018 said the majority had redeployed the 1944 logic and replaced one gravely wrong decision with another; the majority said the older case had nothing to do with the one in front of it. The fascistic structure: an objection made against the institution was accepted, canonized, and quoted back with approval by that institution, in the same opinion that let the thing objected to proceed. Ask the artifact question. The demand was that this never be done again, and what it came back as is a formula of repudiation: quotable, canonical, and available to the Court in the act of permitting the thing again. The objection had become citable. Nothing about the outcome moved — the ban stood — and increasingly for the Roberts Court that is the pattern, which is why the older form of this question, whether the channel ever changed an outcome, gets the right answer here for the wrong reason. It happens to be no. Where an institution has the room to add an axiom instead, it will be yes, and the recuperation will be the same.

That vocabulary answers how a wrong survived its own condemnation, but it does not answer whether the thing was wrong. (I can answer that. The thing was wrong.) The fascistic structure is what stands between such a judgment and any effect. I describe the structure here in mechanical terms because you can write a specification against a mechanism.

\runin{Two segmentarities}
Deleuze and Guattari's molar and molecular are not two sizes \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}. They are two kinds of segmentarity, and both of them run at every scale. Rigid segmentarity divides in advance: the category exists before the case, the binary is given, and what does not fit is made to fit. Supple segmentarity is what actually moves underneath — a threshold crossed one case at a time, a category widened by the people applying it, a practice that becomes standard without anyone having decided it. A state has both. So does an office, so does a review queue, and so does the person filling out the form. Reading the pair as large and small is the mistake that makes fascism look like something that happens at the top and reaches everywhere else later.

\runin{What the four find, and what sorts it}
The structural signature is shared with ordinary institutional decay, and read on its own it fits every badly run organization on earth. It also fits regimes this book does not call fascist. NKVD Order 00447 set regional numerical quotas against a four-month deadline and gave three-man boards several hundred cases in a sitting of about half a day, with the accused absent and undefended \autocite{werth2010nkvd00447}, and an instrument reading rate of action against capacity to check flags that as hard as it flags the twenty seconds of the opening chapter. The flag is correct and the inference somebody will draw from it is not. A hardening of that kind inside a state does not make the state a fascist one, and nothing operating at the scale of an office was ever going to sort one regime from another.

What separates fascism from the decay the four also find is five further features, and they come from the study of movements: mass mobilization, which obedience does not require; a leader cult; a scapegoated out-group, which Eco puts at the origin \autocite{eco1995urfascism}; contempt for procedural legality as such; and deniability as technique, the violence disowned at the top and rewarded at the bottom. Each has a restatement at the scale of an office that ordinary decay does not exhibit. Mass mobilization becomes participation exceeding what the rule requires: staff who volunteer for the disfavored task, elaborate on it, take pride in it. A leader cult becomes personal loyalty overriding role — exceptions granted by proximity to someone rather than by procedure, and a decision reversed because of who asked. The scapegoat becomes an internal category defined so that adverse treatment of it is the category's purpose: the flagged applicant, the difficult claimant, the file everyone knows is handled differently. Contempt for procedural legality becomes procedure enforced downward and waived upward. And deniability keeps its shape: the practice is disowned in the policy and rewarded in the promotion. The four find a sick institution; the five find what it is sick with.

The five concern what an institution rewards in practice rather than what it writes, and public records disclose that only in part. An outsider reaches whichever of them happened to leave a document behind — a wage, a quota, a contract ended early — and reads the rest by inference or not at all. That is a limit on the definition and not a gap in it. The four leave documents and find decay everywhere; the five sort, and two of them leave none. A clearance on the five is a statement about what was observable and not about the institution, and a negative result carries only as much information as the record it was run on. Where this book runs the five it says which ones it could reach and reports the rest as undetermined, which is the honest form of the result and the form anyone outside an institution will get.

Tested, the instrument has one result, and it is a result about the instrument. Run on the machinery that produces these systems — the annotation pipeline, the feedback pipeline, and the discourse this book belongs to — the five clear every case, and the recuperation chapter is where they are run. Every office-scale case they have been run on has cleared, and the cases the definition catches are regimes and movements, read in the vocabulary borrowed unrestated. A definition tested only on cases it clears has not been shown to sort. What the list has is a form that could sort a case, and not yet a case it sorted; the word in the title names the inheritance of that form and not a finding it has returned.

Neither list sorts a genus. That is done at the scale of a movement, by properties with no office-scale form: Griffin's palingenetic ultranationalism, a myth of the nation's death and return carried by a mass and aimed at a people \autocite{griffin1991nature}, and Paxton's mass party of nationalist militants in uneasy collaboration with traditional elites \autocite{paxton2004anatomy}. This book borrows them for one purpose, to say what its instrument is not, and never runs them on anything.

The restatements do not distinguish among regimes either. Run them on the Great Terror and most of them register: the denunciation campaigns are participation exceeding what the rule required, the leader cult needs no argument, Order 00447 enumerates a category whose adverse treatment is the category's purpose, and the 1938 turn against excesses committed on instruction has deniability's shape \autocite{getty2002excesses}. What clears it as a genus is on neither list, because it does not exist at the scale of an office. It is the movement material of the paragraph above, left in its original form.


\runin{Fascism is not totalitarianism}
The two words get used as though one were the stronger dose of the other. The contrast drawn here is Deleuze and Guattari's, taken from Virilio, and it is a reading and not an agreed taxonomy \autocite[Plateau~9]{deleuze1987plateaus}: on it a totalitarian state is conservative in the strict sense of the word, wanting the bureaucracy to hold, the categories to stay where they were put, the files accurate and the machinery running, and the horror of it is how well it works. Arendt's own account of totalitarianism runs the other way, a movement in perpetual motion that dissolves stable institutions rather than preserving them \autocite{arendt1951origins}, and her Eichmann is borrowed below for the figure and not for the theory. Arendt's Eichmann is the figure such a state is supposed to produce, taking part in mass murder through schedules, promotions and the vocabulary of his office rather than through conviction \autocite{arendt1963eichmann}.

Bettina Stangneth disputes that portrait. Working from some seventy hours of interviews Eichmann gave the journalist Willem Sassen in Buenos Aires in 1957, unavailable to Arendt when she reported the trial, she finds that the low-grade clerk who only followed orders was a defense assembled for Jerusalem and contradicted by what the same man had said in Argentina with nothing at stake \autocite{stangneth2014eichmann}. This evidence weakens an explanation of Eichmann's conduct based on unthinking compliance. Set against the five, it is the first of them arriving with a name. Participation exceeding what the rule requires is what the Argentine material shows, years after the rule had stopped requiring anything of him at all. The four features find a structure whether or not anybody intends it, which is why this book never has to establish that anyone did; the five find what a party did more of than it had to, and Eichmann is the case where somebody eventually made that observation, out of material that had been sitting in a box since 1957.

Fascism's distinguishing mark runs the other way, and unlike the four features it is a fact about a regime rather than about an office. Deleuze and Guattari name it a line of abolition \autocite{deleuze1987plateaus}. Where a totalitarian state seals every exit, fascism is built on an exit it converts into pure destruction, and the destruction does not stop at the enemy it was raised against. The reading is Paul Virilio's before it is theirs: he called that state suicidal rather than totalitarian, which is the difference between a machine that has failed and a machine doing the thing it was accelerating toward \autocite{virilio1976insecurite}. A regime that orders the wrecking of its own country's infrastructure while it is losing the war is not a bureaucracy failing to hold. It is that line arriving where it was always going. Not every demolition in a retreat is that. Denying resources to an advancing enemy in order to go on fighting is an operation with a war still inside it, and the order of 19 March 1945 presents itself as exactly that: the nation's struggle for existence requires every means, within Reich territory included, to weaken the enemy's fighting power. The reasoning that makes it the other thing is not in the document. It is in the memorandum Speer sent ten days later, quoting back the philosophy he had been given — that the nation had shown itself the weaker one and the future belonged to the stronger nation in the east \autocite{ghdi1945nerodecree}. So the distinction is in the direction of travel rather than in the stated reason, and this instance is a caution about the instrument: run on the order alone it sorts the wrong way, and what sorts it correctly is a sentence somebody wrote down that the order was careful not to contain. The mechanism that matters here is the reduction of judgment to a component's prescribed operation. Acceleration can do that by making action outrun scrutiny. The dehumanizing relation is the object of the argument; it does not require a claim that every fascism must move at a particular speed. The canon has this in attitudinal form. Eco's cult of action for action's sake is action taken before or without reflection, with thinking treated as a weakness \autocite{eco1995urfascism}. What changes here is that the attitude becomes a rate: the reason reflection does not happen is that the institution is running faster than reflection takes, and a rate is something somebody can measure. What the contrast buys is a separator at the scale of a regime and nothing at the scale of an office. It is kept apart from the four for that reason, and the reading below never runs it on a deployment.
\runin{Resonance}
Micro-fascisms are not miniature regimes and they do not wait for one. The office that converts refusal into a feedback form is doing its own work, on its own account, whether or not a regime ever assembles out of a thousand offices like it. That is Deleuze and Guattari's claim: these things exist first and everywhere — in the couple, the school, the neighborhood, the workplace — each one a small hardening that stands on its own. What a fascist state does is not invent them. It induces accord among them, so that a thousand separate hardenings line up and amplify one another instead of cancelling out. This is also the answer to the objection that the field's minimal definition is about movements and an escalation ladder in a benefits agency is not one. The ladder is not a small movement. It is the thing itself, in the place where it is lived, and a movement is what happens when enough of them are brought into tune. What tuning adds is not only reach but speed: hardenings that reinforce one another stop having to argue with each other, and an institution that has stopped arguing with itself can move as fast as its machinery allows.

That is what separates fascism from decay wherever the reading is done. Decay is an institution failing to do what it says. What the five find is an institution succeeding at something else, with participation, a target, and a story about why the rules do not apply to whoever is applying them. The four name the hardenings; the reading asks what brings them into accord. The five ask what the institution rewards, whom it targets, and how it exempts those doing the targeting. The reader has to show those connections in the institution at hand. They are harder to observe, since each requires knowing what was rewarded rather than what was written.

These criteria widen what \textit{fascism} covers. On this reading, \textit{antifascist} names the political inheritance of a structural claim: where the analysis comes from, and what it was built to fight. In their mass-movement form the discriminators tell a historian, a prosecutor, or a citizen that a movement has escaped ordinary politics. In the form restated above they are not a “fascism detector” and are not a stage on the way to one. They are a way of reading an institution somebody can name, run by a person who has to say what they looked at. Some of these features are observable in principle, but “concentrated, unaccountable power” as a bulk property is not among them.

That the hardenings are nearly everywhere is an inheritance I take from Deleuze and Guattari; this reading has not established it. A proposed tuner can turn out not to do the work attributed to it, and failing to show accord does not give the institution a clean bill of health. Micro-fascisms are already everywhere, doing their own work, and they were before any of this was built; what the reading is for is not their presence but their accord — whether something is bringing them into tune, and what. The tendency is permanent; what varies is how far it has got in a particular place, and whether it is getting further. The reading is an accusation waiting to happen: taken up by whoever wants it, it supplies a technical-sounding case that any given opponent is operating fascistically.

The opposite failure is no safer. A reading pressed until it finds nothing supplies false assurance, and an absence of findings reads as evidence of health. Neither is an engineering problem, and automating the reading would produce both at scale while committing the second feature on this list: a number standing in for the judgment, arrived at by someone looking for exactly that.

Both failures have one discipline against them and it is not a procedure. What Foucault offered under the name of a non-fascist life was an art of living, and an art of living is not a position somebody takes but something practiced, daily, on oneself \autocite{foucault1977preface}. The operational form of that, for a reading of the kind described here, is that it gets run on the party holding it — on the machinery that party is standing on — before it is aimed anywhere else.
