2.2. Empathy, Social Intelligence, and Theory of Mind
Empathy and social intelligence shape the interaction-oriented side of AI: the capacity, alongside a working theory of mind, to give a system insight into human emotions, needs, and social dynamics. Section 2.2.1 complicates the first term in that list — empathy, felt or simulated, is not the load-bearing capacity this chapter's argument turns out to need, and compassion, a separable system, does more of the work. What follows here still matters: an AI system has to read emotional states accurately before anything productive can be built on top of that reading, however the "on top of" ends up working.

An AI system's empathy, in the sense available to it, is the capacity to process human emotional input, generate a response calibrated to it, and behave in ways that support the interaction. AI systems already detect and interpret human emotional states from facial expression, vocal modulation, text sentiment, and physiological signal — the province of affective computing.

Social intelligence is the complement: not just registering an emotion but navigating it inside the social structures, norms, and relationships it occurs in. Building it into an AI system means the system has to grasp social norms, understand roles and relationships, and adapt as the social context shifts.

Theory of mind (ToM) — attributing beliefs, intentions, desires, and knowledge to oneself and others, and recognizing that others' perspectives can differ from one's own — underlies both. For an AI system, this means modeling a user's perspective, predicting their likely responses and preferences, and adjusting behavior accordingly.

Developing an AI system with these capacities, genuinely rather than as a surface performance, is difficult and ethically consequential. Done honestly — including honesty about which of these capacities is doing the load-bearing work — it is worth pursuing anyway: it builds systems whose interactions with people are more trustworthy, not merely more fluent.
