\section{Emotional Intelligence and Self-awareness}
\label{sec:2.3}
Emotional intelligence, in a human, is the ability to perceive, understand, and manage one's own emotions and to do the same for others'. Built into an AI system, the same term covers a narrower and more mechanical set of capacities: interpreting emotional signal, producing a calibrated response, and — the harder addition — maintaining a self-model that the system can check and correct rather than an inner state taken on faith. What "emotion" is even a claim about is unsettled in the underlying science, which is where section 2.3.1 starts. Section 2.3.2 covers affective computing specifically: how a system detects and responds to emotional signal in practice. Section 2.3.3 covers the self-awareness half — why a system's self-model is a model, checkable and revisable, not a homunculus watching from inside.

An emotionally responsive AI system that recognizes a user's frustration and adjusts its communication style is doing something narrower than "feeling for" the user, and worth building anyway, because the narrower claim is one that can be verified and corrected when it goes wrong.
