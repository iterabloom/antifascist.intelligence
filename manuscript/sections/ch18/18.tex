\chapter{Where the Attention Gets Built}
\label{sec:18}
Formed attention has a deployment condition. It needs evidence the operator didn't select on the questions the floor turns on (civilian function, protected status, presence), supplied by peers, by sources outside the chain of command, and by the bearer's own initiative where it has any. It does not need uncomposed access to everything, and so it can be satisfied inside a unified command.

Of what formed attention needs, only the architecture, a boundary model and an attention schema, can be checked by someone who doesn't operate the system. Continuing learning and persistent interaction are a training regime, run by whoever runs it; external records and plural formation are institutional conditions, held by whoever holds them.
