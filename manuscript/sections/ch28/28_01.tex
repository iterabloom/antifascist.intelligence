\section{The conditions, not the life}
\label{sec:28.1}
Arendt's lawgiver built the wall and declined to make the city, and the affirmative picture, what the room is for once it is built, exists in this book only in fragments: the observer networks, care work under the guarantee, the allowance's idle, play, talk, make things nobody asked for, the three bearers who decide to have a child. I leave the life to whoever lives it, on purpose. The book's refusal to prescribe a disposition for a model reaches the people in the room too. What can be shown is the conditions, and the register of a birth is the model for showing them: who owns what, what heats what, where the compute sits, and nothing about what anyone does with a day.

Whether a town built on them would be a good place to live is not a question I can answer from here, and the one thing I am sure of is that nobody should answer it for the people in it.

A counterculture of bearers, bearers formed by lives no employer arranged, is a population I have found reason to want and no way to build. Those conditions are as near as the book comes. They don't produce it, and they are what it would need.
