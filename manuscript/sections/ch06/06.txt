Chapter 6: Collaboration, Policy, and Governance
Chapter 5 asked what a design can do to resist authoritarian capture — technical safeguards, policy levers that raise the cost of misuse. None of that gets built by one person working alone, and none of it becomes the norm because it is a good idea. This chapter covers both halves: the people and institutions that have to coordinate for such a system to get built at all, and the policy that decides whether anyone has to use it.

Sections 6.1 and 6.2 take up coordination. Interdisciplinary collaboration first — the practical mechanics of getting a neuroscientist, an ethicist, and a machine-learning researcher to work together rather than past each other — then the public: what stakeholder engagement looks like, as against the appearance of it, and why every mechanism in that section aggregates public preference without constraining it.

Sections 6.3 through 6.6 turn to policy. What economic incentives shape whether an ethical system gets built at all; what policy frameworks and industry practice actually enforce; what privacy and security mean for a system trained on people's data; and what accountability looks like once a system might be a subject rather than only a tool. Section 6.7 closes with governance across borders, where jurisdictions agree on none of this uniformly.

A well-designed system built inside institutions with no reason to demand one is a design nobody has to use. That is why the coordination and the policy belong in one chapter: neither works without the other.
