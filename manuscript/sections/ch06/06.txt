Chapter 6: Collaborative Research and Development
Chapter 5 asked what a design can do to resist authoritarian capture — technical safeguards, policy levers that raise the cost of misuse. None of that gets built by one person working alone. This chapter is about the people and institutions that have to coordinate for it to happen at all: researchers across disciplines who do not start out sharing a vocabulary, a public whose consent the work depends on but rarely has, research communities and governments that agree on a handful of shared standards and disagree on almost everything else, and the different regional rules any of this has to operate inside.

Section 6.1 takes up interdisciplinary collaboration — the practical mechanics of getting a neuroscientist, an ethicist, and a machine-learning researcher to work together rather than past each other. Section 6.2 turns to the public: what stakeholder engagement looks like, as against the appearance of it. Section 6.3 covers cooperation between researchers and institutions across borders, including the technical standards the engineering community has built on its own, distinct from the treaty-and-declaration landscape chapter 7 covers. Section 6.4 closes with what happens where those norms meet a specific jurisdiction's law, which is rarely the same law twice.
