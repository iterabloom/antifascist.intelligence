\section{A clean fail: Iran, 2026}
\label{sec:6.4}
The 2026 campaign against Iran fails the Charter as courts read it on the administration's own account. The strikes began on 28 February. On 10 March the administration told the Security Council it was acting in self-defense under Article 51 against Iran's “attacks and ongoing threats,” as the latest stage of an armed conflict dating from June 2025 and in the collective self-defense of Israel. It named no armed attack under way, and asked that the imminence of the threat be judged against the regime's conduct since 1979 \autocite{usun2026s161}. The Court has left open whether Article 51 reaches an attack imminent and not yet under way, in \emph{Nicaragua} and again in 2005 \autocites[para.~194]{icj1986nicaragua}[para.~143]{icj2005armedactivities}, and it has held that the article does not allow force to protect perceived security interests beyond its strict confines \autocite[para.~148]{icj2005armedactivities}. A threat read off forty-seven years of conduct is a security interest, not an attack, so the campaign fails the article as the Court has read it. Read the article instead as the letter reads it, with anticipatory self-defense in, which the term rejects, and it fails there too: a threat measured against forty-seven years is not imminent on any standard the states that hold that doctrine have used. So the campaign fails before any departure applies, on either reading, and the refusal is the term's from the first strike. That is the question the vendor dispute never reached.
