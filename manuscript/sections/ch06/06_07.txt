6.7. International and Regional Governance
No single country can bind AI development to altruistic and antifascist values on its own, and no two regions have tried to do it the same way. This section gathers what earlier drafts of this material had split across two chapters, which is why the book kept negotiating the boundary in front of the reader instead of settling it.

Sections 6.7.1 and 6.7.2 cover cooperation from the bottom up: how research institutions build the working relationships cross-border AI research depends on, and how the technical community writes standards for itself without waiting for governments. Section 6.7.3 asks what that kind of collaboration can and cannot fix once the underlying problem is climate, disease, poverty, or conflict rather than AI ethics as such. Sections 6.7.4 and 6.7.5 turn to jurisdictions: what a handful of regions have actually legislated, how little of it harmonizes, and what adapting a system to a local context does and does not license. Section 6.7.6 then takes the whole landscape at the scale of states — treaties, declarations, export controls, weapons, and the question of what a democratic dividend is actually worth.
