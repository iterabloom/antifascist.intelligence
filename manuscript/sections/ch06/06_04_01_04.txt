6.4.1.4. Canada
The Pan-Canadian Artificial Intelligence Strategy, launched by the Canadian Institute for Advanced Research (CIFAR) in 2017, represents a key initiative in Canada's AI development landscape. Aimed at fostering AI innovation, cultivating talent, and ensuring that AI advancements benefit all Canadians, the strategy seeks to establish Canada as a global leader in AI research and development. By promoting interdisciplinary collaboration among AI researchers, ethicists, policymakers, and stakeholders, the strategy directly addresses AI ethics and value alignment.

The Montreal Declaration for Responsible AI, drafted in 2018, provides another significant effort to address AI ethics in Canada. This declaration outlines a set of ethical guidelines for the development and deployment of AI systems, emphasizing human rights, well-being, autonomy, privacy, and social solidarity. With widespread support from academic institutions, industry partners, and government organizations both in Canada and internationally, the declaration contributes to shaping ethical AI practices.

In terms of specific examples, the University of Toronto's Centre for Ethics established the Ethics of AI Lab. This initiative brings together interdisciplinary researchers to address the ethical, legal, and societal implications of AI technologies. The lab fosters collaboration between computer scientists, ethicists, social scientists, and other scholars to develop ethical AI frameworks and policies relevant to the Canadian context.

Additionally, the Canadian government implemented the Directive on Automated Decision-Making, setting out guidelines for the responsible use of AI in federal government decision-making processes. This directive emphasizes transparency, accountability, and human rights in the deployment of AI systems. It requires government departments and agencies to assess the potential risks and benefits of using AI in their decision-making processes and to ensure that AI systems are designed and implemented with respect for human rights, privacy, and fairness.

By examining the experiences of Canada and Singapore, we can better appreciate the importance of coherent, collaborative AI governance at the regional level. Both countries endorse interdisciplinary collaboration and emphasize the need for public access to information about AI development. Through their respective regulatory frameworks and initiatives, these countries are shaping an ethical AI agenda that promotes responsible, human-centric AI development.
