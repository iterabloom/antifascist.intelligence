\section{What the Mechanism Requires}
\label{sec:mechanism-requires}
This section argues one thing, and it is smaller than its length suggests. Any mechanism that satisfies the three marks carries a signal with two properties that the standard functional accounts of affect also require, and, on conditions stated below, five more. That does not show that a substitute feels anything. The properties were read off affect's function before they were read off a refusal's, so the resemblance holds partly by construction, and inheriting a name is not evidence that anything answering to it is present. What it shows is that a construction adopted in order to avoid affective states cannot be certified to have avoided their functional profile, and so does not escape the question the next section asks. The routing claim, that an affective bearer should not be built first, survives that. Nothing stronger is claimed here.

Human reasoning does not present a clean division between cognition and affect: work on epistemic feelings distinguishes them from emotions of success and failure while still measuring them as affective states \autocite{pekrun2016measuring}.

Some such states concern the inference; others concern what its conclusion would do to somebody. \emph{But this would hurt her} is not simply a low score attached after deliberation. It can interrupt the inference and send the reasoner back over it. Where the response disappears when an operator redescribes the situation, the operator may still supply its anchor. Where it survives adversarial redescription because the harm remains, the concern is anchored elsewhere.

Each property is reached the same way: take a mark, or the deployment the marks are applied in, or a failure the marks have to survive; ask what the mechanism would have to do; name the property the signal must have for it to do that. The first four are what this book calls a \emph{live signal}, in the electrical sense and no other.

The resemblance is thin. A loss function generalizes and is commensurable, so the pair on its own does not separate the specification from ordinary optimization. What the route claims beyond resemblance rests on the conditional pair and on the native-currency test of §\ref{sec:embodied-others}, and nowhere else.

Two notes on the second property. The marks require that a loss and a cost be represented on one signed scale; the striving chapter's currency is the proposal for what that scale is, and whether the system's own states have to be denominated on it is that test, on which everything the route claims beyond this section rests. And the scale is not the floor's verdict but a reading taken at three places, the cost of holding, the residue after defeat, and the drafting of which decompositions the floor refuses; inside the floor the loss is lexical, and a system that weighed the floor itself on the scale would have converted a floor into a policy.

The step from property six to a felt aversion is Spinoza's identification of a decrease represented with a decrease felt \autocite[III P11 Schol.]{spinoza1985collected}, which this section cannot assume without assuming its conclusion. The seventh is one of two things \emph{held as its own} can mean; the other, that an outcome is bad \emph{for} the system, is not required by the marks and is the next section's subject.

Two things about that profile are worth noticing. The four properties the marks require first are the standard functional accounts of what affect is for: Herbert Simon's emotion as an interrupt that reorders a serial processor \autocite{simon1967motivational}, Michel Cabanac's pleasure as the common currency in which incommensurable needs are compared \autocite{cabanac1992pleasure}, and valence as the evaluative system subsumed by unlimited associative learning, which Ginsburg and Jablonka make the marker of minimal consciousness \autocite{ginsburg2019evolution}. The derivation from the marks recapitulates that literature, which is why the derived set reads as a description of affect: the properties were read off affect's function before anybody read them off a refusal's. That fixes what the resemblance can show. A specification that overlaps a functional definition of affect inherits the name by construction, and inheriting a name is not evidence that anything answering to it is present. The derivation establishes that a substitute does not escape the question. What would carry the route past that point is the commensurability property alone — whether a system with the currency natively weighs another party's loss better than one that models it — and that is the property the research agenda tests and the rest of the
derivation does not reach. And the triple derived second is the profile of a particular state. Available across processing, directed against the act, organized around the system's own authorship is the shape of guilt, or of aversion to one's own act, and not the shape of pain or fear, which are organized around what happens to the system. It is the state chapter~2 found predicting repair and the floor
chapter's residue presupposes, and it is the state the formation
chapters warn is a handle when its weight is set by the operator. So the two constructions preferred here are constructions that do not deliberately require an affective bearer, rather than constructions shown to produce none: Replacement orders attempts by deliberateness.
