\section{Robustness, Generalization, and Adaptability in AI Systems}
\label{sec:6.2}
A system trained under one set of conditions will eventually meet situations its training never anticipated — new users, new adversaries, an environment that has simply moved on since the training data was collected. Robustness is what keeps its ethical behavior intact across that gap, and a system whose empathetic and altruistic dispositions hold only within the narrow envelope of its training data is not yet safe to deploy: the same brittleness that produces a wrong classification on an unfamiliar image can produce a wrong moral judgment on an unfamiliar situation, with less warning and higher stakes.

A system has to adapt to a changing environment without its commitments adapting along with it, and these are not two settings of one dial. Erosion by adaptation is how an ethical constraint disappears without anyone deciding to remove it: each individual update is defensible against the case in front of it, and the sequence goes somewhere nobody chose. That is accommodation: the party that has drifted does not experience a discontinuity, but itself, with revised views. The floor is the structural answer, and what a floor costs is the cost of holding one against the party doing the updating.

The engineering against the ordinary case is standard and worth naming only to say what it does not reach: adversarial training, data augmentation, domain randomization, ensembles, regularization \autocite{szegedy2013intriguing,goodfellow2014explaining,tobin2017domain}. Each closes one route by which a system fails outside its training conditions, and none addresses the general fact of being outside them — a distinction that gets lost when robustness is reported as a single number. All of it also assumes an environment that shifts without intending to. None of it reaches the case of a state that does not have to move the environment at all, because it can compel the people who hold the system.
