6.3.4. Addressing Workforce Disruption
AI systems that automate entire job categories disrupt work at a scale most labor-market institutions were never built to absorb. Section 6.3.3 traces where that is already showing up and makes the case for a federal jobs guarantee as the floor beneath everything else. What sits on top of that floor is a shorter list than the policy literature suggests, because several of the usual items turn out to be versions of one another.

Retraining and lifelong learning, publicly funded and available at every career stage, including to workers whose employers invest nothing in them, is the standard first answer. Section 8.3.2 covers what the evidence says about how retraining performs on its own, and it is discouraging enough that retraining belongs alongside an income floor and not in place of one.

The floor itself has two candidate forms, and choosing between them is a moral question before it is an economic one. Finland's basic income experiment paid 2,000 unemployed people an unconditional monthly stipend and found modestly higher life satisfaction and lower psychological strain among recipients, with no significant negative effect on employment [[cite:C0386]]. That disposes of the standard objection to unconditional transfers, which is that they stop people working. I would still put a jobs guarantee ahead of a basic income, on a ground the experiment cannot settle: people ought to have the chance to contribute before anyone decides they are unemployable.

The remaining measures are real and smaller. Broadened social insurance — unemployment benefits, healthcare, affordable housing — protects people who cannot or choose not to work through a period of displacement. Designing work so that AI systems augment human judgment instead of substituting for it produces roles needing both, which neither full automation nor unassisted labor generates on its own. Giving smaller firms access to AI resources and expertise keeps employment from concentrating in step with the capability, the dynamic section 6.3.2 describes. Each of these keeps the floor from being the only thing holding.
