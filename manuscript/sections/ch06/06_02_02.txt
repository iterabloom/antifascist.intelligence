6.2.2. Public Deliberation and Participatory AI Governance
In this section, we present several examples of how public deliberation and participatory AI governance can be facilitated, emphasizing the importance of diverse stakeholder engagement and decision-making processes that incorporate societal values and concerns.

(1) Citizen Assemblies: By funding citizen assemblies, governments can foster informed public input on AI ethics and policies. These assemblies, composed of diverse individuals selected through a randomized, stratified sampling process, enable citizens to engage in facilitated discussions and produce recommendations for AI policy and ethical guidelines. The Citizens' Assembly in Ireland, which has convened to deliberate on complex issues such as abortion and climate change, serves as an example of a model that can be applied to AI development.

(2) Public-Private Partnerships: Collaborative initiatives between government agencies, AI developers, and civil society organizations can facilitate the sharing of knowledge, resources, and best practices to address AI ethics and governance challenges. An example of this is the Partnership on AI, a coalition of tech companies, academic institutions, and non-governmental organizations committed to studying and promoting best practices for AI development that align with human values and societal interests.

(3) Online Platforms for Public Input: Online platforms that enable citizens to provide feedback on AI policy proposals, ethical guidelines, and deployment strategies can help policymakers and developers better understand public concerns and priorities, thus ensuring AI systems are designed and deployed in a manner that respects societal values. The European Commission, for instance, has utilized online public consultations to gather citizen input on topics such as AI ethics and data protection.

(4) AI Ethics Committees: Independent AI ethics committees, comprising diverse stakeholders such as ethicists, researchers, practitioners, and community representatives, can provide valuable guidance on ethical considerations in AI development. These committees can review AI projects, recommend ethical improvements, and raise concerns about potential negative impacts. A real-world example is DeepMind's Ethics Advisory Panel, which advises the company on the ethical implications of its AI research and applications while maintaining financial independence from the organization to ensure objectivity.

(5) Public Awareness Campaigns and AI Literacy Programs: To foster informed public deliberation on AI ethics and governance, governments, educational institutions, and civil society organizations can develop public awareness campaigns and AI literacy programs. These initiatives aim to help citizens better understand the implications of AI development and empower them to engage in meaningful discussions about AI ethics and policy. For example, Finland's national AI strategy includes a program called "Elements of AI," which offers free online courses on AI basics and aims to educate 1% of the Finnish population on AI and its societal implications.

Fostering public deliberation and participatory AI governance requires a multifaceted approach that includes citizen assemblies, public-private partnerships, online platforms for public input, AI ethics committees, and public awareness campaigns. By engaging diverse stakeholders and incorporating societal values and concerns into the decision-making process, we can develop AI systems that are not only ethical and altruistic but also promote democratic values and resist authoritarian tendencies.
