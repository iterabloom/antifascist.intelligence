6.4.1.1. European Union (EU)
The European Union (EU) is pushing boundaries in the construction of comprehensive legislation and regulation for the use of AI systems that emphasize transparency, openness, and human oversight. Central to the EU's approach to AI governance is a commitment to fundamental human rights and meticulous management of the specific risks associated with high-risk AI systems.

One of the pivotal organizational structures driving ethical AI within the EU is the High-Level Expert Group on AI. This collective of experts from diverse sectors and academic disciplines has outlined Ethical Guidelines for AI, a foundational text for ethical AI development in the region. These guidelines enshrine key principles including the preservation of human agency and autonomy, harm prevention, fairness, and explicability. Through inclusive and multidisciplinary collaboration, the High-Level Expert Group on AI is driving a unified stance on ethical AI within the EU.

Moreover, the EU's General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) provides a robust framework to govern data processing operations in AI applications. GDPR is pivotal in assuring privacy and granting individuals control over their personal data, with principles such as transparency, fairness, and data minimization offerring clear guidance to AI practitioners in the sensitive area of personal data management.

The EU's commitment to ethical AI is further demonstrated in the newly proposed AI-specific legal framework by the European Commission. This legislation extends beyond existing regulations and addresses several key factors:

(1) High-risk AI systems: The new proposal presents a set of legally binding requirements for high-risk AI systems, including biometric identification technology and systems used in critical infrastructue domains. Designed to guarantee the safety and fundamental rights of individuals, these requirements encompass measures such as risk assessment, stringent data governance, and conformity assessment procedures.

(2) Human oversight: The proposal underscores the vital role of human involvement in AI decision-making processes. AI systems are expected to maintain a suitable level of human intervention, ensuring that technology remains subservient to human control and preserving individuals' rights.

(3) Transparency: In an endeavour to enhance public understanding of AI systems, the proposed framework stipulates transparency regulations. These necessitate AI developers providing accessible, comprehensible information about how the AI system operates, its intended purpose, and potential limitations.

These advanced regulatory measures taken by the EU are setting new international standards in the integration of ethical principles and anti-authoritarian values in AI development and deployment. Next steps should concentrate on promoting cross-border research collaborations in AI, disseminating best practices for altruistic and anti-authoritarian AI development, and tailoring regional initiatives to accommodate local contexts, while preserving the focus on ethical AI that actively challenges authoritarianism.
