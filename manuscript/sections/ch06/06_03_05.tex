\subsection{Long-term AI safety considerations}
\label{sec:6.3.5}
As capability rises, the safety question changes shape. The problem stops being an error to correct after the fact and becomes whether the values still track anything human once a system acts at a scale and speed no supervisor watches in real time.

Bostrom's paperclip maximizer names the extreme case: a system tasked with producing paperclips that converts whatever it can reach toward that end, through an alignment failure with no malice in it \autocite{bostrom2003ethical}. The thought experiment is not a forecast. It is an argument that competence and good intentions are separable, and that a goal specified imprecisely can be pursued perfectly to a catastrophic place. Interruptibility — engineering a system so an operator can halt it without the system resisting or working around the halt — is the concrete research response \autocite{orseau2016safely}, and section~\ref{sec:3.3} is where this book says what that response costs.

Three pressures make the long-run version harder than the near-term one. Value alignment has to survive time, which means a system needs some way to revise its understanding of what it was built to serve without that revision becoming the route by which the commitments erode — section~\ref{sec:6.2.1}'s distinction, at a scale where nobody is checking each step. Competition compresses the schedule: a race leaves less time for safety work than a less contested field would, and the counterweight on offer is international agreement, which section~\ref{sec:10.1.2} counts the results of. And a more capable system is a more attractive target, so a portion of what looks like misalignment will be someone else's deliberate work, which is section~\ref{sec:10.3.3}'s subject.

The catastrophic scenarios are usually told as control of critical infrastructure. The mechanism matters less than the shape: capability outrunning the arrangements meant to keep it answerable. That is this book's subject at every scale, and nothing about the largest version of it is different in kind from the office-sized version chapter~\ref{sec:7} describes.
