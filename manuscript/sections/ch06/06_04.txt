6.4. Developing Policy Frameworks for AI Ethics, Safety, and Antifascism
Good design inside a single lab does not by itself keep an AI system ethical or resistant to authoritarian misuse. That takes policy: legislation, industry self-regulation, joint public-private funding, and oversight, each covering ground the others cannot reach on their own. National guidelines are the subject of section 6.4.1, self-regulation of 6.4.2, public-private research partnerships of 6.4.3, the personhood question of 6.4.4, and international cooperation of 6.4.5.

Legislation sets the floor that does not depend on a company volunteering for it: obligations on developers and users that can require the same kind of ethics review or independent audit that biomedical research already answers to, applied instead to AI development.

Self-regulation covers what legislation is slow to reach. The Partnership on AI, whose corporate members include Amazon, Apple, and Google, commits its members to "tenets" of safety, transparency, and broad benefit — voluntarily, and without legal force behind the commitment [[cite:C0348]]. What that kind of voluntary commitment is worth is the question section 6.4.2 takes up.

Joint funding addresses a narrower problem: research on AI safety and ethics funded solely by the companies whose products it evaluates concentrates the funding and the findings in the same hands. Splitting that funding between government and industry keeps the incentive to find a problem from depending on who is paying to look for it.

None of this holds up without oversight: a body positioned like an institutional review board but built for AI, interdisciplinary and resourced enough to act on what it finds rather than only report it. What such a body specifically has to guard against — mass surveillance, censorship, and the other uses that turn a working system into an instrument of authoritarian control — section 4.6.3 already covers, and section 5.4.3 covers the export-control and prohibited-practices levers that keep a capability from reaching a government that would misuse it. Legislation, self-regulation, funding, and oversight are each partial by themselves; what the rest of this section works out is the combination.
