6.1.3. Developing Shared Platforms and Tools for an AI Research Ecosystem
Funding and forums get researchers talking to each other. Shared infrastructure lets them actually work on the same material — the same dataset, the same codebase, the same evaluation suite — which is a different and harder problem.

Some of this already exists. AI Ethics Lab, founded in 2017 by philosopher Cansu Canca, is a standing practice hub where computer scientists, legal scholars, and philosophers work through specific AI-ethics problems together, rather than publishing separate takes on the same question [[cite:C0460]]. The AI Alignment Forum, built in 2018 by the team behind LessWrong to replace a narrower forum the Machine Intelligence Research Institute had maintained, does the same work for technical alignment research specifically, giving researchers a shared place to post work in progress rather than wait for a conference cycle [[cite:C0461]]. Section 4.5.3 covers a third piece of this same infrastructure: the Hugging Face Transformers library, and what open-source tooling does for reproducibility across labs.

What is missing is the equivalent for evaluation. A shared benchmark that measured ethical reasoning, empathetic response, and altruistic behavior the way existing benchmarks measure raw task performance would let separate labs' claims about a system's ethical behavior actually be compared, rather than taken on the strength of whichever paper described them best.

Section 6.1.2 covers what a competition built around a shared real-world problem, rather than a leaderboard metric, can do toward that same goal.
