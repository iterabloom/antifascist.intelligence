6.4.1.9. Saudi Arabia
Saudi Arabia has been actively advancing the development and adoption of artificial intelligence, striving to incorporate ethical considerations into its burgeoning AI ecosystem. The National Center for Artificial Intelligence (NCAI) and the National Information Center (NIC), under the guidance of CEO Majid Altuwaijri, lead efforts to establish ethical frameworks and guidelines for AI development within the country.

A prominent example of Saudi Arabia's commitment to ethical AI research and development is the NCAI's emphasis on generating AI systems that align with human values, adhere to ethical principles, and promote social good. As a result, partnerships with international institutions, such as OpenAI and the Partnership on AI, have been established with the self-reported goal of responsible and collaborative AI development.

In an effort to contribute to global discussions on AI ethics and safety, Saudi Arabia actively participates in international conferences and workshops. The Saudi Data and AI Authority (SDAIA) oversees AI and data-related policies, regulations, and initiatives within the country, and has engaged in international events, such as the Global AI Summit, to exchange knowledge and foster best practices in ethical AI development.

Recognizing the importance of AI and its ethical implications, Saudi Arabia's Vision 2030, a strategic framework aimed at diversifying the economy and developing public service sectors, has highlighted AI. The government has initiated the development of a national AI strategy that will include guidelines and principles for AI ethics and safety, emphasizing transparency, accountability, and privacy.

Saudi Arabia also acknowledges the importance of interdisciplinary collaboration between AI developers, ethicists, and other stakeholders, investing in AI education and workforce development. The establishment of the Saudi AI Academy exemplifies this commitment, aiming to train and develop AI talent within the country.

Collaborative research projects with local and international counterparts have been launched by the NCAI. For example, in partnership with the King Abdullah University of Science and Technology (KAUST), a project has been initiated to develop AI systems capable of recognizing and responding to human emotions.
