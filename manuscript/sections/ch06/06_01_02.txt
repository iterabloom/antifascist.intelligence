6.1.2. Establishing Joint Research Initiatives and Programs
Joint research initiatives turn interdisciplinary collaboration from an event into an institution — a standing lab, a competition, a grant program that outlasts whoever wrote the first proposal.

Academic institutes do this by giving interdisciplinary AI-ethics work a permanent home inside a university rather than a one-off grant. Stanford's Institute for Human-Centered AI, launched in 2019 and co-directed by computer scientist Fei-Fei Li and philosopher John Etchemendy, is one example [[cite:C0456]]. The Ethics and Governance of Artificial Intelligence Initiative, launched in 2017 by MIT's Media Lab and Harvard's Berkman Klein Center together, is another — built explicitly as a joint project between the two institutions rather than housed in just one [[cite:C0457]].

Competitions do similar work by giving an interdisciplinary team a shared, real-world problem to solve together rather than a shared committee to sit on — the IBM Watson AI XPRIZE (section 4.5.3) being the clearest example of the structure working as designed.

Funding programs are the more direct lever, and the more specific ones matter more than the general ones. The National Science Foundation's Fairness in Artificial Intelligence program, run jointly with Amazon since 2019, funds research specifically at the intersection of computer science and the social-science work of defining what "fair" means for a given system [[cite:C0458]]. On a larger scale, the European Commission's SHERPA project, funded under Horizon 2020 from 2018 to 2022, put eleven partner organizations across six countries — spanning academia, industry, civil society, and standards bodies — to work on the ethical and human-rights dimensions of AI and big-data systems together [[cite:C0459]].

Joint programs built explicitly around cross-border, government-to-government coordination are a related but distinct mechanism, covered in section 6.7.1. What belongs here is the funding and institutional structure that makes interdisciplinary work possible in the first place, wherever it happens.
