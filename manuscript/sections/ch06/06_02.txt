6.2. Stakeholder Involvement and Public Engagement
The co-construction of ethical and anti-authoritarian superintelligence calls for deliberate involvement of the broader society as active stakeholders in the AI discourse. To democratize AI development, it is critical to advocate for public participation and engagement that encompasses different sections of society, including authorities, AI developers, end-users, marginalized communities, and civil society organizations. This approach ensures the tech is democratically shaped to cater to various societal values and needs, thereby preventing the monopolization of knowledge and power by a few stakeholders. 

AI's socio-political implications necessitate regulatory overseers, who carry decisions about usage parameters, the legal rights concerning data and privacy, and accountability. Simultaneously, developers and researchers in the AI arena, deeply involved in creating the technology, are masters of understanding its opportunities and potential pitfalls. They need to collaboratively integrate socio-political, ethical, and technology constraints with an interdisciplinary mindset.

End-users, often unnoticed stakeholders, are central to the evolution of AI. Their diverse needs, preferences, and constraints define the usability and accessibility of AI. Involving end-users is crucial in preventing bias, designing usability, and promoting the extensive reach of the technology. 

Underrepresented communities often bear the brunt of the digital divide. Their inclusion is pertinent to prevent stratified advancements and leverage AI as a tool for social equality. They offer unique insights into how AI can be shaped to cater to a wider range of contexts and conditions, extending the technology's benefits to marginalized sections.

Civil society organizations play a significant role in advocating for ethical norms, maintaining checks and balances, and amplifying unheard voices. Their involvement in AI discourse often brings forward pertinent socio-political questions that lie in the blind spots of technologists. 

To ensure diverse stakeholder representation, one vital approach might involve the creation of AI juries, inspired by the concept of 'citizen juries' that allow non-expert citizens to deliberate over important societal topics. AI juries would explore technologically feasible solutions that could then feed into political and regulation-determining processes. An example of this is Taiwan's 'vTaiwan' initiative that actively involves the public in the formation of digital policies. 

Further, promoting AI literacy is of paramount importance. The Dutch nation-wide public initiative 'Nederland Digitaal' that aims at empowering citizens through digital literacy is a practical example of this. 

The development of anti-authoritarian and altruistic superintelligence needs to be a democratic endeavor ensuring representation, participation, and consultation across society.
