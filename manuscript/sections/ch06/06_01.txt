6.1. Interdisciplinary Collaboration and Knowledge Exchange
Building benevolent superintelligent AI takes more than one field. Machine learning sets what a system can do; cognitive science and moral psychology describe what actually tracking human welfare would require of it; ethics asks whether the result is good. None of these fields gets the system right by itself, and how they combine matters as much as what each contributes on its own.

Funding structured around that convergence — a grant that requires a machine-learning lab and an ethics or psychology department to work the same problem rather than adjacent ones — does something a single discipline's normal grant cycle does not: it puts people who would not otherwise share a budget, a dataset, or a deadline into the same project. Section 6.1.2 covers what that funding has looked like in practice.

Workshops and conferences that gather a broad spectrum of experts do similar work on a shorter cycle, giving ideas, strategies, and failure modes a room to be argued over rather than published past each other in separate journals. Stanford's One Hundred Year Study on Artificial Intelligence, launched in 2014 with a gift from computer scientist Eric Horvitz and Mary Horvitz, is a working example: rather than a single report, it convenes a new standing panel roughly every five years to assess where AI's advance has actually changed a specific set of human concerns, from healthcare to employment [[cite:C0451]].

Open access to research resources, methodologies, and findings works on a different axis: instead of putting people in the same room, it removes the reason not to build on someone else's work. The Partnership on AI, covered in more detail in section 7.5, is built on that premise at industry scale — that best practice develops faster in the open than behind a dozen labs' separate closed doors.

Sections 6.1.1 through 6.1.3 take up three of these mechanisms in turn: cross-disciplinary communication and cooperation, joint research programs, and shared platforms and tools.
