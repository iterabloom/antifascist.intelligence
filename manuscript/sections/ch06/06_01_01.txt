6.1.1. Cross-disciplinary Communication and Cooperation
Cross-disciplinary communication matters only where it actually happens: a conference, a lab, a classroom, a shared codebase. Where it happens determines how much of it survives contact with each discipline's own incentives.

A dedicated venue solves the easier half of the problem. A conference convened specifically on AI's ethical and societal questions gathers the people already persuaded the questions matter, and its proceedings are read by those same people. The harder trick is siting the work where the unpersuaded already are. The Workshop on Machine Learning Safety has run inside NeurIPS, a general machine-learning venue, rather than beside it as a meeting of its own [[cite:C0453]]. The siting is the substance: it puts alignment work in front of researchers who would not have registered for a separate event, and submits that work to an audience holding no prior commitment to finding it important.

A joint research center does something a standing committee cannot, for a related reason. The Center for Human-Compatible AI at UC Berkeley pairs AI researchers with cognitive scientists and philosophers inside a single institution's budget [[cite:C0454]]. A center has to make hiring decisions, and every hire is a claim about which combination of fields the problem actually requires. A committee is never forced to make that claim, which is why its membership tends to record which departments wanted a seat.
