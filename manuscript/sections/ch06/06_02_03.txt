6.2.3. AI Literacy Outside the Classroom

Section 7.1.5 covers how AI literacy gets built into schools, universities, and dedicated public courses, Finland's Elements of AI among them. What is different here is bringing AI into the places people already are — a museum visit, a policy debate, a town hall — rather than a course somebody has to seek out.

<<list>>
1. Public engagement on specific policy questions. The Ada Lovelace Institute has run public engagement on facial recognition specifically, including a Citizens' Biometrics Council that brought 50 members of the UK public together to deliberate on how biometric technology should be regulated, and published the first national survey of UK public opinion on the technology [[cite:C0477]]. Section 6.2.2 covers the citizen-assembly format this borrows from.

2. Museum and science-center exhibitions. The MIT Museum's permanent "Robots and Beyond" exhibition, opened in 2022, puts 50 years of MIT's AI and robotics research in front of a visitor who will never take a computer science course, tracing the field from early robots like Kismet and Cog through work happening at the Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory today [[cite:C0478]].

3. Direct public outreach. Researchers and practitioners visiting schools, community centers, and local events to answer questions and explain what an AI system actually does reaches people neither a curriculum nor a museum exhibit does on its own.
<</list>>
