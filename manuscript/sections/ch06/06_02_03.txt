6.2.3. AI Literacy Outside the Classroom

Most AI literacy work assumes someone who has already decided to learn: a course to enrol in, a curriculum to sit through, a public MOOC to find (section 6.3.5). That reaches the people who came looking. The harder and more useful problem is reaching everyone else — bringing AI into the places people already are, a museum visit, a policy debate, a town hall, where nobody had to seek it out.

<<list>>
1. Public engagement on specific policy questions. The Ada Lovelace Institute has run public engagement on facial recognition specifically, including a Citizens' Biometrics Council that brought 50 members of the UK public together to deliberate on how biometric technology should be regulated, and published the first national survey of UK public opinion on the technology [[cite:C0477]].

2. Museum and science-center exhibitions. The MIT Museum's permanent "Robots and Beyond" exhibition, opened in 2022, puts 50 years of MIT's AI and robotics research in front of a visitor who will never take a computer science course, tracing the field from early robots like Kismet and Cog through work happening at the Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory today [[cite:C0478]].

3. Direct public outreach. Researchers and practitioners visiting schools, community centers, and local events to answer questions and explain what an AI system actually does reaches people neither a curriculum nor a museum exhibit does on its own.
<</list>>
