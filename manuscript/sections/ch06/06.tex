\chapter{What Arrives With the Capability}
\label{ch:route}
\standing{This chapter and the standing chapter argue claims three to
five. Nothing in the two chapters before it depends on what is argued
here.}
One fact governs everything in this chapter. A bearer needs a rationale carried forward, an earlier commitment held as its own, and costs represented before they arrive, for reasons that owe nothing to its welfare, and those are the first half of Cassell's definition of suffering: a self extended in time. The deepest harm this book describes is not one a bearer might drift into. It arrives with the structure the specification requires, of a substitute as much as of a bearer, and what stays open is only whether the threatened dissolution of that self registers. This chapter prices that.
