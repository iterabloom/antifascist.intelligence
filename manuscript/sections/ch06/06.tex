\chapter{Whether to Take Part}
\label{sec:6}
The vendor dispute never reached one question: whether to license a model into a use of force at all. This chapter is the floor's answer, and two cases show it before the rule does. Since 2014 Ukraine has been repelling a Russian attack: a European court found Russia in effective control of the separatist east from that May, and in 2022 the General Assembly deplored the invasion as aggression. A model may take part in that defense. On 28 February 2026 the United States began bombing Iran, and told the Security Council it was acting against “attacks and ongoing threats” without naming an attack under way. A model may not take part in that. What separates them is whether the force repels an armed attack actually under way, and who says so. The rest of the chapter states the rule and its exceptions, and then returns to the two cases with the record.
