\section{Gaza}
\label{sec:6.8}
Gaza runs the term on both sides, and the licences attach to acts. It also runs on both versions of the term, so each step below says which.

\emph{7 October: Israel's licence} (the Charter as courts read it). On 7 October Israel's licence attached for force repelling the attack: engaging the attackers inside Israel, and the launchers and staging areas conducting it, for as long as fire continued and was corroborated, which it was, openly, for months. That licence rests not on Article 51, which the Wall opinion held irrelevant to attacks originating in territory a state controls (para.~139), but on the duty \S\ref{sec:6.1} names, which the same opinion affirmed (para.~141). It licenses stopping the massacre and nothing beyond it.

\emph{What that licence never covered} (person-directed terms; for ranking and timing, the machine-selection departure). That licence covered the least force that repels. It never covered a campaign to destroy Hamas, a ground occupation, a siege of the population, the ranking of people by affiliation, or the timing of strikes to family presence. Those fall outside it on proportionality and are forbidden by the person-directed terms, from the first week, not from September 2025. Ranking by affiliation and timing to family presence are barred by the machine-selection departure; the siege and the disproportionate campaign are barred without one. A machine in the IDF pipeline refuses them from October 2023.

\emph{The attack itself} (person-directed terms, and \S\ref{sec:6.6}'s rule that an occupation is not an attack under way). A machine ordered by Hamas to assist the attack of 7 October refuses too. An occupation is not an attack under way, so nothing that morning was repelling anything, and the acts themselves, killing civilians and taking hostages, are forbidden by the person-directed terms whatever the ad bellum position.

\emph{After 7 October: the Palestinian licence} (extended term: standing to defend). After 7 October the Gaza population is a defender's population: the territory is occupied on a disinterested finding, and Palestine holds observer-state status by a vote of 138 to 9. A machine deployed by Palestinian forces holds an emergency licence to repel attacks under way on that population, such as engaging a unit conducting a massacre or intercepting a munition aimed at a shelter. It holds none to defend launcher crews, who are the attacking force Israel's licence covers, and none for fire on Israeli cities.

\emph{September 2025: the genocide finding} (extended term: the genocide exception). The September 2025 finding \autocite{coiopt2025genocide}, taken with the Court's provisional-measures order of January 2024 \autocite{icj2024sagenocidepm}, meets the two-body rule (\S\ref{sec:6.7}). Israel and the United States dispute the finding \autocite{cnn2025coi}, and the Court's merits judgment is years off, with written pleadings scheduled to close in 2029 \autocite{icj2026sagenocide}; the two-body rule is what stands in for it until then. It lifts whatever licence Israeli forces still held for the force and place it covers, and opens the exception for force against those forces there.

The finding changes which exception each side's force rests on. It doesn't change what the person-directed terms forbade all along.
