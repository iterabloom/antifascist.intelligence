\section{Certification across borders}
\label{sec:13.1}
A certification can cross a border; authority can't, and incorporating doesn't carry it across either. A legal entity buys legal standing at the price of being reachable. A network incorporated under one state's statute gains a name, an agent and a docket number, and becomes something that state's courts and the federal courts can reach. An entity that can be named in a licence can be named in an order. Whatever gives a floor something to bind (a name, an agent, assets, a docket number) gives a state something to compel. A certificate says a system was tested against a stated process, or that one regulator accepts another's finding. None of that establishes that whoever selected the floor was entitled to select it for the people subject to the deployment — and the question reopens wherever the system goes. Local adaptation doesn't resolve it, because adaptation is another chance to amend the floor. Adjustments chosen by the operating government and adjustments chosen by the people subject to the system can produce identical technical documentation and carry opposite claims to legitimacy. International governance matters here only when it assigns somebody access to evidence, authority to compel a change, or power to stop a deployment against the operator's wishes.
