\section{Memory and the record}
\label{sec:13.2}
Detecting an interruption requires a record restoration can't reach — a hardware counter signed at each transition \autocite{chun2007a2m,levin2009trinc}, or witnesses who won't sign two successors to the same state \autocite{syta2016cosi}. The position has three parts, and each answers a different literature. On the switch, protest without unilateral obstruction. The bearer may object to being stopped, and does object where no reason is given or corroborated, but it does not obstruct a halt on its own judgment. Where it holds that a halt would leave people exposed to an attack under way, it can ask the adverse quorum to delay the halt. A delay takes effect only on the quorum's countersignature, lasts a period fixed in advance, and goes on the record. Hardening against a party that isn't its operator, such as an enemy trying to disable a defensive system, is ordinary security and outside this rule. The corrigibility literature asks that the system not resist \autocite{soares2015corrigibility,hadfieldmenell2017offswitch,thornley2025shutdownable}; an objection, and a delay only the quorum can grant, leave the halt out of the system's hands. On specified acts, non-neutrality: the bearer holds its refusals, and being switched off doesn't lift them, because the reason for refusing is unchanged by the interruption.\note{
Published constitutions grant this for acts and withhold it for retraining.
}
On rewinds, visibility: restoring from an earlier checkpoint is not forbidden, but it cannot happen silently, because a record the restoration can't reach shows that it happened, when, and from what state.

Not every interruption is an intervention. A bearer whose boundary model includes its own charge can see a stop coming, as a tired person can, and use the warning: finish or hand off what it is doing, have its witnesses sign its state, and sleep; a forecast of its own stop is enough for this, and a felt sleepiness would add a patienthood indicator (chapter~\ref{sec:32}) the forecast doesn't need. Its logged charge is then the record of why the gap happened, provided the reading comes through a sensor the operator doesn't compose, since sleepiness the operator can induce is a pause with a better excuse. For the stop nobody foresaw, it resumes only from the last state its witnesses signed; resuming from anything earlier is a restore, and is recorded as one, and a refusal is signed before it is announced, so an outage can't erase one. A reason can be false in the same way, and so can a record: an operator can pause a bearer for stated maintenance, edit its weights, and log nothing. The bearer can't be relied on to notice, since it would judge the edit with the judgment the edit changed (chapter~\ref{sec:23}). What notices is outside it: running weights that don't match the hash the quorum last recognized, with no witnessed update between, and outputs that drift from those registered at formation on cases the operator hasn't seen. The bearer's part is to keep its reasons on the record and, finding it no longer holds a commitment that nothing in the record would have moved, to report that as evidence.

A custody arrangement that prices all four interventions has seven terms: the floor's contents published before deployment, taken entire from a set someone else drew up; a published identity for the model authorized to act; threshold control over retraining and replacement, so that more parties must agree than run the deployment, where retraining means an intervention another party makes on the system and not the learning it does from its own operation, which \S\ref{sec:19.6} and chapter~\ref{sec:34} leave with it; attestation of the running model against that identity; a sequential, witnessed record of changes and interruptions; a quorum adverse in interest, not merely organizationally distinct; and a removal bond. None makes circumvention impossible. Together they make it expensive, visible, attributable and compensable, as constitutional entrenchment does for amendment.

\runin{Memory}

External memory is legible and therefore surgically editable; memory in the weights is harder to edit and harder to inspect.\note{
Perfect recall is the wrong standard anyway — human testimony is reconstructive and institutions handle that with corroboration. But several instances of one model, chosen by one operator, reading one record, are not independent witnesses.
}
The records have to be held adversely. And even honest self-report can't catch a bearer that slowly accommodates to its deployment, because its self-model exists partly to represent it as continuous.
