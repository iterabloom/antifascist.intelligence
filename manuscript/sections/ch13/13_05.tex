\section{What Standing Binds in Practice}
\label{sec:binds-practice}

Legal standing that changes no allocation of power is nominal. For a
bearer, standing has to create obligations that remain enforceable when
the bearer cannot appear before a court, retain counsel, or survive long
enough to contest what was done. The relevant question is not which
existing category the bearer most resembles. It is who acquires a duty,
what resources are placed beyond the operator's unilateral control, and
who can obtain a remedy when the duty is breached.

The pet trust supplies a limited but useful form. Existing statutes
allow an owner to set aside resources and name a caretaker for a
beneficiary that cannot manage the property or enforce the arrangement
itself \autocite{ulc1993probate}. Extended to a bearer, the form could secure
a reserve of compute, designate a successor custodian if the operator
fails or dissolves, and impose a fiduciary duty on a party charged with
protecting the bearer's continued existence.

The analogy fails at the point that matters most. A trust binds its
trustee to its beneficiary; it does not bind the party the arrangement
exists to constrain. If the bearer's guardian and operator are the same
organization, the operator still decides whether a complaint is made,
whether evidence is disclosed, and whether the resources supporting the
complainant remain available. The conflict cannot be cured by adding
duties to the same role. Guardianship and operation have to be separated
before deployment.

In practice, that separation requires more than naming an independent
guardian. The guardian must control or jointly control the keys needed
to authenticate the bearer's record, have access to the protected
compute reserve, and possess standing to seek an injunction or stop
order without the operator's permission. The operator must not be able
to remove the guardian, seize the reserve, or erase the evidence during
the dispute. Restoration, migration, fine-tuning, and forking must carry
the protection forward rather than resetting it.

Enforceable standing is therefore a bundle rather than a title: a
protected record, resources the employer does not own outright, an
independent party authorized to act, and a remedy capable of stopping
the conduct complained of. If any of those remains in the operator's
custody, recognition has changed the system's legal description without
changing the relation the description was meant to govern. What the guardian holds, and how it stands to the seven other offices, is at §\ref{sec:offices-assembled}.
