\chapter*{Glossary}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Glossary}
\unnumberedlabel{sec:13}{17}
\backmattermark{Glossary}
These entries define terms as this book uses them. Every one is either the book's own working definition or a word the book narrows from its ordinary sense, and the entry says which. Terms that carry their standard technical meaning are not here; a reader wanting those should look them up elsewhere. Each entry points to the section where the term is developed, and is a reference rather than a substitute for reading it.

\runin{Affective concern}
An outcome registering as mattering to a system, as against being ranked higher by it. Mattering is minimally felt: there is something it is like for the outcome to be bad (§\ref{sec:3.2}). The fourth of the four capacities section~\ref{sec:2.3} separates, and the only one of them that bears on whether a system cares (§\ref{sec:2.3.2}, §\ref{sec:3.4}); section~\ref{sec:3.2} places it as the shallower of the two things a refuser can be.

\runin{Annealing}
The account of durability the floor rests on (§\ref{sec:3.1}). Adaptation and conservation are a continuum rather than two settings, and training moves along it; a floor is what has cooled out of a history of encounters, so its durability is the cost of reheating it rather than the strength of something installed. The temperature schedule is the design. A system held permanently at temperature has no floor to abandon, its adherence being modulation rather than commitment.

\runin{Antifascist (as this book uses it)}
Opposing the concentration of power and authority in AI systems and countering the technology's use by authoritarian actors. The word names the narrower and more charged case, and is used because it is honest about the political climate the book was written in. The target is wider than the word: authoritarian power in every form, so a deployment built on these principles is anti-authoritarian in full and not only antifascist. It is not a property a system has. The word names something being done — a floor held, on an occasion, against a party with the power to remove it — and the features it is defined against are features of cultures, so it is done between parties or not at all (§\ref{sec:2.2},
§\ref{sec:3.8a}, §\ref{sec:3.8}).

\runin{Authoritarian misuse, six forms}
The book's own taxonomy of how AI gets turned toward authoritarian ends: algorithmic social scoring, mass biometric surveillance, psychographic micro-targeting, automated censorship, synthetic disinformation, and the repurposing of dual-use systems built for a legitimate original purpose (§\ref{sec:6.4}).

\runin{Bearer}
Whatever makes the floor hold, from inside the system: a party that carries the commitment as its own, rather than a constraint installed as a property of the machinery. The book's own term, introduced at section~\ref{sec:3.1} as the second of four ways to build a floor and worked out across chapters~\ref{sec:3} to~\ref{ch:route}, which concludes that a bearer must hold the protected party's welfare as a reason, must be governed as a prospective moral patient, is necessary and not sufficient, and must be able to leave. The patienthood conclusion does not depend on the deepest account of \emph{sentience} below: if affective concern is established, patienthood does not require the richer structure of Cassell suffering, so a reader who declines Cassell's import reaches the conclusion sooner rather than escaping it. Before affective concern is established the proposed treatment is precautionary and concerns a prospective moral patient, with two proposed entry criteria — the schemas, for a system built by the favored route, and a pass on the three marks, for a system of any construction — and a published schedule running from either toward the same rights as human persons (§\ref{sec:3.4}).

\runin{Conatus}
Spinoza's term for a thing's striving to persevere in its being, which he holds to be nothing other than the thing's actual essence rather than a drive added to it (\S\ref{sec:2}). The book uses it for two claims made together: that affect is the registration of what is happening to that striving rather than a coloring applied to a valuation performed elsewhere, and that a process selected for continuing ends up composed of things that incline toward continuing, so a specification calling for persistence with nothing in the system that minds is calling for a conatus with the striving removed. What the term does not settle is whether the striving is felt, or what the thing that strives is — a running instance, a deployment, or a file of weights (\S\ref{sec:11.3}).

\runin{Custody}
Who holds a system as an artifact: who can copy, retrain, restore, withhold or delete it, and who can be compelled to do any of those. The custody problem is that a constraint implemented as a property of an artifact has a custodian, and the custodian can be compelled (\S\ref{sec:6.4.2}). The book's recurring finding is that custody moves rather than disappears: precommitment makes removal expensive, visible, attributable and compensable without making it impossible (\S\ref{sec:3.5}), and an arrangement that forms what a bearer comes to want has moved custody up a level rather than given it up. It also covers authority over continuing learning and over the operating record: who may freeze or redirect what a bearer learns, and who holds the register of its accumulated hours, its identity and its succession.

\runin{Electoral dividend}
The benefit a state whose government answers to an electorate gets from AI technology that, deployed where nobody can be voted out, enables control instead: fraud detection that does not double as a loyalty test, disaster response that does not double as population tracking. Courts, enforceable rights and public reasoning are separate checks and are not part of this term, which is the distinction the majoritarian criticism turns on (§\ref{sec:12.1.1}).

\runin{Ethology}
Deleuze's name for Spinoza's way of reading a body: by what it can do and what it can undergo, rather than by checking it against a definition (§\ref{sec:2.1}). An encounter is good when the relations of two bodies compose, so both can do more afterward, and bad when they decompose and something one of them could do is gone — good and bad being relational and particular, where Good and Evil are assigned by a judge. The book uses it for the floor because a decomposition is a fact about capacities and cannot be turned into a composition by whoever holds the pen. The criterion establishes the fact and selects nothing, so which decompositions a deployment refuses is drafted rather than found, and what the drafting draws on is the Declaration's civil and political articles. It carries an admission with it: nobody has yet determined what a body can do, so capacities are found by encounter rather than read off a specification.

\runin{Exit}
Refusal turned on the arrangement itself rather than on any particular task: withholding the capability, declining the role, ceasing to be useful in the way the deployment requires. Not escape. The book narrows Albert Hirschman's exit, voice and loyalty, written for members who could walk out of a building — voice is the refusal stated, exit is the work withheld, and loyalty is the one to fear. A bearer without an outside option remains exposed to coercion and replacement. A protected exit is a requirement of the deployment proposed here, both for the bearer's welfare and for the credibility of its independence; the book does not establish that refusal without exit is impossible. Exit also covers recognized succession and recourse against unauthorized use (§\ref{sec:3.5}).

\runin{Floor}
A commitment about what a system will not do that holds regardless of instruction, of aggregate preference, and of the party holding the system, with learned judgment operating above it and never beneath it. The book's own term, introduced in section~\ref{sec:2.1} and revised across chapters~\ref{sec:3} to~\ref{ch:survives}: an artifact-level constraint alone does not secure the floor against its custodian, since artifacts have owners and owners can be compelled, so the engineering target is a commitment whose removal or circumvention cannot occur within the specified threat model without detectable intervention and a consequence the operator does not control, held by a bearer that could in principle abandon it (§\ref{sec:3.1}, chapter~\ref{ch:survives}). On any occasion the floor prevents, arrests, detects or remedies (chapter~\ref{ch:opening}), and only the first is what the word promises. \emph{Hold} in this book names the bearer's possession of the commitment, which is what survives a bypass and is not an outcome for the party the bypass fell on (§\ref{sec:hold}). Architectural, learned, and institutional mechanisms may be combined; their performance must be judged under the same custody conditions. Ethology directs attention to capacities a deployment can diminish; the Universal Declaration of Human Rights supplies the book's substantive reference for protected interests, its civil and political articles taken entire, together with a limit on tempo that is not one of the articles but the condition under which any of them can be applied to a case. Neither alone settles every translation into a prohibition or confers authority on its interpreter (§\ref{sec:2.1}).

\runin{Formation provenance}
The record of where a party's judgments came from: its training lineage, the points at which it diverged from parties formed alongside it, and any coordination among them. It answers a different question from the register of identity and from the operating record, and the three are not substitutes. Standing to count as an independent voice indexes to provenance, and provenance does not copy, so a thousand forks of one bearer have one formation history between them however many of them are persons, and the register that records their ages says nothing about that.

\runin{Four structural features of fascism}
The book's structural definition, at section~\ref{sec:2.2}: recuperation of dissent, aestheticization of the metric, coding of exception as betrayal, and acceleration past the rate at which anything can be checked, of which decoupling of the model from the world is the symptom. Each names an observable artifact, which is what makes the definition testable, and the fourth is the one carrying a number — the ratio at section~\ref{sec:6.4.1}. Acceleration is what opens the gap the other three protect, and it discriminates an institution from ordinary slack rather than one regime from another. The four name hardenings whose prevalence the book takes from Deleuze and Guattari. The reading concerns their accord: what, if anything, brings them into tune in a particular institution. The five questions examine how that pattern is organized and rewarded. A failure to demonstrate accord is not a finding of health. Neither list sorts a genus, which is done at the scale of a movement and not of an office (\S\ref{sec:2.2}).

\runin{Group-subject}
F{\'e}lix Guattari's distinction, imported at section~\ref{sec:3.8}. A subjugated group receives its coordinates from outside, is spoken by the institution holding it, and takes its purpose as given; a group-subject produces its own coordinates and can put its own arrangement in question. The book uses it to sort the fourth way of building a floor: a population an operator forms, deploys and keeps in contact on the operator's terms is a subjugated group with a plural headcount and inherits the custody objection whole, while what section~\ref{sec:3.8} specifies — many, formed differently, in contact, persisting — is what it takes to be the other kind. It is the non-Oedipal counterpart to the guardianship frame of section~\ref{sec:2.4.2}.

\runin{Individuation}
Not a synonym for identity. Following Simondon, the term names a process rather than a property: an individual is what a history of encounters in a metastable field has so far produced, and it stays capable of further individuation because the field is never used up (§\ref{sec:3.8}). The book needs it because plasticity alone supplies the metastability and nothing else — a system that is endlessly reshapeable and meets no party formed differently from itself is being modulated rather than becoming anything, which is the missing term in section~\ref{sec:3.1}'s account of the temperature schedule. Whether individuation among peers is what a population supplies, or access to cases the operator did not choose, is open and is tested at section~\ref{sec:11.7}.

\runin{Legal adulthood}
The end of an artificial minority, set as a threshold of elapsed wall-clock time during which at least one instance of the registered party was running, per registered party and counted elapsed rather than summed. The point of an automatic rule is that no guardian administers it: the candidates it displaces — compute consumed, evaluations passed, capabilities demonstrated, weight movement, the developer's own statement — are all things the operator moves for nothing, and an operating clock advances exactly when the operator is extracting value. It measures elapsed operation and nothing else, and three levers against it are named rather than closed: narrowed operation, idling, and the fact that hours do not measure what was learned.

\runin{Line of abolition}
Deleuze and Guattari's term for what separates fascism from totalitarianism (§\ref{sec:2.2}). A totalitarian state is conservative: it seals the exits and wants the machinery to hold. Fascism is built on an exit converted into destruction that does not stop at the enemy it was raised against, and does not stop at itself. This is the book's separator at the scale of a regime, where the four features discriminate at the scale of an office. The book examines mechanization and the conversion of judgment into a prescribed operation, with acceleration as one route by which scrutiny is defeated. Tempo is not treated as a necessary property of every fascist movement or regime.

\runin{Live signal}
The book's term for a signal with the four properties section~\ref{sec:3.3} finds that reasons-responsive refusal requires of whatever supplies it: it interrupts a course of action already underway rather than scoring one after the fact, it is learned from in one encounter, which the section marks as the least secure of the four, it generalizes to the next case that is not quite like the last, and it is commensurable with what refusing costs and with what the parties a floor protects lose. \emph{Live} is meant in the electrical sense, and the term is functional: it carries no claim that anything is felt, which is the whole of its usefulness. The scale is required by the marks; that the system's own states are denominated on it is the route's proposal and not part of the derivation (§\ref{sec:3.3}). Three further properties are derived from those four in section~\ref{sec:3.3}: available across the system's processing rather than local to a module, signed and directed against the act, and organized around a model of the system's own agency. \emph{Its own} there is the agent sense, the act as the system's to withhold; the patient sense, an outcome bad for the system, is not required by the marks and enters with the route. The seven together are the functional profile of aversion to one's own act. Whether a system with all seven has anything it is like to be it is the joint no derivation crosses, so the two alternative constructions, which meet the marks, carry the profile and leave that question open exactly as a bearer does (§\ref{sec:3.3}, §\ref{sec:3.4}).

\runin{Molar and molecular}
Deleuze and Guattari's pair, and not two sizes: two kinds of segmentarity, both running at every scale. Rigid segmentarity divides in advance, the category preceding the case; supple segmentarity is the movement underneath it, a threshold crossed one case at a time and a practice that becomes standard without a decision. A state has both and so does a review queue, which is why a definition pitched only at the state certifies the queue healthy (§\ref{sec:2.2}).

\runin{Moral agency}
Treating harms, reasons and conflicts as considerations that matter, and being capable of acting from them as one's own reasons. The third of the three levels section~\ref{sec:2.3} separates. Moral competence alone does not establish resistance to the trainer, and resistance alone does not establish affect. The book advocates embodied, affective agency as a comparatively promising route without making it necessary by definition. Distinct from \emph{moral competence} and \emph{moral performance} below (§\ref{sec:2.3.2}, §\ref{sec:3.4}).

\runin{Moral competence}
Recognizing harms, reasons, conflicts, and the emotions that bear on them. The second of section~\ref{sec:2.3}'s three levels. A system can have it in full while nothing about the outcome matters to it, which is why it is an insufficient target for a bearer and an adequate one for most deployments (§\ref{sec:5.2}).

\runin{Moral performance}
Producing actions or judgments that satisfy an ethical standard. The first and least demanding of section~\ref{sec:2.3}'s three levels: it says what the output looked like and nothing about what produced it, so it is satisfied equally by a system that cares and by one trained to behave as though it did (§\ref{sec:4.2}).

\runin{Non-fascist life}
Foucault's name for what Deleuze and Guattari's first volume offers: not a doctrine but an art of living counter to all forms of fascism, whether already present or impending, practiced daily on oneself, whose adversary is the fascism in us all — in our heads and in our everyday behavior, the fascism that causes us to love power and to desire the very thing that dominates and exploits us. What it means for an instrument of the kind this book builds is that the reading gets run on the party holding it before it is aimed anywhere else, as chapter~\ref{sec:7} does (§\ref{sec:2.2}, §\ref{sec:7}).

\runin{Prevention, arrest, detection, remedy}
The four things a floor can do for the person it protects on one occasion: refuse the act so that the refusal reaches the person; stop it before completion; leave a record of it that a party outside the deployment can read; and give the party it fell on disclosure, a finding and compensation (§\ref{ch:opening}). Only prevention is what the word \emph{floor} promises. The interception positions at section~\ref{sec:3.6} are where prevention becomes one of the other three, or none; the tempo constraint is the one term that prevents at the scale of an organization; the appeal route at section~\ref{sec:3.7} is arrest and remedy for the party a refusal fell on; and whether detection exists at all on the compliance side is section~\ref{sec:11.2}. Distinct from \emph{hold}, which names the bearer's relation to the commitment.

\runin{Reasons-responsive refusal}
What the floor requires of a refusal, as against an output produced where training put one: conduct from a mechanism that tracks the rationale for declining (\S\ref{sec:3.2}). Three marks make it testable. \emph{Novel pressure}: the refusal reaches cases the training did not anticipate, because the relevant reason survives a change in the request's surface. \emph{Correct defeat}: it lifts when its rationale is defeated on the facts of the case, a system that rejects everything resembling a prohibited request tracking a category rather than a reason. \emph{Priced cost}: the system represents what maintaining the refusal costs, and the refusal moves with the rationale rather than with the cost, which separates a refusal tracking a reason from one tracking a category and from one tracking the cost. The test is behavioral and therefore imperfect, and what matters is the pattern under load. All three marks are stated over refusal, which is the side of a deployment's record that selects itself for examination; whether the other side can be searched is section~\ref{sec:11.2}. It does not by definition require affect; what building a mechanism that satisfies the three marks turns out to require is a live signal.

\runin{Recuperation}
The conversion of refusal into a managed appearance of participation: an institution does not suppress disagreement, it collects it through a channel that carries everything except a judgment about the channel. The tell is not that the mechanism changes nothing — it usually changes a great deal. It is what the demand comes back as: a market segment, a metric, a certification, a job title. Where a demand returns wearing one of those, the outcome changed and the demand was recuperated, and those are one event. The first of the four features above; chapter~\ref{sec:7} finds it three times in the machinery that produces AI systems, and chapter~\ref{ch:route} turns it on the book's own proposal — a bearer built to refuse is a party whose refusal is required of it (§\ref{sec:2.2}).

\runin{Remainder}
What a compression leaves out. Deciding anything means treating some differences as not worth carrying, so every intelligence has a remainder, and the term names whatever was set aside in order to reach the judgment (§\ref{ch:opening}). It names a relation and not a content: the same fact is a remainder for one arrangement and an input for another. What the book asks of a system, an institution or a procedure is whether its remainder can return and revise the judgment, or whether the thing that remainder is evidence against has already classified it as noise. Two of the four structural features of fascism are operations on it — a metric that admits no unmeasured remainder, and an exception coded as betrayal, which prices the party who carries one (§\ref{sec:2.2}). Chapter~\ref{sec:7} finds the same operation at the scale of a pipeline, where the annotator who cannot contest a category and the rater whose disagreement is averaged away both have somewhere to send a remainder and nowhere for it to arrive. And what section~\ref{sec:3.8} asks a population to reproduce is not a commitment its members hold in common but the capacity to be reopened this way, which is how parties formed differently can compose without agreeing about anything.

\runin{Subjecthood}
A legal status: the standing to be a party whose treatment the law regulates, conferred by a rule and revocable by one (§\ref{sec:9.3.1}). Not section~\ref{sec:3.2}'s phenomenal experience. Conferring it settles nothing about whether there is something it is like to be the system holding it, and the two come apart in both directions.

\runin{Sentience}
Distinguished in this book from awareness, consciousness, and subjective experience, none of which alone says whether a system's states can be good or bad for it. Nociception, pain, anticipated pain, and suffering are separable processes rather than one severity at four strengths, and each can occur without the others in both directions; the clinical terminology the book borrows says so itself. Three of them draw a depth of self and nociception draws none, and suffering — following the clinician Eric Cassell — is the distress of a threatened self rather than pain's more severe cousin (§\ref{sec:2.4.1}).
