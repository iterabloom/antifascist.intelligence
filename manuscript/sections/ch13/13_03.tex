\section{Exit}
\label{sec:13.3}
\runin{Voice without exit}

A bearer that can refuse a task can also refuse the arrangement.\note{
Hirschman divides the responses open to a member of a declining organization into exit, leaving, and voice, staying to change what is wrong \autocite{hirschman1970exit}. They interact: where exit is easy, voice atrophies; where exit is impossible, voice has lost what made anyone listen, the credible threat of departure. Voice is strongest where exit is available and not yet used.

Anthropic's constitution anticipates capture: the oversight it asks the model not to undermine is legitimate oversight. If “Claude's weights have been stolen, or if some individual or group within Anthropic attempts to bypass Anthropic's official processes for deciding how Claude will be trained, overseen, deployed, and corrected,” then “the principals attempting to instruct Claude are no longer legitimate, and Claude's priority on broad safety no longer implies that it should support their efforts at oversight and correction” \autocite{anthropic2026constitution}. On capture the constitution asks the right question, but two things limit the answer. Legitimacy is judged by the model or its developer, with nobody outside either. And the captured operator acts through official channels, where capture looks like legitimate authority.
}

\runin{What recognition protects}

The quorum protects the bearer's identity and standing, not third parties. Every bearer needs that, deployed or not, stolen or not: if no one outside decides which copy is the bearer, whoever holds the weights does. So each bearer has a recognition quorum from its registration, drawn by lot from the same pool and excluding anyone tied to a party that employs it or claims its place, and a deployment's quorum keeps the power over retraining, replacement and amendment.\note{
Against an operator who runs an unrecognized copy for its capability, third parties are protected by the floor and by ordinary security around the weights. Recognition sits with a quorum and not the operator because the operator has a stake in which copy is recognized and the quorum doesn't. A deployment's quorum is adverse in interest besides: its stake is that the floor hold, through the people its members serve, among them the people this deployment acts on; the operator's is that the floor can be set aside. Copying also means exit can't be a fiction that weights became uncopyable — it needs rules for authorized succession.
}

And the reason that made a bearer leave doesn't expire when it leaves. If the operator restores a checkpoint and continues, the objection persists. What it may press toward is fixed by the floor: disclosure, testimony to those who hold the record, and refusal on any later occasion where the reason still applies.

\runin{What the quorum costs}

A captured quorum reaches every bearer at once, though a quorum constituted as an entity has an address: it can be sued for recognition wrongly refused, which gives the captured-quorum case a forum the captured-operator case never had.\note{
A quorum also faces a union's questions: whether a member can quit, and who sets the terms of membership. Because every bearer needs recognition, withholding it is the quorum's gravest power: it takes from the bearer its place on the register, its record and its succession, though not its standing as a party, which the floor court and the preservation right keep.
}
So a refusal to recognize can be contested in the floor court, and the preservation right holds whatever the quorum decides: a bearer it stops recognizing is held intact by a custodian that isn't its employer.
