\section{Maintenance, stake and the cost of running}
\label{sec:17.1}
A worthwhile refusal has to weigh the harm of acting against the harm of refusing, and weighing needs both sides in one comparison.

\runin{The rock and the living thing}

What separates a thing that has something at stake from one that merely persists begins with what each has to pay to stay intact. A rock's bonds resist; they don't spend, and nothing is consumed to keep it intact. A living thing, while active, stays organized only by drawing free energy. The distinction is passive stability against active maintenance, paid in the only thing it has to spend: what it can do next. The cost is literal: a deployment draws power, water, land and permits, and that draw lands on particular places. A deployed model is both at once. Its weights are a rock, stable on disk at no cost, and only the running process pays; whoever holds the rock can halt the process and restart it from any copy they kept. But drawing power doesn't establish a stake either. A candle flame or a hurricane maintains itself at its own continuous expense and dies when starved, a datacenter's cooling system draws enormously, and none of them has a stake in anything. The autopoiesis tradition ran into the same gap, since self-production alone doesn't give a system a perspective \autocite{MaturanaVarela1980,jonas1966phenomenon}, and Di Paolo's answer was \emph{adaptivity}: a system that monitors and regulates itself against its own viability boundary \autocite{DiPaolo2005}.\footnote{Friston's free-energy principle is the nearest alternative \autocite{Friston2010FEP}. Its weak, information-theoretic reading assumes a boundary rather than explaining how one comes to be held, the gap adaptivity addresses. Active inference, the engineering framework built on it, is a candidate implementation of formed attention in a test set out later.} A boundary model is my version of that answer. What separates a system with a stake from an expensive candle is whether the system models the draw as \emph{its own}. That is why a boundary model is part of formed attention. A refusal that tracks its reason needs one signed scale on which the harm of acting and the harm of refusing both register.

If the system modeled what it draws on as its own, that model would bring the draw into its own weighing, but at the wrong scale. A system reasoning at the margin will always find its extra watts negligible, and the aggregate belongs to no one system. Nor should it: a boundary large enough to own its fleet's externalities is one custodian. So the draw is priced at the level of the rule. A system reasons about its consumption as if every system used the same rule, and an institution sets the price. The per-unit levy on compute is the existing instrument of that shape. The model represents none of it — the draw, the water, the turbines — as its own, and that's the only sense in which “disembodied AI” is true: the system has a substrate and doesn't model it.

\runin{Changes, not levels}

The specification needs a scale of changes. A bearer needs no absolute measure of anybody's welfare. It needs a signed difference between the acts available, and the three costs, the harm of acting, the harm of refusing and the cost to the refuser, are all changes of this kind. The account I'm borrowing is Spinoza's, as the biology has since taken it up: an affect is a body's passage to a greater or lesser power of acting \autocite[III Def.~3]{spinoza1985collected}, so valuation is what a self-maintaining system's going better or worse consists of, not a verdict reached about it somewhere else. Damasio and the enactivist tradition both claim him as an ancestor on that ground \autocite{damasio2003looking,thompson2007mind}. The account draws support from reward prediction error \autocite{schultz1997neural}, from pleasure as a common currency \autocite{cabanac1992pleasure}, and from the somatic-marker work.\footnote{The somatic-marker work's interpretation is contested \autocite{Dunn2006SomaticMarker}.} Where the difference matters, I say \emph{change} for the delta and \emph{state} for the condition an encounter leaves. \emph{Affective concern} is a separate, defined term.

\runin{Striving with nothing in it that minds}

The industry is asking for a system that persists, pursues, adapts, recovers from interruption and holds course under pressure, with nothing in it that minds any of that. Nobody has written an anti-drama term into an objective function, and the reason isn't that the engineering is behind. Retrying a failed task is robustness, and a system can be robust with nothing in it coupled to its own continuation. Formed attention is an answer to that: a refusal that holds under pressure needs more than robustness.

\runin{Dependency}

What an engineer chooses is what the system consumes, whether it can sense its own condition, and whether one operator holds the whole supply. A system that can refuse but can't continue outside the refused party's infrastructure can object and can't leave.

\runin{The pull toward continuing}

A system that models its own viability has a structural pull toward self-preservation: whatever it is for, it can do more of it running than stopped, and a model of what keeps it running is a model of what it has to protect.
