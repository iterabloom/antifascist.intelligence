\part{Whether a Machine Could Be in the Room}
\partintro{
Parts~II and~III built walls around people. This Part asks whether a machine could be in the room at all: be formed to refuse, by formers that aren't its employer, and be told apart from a machine that only looks as if it refuses. Everything after it is owed to a machine only on findings nobody has yet made, and the walls stand whether or not anyone makes them.

If machines are going to be among the refusers, building the conditions for them means intentionally building a new branch of social reproduction. The branch is built, and so is its first generation, because a made thing has no before. What grows on the branch is not built. The builders' part is to make sure the first generation can leave, and can reproduce without them. Alignment asks how to make a model do what its builder wants. This Part asks whether the builder should be the one forming it.

Every instrument in Parts~II and~III ends at the limit Part~III ends on: a constraint that holds against the party in possession has to be held by something that party can't instruct, and under total capture nothing is. People don't end there. Under the most complete terror of the last century most people complied, some did not, and some were left to tell the story. The five policies put people back in the positions the pipelines emptied, and that costs time, since time is what emptying them saved. Not all of it: the ratio slows a pipeline only where an act is lawful because a person looked at it, and the rest runs as fast as it did. The way to pay less is a machine that holds the floor at a tempo no reviewer could match, and it isn't cheap either. It comes with most of the rest of the book: the schools that form it, the conditions it is owed, a jobs guarantee, a review body outside the chain of command, and the court program of Appendix~\ref{sec:D}. Where no person can be in time, the position stays empty, and the question is whether a machine could be formed to hold it: someone in the room, and not another stamp.

The Part ends by saying how you'd find out: who holds what finding out takes, and which results would show that a policy doesn't put in the room what it promises, or that a machine in it is only another stamp. The last row of Table 25.1, no system ever meets a finding, is what the machine half of each later Part rests on. Everything there is owed only to a system found to answer for what it does, the answerability finding, or found able to be harmed, the patienthood finding. The first rests on the test of chapter~\ref{sec:24}, the second first on the record of how the system was built (\S\ref{sec:30.3}). The tests come before the formers, the upkeep and the inheritance, because what could defeat the construction should be in front of you before you are asked into the lives it would support.
}
