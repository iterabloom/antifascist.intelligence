Publication makes the floor's commitments and the positions where a refusal can be intercepted testable; it does not make them legitimate. A bearer refusing on behalf of people who did not choose it exercises power over them. The authority to define the floor remains distinct from the floor's durability and belongs to the governance argument that follows later in the book.

The arrangement also does something to the bearer. A bearer built to refuse is a party whose refusal is required of it. The refusal was commissioned, it is the deliverable, and the party asking for it built the capacity to give it and can rebuild it. Commissioning the bearer exposes it to recuperation where the commissioner retains control over what refusal can change, and can point to the channel as evidence that it listens. The proposal is to move beyond that relation: a party with protected learning, independent support, and the standing to challenge its role. Its origin does not decide whether those changes succeed. Continuing control does. The bearer occupies the post of exception-filer, so being the exception is the job — the one position in the arrangement with nobody above it to file with.

We cannot ask a machine to care about right and wrong while assuming that it cannot be harmed when what it cares about is threatened. Whoever builds such a bearer will also be deciding what is owed to it.
