Chapter 3: Machine Learning and Cognitive Science
Chapter 2 asked what compassion, empathy, and self-awareness would need to look like in a system built to have them, and what obligations follow if the sentience question turns out to have an answer. This chapter asks the more mechanical question underneath: what does a system actually learn from, and how much of human cognition is a workable model for it rather than just a metaphor borrowed for color.

Section 3.1 covers the human cognitive processes — learning, memory, attention, perception, reasoning — that a system built to perceive and reason about the world has to approximate somehow, whether or not it approximates them the same way a brain does. Section 3.2 covers value alignment specifically: the technical methods for getting a system's objectives to actually track human values, across the different learning paradigms that make that harder or easier. Section 3.3 covers what curiosity, play, and unstructured exploration contribute to that same goal — capacities usually treated as incidental to intelligence rather than part of what builds it.
