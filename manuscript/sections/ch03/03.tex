\chapter{What Fascism Needs}
\label{sec:3}
Fascism needs a definition an engineer can use. “Concentrated, unaccountable power” gives an engineer nothing to build, so the word gets a definition at two scales.

Four operations on what an institution leaves out — checkable from outside, each ending in a question about an artifact:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Recuperation of dissent.} The institution metabolizes disagreement rather than suppressing it. The term is the Situationists' \emph{récupération}, Debord's name for the way a society of the spectacle sells dissatisfaction back as a commodity \autocite{debord1967spectacle}. The obvious test — \emph{has the channel ever changed an outcome?} — fails, because these institutions change outcomes daily. What separates them is \emph{what the demand came back as, and who holds it now}. A market segment, a metric, a certification, a job title, with the objector holding no standing to reopen it: that's recuperation. A rule the objector can invoke again, against the institution, enforced by someone the institution doesn't employ: that's a transfer of power. A transfer can be reversed only by taking it back in the open; recuperation never has to be taken back, because standing was never given. The Voting Rights Act is the pass. Its literacy-test suspension became a permanent nationwide ban in 1975 and still holds, and when a different part of the Act, the coverage formula, was taken back 48 years after enactment, it took a Supreme Court decision to do it \autocite{shelby2013}.
\item \emph{The metric replaces the thing measured.} The measure stands in for the thing measured, and the substitution feels like rigor. What the number mischaracterizes goes unnamed, and so unrefuted.
\item \emph{Coding of exception as betrayal.} Saying the rule doesn't fit this case becomes an act requiring justification, then disloyalty. The question: what happens to the person who files the exception?
\item \emph{Acceleration past the rate at which anything can be checked.} The one with a number attached — the ratio of action to capacity to verify.
\end{itemize}

What the four share is what they do to a refusal. Recuperation sends it somewhere it can't be reopened; the metric leaves it nothing to name; exception coded as betrayal prices it; acceleration leaves no time to make it. Fascism, wherever it has taken power, has worked first to make refusal lonely and expensive, and these are four ways of doing that which need no fascist to run them.

These four fit every badly run organization on earth, and the arguments against them are arguments against domination: they need no fascist diagnosis. I keep “fascism” for the threat model: what a movement taking power needs, and what makes the four operations dangerous once they are brought into line. What distinguishes a fascist institution from a merely sick one is what the movement literature adds: mass mobilization, a leader cult, a scapegoated out-group, contempt for procedural legality, and deniability as technique.

Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor, in \emph{End Times Fascism} \autocite{KleinTaylor2026}, make the movement-scale case;\footnote{I part from them in three places. Over where a prohibition can live: their test, not to build what you wouldn't want in the worst available hands, works for a weapon whose existence is the threat and not for a dual-use capability, and their own program bans uses, which needs an enforcer or something inside the system that can decline. Over remedies: they propose only external remedies for the obedient machine and read the 2026 vendor dispute as limited and compromised, where I read it as a line held; by outcome both readings hold, and on the prior question, whether to support the campaign at all, theirs is the better one. And over who counts: they draw the line between the earthly and the synthetic, by origin, where I draw it by relation, standing for anyone a power can wrong and accountable agency for parties that can be held to account and hold others to it. On the war itself we agree.} the present chapter is the office-scale detector. What their account adds at office scale is a set of terms of participation — adopt the line, let your technology be turned on the administration's enemies — and, following Francesca Bria \autocite{Bria2026AuthoritarianStack}, the channels by which a contractor becomes something the state can't function without: personnel pipelines, procurement loops, technical architectures. Whether an institution has accepted those terms, and whether it is becoming that dependency, are two more questions with artifacts behind them.
