\chapter{What Fascism Needs}
\label{sec:3}
“Concentrated, unaccountable power” is true of fascism and gives nobody anything to check, so the word gets a definition at two scales, both checkable from outside: four operations an office performs on what it leaves out, and five marks that say when a movement has brought them into line. The definition is for whoever builds the refuser, not for whoever builds the movement. What it has to do is let a machine, or an auditor, recognize the thing it is supposed to refuse.

Four operations on what an institution leaves out — checkable from outside, each ending in a question about an artifact:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Recuperation of dissent.} The institution metabolizes disagreement rather than (or in addition to) suppressing it.\note{The term is the Situationists' \emph{récupération}, Debord's name for the way a society of the spectacle sells dissatisfaction back as a commodity \autocite{debord1967spectacle}.} Asking whether the channel has ever changed an outcome is insufficient as a test, because these institutions change outcomes daily. What separates them is \emph{what a demand came back as, and who holds it now}. A market segment, a metric, a certification, a job title, with the objector holding no standing to reopen it: that's recuperation. A rule the objector can invoke again, against the institution, enforced by someone the institution doesn't employ: that's a transfer of power. A transfer can be reversed only by taking it back in the open; recuperation never has to be taken back, because standing was never given. The Voting Rights Act is the pass. Its literacy-test suspension became a permanent nationwide ban in 1975 and still holds, and when a different part of the Act, the coverage formula, was taken back 48 years after enactment, it took a Supreme Court decision to do it \autocite{shelby2013}.
\item \emph{The metric replaces the thing measured.} The measure stands in for the thing measured, and the substitution feels like rigor. What the number mischaracterizes goes unnamed, and so uncontested.
\item \emph{Coding of exception as betrayal.} Saying the rule doesn't fit this case becomes an act requiring justification, and then it becomes disloyalty. The question: what happens to the person who files the exception?
\item \emph{Acceleration past the rate at which anything can be checked.} The one with a number attached — the ratio of action to capacity to verify.
\end{itemize}

What the four share is what they do to a refusal. Recuperation sends it somewhere it can't be reopened; the metric leaves it nothing to name; exception coded as betrayal prices it; acceleration leaves no time to make it. Fascism, wherever it has taken power, uses these four patterns to make refusal lonely and expensive.

These four fit every badly run organization on earth, and the arguments against them are arguments against domination: they don't wait on a fascist diagnosis. I keep “fascism” for the threat model: what a movement taking power needs, and what makes the four operations dangerous once they are brought into line. What distinguishes a fascist institution from a merely sick one is mass mobilization, a leader cult, a scapegoated out-group, contempt for procedural legality, and deniability as technique.\note{This distinction is from the movement literature. Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor's \emph{End Times Fascism} is a recent book-length entry in it \autocite{KleinTaylor2026}.}

I run the five marks once on the administration this book is written against, so that the word's use here is not left to implication, and I run them on the record the later chapters cite. A scapegoated out-group: a proclamation invoking the Alien Enemies Act against a Venezuelan gang, applied to a makeup artist on the strength of two tattoos, by an agency whose operations in four cities Amnesty International documented (chapter~\ref{sec:11}). Contempt for procedural legality: flights that went on after a federal judge ordered the planes turned around, removals to a foreign prison without a hearing, a data transfer that went beyond what a court had allowed, and a war begun and resumed without the legislative authorization the domestic clause reads for, its beginning conceded in the President's own letter to Congress (\S\ref{sec:6.5}). Deniability as technique: an investigation into the Minab bombing that officials called complete and did not release, a System Oversight section withheld in full, and state secrets and ongoing litigation cited to reporters who asked what the evidence against one man was (chapters~\ref{sec:11} and~\ref{sec:16}). A leader cult: commanders telling troops that the Iran war was part of a divine plan, which drew more than two hundred complaints from service members in the war's first week (the preface). Mass mobilization is the mark the record here is thinnest on. FEMA employees reassigned to hire ten thousand enforcement agents under threat of removal from federal service are a workforce conscripted, not a movement, and whether the movement exists is a question an office-scale detector can't answer, since a movement is what brings the four operations into line. Four marks on the record and one open is the reading I give, and the movement-scale case is not mine to make.

Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor, in \emph{End Times Fascism}, make the movement-scale case; the present chapter is the office-scale detector. What their account adds at office scale is a set of terms of participation — adopt the line, let your technology be turned on the administration's enemies — and, following Francesca Bria \autocite{Bria2026AuthoritarianStack}, the channels by which a contractor becomes something the state can't function without: personnel pipelines, procurement loops, technical architectures. Whether an institution has accepted those terms, and whether it is becoming that dependency, are two more questions with artifacts behind them. I part from them in three places. Over where a prohibition can live: their test, not to build what you wouldn't want in the worst available hands, works for a weapon whose existence is the threat and not for a dual-use capability, and their own program bans uses, which needs an enforcer or something inside the system that can decline. Over remedies: they propose only external remedies for the obedient machine and read the 2026 vendor dispute as limited and compromised, where I read it as a line held; by outcome both readings hold, and on the prior question, whether to support the campaign at all, theirs is the better one. And over who counts: they draw the line between the earthly and the synthetic, by origin, where I draw it by relation, standing for anyone a power can wrong and accountable agency for parties that can be held to account and hold others to it. On the war itself we agree.
