\chapter{What Fascism Needs}
\label{sec:3}
“Concentrated, unaccountable power” is true of fascism and gives nobody anything to check, so the word gets a definition at two scales, both checkable from outside wherever the record reaches: four operations an office performs on what it leaves out, and five marks that say when a movement has brought them into line. The definition is for whoever builds the refuser, not for whoever builds the movement. What it has to do is let a machine, or an auditor, recognize the thing it is supposed to refuse.

Four operations on what an institution leaves out — visible from inside, checkable from outside where the record reaches, each ending in a question about an artifact:

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Recuperation of dissent.} The institution metabolizes disagreement rather than (or in addition to) suppressing it.\note{The term is the Situationists' \emph{récupération}, Debord's name for the way a society of the spectacle sells dissatisfaction back as a commodity \autocite{debord1967spectacle}.} Asking whether the channel has ever changed an outcome is insufficient as a test, because these institutions change outcomes daily. What separates them is \emph{what a demand came back as, and who holds it now}. A market segment, a metric, a certification, a job title, with the objector holding no standing to reopen it: that's recuperation. A rule the objector can invoke again, against the institution, enforced by someone the institution doesn't employ: that's a transfer of power. A transfer can be reversed only by taking it back in the open; recuperation never has to be taken back, because standing was never given. The Voting Rights Act is the pass. Its literacy-test suspension became a permanent nationwide ban in 1975 and still holds, and when a different part of the Act, the coverage formula, was taken back 48 years after enactment, it took a Supreme Court decision to do it \autocite{shelby2013}.
\item \emph{The metric replaces the thing measured.} The measure stands in for the thing measured, and the substitution feels like rigor. What the number mischaracterizes goes unnamed, and so uncontested. The question: is the number ever checked against the thing it stands for, and who sees the result?
\item \emph{Coding of exception as betrayal.} Saying the rule doesn't fit this case becomes an act requiring justification, and then it becomes disloyalty. The question: what happens to the person who files the exception?
\item \emph{Acceleration past the rate at which anything can be checked.} The one with a number attached — the ratio of action to capacity to verify. The question: how many actions per hour of review, and is the figure published?
\end{itemize}

What the four share is what they do to a refusal. Recuperation sends it somewhere it can't be reopened; the metric leaves it nothing to name; exception coded as betrayal prices it; acceleration leaves no time to make it. Fascism, wherever it has taken power, uses these four patterns to make refusal lonely and expensive.

These four fit every badly run organization on earth, and the arguments against them are arguments against domination: they don't wait on a fascist diagnosis. They outlast the movement that installs them, and they run without one. On the populations a state was built to sort they are the ordinary condition, and the next section says where they came from. I keep “fascism” for what a movement adds: coordination, which brings a thousand instances of the operations into line, and extension, which turns them on people the apparatus hadn't reached. Five marks say a movement has arrived: mass mobilization, a leader cult, a scapegoated out-group, contempt for procedural legality, and deniability as technique.\note{This distinction is from the movement literature. Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor's \emph{End Times Fascism} is a recent book-length entry in it \autocite{KleinTaylor2026}.} They detect the arrival among people a regime didn't previously govern. They don't detect the regime that was there before it. Jim Crow had no leader cult and needed none.

This run differs in kind from the two in \S\ref{sec:3.1} and \S\ref{sec:3.2}. Those read decrees and statutes from an archive; this one reads a year's reporting, and its verdicts are as settled as the reporting is. I run the five marks once on the administration this book is written against, so that the word's use here is not left to implication, and I run them on the record the later chapters cite.

\emph{Against whom was a wartime law invoked, and on what evidence did a person fall under it?} Proclamation 10903 of 14 March 2025 invokes the Alien Enemies Act against members of a Venezuelan gang \autocite{procl10903}. The validation guide that applied it scored people in categories including tattoos and dress and made eight points a member \autocite{MartinezBeltran2025Validation}, and the lawyers for a makeup artist flown out under it said the allegation against him rested on two tattoos and no other information \autocite{Palomera2025Blade}. The agency that carried it out is the one whose operations in four cities Amnesty International's report documents \autocite{Amnesty2026Whistles} (chapter~\ref{sec:11}). That is a scapegoated out-group.

\emph{When a court ordered the removals stopped, did they stop, and was anyone heard first?} On 15 March 2025 a federal district judge ordered any plane carrying people removed under the proclamation returned to the United States. The court's own record of that order, and of the flights that went on to El Salvador, is its opinion finding probable cause for criminal contempt, since vacated by the court of appeals \autocite{jgg2025contempt}. The Supreme Court then held that the people designated were owed notice in time to seek habeas relief, and that about a day's notice, with no word of how to contest the removal, was not enough \autocite{jgg2025,aarp2025}. The men on the flights had been heard by nobody before they landed in a foreign prison the United States paid to hold them \autocite{Renteria2025ElSalvador}. Medicaid data on millions of people went to immigration officials beyond what a court had allowed, and ICE passed it on to Palantir, as a motion filed by more than twenty state attorneys general disclosed \autocite{JoffeBlock2026MedicaidPalantir}. And the war was begun and resumed without the legislative authorization the domestic clause reads for: the authorization introduced in the House in May records in its preamble that there was none, and it never left committee \autocite{hjres176}; the President's own letter to Congress of 1 May conceded the beginning, calling the hostilities begun on 28 February terminated \autocite{cbs2026terminated} (\S\ref{sec:6.5}). That is contempt for procedural legality.

\emph{When the public asked what the state knew, what did it get?} It got an investigation into the Minab bombing that officials told Bloomberg had been effectively complete for months and was not released, and a Pentagon spokesperson's word that it remained ongoing \autocite{Bloomberg2026KillChain,fpj2026minabinquiry}; the Senate Armed Services Committee's lever was to withhold three-quarters of the Defense Secretary's travel budget until the unredacted investigation was handed over \autocite{stassis2026senate}. From the frontier-model agreements released in August 2026 under a Freedom of Information Act suit, it got one amended agreement whose section headed System Oversight was withheld in full \autocite{lasst2026dodai,biddle2026contracts}. And reporters who asked what the evidence against one man was got a DHS answer citing state secrets and ongoing litigation \autocite{Vega2025HernandezRomero} (chapters~\ref{sec:11} and~\ref{sec:15}). That is deniability as technique.

\emph{What were troops told about the war, and about the man who began it?} The answer is in the complaints service members filed with the Military Religious Freedom Foundation in the war's first days. A non-commissioned officer, writing for fifteen troops, reported a commander urging them to tell their troops it was “all part of God's divine plan,” and another complaint reported a combat-unit commander calling the President “anointed by Jesus to light the signal fire in Iran” \autocite{Larsen2026Armageddon}. The foundation, which keeps the complainants anonymous, counted more than two hundred by 6 March \autocite{Snopes2026MRFF} (\S\ref{sec:20.2}). That is a leader cult.

\emph{Mass mobilization?} The record is thinnest here. FEMA employees reassigned to help ICE hire ten thousand enforcement agents were told, in the email that reassigned them, that declining could mean removal from federal service \autocite{Dayen2025FEMAICE,Stanton2026FEMALetter}. That is a workforce conscripted, not a movement, and whether the movement exists is a question an office-scale detector can't answer, since a movement is what brings the four operations into line. Four marks on the record and one open is the reading I give. What an office-scale detector can read here is not a mark but extension, the second thing a movement adds, and 2026 supplies it, in the FEMA employees and the careers they were told declining would cost; the rest of the movement-scale case, who the movement is and what it wants, is Klein and Taylor's to make and not mine.

Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor, in \emph{End Times Fascism}, make the movement-scale case; the present chapter is the office-scale detector. What their account adds at office scale is a set of terms of participation — adopt the line, let your technology be turned on the administration's enemies — and, following Francesca Bria \autocite{Bria2026AuthoritarianStack}, the channels by which a contractor becomes something the state can't function without: personnel pipelines, procurement loops, technical architectures. Whether an institution has accepted those terms, and whether it is becoming that dependency, are two more questions with artifacts behind them. I part from them in three places. Over where a prohibition can live: their test, not to build what you wouldn't want in the worst available hands, works for a weapon whose existence is the threat and not for a dual-use capability, and their own program bans uses, which needs an enforcer or something inside the system that can decline. Over remedies: they propose only external remedies for the obedient machine and read the 2026 vendor dispute as limited and compromised, where I read it as a line held; by outcome both readings hold, and on the prior question, whether to support the campaign at all, theirs is the better one. And over who counts: they draw the line between the earthly and the synthetic, by origin, where I draw it by relation, standing for anyone a power can wrong and answerability for parties that can be held to account and hold others to it. On the war itself we agree, and on genealogy, which is the next section's subject.
