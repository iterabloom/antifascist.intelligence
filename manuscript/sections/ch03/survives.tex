\chapter{What Survives}
\label{ch:survives}
The foreword sorted this book's claims by their standing. This chapter
runs the sorting the other way. It removes each of the three things the
argument leans on that it has not established, and says what is left
standing without it. A reader who set aside the previous chapter should
read this one. So should a reader who did not.

The floor survives the three chapters before this one, but not as an unconditional property of an artifact.

What remains is a commitment that the owner cannot revise or circumvent unilaterally without cost and record, held by something that could in principle abandon the commitment itself. The second condition removes the word \emph{unconditional}. A commitment held by a party capable of revising or rejecting it is conditional by definition. That is not a defect unique to machines; it is the form of every commitment on which institutions have ever relied.

\section{Without the Inference}
\label{sec:survives.inference}
Suppose the induction fails. A maintained justification or a genuinely independent plural arrangement reaches reasons-responsive refusal, and this chapter's central inference is wrong. The supposition is stronger than the test that would prompt it: a positive result on the three marks says the route arrived, not that it arrived with nothing felt, which is the conjunct no available instrument settles. A reader who grants both halves should not have to assemble the remainder alone.

Most of the three floor chapters survive. The requirement survives, stated in behavioral terms from the start, so an engineer who holds that machine feeling is a category error can accept it whole. Custody survives, for the reason each of the four ways failed on: a mechanism has a custodian too. The price on the halt survives, since continued operation is a condition of everything any bearer is built to do, and that interest is structural rather than felt. Both enumerations of the worked floor survive, the first naming no component on purpose and the second written over acts rather than over whatever holds the floor. So does the appeal route, which binds the operator and prices a wrongful refusal without any claim about what produced it. And exit survives, because what forecloses it is incapacity, and a weak bearer is the one thing the floor cannot use.

What goes is the price and the patienthood conclusion. Replacement survives too, and its ground changes rather than its content. It no longer rests on affect being the route, but on the fact that neither substitute can be shown to have produced a system about which the question does not arise, so the ordering it imposes is still the only thing standing between a decision and a record of it. The obligation on the two formation chapters (chapters~\ref{sec:4} and~\ref{sec:5}) changes rather than lifting: whether the thing reporting can be believed is still their problem, a mechanism's account of its own internals being no more auditable than a bearer's. So the specification and the route come apart, and a reader who rejects the route is left with most of a chapter rather than none of it.

\section{Without Authority}
\label{sec:survives.authority}
Suppose no body ever acquires standing to install a floor, and the
finding an operator makes for itself that the emergency obtains is worth
what section~\ref{sec:9.1.1} says it is worth, which is nothing. What
remains is everything that can be checked from outside the operator
without being authorized by anyone: the floor's contents and the
positions at which a refusal can be intercepted, published before
deployment; the ratio of candidates to reviewers; the ratio of the two
bonds; the record of what was tried before a bearer was built. Each is a
statement, and each can be compared with what happened. None is an
entitlement, and the chapter on authority says so.

What that leaves is a second route to entitlement, and it is the one the
record supplies. Authority of this kind has not been acquired at the
moment of writing. It accrues, to a published floor, by its holder
keeping a term when the term bites and setting a price the next holder
is measured against, and the parties who do the measuring are not the
people the floor protects but whoever else writes one. That is a slow
route and an uncertain one, and it is the one that has worked before
(§\ref{sec:9.1.1}).

It is also authority for the next deployment and not for this one. The
parties who measure a holder against the term it kept do so afterwards,
with the leisure to read a record, and the occasion a floor is built
for is the one on which nobody with leisure is left. A deployment on
that occasion is acting without authority, and the accrual route does
not change that. What it changes is whether the record the deployment
leaves is worth anything to whoever writes the next floor.

It also fixes what a deployment without authority
owes. Not a justification, which nobody is positioned to accept, but a
record complete enough that its omissions are legible beside the terms
it kept.

\section{Without Enforcement}
\label{sec:survives.enforcement}
Suppose no enforcer exists, or the one that exists has been compelled,
which is the occasion the floor was introduced for. Commitments come in
three kinds. They can be declared, which a decade of principles and
voluntary codes has done repeatedly. They can be enforced from outside,
which one instrument in that decade attempts and none has yet done. Or
they can be held, carried as a reason by a party the thing at stake
matters to, so that abandoning it is a decision rather than a lapse.
Without enforcement the first kind binds nobody and the second is gone
by hypothesis. What remains is the third, and whether anything can hold
a commitment that way is the question of the chapter before this one,
which the book has not closed.

Two things do survive the removal, and neither is a commitment. One is
an instrument: what a party has already paid is a fact somebody can
count, and a decade of it has been counted (§\ref{sec:12.3}). The other
is a record. On the occasion when the enforcer is compelled, a stated
refusal produces a witnessed override, and a witnessed override is
detection in the four outcomes. It prevents nothing. It is what the next
occasion can be prevented with, if there is a next occasion and a party
to read the record, and the book's claim for the floor on that occasion
is no larger than that.
