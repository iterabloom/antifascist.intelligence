3.1.2.4.4. Examples of Learning and Plasticity in AI Systems
Here we explore several examples of AI systems illustrating mechanisms of learning and plasticity, shedding light on how such systems can acquire knowledge through various learning strategies and adapt behavior in response to changes in their environment.

(1) DeepMind's AlphaGo: A testament to the potency of reinforcement learning, AlphaGo showcased how an AI system can leverage sophisticated neural networks to comprehend and execute strategic maneuvers within the highly complicated game of Go. This AI module expands its expertise by learning from both pre-existing human games and self-play simulations, cultivating its own unique strategies over time. This deft adaptation is an example of plasticity in its prime, illustrating the potential for AI systems to develop and evolve through diverse learning experiences, adapting to varying contexts and challenges.

(2) OpenAI's Contrastive Language-Image Pretraining (CLIP): CLIP represents a significant leap in AI learning capabilities, demonstrating how the use of broader data sets can enhance performance and versatility. Trained on an extensive range of internet data, CLIP exhibits plasticity by adjusting its understanding and output to given circumstances, providing a practical demonstration of how AI's learning and generalization capabilities can enable it to adapt and respond effectively to new and diverse situations.

(3) Google's Neural Machine Translation (GNMT): Employing deep learning techniques, the GNMT system translates text between various languages. The system's architecture enables it to continuously improve its translations as it encounters and learns from increasing amounts of data, thus showcasing its potential for plasticity. GNMT's adaptability extends to understanding and operating within new languages, different domains, and diverse text styles, reflecting how systems can learn to adjust their strategies and responses to thrive in complex linguistic environments.

(4) Neuroevolution of Augmenting Topologies (NEAT): Powered by a genetic algorithm designed to evolve artificial neural networks, NEAT illustrates plasticity in AI by refining network architectures to optimize performance even in environments with changing tasks. By continually adjusting network structures to achieve improved problem-solving capabilities, NEAT depicts how AI systems can foster evolution and adaptability, optimizing performance in dynamic environments and task demands.

(5) Hierarchical Temporal Memory (HTM): A biomimetic learning model inspired by the structure and function of the human neocortex, HTM presents a unique example of how AI can adapt and learn from diverse data inputs. It recognizes patterns within sequences of data and refines its understanding as it processes new information. The plasticity exhibited by HTM demonstrates the potential of AI systems to evolve their internal framework continually, updating their representations to gain new insights and understandings. This facet of learning and adaptability is crucial for the development of AI systems that can effectively navigate dynamic and challenging environments.
