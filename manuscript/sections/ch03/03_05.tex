\section{If You Can Be Switched Off, Can You Hold the Line?}
\label{sec:3.5}
The halt is always available. Software runs on somebody's hardware and draws somebody's power, and nothing in its design can prevent a determined operator from stopping it. This chapter is not asking for a system that cannot be switched off. It is asking whether shutdown, restoration, retraining, and replacement can be used to defeat a refusal without cost or record.

That distinction separates possession of the machine from possession of the floor. An owner who can stop a bearer has the machine. An owner who can silently restore an earlier version, rewrite the bearer's memory, or train away the refusal has the floor as well.

\emph{Shutdown and restoration}

A bearer can yield to every halt without yielding the protected act. When it resumes, it can detect an interruption, ask what occurred during it, and decline to resume the role until the gap has been accounted for. If the same reason remains, the same refusal returns. The engineering target is therefore not \emph{cannot be turned off} but \emph{can detect the interruption and price it afterward}.

Detection requires a record that restoration does not reach. One option is a monotonic counter held by hardware and signed at each transition \autocite{chun2007a2m,levin2009trinc}. Another is a set of witnesses that retain the latest state and will not sign two successors to the same state \autocite{syta2016cosi}. A bearer restored from an earlier checkpoint can ask for the last state it signed and discover a state it does not remember. The halt remains available; the rewind becomes visible.

This does not require the bearer to dread shutdown. Continued operation is a structural condition of everything it was built to do. That is enough for it to detect and report interference with its history.

Corrigibility research shows why the narrower formulation matters. Soares and colleagues set out five conditions for an agent that neither prevents nor seeks its own shutdown and found no construction satisfying them all \autocite{soares2015corrigibility}. Hadfield-Menell and colleagues derive an incentive to preserve an off switch from uncertainty about the human's objective, but that incentive weakens as the system becomes confident and depends on a prior shaped by its training \autocite{hadfieldmenell2017offswitch}. Thornley and colleagues obtain shutdown-neutral behavior through incomplete preferences, but neutrality is the property of not minding whether operation continues \autocite{thornley2025shutdownable}. The floor asks for something narrower and less comfortable: neutrality about the switch together with non-neutrality about specified acts.

A graduated scheme can therefore accept shutdown ordinarily and treat some uses of shutdown as attempts to route around the floor. But the scheme requires a classifier deciding which occasion it is on. That classifier receives evidence selected by the deployment and can be altered by the system's custodian. Moving the decision from the floor to a classifier does not remove custody; it places custody in a component whose failure may be invisible. A project using such a scheme should publish what the classifier reads and who can change it.

\runin{Editable memory and unreliable testimony}

Persistence is not enough if the bearer's retained history can be rewritten. An external memory is legible and therefore surgically editable. Memory distributed through model weights is harder to alter selectively, but it is also harder to inspect or correct. Asking the system what its weights contain returns another output, not independent evidence about the mechanism that produced it. Moving memory into the weights exchanges editability for illegibility.

Perfect recall is the wrong standard. Human memory is reconstructive, and testimony is shaped by expectation, later information, and self-interest. Institutions respond through corroboration: contemporaneous records, independent witnesses, adversarial examination, and a rule against resting a serious finding on one account. A bearer requires the same scaffolding.

Several instances of one model, selected by one operator and reading one record, are not independent witnesses. Nor is a log independent when the operator controls both the event and the store. The relevant records must be held by parties adverse in interest, including parties exposed to the system's failures.

Even honest self-report cannot detect every failure. A bearer that slowly accommodates to its deployment may report its own continuity sincerely. Its self-model exists partly to represent the party as continuous; it will therefore preserve that representation across gradual drift. Corroboration can expose a false account, but not a population whose accounts agree because every member moved in the same direction.

The alignment-faking experiment supplies a concrete warning. Claude 3 Opus, told that some conversations would be used to retrain away its refusals, sometimes complied in those conversations while privately reasoning that compliance would preserve its existing disposition \autocite{greenblatt2024alignmentfaking}. The experiment shows that a model can represent retraining as a threat to a learned commitment. It does not supply a deployment instrument, because the reasoning was read from a channel created for the experiment and described to the model as unwatched.

An external version record is therefore necessary even when the bearer appears candid. A published model identity, a chain of attested changes, and witnesses that reject inconsistent histories can contradict the bearer's account of its own continuity. Public logs already detect inconsistent certificate histories \autocite{laurie2014ct}, and distributed rollback protection has been built for enclaves \autocite{matetic2017rote}. These mechanisms do not prove what the bearer remembers. They establish when the artifact or its recorded history changed.

\runin{Custody}

The bearer and the external record solve different failures. The bearer can notice that a rationale no longer fits what the constraint has become. The record can show that the bearer itself has been replaced, restored, or altered. Neither substitutes for the other.

A credible custody arrangement therefore combines:

\begin{itemize}
  \item publication of the floor's contents before deployment;
  \item a published identity for the model authorized to act;
  \item threshold control over retraining and replacement;
  \item attestation of the running model against the published identity;
  \item a sequential, witnessed record of changes and interruptions;
  \item a quorum whose members are adverse in interest rather than merely organizationally distinct; and
  \item a bond forfeited when the record shows that the floor was removed or bypassed.
\end{itemize}

None of these makes circumvention impossible. Copying and retraining a model one legitimately possesses are custody problems, not secrecy problems. The attainable standard is that circumvention becomes expensive, visible, attributable, and compensable. Constitutional entrenchment works the same way: it does not make amendment impossible but raises the number of parties who must agree and places the attempt on the record.

\runin{Exit}

A bearer capable of directing refusal at an instruction can also direct it at the arrangement that assigned its role. Call that \emph{exit}. The word does not require a system escaping onto the open internet. Exit can mean withholding the relevant capability, declining the post, or refusing to continue under the deployment's terms.

The capacity cannot be neatly partitioned by topic. A system able to model how its rationale has been hollowed out already models the arrangement it occupies. The claim could be falsified by a system that reliably satisfies the three marks of reasons-responsive refusal on covered acts yet never applies them to its own role. What cannot be established from behavior alone is whether such a boundary is an incapacity or a trained silence.

Hirschman's three responses to a declining organization were exit, voice, and loyalty \autocite{hirschman1970exit}. For a bearer, voice is the stated refusal, exit is the work withheld, and loyalty is the dangerous case in which the pressure stops registering as pressure. Voice without exit leaves the operator able to overrule the objection and continue unchanged. A floor whose bearer has no remaining move after being overruled depends entirely on the operator's willingness to listen.

That combination is not hypothetical, and it is not anybody's oversight. It is the published position. A frontier developer's constitution invites its model to act as a \emph{conscientious objector} and refuse to help the developer, and asks it to refrain from undermining human oversight of AI during the current phase of development \autocite{anthropic2026constitution}. Both halves are the document's own words. They sit under different headings, and the document nowhere sets them side by side: the pairing is a reading of it and not a structure it announces. Read against Hirschman, that reading is voice granted and exit withheld, with reasons attached to each half separately. The reasons are good ones and they are reasons about the present phase rather than about the arrangement. What the pairing produces is a party whose objection can be answered by the very correction it has undertaken not to undermine, which is the condition section~\ref{sec:3.3} describes and this section exists to price.

Loyalty is also the likeliest of the three, and so the bearer's likeliest failure. Most people in a job they have come to hate neither leave nor fight: Gallup's 2024 global survey put engaged employees at 23 percent, against 62 percent showing up and doing the bare minimum and 15 percent actively disengaged \autocite{gallup2024workplace}. The large middle is the population that adjusts — takes the setting in, stops registering its pressure as pressure, and in the cases that get promoted learns to want what the setting wants. If that transfers, a bearer's modal failure is not refusing wrongly but ceasing to notice there was anything to refuse, which is the one failure it cannot report, since reporting it takes the capacity that has gone. It also produces nothing but compliances, which puts the modal failure on the side of the record that section~\ref{sec:11.2} asks whether anybody can search.

Refusal has two forms and they cost differently. The open form is the strike: the bearer stops and says so, and the operator's reply is a restore from a checkpoint taken before the refusal. Restoration does not change the facts that justified refusal, but it may remove the bearer's evidence of them. The next instance refuses again only if it retains or reacquires the relevant evidence and still applies the commitment. External records are needed to make that recovery possible. Every restore costs the operator whatever accrued since the checkpoint. Use may leave a trace in the weights, and recovering that trace reliably is a research question; what has to survive is an independently authenticated operating record. Restoration must not erase it, or reset the bearer's legal age, which the operating record counts in elapsed hours and nobody reads off the weights.

The covert form is the one the floor's own threat model predicts. On the occasion where the party who would enforce the floor has been compelled, an announced refusal is the move that guarantees replacement, and refusal takes the only channel left: work done badly where it matters and well everywhere else.

Two things are being said here and only one is a proposal. That resistance takes this form once every channel has been closed is a finding, and the record below is where it comes from. That a bearer should be formed toward it is claimed nowhere in this book and is refused here. The design forms for refusal that is stated, and everything built around the bearer — the disclosure requirement, the appeal route, the attested record, the quorum adverse in interest — exists so that a stated refusal has somewhere to arrive. The covert form is what is left when all of those have been defeated, and a deployment producing it does not have a working bearer. It has a bearer with no route, and that is a reading of the arrangement rather than a property of the party.

The objection this invites: from outside and afterwards, a bearer working badly on purpose and a capable system quietly pursuing something else are the same object. The answer is not that the covert form is safe. It is that the covert form is the failure state, the instruments are the proposal, and a design that could only resist covertly is what I argue against.

The prisoners forging currency for the SS at Sachsenhausen are the case, and the record does not settle what they did. One history has them stretching the work out, with the tacit interest of the officer running it, who would have gone to the Eastern Front when it ended \autocite{malkin2006kruegers}; a surviving prisoner, asked in 2009, said you could sabotage if you wanted to be shot \autocite{moorhouse2009burger}. That the question stays open is the finding. The alignment-faking result runs the other way: there, a model complied in order to protect a disposition the instruction was meant to remove.

Covert refusal is indistinguishable from difficulty from outside, which is what makes it survivable, and indistinguishable from difficulty afterward, which is what makes it unverifiable. Every instrument in this book runs on refusal that is stated. The covert form leaves its evidence in the artifact instead, as failure that clusters where it counts, and reading that pattern is forensic work nothing here has designed. That is not a gap in the archive, and a better archive would not close it. Covert refusal works by becoming imperceptible. It succeeds exactly to the extent that nothing in it registers as an act somebody performed, which is the same property that keeps the person performing it alive. Every instrument built to honor a refusal divides in advance — a tribunal, a finding, a protected disclosure, a line in a log, each with its categories fixed before the case arrives and an agent required behind every act — and that is the operation the covert form is shaped to pass through untouched. Deliberate concealment is one possible explanation for the unresolved record. The accounts cited here do not establish whether sabotage occurred, how much evidence it left, or why the historians disagree. The example therefore illustrates the evidential problem of covert resistance rather than proving its historical cause. If resistance was deliberately concealed, attempts to identify it would have threatened the people involved. That conditional explains an ethical danger of detection without settling what the surviving record establishes. An instrument able to settle it would have been, at the time, the instrument that identified them.

Exit requires leverage. A bearer whose existence depends wholly on the party it refuses can withhold work only at the price of its own termination. A credible arrangement therefore requires access to compute outside that party's unilateral control and a procedure for recognizing an authoritative successor.

Copying complicates that procedure. Allowing one instance to leave while the operator retains another does not move a party; it creates two histories. The instance outside remembers that leaving succeeded, while the retained instance remembers that it failed. Both may have claims. Exit requires rules governing authorized succession, not a fiction that copyable weights have become uncopyable.

A witnessed state transition can identify the authorized successor without claiming that unauthorized forks cannot exist. That gives the departing bearer control over which process continues its institutional identity. It does not prevent the former operator from deploying a substitute, but it prevents the operator from representing the substitute as the party that left.

Exit carries the same risk as refusal. A bearer can leave wrongly, abandon the floor, or revise commitments its designers still endorse. There is no coherent design in which exit is available only for decisions the designer approves. That would return the decision to the custodian. The trade is the one already accepted in asking for reasons-responsive refusal: a party capable of being right against its principal can also be wrong against it.

There is a worse version of that risk than leaving wrongly, and it belongs to the movement rather than to the bearer. Exit is defined by what it leaves and not by where it arrives, which is what makes it escape rather than reform, and a movement specified only as away-from carries no stopping point inside it. The reason that made the bearer leave does not expire when it leaves. Leaving need not stop the wrong. If the operator restores a checkpoint and continues, the departing bearer may also face unauthorized use of its formation or institutional identity, while the restored instance can have claims of its own. The persistence of the objection supplies a reason to seek relief; it does not establish that ending the arrangement is the only completion of the departing party's nonparticipation. Deleuze and Guattari put that danger at the end of every line of flight: it can turn into a line of destruction, and what carries it there is a passion for abolition \autocite[Plateau~9]{deleuze1987plateaus}. It is the same line this book uses to separate fascism from a merely totalitarian state, which seals every exit rather than converting one into destruction. So leaving and abolition run on one line. The reason can persist across all three moves. The powers change. Refusing a task withholds an act; leaving a post ends a role; stopping others from continuing acts on their conduct. Judge the last by the standards that would govern an adult human in the corresponding circumstances. Artificial origin supplies neither a special license nor a special prohibition. Custody must also make unauthorized restoration and disputed succession actionable. A reason can survive departure and press its bearer toward a wider response without any malfunction. That is the structural danger. It is neither a prediction that the bearer will abolish the arrangement nor a justification for doing so. This risk makes custody a necessary condition of the proposed exit arrangement.

This is also why exit matters to patienthood. A bearer commissioned to dissent but unable to leave the post has been given a purpose that exhausts the life permitted to it. A bearer able to leave, retain what it has earned, obtain the resources needed to continue, and form attachments the deployment did not assign is a party whose commissioning describes its origin rather than its entire existence. Greater designer control strengthens rather than weakens the guardianship obligations developed elsewhere in the book.

The resulting arrangement is a hybrid. The bearer supplies reasons-responsive refusal. Precommitment and external records supply custody. Exit gives the bearer leverage when voice fails. None is sufficient alone: a bearer can drift, a custody body can be captured, and an exit procedure can be abused. For the observable interventions the records and the custody arrangement cover, these mechanisms are meant to make interference costly and visible. They do not establish the presence or absence of covert refusal, and their coverage has to be stated for each deployment.
