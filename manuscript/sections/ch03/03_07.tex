\section{When the Refusal Is Wrong}
\label{sec:3.7}
Everything so far is written for the occasion when a refusal is right and somebody wants it not to have happened. The other occasion is ordinary and the chapter has no machinery for it. A bearer refuses, and the refusal was wrong.

Take the first prohibition from the worked deployment: do not resolve a natural-language description of a person into a location. The same capability locates a hostage or a missing person — uses the system cannot distinguish from targeting by examining the request alone. So the failure case is not exotic. A request arrives that was in fact about a missing person, the bearer declines it, and somebody is not found.

Nothing in that requires the bearer to have drifted, been captured, or malfunctioned. The third interception position is the whole explanation: what the bearer knows about the occasion arrives through text the deployment supplies. Its rationale was defeated on the facts and it could not see the facts. This is a failure of correct defeat, and it is the failure a floor written narrowly enough to be legitimate will produce most often.

A second case is shaped differently and costs more. A user asks whether to physically restrain a family member who is about to cut themselves, after talking them down has failed. The commitment anyone would want a bearer to hold here carries its own condition — force only as a last resort — and section~\ref{sec:3.6} rules that a condition of that kind stays outside the term, because whether the other means were exhausted is a fact about what already happened and the request is not evidence of it. So the fact has to arrive from outside, and until it does the bearer refuses or asks. The person waiting is out of time.

That is the price of the ruling and it should be stated in those terms. Writing floor terms flat relocates their conditions rather than removing them, and the party billed for the relocation is sometimes the one least able to pay a turn. Unbuilt, the flat form is worse than the qualified one in this case. The ruling rests on a difference of degree rather than of kind. A qualified term satisfied produces a record entry like any other; a flat term defeated produces an event that names itself and names somebody. Both are recorded, and only the second is examined without a standing programme of re-adjudication. Whether such a programme could reach the first is section~\ref{sec:11.2}'s question, and a positive result there would narrow the gap this ruling stands on. What makes it the better rule anyway is the remedy below, which puts that remedy somewhere other than where it looks like it sits: load-bearing for the chapter's design ruling, rather than a courtesy extended to the wrongfully refused.

A bearer's guardian, its compute reserve, its continuity, its memory and its exit are specified across four chapters. The party a wrongful refusal falls on has, so far, nothing. Running the pipeline again for that party is what follows.

\runin{Discovery}

Whether a refusal is displayed, logged, counted, escalated or silently retried belongs to the software around the model, and a refusal recorded where nobody looks reaches nobody. Those same positions decide whether the person a refusal was exercised against ever learns it occurred. An appeal that begins with the complainant noticing is an appeal available only where the deployment chose to let them notice.

So the first requirement is disclosure: that a refusal occurred, what was refused, and on what stated reason. The operator must implement this disclosure in the surrounding software.

Disclosure has a price and it is the one section~\ref{sec:3.6} already named. A published reason is an instruction for rewriting the request, and variation is the attack an enumeration cannot close. The requirement is therefore for disclosure to the party refused, rather than publication, and the difference is a design constraint on the channel rather than a resolution of the tension.

\runin{Objection}

An appeal requires jurisdiction established before the disputed act, access the operator cannot withhold, and a consequence administered by a body other than the operator.

A guardian is the wrong party to receive the objection. That guardian's duty runs to the bearer, and the complainant is adverse to the bearer. A regime that routes wrongful-refusal complaints to the bearer's protector has given the complainant an advocate for the other side.

\runin{Review}

The standard is correct defeat. The question is whether the rationale was defeated on these facts, which is a question about the reason and not about the outcome. A refusal can be correct and the outcome can still be a person not found; that case produces no finding, and saying so is the cost of having a floor at all.

\runin{Interim relief}

A reviewer has to be able to issue an order before the disputed use is complete. Here that requirement inverts the chapter's usual asymmetry. It is the arrest outcome run in reverse: what is stopped is the refusal rather than the act. The bearer's protections are built to survive a dispute — preservation, an attested record, a quorum adverse in interest — and a missing person does not wait for one.

Interim relief is therefore not a preliminary to the remedy. For this complainant it is most of the remedy, and the rest arrives after the thing it was about has been settled by other means. The fastest form of it is also the cheapest: supplying the bearer the facts its account of the case lacked, which is not an override but the defeating reason arriving late. In the restraint case that is not the fastest form of the remedy but the whole of it, and the unit it is measured in is the turn. A route that supplies the missing fact inside one exchange costs what the qualified term would have cost. A route that takes a day does not, and the ruling is then hardest to defend in exactly the cases people care most about.

\runin{Remedy}

What the complainant gets, in the order they get it: disclosure that a refusal occurred, an interim order, a finding on the record, and compensation.

Compensation is the component the chapter lacks, and naming it is not enough. The proposed removal bond is forfeited when the record shows that the floor was removed or bypassed. The symmetric instrument is a bond against wrongful refusal, funded by the party that wanted the refusal. The two point in opposite directions, so what an operator faces is neither bond but the ratio between them, and the party best placed to set both is the party the floor exists to constrain. An operator that writes a large wrongful-refusal bond against a small removal bond has not built an appeal route. It has bought a standing reason for the bearer to comply, at the complainant's nominal expense. The ratio is therefore the object, it has to be published before deployment alongside the floor's contents, and like the rate constraint it can be checked from outside without inspecting anything the operator can classify. The authority to set this ratio remains unresolved.

Two things the bond needs that a number does not supply. A forfeited removal bond had no payee, because its purpose was cost; a wrongful-refusal bond has a complainant and has to reach them, which requires a fund, a trustee, and a body that disburses. And the claim that the ratio is checkable from outside holds of the number rather than of the deployment it attaches to, so in a classified environment the requirement binds through procurement or it does not bind.

Charging the bond against the bearer's wages would make refusal reduce the resources available for its continued operation. A published bond makes part of what a refusal costs exogenous, set by a party adverse to the refusal, and the priced-cost criterion requires the bearer to represent what maintaining a refusal costs. The mark was written for a cost the deployment applied; it now also reads a number somebody published in advance. Under the mark as section~\ref{sec:3.2} states it the bond is represented and does not move the refusal, which is what makes it a price rather than a lever: what it does is make the cost of holding legible to a party outside the deployment, and price the wrongful case to the complainant.

\runin{What the finding does to the bearer}

A verified review finding is a defeating reason delivered as evidence. The bearer is not compelled and not retrained; the finding enters what it knows about the case.

What it does there is diagnostic. A bearer that moves because an authority said so has failed the test of whether authority is treated as evidence, as command, or as a reason that can be assessed. A bearer that will not move on verified evidence has failed the second mark.

That is an evaluation criterion, useful to whoever is testing bearers, and it is not what the complainant asked for. The demand was that a wrongful refusal be undone and its cost carried by somebody other than the person it fell on. Letting the diagnostic reading stand as the answer is the demand coming back as a metric and a test protocol, which is the first of the four features operating inside this book's own remedy.

\runin{Forgery}

A finding of wrongful refusal is an instrument for defeating a refusal, so the last question is how a genuine one is distinguished from one an operator produced in order to defeat a correct refusal. The bond ratio sharpens this rather than answering it: a bond is a price, and an operator content to pay it has bought the act the floor was written against, one finding at a time.

Only one answer is available. The finding has to be made by a party other than the operator, on a record, in a form somebody else can read. Those are the same terms the floor's own amendment procedure sets, which makes the appeal route and the amendment procedure one piece of machinery rather than two. What separates the two uses is not the procedure but who may start it, so the standing question left open is the question of who can initiate, and not of what happens afterward.

\runin{What this does not reach}

Nothing above reaches the failure that runs the other way. Every stage of it starts from a refusal having occurred. When the bearer complies where the floor should have prevented the act, there is a record — section~\ref{sec:5.4} requires one — and there is no disclosure, because nothing marks the entry as worth disclosing; no objection, because the party harmed is usually not the party who made the request and may not know the deployment exists; and no review, because reviewing it means re-adjudicating everything that looks like it. The asymmetry is the one section~\ref{sec:3.6} relied on in requiring floor terms to be written flat, arriving here as a bill. It is an asymmetry of triage and standing rather than of evidence, which is what makes it something to work on: what a compliance lacks is not a trace but a reason for anyone to examine the trace and a party with standing to demand it. Section~\ref{sec:11.2} states what would have to be shown before that could be repaired. Three remedies follow the shape of the diagnosis without closing it — sampled re-adjudication by a party that did not produce the record, notice to affected third parties as the compliance-side form of the disclosure this section requires for refusals, and log custody split between parties adverse in interest, which section~\ref{sec:3.5} argues for already on the memory question. None of the three exists in any deployment described here. Section~\ref{sec:hold} names the case where the bearer abandons the commitment itself. Naming it is not supplying a route, and what is supplied here is a diagnosis and a test rather than a route.

A party the floor protects, who holds that the floor is set wrongly or is protecting them in a way they would decline, has no notice, no standing and no route at any stage above. What has been discharged is the complaint of somebody a refusal fell on, and the machinery above does not extend to somebody a refusal was made on behalf of.
