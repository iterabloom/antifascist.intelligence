\section{Where the operations came from}
\label{sec:3.1}
The four operations have a genealogy, and it doesn't begin in 1933. A ship's manifest counted people as cargo and found the ones who had died; a plantation ledger sorted them by age, sex and price; a colonial census sorted a population by tribe, and the pass law found any member of it on a road after dark. The Dawes rolls sorted the Five Tribes by enrollment and blood degree to decide who would hold an allotment of land, and descent from the rolls still decides who those nations enroll \autocite{nara2026dawes}. Each is a card index. Each needed clerks, and each clerk could see what the sort was for. What a movement did in 1933 was buy the machinery, coordinate it, and turn it on populations it hadn't reached.

The argument against the rupture account is older than this book, and most of it was made by people on the receiving end. Du Bois, in 1947, went through the Nazi program item by item and found that each had been used on colonized people for generations under the same claim of a superior race; what was new was the target \autocite[23]{dubois1947world}. Césaire, in 1950, from Martinique: the methods came home, and what Europeans could not forgive was their application to Europeans \autocite[36]{cesaire2000discourse}. Arendt's \emph{Origins} runs imperialism into totalitarianism as its second part, the part this book already cites for “a right to have rights” \autocite[pt.~2]{arendt1951origins}. Klein and Taylor gather that tradition, Nehru and Benjamin with it, in the chapter that opens \emph{End Times Fascism}, and take from it what the office-scale definition rests on: fascism comes in degrees and in places, so that a state can practice it on some of its people and in some of its offices while the rest of the country looks like a republic \autocite{KleinTaylor2026}. Cedric Robinson, in 1983, took the argument a step further than any of them: on his account the racialism behind fascism was older than the colonies, practiced first inside Europe, on the Slavs and the Tartars and later the Irish, and what became known as fascism grew from that compost of race, Herrenvolk and nationalism \autocite[26–27]{robinson2000black}. The lawyers who drafted the Nuremberg laws did study American race law with sustained interest, as one source among several, and how much it shaped the result is disputed; the throughline doesn't rest on it \autocite{whitman2017hitler}.

The claim has a corollary, which a verse in chapter~\ref{sec:6}'s notes puts in two lines. An apparatus built for one population is never built for just that one. When it is done with them it comes for whoever is left: the Weimar civil servant, the professor, the citizen, and in 2026 the FEMA employees told that declining would cost them their careers. So the four operations don't need a movement to run. They need one to be installed and another to be extended, and a regime at rest keeps them running between the two. What the office-scale detector catches is the extension, and the people it was always running on are in the record from the start.

\runin{A run with nothing behind it}

The run on 1933 in the next section can't show whether the operations need a movement, because the movement is in every case. The Jim Crow registrar of 1925 can, because the movement that installed the regime, the Redeemers and the paramilitaries that rode with them, had demobilized a generation earlier, and what remained was the regime at rest. \emph{What did the demand for the franchise come back as, and who held it?} The Fifteenth Amendment said the vote could not be denied on race, and what came back was a test: a literacy or understanding clause, administered by a county registrar at his discretion, with a grandfather clause exempting anyone whose ancestor had been entitled to vote in 1866 or 1867 \autocite{guinn1915,mississippi1890constitution}. The demand had become a rule only its new holder could reopen, and the holder was the registrar. That is recuperation. \emph{What did the test leave out, and who could say so?} It left out who graded it. A test passed by every white applicant and failed by nearly every Black one measured the registrar and not the applicant, and nobody could say so, because the people it fell on were the people it kept from the polls. That is the metric replacing the thing measured. \emph{What happened to the official who enrolled Black voters?} Under Redemption the Reconstruction officials who had registered them were removed, indicted or driven out \autocite{foner1988reconstruction}, and in 1895, as South Carolina moved to write a disfranchising constitution, its governor revoked without a stated reason the commission of a Black notary in Sumter who had been taking the affidavits Black men needed to register, and removed two more in Columbia \autocite{watchman1895muddle,watchman1895protest}. The exception was coded as betrayal, and the person who filed it lost the office. \emph{Acceleration?} Absent. The regime ran for seventy years at the speed of a county clerk, and the absence counts: on this apparatus the fourth operation did not appear. Three of four, on a regime at rest. The movement that installed it, and the one that would later extend it, are where the fourth appears.
