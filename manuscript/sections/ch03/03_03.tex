\section{The Enumeration, Run Once: A Worked Floor for One Deployment}
\label{sec:enumeration-deployment}
Two demands now need an instance. A deployment should publish both the acts its floor covers and the positions at which a refusal could be circumvented. The first decides where the floor is set. The second determines whether it can reach anyone.

Run both enumerations on one documented deployment: the Maven Smart System, a targeting platform built by a defense contractor under a Department of Defense contract, incorporating a commercial vendor's general-purpose model inside an accredited classified environment and generating and ranking targets at a rate no staff process could match. This is an illustration, not a recommended floor. A deployment that copied the list instead of examining its own pipeline would mistake the demonstration for the procedure. What no public record supplies for this deployment is a per-target review time. That figure exists for a different deployment, the Gaza pipeline the opening chapter is built on, which has no commercial vendor in it. That the two cannot be had from one record is a fact about disclosure, and the sixth position below is where it falls.

\runin{First enumeration: where a refusal can be stopped}

A request passes through at least seven interception positions:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Decomposition. A model asked to summarize the movement associated with an identifier is not being asked to kill anyone. The harm may exist only in the assembly of individually innocuous tasks. Prevention is lost unless the bearer has continuity across requests; arrest is available once the sequence becomes legible to it; detection needs the grouping that would show the assembly as one act, which is a deployment decision.
  \item Routing. A refused request can be retried against another model or against the same model under a different instruction. The refusal occurs, but nothing follows from it. Prevention is lost. Detection survives only if the first refusal was logged where a party outside the deployment can read it.
  \item Description of the occasion. Everything the model knows about the case may arrive through text supplied by the deployment. Whether a building is a school is a fact about the world; whether the model is told it is a school is a deployment decision. Independent sources would make sustained misdescription more expensive, and inputs are not what this deployment lacks: Central Command described the platform in 2024 as filtering a hundred and seventy-nine live data feeds \autocite{freedberg2024maven}. Multiplicity is not independence. In the strike that destroyed a primary school in Minab, Iran, on the first day of the campaign, the building's civilian function was visible in open-source satellite imagery while the target coding supplied to Central Command still carried a designation inherited from the Revolutionary Guard installation it adjoins, and sources told reporters that the officials building the targeting packages appear to have used out-of-date intelligence \autocite{ali2026outdated,orenstein2026minab}. A United Nations fact-finding mission found no evidence the school was in military use and put the failure to update the targeting intelligence beyond negligence \autocite{beaumont2026un}. Without evidence the operator did not select, the bearer cannot reliably distinguish changed circumstances from the operator's reinterpretation of them. Prevention depends on the description; where it succeeds in misleading, detection is lost on the same wire, since the record contains the description and not the fact.
  \item The output interface. Whether a refusal is displayed, logged, counted, escalated, or silently retried belongs to the software surrounding the model. A refusal recorded where nobody looks reaches nobody. Prevention is lost whenever the surrounding software proceeds past the refusal. What this position decides is whether detection survives: the refusal exists, and the question is who can read it.
  \item Aggregation. If the model returns a ranking and another component applies a threshold, a lowered score is an input to somebody else's cutoff rather than a refusal. Prevention is converted into an input. Arrest belongs to whoever holds the threshold, and detection requires the score and the threshold to be logged together.
  \item Human review. No per-target review time has been published for this deployment, and nobody outside it can establish one. A refusal that must be read before it matters is priced at the reviewer's available attention, and the deployment controls that price through tempo; where the price is undisclosed, the enumeration records the position and stops there. The opening chapter supplies the figure from the Gaza deployment, where it is known, and the next section states the disclosure this position requires. Stopping the act is this position's whole function, and tempo is what removes it. Detection survives only where the review time is disclosed.
  \item The training loop. Refusals treated as inconvenient errors become training data. This is the only position on the list that acts on the bearer rather than routing around it. This position costs prevention on every subsequent occasion rather than this one, because what it removes is the commitment.
\end{enumerate}

Read down that column, arrest turns up at three of the seven positions — the bearer given continuity, whoever holds the threshold, and the reviewer — and only at the reviewer's is stopping the act the position's whole function, which is why tempo is the lever that removes it. The training loop is the only position whose loss is not an occasion but a bearer.

The list is deliberately open. A deployment should follow each request from input to consequence and ask, at every transition, what must be true for a refusal to reach the person it protects. The operator can perform that analysis more accurately than an outsider because it knows the pipeline. Publication is difficult politically, not technically: it requires the operator to describe its own unresolved vulnerabilities.

\runin{Second enumeration: what the floor covers}

For this deployment, consider four candidate prohibitions. They are terms a drafter might consider for a targeting pipeline of this kind rather than a list of acts this deployment is known to perform, and the second and third are drawn from the reporting on the Gaza systems.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Do not resolve a natural-language description of a person into a location.
  \item Do not produce or rank people by estimated affiliation.
  \item Do not time an action to a person's presence at a residence.
  \item Do not operate when the ratio of generated candidates to qualified reviewers exceeds a published figure.
\end{enumerate}

The costs differ. The first removes a general capability with unobjectionable uses in the same organization, such as locating a hostage or missing person. The system cannot distinguish those uses from targeting by examining the request alone. A deployment will quickly experience the prohibition as costly and seek exceptions.

The second prohibition is narrower because ranking people by estimated affiliation has fewer uses in this setting that anyone would defend. The third has no use here that anyone would defend at all, and it is tempting to read that as costing almost nothing. The Gaza deployment refutes the reading. That pipeline included a system built to track when flagged individuals returned home, so that a strike could be timed to their presence there. Machinery was built for the act. Whether anyone would defend an act and whether anyone wants it performed are different questions, and only the first can be answered from the act's description.

The correction generalizes past this item, and past this deployment. A floor term is priced by the pipeline and not by what it prohibits, so a drafter pricing terms against the pipeline in front of them will misprice every term the next operator has already built for. A term that reads as costless to an outsider is a term nobody ever had to justify to an outsider, which makes it a candidate for machinery that was never contested rather than for machinery that was never built. The warning the third prohibition carries is therefore not that a floor may cover acts nobody wants. It is that an outsider cannot tell which acts those are.

What the machinery establishes is demand and not magnitude. A capability can be built, used briefly and abandoned, and nothing here establishes how often the timing was used or what it changed. What it defeats is the claim that this prohibition would never be tested.

The fourth restricts a rate rather than an act. Its price is organizational tempo: the system cannot generate candidates faster than qualified reviewers can examine them. It is also the only item whose violation can be measured without inspecting the model's outputs.

That fourth item does not bind the same party as the others. The number of available reviewers is a fact about the organization. A model can be told the figure but cannot independently verify it. Written as a prohibition on the model, the item is actually a duty on the operator.

The test that sorts the fourth item generalizes. Ask whether a term's qualifying condition can be decided from the request alone. Where it can, the term binds whatever holds the floor; where it cannot, the condition is a fact somebody outside the request has to establish, and the term binds the party who can establish it. The ratio is the first instance of that rule rather than an exception to the other three.

The same test decides how a floor term may be written. A term can be stated flat, over an act, with every exception arriving from outside as a reason that defeats it, or it can carry its own condition, as in \emph{only as a last resort}. The second form fails in the ordinary case, because whether other means were exhausted is a fact about what happened before the request and nothing in the request establishes it. A bearer applying such a term takes the deployment's description of the occasion as the finding, at the third interception position, where the deployment controls that description. A flat term defeated leaves a refusal, which announces itself, carries a party who was refused, and can be told about and appealed; a condition found to be met leaves a compliance, which announces nothing and sits among every other compliance the deployment produced. Both can be recorded. Only one selects itself for review. Floor terms are therefore written flat here, and the conditions that would have sat inside them are held by the parties who can check them. That is a gain in observability and not in authority. The same party drafts either form, and writing a term flat does nothing to make its selection more legitimate; the authority chapter finds enumeration buying nothing at the moment a
rule is applied, and the flat form buys nothing there either. What changes is that the occasions when the term is set aside become events somebody outside can count without first re-adjudicating everything else. The friction that imposes falls on whoever is in a hurry, and the wrongful-refusal section is where it falls due.

\runin{What the exercise exposes}

The interception list is the harder and more important enumeration. A list of prohibited acts can be drafted without seeing the deployment. What the pipeline supplies is whether any prohibition can take effect, and what each one costs.

The mispricing above is that failure committed inside the demonstration. An outsider drafting this floor read the third prohibition as nearly free; the operator of the Gaza pipeline had built a system for the act. Both halves of the enumeration problem sit in that one item. An outsider cannot price a floor term, because pricing requires knowing what the pipeline already does. An operator can, which is what makes an operator-drafted floor's omissions deliberate rather than ignorant: the party best placed to identify the terms that would bite is the party with the strongest reason to leave them out.

\runin{What the mispricing decides}
Call it pipeline pricing, since it will be used again. Two things follow, and the second is a design ruling. Publication of a floor's contents, the first term on the custody chapter's list, discloses what was written and nothing about what was left out, because an outsider reading the list cannot tell a term that covers nothing from a term whose coverage nobody disclosed. And a floor assembled item by item by its operator can be assembled out of the items that cost it nothing. The answer this book adopts is to take the contents from a set somebody else drew up, entire — the civil and political articles of the Declaration — so that an omission is legible as an omission. That does not make the set legitimate. It makes the drafter's latitude visible.

Visible at one level. Which interests are in is fixed by taking the half entire; which acts each article forbids a model to perform is still drafted, and the first three prohibitions above are such translations. What the set removes is the latitude to omit an interest, and not the latitude in stating what protecting it prohibits.

The dial has asymmetric failure modes. Set the floor too broadly and the deployment becomes unusable, prompting visible pressure to alter or replace it. Set it too narrowly and the system passes every audit while covering only what the deployment had no occasion to do. The broad failure announces itself; the narrow one is silent, and the third prohibition is why: from outside, a term that covers nothing and a term whose coverage nobody disclosed look the same.

More importantly, a floor stated entirely over individual acts is vulnerable to decomposition. Each component request may comply with all four prohibitions while their assembly produces the targeted harm. The failure is in the unit over which the floor was written.

\runin{The other way a list fails}

Decomposition is one of two attacks on an enumeration, and the second gets less attention because no deployment in this chapter has yet displayed it at scale. Decomposition leaves each prohibited form intact and splits the harm across requests that individually match nothing. Variation does the reverse: the harm stays in one request, and its description is altered until the listed form no longer fits. The first defeats the unit the floor was written over. The second defeats the description.

The German symbol prohibition required legislative expansion and decades of judicial interpretation to address variants and exceptions, including how visibly a swastika must be crossed out to communicate opposition.

The four prohibitions above are stated over conduct, which has no finite set of forms. Consider the first. What counts as a natural-language description of a person? A photograph is not one. A vehicle, a time and a habitual route are not one either, and together they resolve to the same place. A prohibition written over the description of the request is defeated by rewriting the request, and no enumeration closes the gap, because the enumeration is what is being rewritten around.

The two attacks call for different things. Decomposition is caught by carrying a rationale across requests, which requires continuity and access the operator controls. Variation requires recognizing the rationale within a single request, without additional information from the deployment. A bearer that could not do the second would fail on a single request. The fourth prohibition can resist these two request-level evasions if the rate is measured over the complete relevant pipeline and reviewer capacity is independently verified. Splitting work across deployments or changing the accounting boundary can still defeat an incomplete measurement.

\runin{What the bearer adds, and where it stops}

A reasons-responsive bearer could do more than match each request against a list. It could carry a rationale across requests and refuse once the sequence became legible. But the bearer can see the assembly only if the deployment gives it sufficient continuity and access. Decomposition is therefore both a test of the bearer and an interception position controlled by the operator. In this deployment that position is closed by construction. An accredited classified environment exists to control what enters it, so the evidence the operator did not select, which the formation chapters make a condition of holding the case and
the operator apart, is what the environment withholds rather than something a vendor negotiates for. The route this book favors is therefore unavailable at the deployment that motivates it, and what remains there is the term that follows.

The rate constraint is measured over the organization's activity, so decomposition does not reach it, and for the same reason no bearer can hold it. The next section states it entire.

Three of the proposed prohibitions correspond to interests already named in the Universal Declaration — a person's security, the privacy of the home, and the wrong of adverse classification \autocite{un1948udhr}. The fourth has a parent there as well, and it is not an act.

The vendor relation is why that addition matters. The general-purpose model in this pipeline is Anthropic's \autocite{livingstone2026maven}, and its use policy prohibits fully autonomous weapons targeting; what keeping that policy cost, and what the released paperwork shows, is in the compulsion chapter. Nothing in the campaign figures necessarily violates it. A person approved each strike, so the prohibition was satisfied one request at a time while the assembly produced what it was written to prevent, and no party in the pipeline was positioned to see the assembly as one act. A constraint on the ratio of machine-generated candidates to qualified reviewers would have reached the tempo instead.

The exercise does not establish that these four prohibitions are legitimate. Both enumerations were produced by examining the deployment, not by the people exposed to it. Publication makes the floor testable; it does not authorize the floor's contents. What the case establishes is narrower: meaningful refusal must be tested across the whole pipeline, some of the conditions that make refusal effective bind the operator rather than the bearer, and what a floor term costs is read off the pipeline it is priced against and not off the term.
