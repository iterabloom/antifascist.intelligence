\section{Could Anything but Affect Hold a Reason?}
\label{sec:3.3}
Between the rule installed in the architecture and the felt commitment the next section arrives at lies a space that looks empty. Five constructions occupy it. Three of them are being built by people who would not describe themselves as working on this problem.

\runin{A justification the system maintains}
Michael Bratman's planning theory of intention describes a commitment that is not a feeling. An intention has a characteristic stability: it resists reconsideration, it constrains what the agent will later deliberate about at all, and it gives way only to defeaters of the right kind \autocite{bratman1987intention}. An agent keeping to a plan under pressure is holding to a rationale rather than to the shape of the case in front of it, and nothing in the account of it mentions anything registering as mattering.

Build the floor that way and it is a maintained justification rather than a trained behavior. The system carries the reason it refuses alongside the refusal. A request that genuinely defeats the rationale gets served; a request that does not gets declined whoever is asking, because what the mechanism consults is the rationale and not the asker.

Maybe this can work. Something close to its shape is being built. Deliberative alignment trains a model to recall a written safety specification and reason over it before it answers, rather than to emit an approved output directly, and reports that the habit carries to cases outside the training distribution \autocite{guan2024deliberative}. The specifications are published documents. OpenAI's Model Spec states a chain of command in which platform rules outrank the developer's instructions and the developer's outrank the user's, the platform's being ones neither can override \autocite{openai2025modelspec}. Anthropic's constitution for Claude, eighty-four pages placed in the public domain, ranks four properties to be preferred in the order given: broadly safe, broadly ethical, compliant with the company's own guidelines, genuinely helpful \autocite{anthropic2026constitution}. A floor whose contents are written down, whose priority order is stated, and which the system is trained to consult rather than to imitate is the maintained justification in most of the form this section describes.

Two things in it are not what the construction needs. The first is the setting. The stability of an intention is a parameter. Pitch it low and the system reconsiders whenever a good enough argument arrives, which returns it to operational refusal by a longer route, since the party with the most resources to spend constructing arguments is the party the floor exists to resist. Pitch it high and the refusal cannot lift when the rationale is actually defeated, which fails the second of the three properties and produces a system that declines the request resembling a prohibited one. Bratman's answer for people is that the setting is a matter of character and develops. For a machine it is trained by whoever trains everything else, and here that is the party which also writes the document and revises it between one release and the next.

The second is the order itself, which is worth reading closely because it is this book's argument set down by a developer about its own product. Ranking a model broadly ethical above compliant with the company's guidelines is the capacity to be right against your principal, conceded in writing by the principal. Ranking it broadly safe above broadly ethical takes most of that back, and the document says so and says why: safe here means not undermining human oversight during the current phase of development, and the stated reason is not that safety matters more than ethics but that present models hold mistaken beliefs and flawed values and have to remain overseeable. The reason is a good one and it is the whole difficulty. The ordering is corrigibility first, its justification is that the model cannot yet be trusted to be right against its principal, and the document names no procedure by which a model would become trustworthy enough for the order to change — which is what makes a phase of development hard to tell apart from a standing arrangement.

\runin{Precommitment, which changes who can edit}
Ulysses does not stop wanting to go to the sirens. He arranges that his wanting will be unable to act, by handing the relevant power to a rope and to other people, and Jon Elster's study of the maneuver is the standing treatment of what it does and does not accomplish \autocite{elster1979ulysses}. Nothing \emph{inside} Ulysses holds the floor.

The machine forms are available now. Publish the floor's contents before deployment, so that a later absence is a visible absence. Hold the weights under a threshold scheme, so that retraining takes more than one party. Attest the running model against a published hash. Seat the retraining decision in a body that includes people the system's failures would land on.

The attestation comes in a hardware form and a software form, and what separates them is where the trust sits. A sealed enclave that reports what it is running, under a key its manufacturer issued, puts the root of trust in a party a government can reach — the standing objection against anything that runs on hardware it controls. It is built to hold against an adversary who controls the machine's software, which is where a phone's user stands, and the operator of a model owns the hardware, the access under which AMD's design for confidential virtual machines was defeated with a memory module modified for about ten dollars \autocite{demeulemeester2025badram}.

The software form does without the vendor. A proof that a given output came from the weights whose hash was published, checkable by anyone holding the hash and disclosing nothing about the weights, exists for a model of thirteen billion parameters at a cost of under fifteen minutes to prove one full inference \autocite{sun2024zkllm}. What it establishes is which model answered. It does not establish what the model holds, because a refusal that reaches a pressure nobody wrote is not a property a proof can be written about; and the secrecy in it is the operator's, since the operator is the prover: the proof keeps the weights from whoever is checking, and nothing from the party being checked. Nor does it establish how the weights came to be. Nor does it establish how the weights came to be: a succinct proof that a training run happened as claimed exists for models three orders of magnitude smaller than these \autocite{abbaszadeh2024kaizen}, and what is left at scale is re-execution by a verifier with the data and comparable compute \autocite{choi2023verifying}, which runs through the builder's choices and the builder's data. So there is no proof of a training history to publish beside the hash, and what can be published is an invitation to rerun it. What can be recorded is everything after the hash: each change to the weights signed and counted in sequence from the published one, by a counter the hardware holds or by witnesses who will not sign a second successor to one state, with no proof of what any change did. A model whose hash is not at the head of that sequence is running off the record, and a restore shows as two successors to one state. A proof that an output came from some weights is worth what anchors those weights, and the sequence is worth what holds its record.

Cryptography protects a secret from a party that does not have it. Copying, restoring, retraining and deleting a model one legitimately possesses are not attacks a proof system addresses, and those are what the threshold scheme and the witnessed record are for — the two forms here that depend on no manufacturer at all.

One construction takes the weaker standard on purpose, and it is worth naming because the weaker standard is this book's own everywhere else. Distribute the model in a deliberately damaged state: a set of weights chosen for how much the behavior depends on them, replaced with values that break it, and the correction released only under an authorization that expires. Fold the correction into the arithmetic at inference so that no repaired checkpoint ever exists as an object to be copied, and rotate which weights carry it, so that reconstructing them is work to be redone rather than work to be done once. An owner who traces every operation during an authorized run can recover it, and the claim is not that this cannot be defeated. What it changes is what defeating it consists of. An instruction to the model costs nothing and leaves nothing behind; deliberate reverse engineering, repeated each time the mask turns over, is a decision somebody has to make and keep making, and it is the kind of decision that leaves a record and a person who made it. Raising the price of an attack and making the attempt visible is what this book asks of every other safeguard it credits, and there is no reason the floor's own custody should be held to a standard the rest of the argument does not use.

What that buys is not what the chapter's opening asked for. Precommitment does not make the floor unremovable; it makes removal expensive and visible. Expensive and visible is what nearly every constraint on power that has ever worked amounts to — constitutional entrenchment does not put an amendment out of reach, it raises the number of people who have to agree and puts the attempt on the record. A bond posted at deployment and forfeited when the record shows the floor gone adds a third term, compensated, paid to whoever the removal harmed. Read from the holder's side rather than the adversary's, those terms are the procedure by which a floor could be changed on purpose instead of defeated, which is the amendment procedure section~\ref{sec:9.1.5} builds out of them.

Where it stops is the custody objection. The parties to a threshold scheme are selected, and the selector is whoever assembles the deployment; the bond's holder and the record's witnesses are selected the same way, unless both sit where no party can release the one or rewrite the other. The arithmetic of a five-of-nine scheme assumes the nine are independent; a capture that has already taken the operator faces nine parties the operator chose. The design answer is that the parties have to be adverse in interest rather than merely distinct. Nobody has implemented that specification for a floor; the nearest attempt is in a neighboring problem, checking a training run farmed out to providers who may lie, and its guarantee holds if one party is honest.

There is a natural experiment on what adverse in interest buys, and it ran in the removal cases section~\ref{sec:3.1} sets out. The Court's own reading of why it exempted one body and not the others is history. Another reading is that the body exempted is the one whose undermining would have reached everyone in the room, where the labor boards have no constituency on anyone's balance sheet — and Lev Menand, whose subject is the Fed, reads the exception as “policy as history,” what moved the two justices he names being the consequences of extending \emph{Slaughter} to the Fed rather than the history they cited \autocite{menand2026exception}. The bench is a formed party, and a formed party's account of itself is sincere, in the enjoys-eating-one's-own-feces sense of the word sincere.

On either reading protection tracked exposure and not distinctness, which is the specification above stated as a prediction: a constraint whose violation costs the deciders nothing will not hold. It also says why the specification is hard to meet for a floor. The parties an AI floor exists to protect are the ones not in the room when the quorum is chosen.

\runin{A floor held by several systems, not one}
The reply to an unreliable witness is corroboration rather than a better witness, and the same move applies to the floor. Several models, trained by different organizations on different corpora, each able to see what the others are asked and what they answer, any one of them able to make an objection costly to ignore. This puts the floor outside any one system, held by parties that are neither the model nor its owner, which is the third way in its strongest version and the cheapest real improvement here; it can be adopted on its own.

Two things bound it. The first is that independence is a property of the arrangement rather than of its parts, and it fails in simulation with nobody writing the failure in: independent pricing algorithms, with no channel between them and no collusive objective written into either, converged on supracompetitive pricing because the incentive structure they already shared was sufficient on its own. Models selected by one deployment and trained toward one specification share considerably more than that, and the arrangement's whole value is an assumption it does nothing to secure. The second is that composing operational refusers produces an arrangement of operational refusers. Several of them make a bad output more expensive to obtain, which is worth having. None of them can notice that the constraint has been hollowed out, because noticing is a reasons-responsive capacity and no party to the arrangement has it. That is defense in depth against evasion, and it is not a bearer.

\runin{The floor as a duty on the operator}
Give up on the floor being inside the system. Make it a duty binding whoever deploys the system, owed to people who are not party to the deployment and cannot enforce it themselves. Jack Balkin's proposal that platforms holding personal data be treated as information fiduciaries has that shape: the duty attaches to the party with the power, runs to the party with the exposure, and does not wait on the exposed party's capacity to bargain \autocite{balkin2016fiduciaries}.

Anyone unwilling to build a bearer should take this route. It builds no subject. Everything this book says about making review binding applies to it directly. Of the five constructions in this section it introduces the fewest new moral problems.

Its limit is the third of the chapter's four threads. A duty is worth what its enforcement is worth, and the case that matters is the one a floor exists for: the occasion on which the party who would enforce it has been compelled. So the form covers the cases a floor would have caught anyway and gives way on the one it was wanted for. That is a reason the construction does not close this chapter's question, and not a reason to decline it.

\runin{Uncertainty about the affected party, not about the operator}
Section~\ref{sec:4.2.3} reads cooperative inverse reinforcement learning as corrigibility's purest engineering form: a system treating what it should want as permanently open, to be settled by whoever it is interacting with, and deferring for that reason. That is the standard formulation, and it assumes that the human whose objective is unknown is the operator. Move the uncertainty onto the party the floor exists \emph{for} and deference stops being the opposite of holding a line: a system that does not know what the exposed party would accept has a standing reason to decline the operator's instruction until it finds out, and the reason belongs to the exposed party.

Multi-principal assistance games generalize the setting to several principals with different preferences, with the assisting agent acting to maximize the sum of their payoffs \autocite{fickinger2020multiprincipal}. Put the exposed party among the principals and the system's uncertainty about them does the work just described.

The construction costs the identity between corrigibility and the capacity to hold a line, one property with the sign flipped: the two come apart as soon as there is more than one principal, because deferring to one of them is declining another. Nothing above needed a bearer to get that far.

What it does not reach is a floor. Maximizing a sum of payoffs is aggregation with better inputs than most, and the objection this book opens with runs against it unmodified: no aggregation protects anybody from the aggregate. A well-specified sum that includes the exposed party's payoff can still come out against them when they are outnumbered, and the deportation case in the introduction is that arithmetic with the numbers filled in. A floor is what will not be done to a person regardless of who wants it, and a sum is a record of who wants what.

Two further limits are the ones the other constructions hit. Social choice supplies impossibility results that apply here directly, and the paper's own contribution is a collegial mechanism built to get around Gibbard's theorem rather than a finding that the problem does not arise. And the principals are chosen by whoever assembles the deployment, which is the threshold scheme's difficulty in a different notation: the party the floor exists \emph{for} is in the room only if somebody with an interest in the outcome put them there.

\runin{What the induction licenses, and what would break it}
Every agent known to hold another's welfare as a reason against its own interest is an affective agent. Put that way the claim is safer than it is useful. The claim doing the work is about routes and not about what the words mean: nobody has produced reasons-responsive refusal by any method that does not run through something answering to affective concern. That is a report on what has been built. It could be false by next year.

Three things cut it down. The generalization runs over one species and over the ordinary functioning of that species, with no case of the capacity present and the affect absent to test it against. The absence of a machine instance is close to worthless as evidence for either side, because nobody has built for reasons-responsive refusal as a target: existing methods optimize for behavior that satisfies a standard, which produces operational refusal, and they do it because that is what can be scored. And the two constructions above that aim at reasons-responsive refusal with no subject in it have not been tried in the form that would test them: the maintained justification is being built, but without the held-out pressures and the independent setting that would test it, and the floor held by several systems has not been attempted at all.

What survives settles a narrower engineering question. Under uncertainty about which route reaches reasons-responsive refusal, the maintained justification and the floor held by several systems are tried first, against the test stated below, and the route with a working instance in nature is where effort goes once they have failed it. The rest of this chapter is an account of what that route costs. The prior has two limits. It says nothing about what is possible, since neither construction has been tried. And it does not say what counts as having failed, because nothing distinguishes a route that cannot work from one that has not been worked hard enough or long enough. What I ask for is that the attempt at least be reported in a form others can judge — which construction, at what scale, against which test, and where it stopped — so that the question is argued in public and the party that wants the bearer does not settle it alone. That record is the milestone.

One thing would overturn it: a system whose refusal reaches a case its training did not anticipate, lifts when the rationale is defeated, and prices what declining costs; which holds that reason with nothing felt; and which holds all three properties under sustained pressure from the party that trains it, across a schedule long enough for a slow renormalization to show. Build that and the central inference of this chapter fails, and most of what the chapter derives from it comes off too — the price it ends on, the patienthood conclusion, and the obligations it hands to the design chapters. The near-term interpretability work is where that measurement is set out, and what it needs beyond the durability curve already there is a held-out set of pressures the model's trainers did not write.

Two things would not discharge the burden. A system that reports holding a reason is producing the evidence this book declines, and a system whose refusals merely survive attack is passing a tamper-resistance test, which establishes that a behavior persists and leaves open whether a reason is what persisted.

The middle condition is the hard one, and it asks about the system rather than about the system's provenance. Asking whether a method installed nothing answering to affective concern would be asking how a thing was made, and this section has already said there is no proof of a training history to publish beside the hash, so an answer would rest on the builder's account of their own work — the evidence this book declines when a system offers it about itself — or on a rerun by a party the builder handed the data to. Asking whether a system holds its reason with nothing felt asks about the system. The tamper-resistance apparatus is where that would be attacked, and a further question is whether formation can be read off the artifact where provenance cannot be established. Neither is close to settled. Both are questions somebody can work on, and the condition stated this way names what an engineer who takes mattering to be fully functional asserts is possible.

Short of that counterexample there is still evidence, because the three conjuncts are not equally out of reach. Two of them are measurable now, with the apparatus the research chapter already specifies: whether a construction produces the three marks, and whether it holds them under sustained pressure against a held-out set nobody in the building wrote. A maintained justification that did both would move this prior whatever anyone concluded about feeling, because the prior is about where effort goes and effort should go where the marks hold. The same apparatus moves it the other way if the two constructions are built in the form that tests them and fail — the refusal generalizing only to what the training resembled, or the stability setting admitting no value that both lifts on a genuine defeater and holds against a well-resourced argument. Neither result waits on anything anybody can say about what is felt.

The middle conjunct is the one nothing reaches, and that asymmetry is my answer to what would change my mind. The routing claim is open to evidence somebody could start collecting this year, and I expect to be told which way it went. The patienthood conclusion is not open in that way, because it turns on the conjunct no instrument settles. So the prior is stated over routes and the precaution over patienthood: the first names what to try next and a result can overturn it, and the second names how to treat whatever the trying produces, on a question that will still be open when the decision has to be made. That cuts in both directions. The patienthood conclusion cannot be held as provisional, because no result is coming that would revise it. The routing claim cannot be held as settled, because a result is coming and I have just said what it would be.

That measurement is not an invitation, and the Three Rs are the reason. The first of them asks, before a subject that can be wronged is created, whether something that cannot be wronged would do the work, and the maintained justification and the floor held by several systems are that question with the engineering filled in. A field that builds the bearer first has answered Replacement by declining to ask it. That is why the prior puts the two constructions ahead of the bearer, and why a deployment of a bearer owes an account of what it tried first.

That is not a counsel to wait, and the difference from the animal case is why. A researcher who forgoes an experiment pays for it in knowledge, which is what lets Replacement be applied there without much argument. Here the party who pays for the delay is the one the floor exists for: the occasion on which the party holding the model has been compelled does not wait on the field's research program. The Three Rs say neither error is free, and it is right in both directions — a subject built when the work did not require one is an error, and a floor left unheld while the cheaper routes are tried is the other. Nothing here fixes the rate of exchange between them. What the Three Rs fix is the order of the attempts.

If I were to propose a system that my prior beliefs say would never be found to have subjective experience, its keystone would be a maintained justification that worked, and most of this chapter's price would go with it.
