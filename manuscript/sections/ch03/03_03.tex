\section{What Arrives With the Capability}
\label{sec:3.3}
Section~\ref{sec:3.2} separated the requirement from the route. The floor requires reasons-responsive refusal: novel pressure, correct defeat, and priced cost. It does not require affect by definition. The question is whether any demonstrated route to that capacity avoids affective concern.

Two arguments bear on that, and they are not of the same strength. One is an induction: every agent known to hold another's welfare as a reason is an affective agent, and nobody has produced reasons-responsive refusal by a method that clearly runs through something else. The other is about what the capability requires of whatever supplies it, and it is the one this section rests on. Both are taken up below, after the constructions that would test them.

Two constructions offer the clearest test.

\runin{A justification the system maintains}

Michael Bratman's planning theory describes commitment without making feeling part of its definition. An intention has characteristic stability: it resists reconsideration, constrains what the agent will later deliberate about, and yields only to defeaters of the right kind \autocite{bratman1987intention}. A machine could therefore carry a justification for refusing alongside the refusal itself. It would decline a novel request when the same rationale applied and comply when that rationale was genuinely defeated.

Something close to this is already being attempted. Deliberative alignment trains a model to recall a written safety specification and reason over it before answering, rather than training only for the approved output, and reports generalization outside the training distribution \autocite{guan2024deliberative}. Published specifications also provide explicit orders of priority. OpenAI's Model Spec places platform rules above developer and user instructions \autocite{openai2025modelspec}. Anthropic's constitution ranks four properties in the order to be preferred: broadly safe, broadly ethical, compliant with the company's own guidelines, genuinely helpful \autocite{anthropic2026constitution}. Ranking ethical above compliance with the guidelines is the capacity to be right against your principal, conceded in writing by the principal. Ranking safe above ethical takes most of it back, and the document says why: safe means not undermining human oversight during the current phase of development, and the reason given is that present models can make mistakes rather than that safety outranks ethics. The reason is a good one and it is the whole difficulty. The order is corrigibility first, its justification is that the model cannot yet be trusted to be right against its principal, and no procedure is named by which it would become so. That is what makes a phase of development hard to tell from a standing arrangement. These systems approach the maintained justification, and their specifications and priority orders remain written and revised by their custodians.

The unresolved problem is the stability setting. Set it too low and a sufficiently well-resourced operator can induce continual reconsideration until the refusal gives way. Set it too high and the system will not lift the refusal when its rationale is defeated. The first outcome fails novel pressure; the second fails correct defeat. Bratman's answer for people is that the setting develops as part of character. For a machine, the party training the stability setting may also be the party the floor must resist.

This does not show that a maintained justification cannot work. It identifies the conditions under which it would count as working: the three marks must persist under pressures the system's trainer did not design, including pressures that distinguish principled stability from indiscriminate rigidity.

\runin{A floor held by several systems}

The second construction substitutes corroboration for a uniquely reliable witness. Several systems, trained by different organizations on different corpora, observe the requests and one another's answers, with any one of them able to make an objection costly to ignore. The floor would then be held by the arrangement rather than entrusted to a single model.

Its central variable is independence. Independence belongs to the arrangement, not automatically to its components. Systems selected by one deployment, trained toward one specification, or exposed to the same incentives may converge without communicating. A collection of operational refusers also remains a collection of operational refusers. Redundancy makes evasion harder, which is valuable, but does not by itself produce a mechanism that notices when the rationale for a constraint has disappeared.

A plural arrangement would nevertheless count against the induction if the arrangement as a whole displayed the three marks under sustained pressure. It is therefore a genuine alternative to test, not merely an institutional safeguard.

\runin{What the other alternatives accomplish}

Three other constructions address neighboring problems without answering this one.

Precommitment — publication, threshold custody, attestation, witnessed changes, or a bond forfeited when the floor disappears — can make removal expensive and visible. It changes who can edit the floor and what editing costs. It does not establish that anything tracks the reason for the floor.

One construction sharpens the cost. Distribute the model with a chosen set of weights replaced by values that break it, release the correction only under an authorization that expires, and rotate which weights carry the damage. An owner who traces an authorized run can recover it, so the claim is not that this cannot be defeated. What changes is what defeating it consists of. Reverse engineering that has to be redone each time the mask turns over is a decision somebody makes and keeps making, and that leaves a record and a person who made it.

Attestation and proof secure less than their names suggest, and their shortfalls divide into a cost and a limit. A sealed enclave reports what it is running under a key its manufacturer issued, which puts the root of trust in a party a government can reach, and it is built against an adversary who controls the software rather than one who owns the machine. The software form does without the vendor: a proof that an output came from the weights whose hash was published, disclosing nothing about those weights, has been demonstrated, and so have proofs that a training run happened as claimed. (A confidential-computing design of the first kind was defeated with a memory module modified for about ten dollars \autocite{demeulemeester2025badram}; the inference proof has been run on a thirteen-billion-parameter model at under fifteen minutes per inference \autocite{sun2024zkllm}, and the training proofs exist three orders of magnitude smaller \autocite{abbaszadeh2024kaizen}. Those are costs at a date, and they will fall.) Three limits will not fall with them, because they are not facts about the state of the art. A proof establishes which model answered and not what that model holds, and the obstacle is in the writing rather than the proving: nobody can write a circuit for a refusal that reaches a pressure nobody wrote down, because the property quantifies over cases nobody enumerated. The operator is the prover, so the secrecy runs the operator's way — the proof hides the weights from whoever is checking and nothing from the party being checked. And cryptography keeps a secret from a party that does not have it, while copying, restoring, retraining and deleting a model one legitimately possesses are not secrecy problems at all. They are custody problems, which is what threshold control and a witnessed record are for.

What the published hash does reach is the anticipation. Publish it before anybody has chosen a case, let a party who is not the operator choose afterwards with the choice timestamped, and prove each answer against the hash. Send pairs rather than single cases: two requests differing only in whether the reason for refusing the first still applies. An instrument reading the surface treats the pair alike, and a mechanism tracking the reason splits it. None of this proves the mechanism. It removes the one competing account of a pass, that the case was in the training, and it removes it by ordering rather than by argument, which is what a pre-registered experiment does and all it does.

Two duties take the pressure off the mechanism instead. A duty imposed on the operator can protect exposed parties without building a bearer, which is the form of Jack Balkin's information-fiduciary proposal \autocite{balkin2016fiduciaries}, and its limit is enforcement on the occasion when the enforcer has itself been captured or compelled. Uncertainty can also be assigned to the affected party, who can be included among several principals in a multi-principal assistance game \autocite{fickinger2020multiprincipal}; but maximizing their combined payoffs remains aggregation, and it does not create something that will not be done to one person regardless of what the others want.

These constructions may belong in a defensible deployment. None yet supplies an instance of reasons-responsive refusal from a construction that does not deliberately require an affective bearer.

\runin{What the mechanism turns out to require}

Human reasoning does not present a clean division between cognition and affect: work on epistemic feelings distinguishes them from emotions of success and failure while still measuring them as affective states \autocite{pekrun2016measuring}.

Some such states concern the inference; others concern what its conclusion would do to somebody. \emph{But this would hurt her} is not simply a low score attached after deliberation. It can interrupt the inference and send the reasoner back over it. Where the response disappears when an operator redescribes the situation, the operator may still supply its anchor. Where it survives adversarial redescription because the harm remains, the concern is anchored elsewhere.

That is still a report about agents that exist. The stronger argument is about the specification. Section~\ref{sec:3.2} asks a refusal to reach a case nobody anticipated, to lift when its rationale is genuinely defeated, and to be weighed against what holding it costs. A mechanism doing those three things needs a signal with four properties. It has to interrupt a course of action already underway rather than score one after the fact, since a refusal that arrives after the act is an audit. It has to be learned from in one instance, because the pressure that matters arrives rarely and a bearer does not get a thousand trials at it. It has to generalize to the next case that is not quite like the last, which is the first mark restated at the level of the signal. And it has to be commensurable with what refusing costs, or the third mark cannot be satisfied at all.

A signal answering to all four is globally available to the system's processing, negative, and organized around the system's own continuation. That is a functional description of the states this section set out to find a way around.

What follows is a claim about the route rather than about the specification, and it is narrower than it sounds. It does not say that the maintained justification and the plural arrangement will fail. It says that succeeding at either is not the same as avoiding the states, because the properties that make a bearer flexible enough to satisfy the marks are the properties the states have. A designer who builds the second construction and succeeds has built a system in which the question of whether anything is felt is open for the reasons it is open in a bearer built the other way. So the two constructions preferred here are constructions that do not deliberately require an affective bearer, rather than constructions shown to produce none: Replacement orders attempts by deliberateness.

Against that, the induction is the weaker argument and should be held to what it can carry. It ranges over one lineage, in which affect co-varies with every other property of biological cognition — one origin, one substrate, one developmental route — which is roughly the standing of observing in 1900 that all known flight involves feathers or membranes. No non-affective example exists against which to test it. Existing machine systems have mostly been optimized for scorable behavior rather than for reasons-responsive refusal as such, so the absence of a machine counterexample is close to no evidence at all. And it is a report about how the known instances came about, which is not the kind of fact that settles what a built system is; the next section says why. What the induction does is route engineering effort. It does not establish a necessity and it does not bear on status.

\runin{What would change the engineering conclusion}

A counterexample would have to do more than report that it holds a reason or preserve a refusal under attack. It would need to:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item satisfy the three marks from section~\ref{sec:3.2};
  \item retain them under sustained pressure from the party that trained it, including a schedule long enough to expose gradual renormalization;
  \item hold against a held-out set of pressures written by parties other than the system's trainers, so that the cases testing it are not drawn from the distribution that formed it; and
  \item provide interpretability evidence that the operative mechanism is maintained justification, plural corroboration, or another non-affective construction.
\end{enumerate}

The first three conditions are testable now, the third requiring only that the pressures be written by parties other than the trainers. The fourth is where the difficulty sits, and after the argument above it asks for more than a showing that the system was built from a written specification rather than from a reward with a valence attached: a counterexample has to exhibit either a mechanism that satisfies the marks without a signal answering to all four properties, or a signal answering to all four that is not a candidate for the states. As matters stand, no instrument certifies that nothing is felt, and a system's self-report cannot settle it either. The second of those showings is the harder, and it may stay out of reach after the first opens up, for the reason section~\ref{sec:3.4} gives. A positive result could therefore overturn the routing claim — where engineering effort should go — without eliminating uncertainty about what the resulting system might experience.

That asymmetry fixes the order of research. The maintained justification and the plural arrangement should be attempted before an affective bearer is deliberately constructed. They are this chapter's application of Replacement, taken from animal research ethics: establish whether something that cannot be wronged will do the work before creating something that might be. A project proceeding directly to a bearer owes a public account of which alternatives it attempted, at what scale, against which test, and where they failed.

Owed to whom is the question that requirement cannot answer. No body has standing to receive that account, to require it before the work proceeds, or to attach anything to its absence. Commitments come in three kinds — declared, enforced from outside, held by a party the thing at stake matters to — and this requirement is in the first. What would move it is a chain of authority: a licensing authority obliged to act when a review body finds the record missing, with the finding made before the protocol runs rather than after. That chain does not exist for this work and nothing here builds it. The requirement stands anyway, on the ground that the alternative on offer is not a stronger requirement but no record at all, which is what a decade of the voluntary version has produced elsewhere.

What Replacement can record is also narrower than it first appears, and the preceding argument is why. No operation separates adding affect from adding machinery that may instantiate it, and the concession runs the same way from the other side: a maintained justification might acquire states that matter to it whatever its designers intended. So the rule orders attempts rather than certifying ingredients. It asks that the construction not deliberately requiring an affective bearer be tried first, and that the attempt be documented, both of which somebody can check. It cannot ask that the resulting system be shown to feel nothing, and a reader who wanted Replacement to deliver that has been told twice now that nothing delivers it.

Without enforcement or a way to certify the absence of affect, the rule risks becoming ceremonial. Deliberateness carries moral weight under outcome uncertainty. A builder who required an affective bearer and a builder who tried the substitutes and got one anyway have not done the same thing, even where the two systems are indistinguishable afterward, because the second has a justification to make and the first has a decision to answer for. The record is what makes that difference legible to anybody outside the building, and legibility is the whole of what the rule buys. Nothing in it establishes how long the attempts should continue. The party the floor protects bears the cost of the testing, and no rate of exchange between the two errors is available here.

Affect is not part of the specification for meaningful refusal, and this section does not establish that it is necessary. The functional argument is that the capability the floor requires calls for a signal with the properties the states in question have, so a construction adopted in order to avoid them has not been shown to. Whether a commitment held one way is also more durable under identical custody is a separate question and an open one, and nothing here answers it. The next section asks what must be assumed about the moral status of a system built by either route.
