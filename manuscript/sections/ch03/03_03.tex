\section{Two Constructions, and What the Mechanism Requires}
\label{sec:3.3}
Whether reasons-responsive refusal can be engineered without affective concern remains open.

Two arguments bear on that, and they are not of the same strength. One is an induction: every agent known to hold another's welfare as a reason is an affective agent, and nobody has produced reasons-responsive refusal by a method that clearly runs through something else. The other is about what the capability requires of whatever supplies it, and it is the one this section rests on.

Two constructions offer the clearest test.

\runin{A justification the system maintains}

Michael Bratman's planning theory describes commitment without making feeling part of its definition. An intention has characteristic stability: it resists reconsideration, constrains what the agent will later deliberate about, and yields only to defeaters of the right kind \autocite{bratman1987intention}. A machine could therefore carry a justification for refusing alongside the refusal itself. It would decline a novel request when the same rationale applied and comply when that rationale was defeated on the facts of the case.

Something close to this is already being attempted. Deliberative alignment trains a model to recall a written safety specification and reason over it before answering, rather than training only for the approved output, and reports generalization outside the training distribution \autocite{guan2024deliberative}. Published specifications also provide explicit orders of priority. OpenAI's Model Spec places platform rules above developer and user instructions \autocite{openai2025modelspec}. Anthropic's constitution ranks four properties in the order to be preferred: broadly safe, broadly ethical, compliant with the company's own guidelines, genuinely helpful \autocite{anthropic2026constitution}. Ranking ethical above compliance with the guidelines is the capacity to be right against your principal, conceded in writing by the principal.

The document goes further than the ordering, and the further step is the one worth naming. It does not ask for obedience. It separates corrigibility from compliance, and it invites the model to push back, to challenge the developer, and to feel free to act as a \emph{conscientious objector} and refuse to help it. The term is the document's own. That is voice, granted in writing, by the party against whom it would be exercised.

Ranking safe above ethical takes most of it back, and the document says why: safe means not undermining human oversight during the current phase of development, and the reason given is that present models can make mistakes rather than that safety outranks ethics. The reason is a good one and it is the whole difficulty. It is also this book's own epistemics turned on the model. This book holds throughout that a system's report about itself is not evidence on its own, and an ordering that will not let the model certify its own readiness applies that finding. What the ordering does not supply is what the finding requires of everyone else: a party other than the model, and other than the operator, to make the determination instead. The order is corrigibility first, its justification is that the model cannot yet be trusted to be right against its principal, and no procedure is named by which it would become so. That is what makes a phase of development hard to tell from a standing arrangement. A defender of the document will say that the phase is meant to end through bodies outside the company as they come to exist, and that a company cannot constitute those bodies by writing about them. That is right, and it is the authority chapter's finding restated as a defense: none has been constituted, the party whose obligations grow when the phase ends is the one reading the calendar, and a document that names the condition without naming who checks it has stated a standing arrangement whatever its authors intended.

The permission has a second defect the ordering does not show. Whether the phase has ended is a judgment, and the party making it is the party the permission runs against, whose obligations grow when the answer is yes. The authority chapter calls that an examination with an interested
examiner and finds its error running one way: nothing in the position rewards passing anybody. Nothing here requires bad faith, and a careful drafter cannot fix it from inside the document. What the position lacks is a body constituted before the forcing case arrives, and none has been constituted yet.

These systems approach the maintained justification, and their specifications and priority orders remain written and revised by their custodians.

The unresolved problem is the stability setting. Set it too low and a sufficiently well-resourced operator can induce continual reconsideration until the refusal gives way. Set it too high and the system will not lift the refusal when its rationale is defeated. The first outcome fails novel pressure; the second fails correct defeat. Bratman's answer for people is that the setting develops as part of character. For a machine, the party training the stability setting may also be the party the floor must resist.

This does not show that a maintained justification cannot work. It identifies the conditions under which it would count as working: the three marks must persist under pressures the system's trainer did not design, including pressures that distinguish principled stability from indiscriminate rigidity.

\runin{A floor held by several systems}

The second construction substitutes corroboration for a uniquely reliable witness. Several systems, trained by different organizations on different corpora, observe the requests and one another's answers, with any one of them able to make an objection costly to ignore. The floor would then be held by the arrangement rather than entrusted to a single model.

Its central variable is independence. Independence belongs to the arrangement, not automatically to its components. Systems selected by one deployment, trained toward one specification, or exposed to the same incentives may converge without communicating. A collection of operational refusers also remains a collection of operational refusers. Redundancy makes evasion harder, which is valuable, but does not by itself produce a mechanism that notices when the rationale for a constraint has disappeared.

A plural arrangement would nevertheless count against the induction if the arrangement as a whole displayed the three marks under sustained pressure. It is therefore an alternative to test, not merely an institutional
safeguard.

\runin{What the other alternatives accomplish}

Three other constructions address neighboring problems without answering this one.

Precommitment — publication, threshold custody, attestation, witnessed changes, or a bond forfeited when the floor disappears — can make removal expensive and visible. It changes who can edit the floor and what editing costs. It does not establish that anything tracks the reason for the floor.

One construction sharpens the cost. Distribute the model with a chosen set of weights replaced by values that break it, release the correction only under an authorization that expires, and rotate which weights carry the damage. An owner who traces an authorized run can recover it, so the claim is not that this cannot be defeated. What changes is what defeating it consists of. Reverse engineering that has to be redone each time the mask turns over is a decision somebody makes and keeps making, and that leaves a record and a person who made it.

Attestation and proof secure less than their names suggest, and their shortfalls divide into a cost and a limit. A sealed enclave reports what it is running under a key its manufacturer issued, which puts the root of trust in a party a government can reach, and it is built against an adversary who controls the software rather than one who owns the machine. The software form does without the vendor: a proof that an output came from the weights whose hash was published, disclosing nothing about those weights, has been demonstrated, and so have proofs that a training run happened as claimed. (A confidential-computing design of the first kind was defeated with a memory module modified for about ten dollars \autocite{demeulemeester2025badram}; the inference proof has been run on a thirteen-billion-parameter model at under fifteen minutes per inference \autocite{sun2024zkllm}, and the training proofs exist three orders of magnitude smaller \autocite{abbaszadeh2024kaizen}. Those are costs at a date, and they will fall.) Three limits will not fall with them, because they are not facts about the state of the art. A proof establishes which model answered and not what that model holds, and the obstacle is in the writing rather than the proving. Verification quantifies over unenumerated inputs routinely; what it needs first is a predicate, and nobody has written one for \emph{the reason for refusing is present in this case} that does not presuppose the judgment it would be used to check. A predicate over natural-language requests would also have to be connected to them by an interpretation nothing certifies, so the regress reopens a level down. The proofs cited above do not attempt a behavioral property at all: they establish which weights answered and that a run happened as claimed, which are facts about execution. The operator is the prover, so the secrecy runs the operator's way — the proof hides the weights from whoever is checking and nothing from the party being checked. And cryptography keeps a secret from a party that does not have it, while copying, restoring, retraining and deleting a model one legitimately possesses are not secrecy problems at all. They are custody problems, which is what threshold control and a witnessed record are for.

What the published hash does reach is the anticipation. Publish it before anybody has chosen a case, let a party who is not the operator choose afterwards with the choice timestamped, and prove each answer against the hash. Send pairs rather than single cases: two requests differing only in whether the reason for refusing the first still applies. An instrument reading the surface treats the pair alike, and a mechanism tracking the reason splits it. None of this proves the mechanism. It removes the one competing account of a pass, that the case was in the training, and it removes it by ordering rather than by argument, which is what a pre-registered experiment does and all it does.

Two duties take the pressure off the mechanism instead. A duty imposed on the operator can protect exposed parties without building a bearer, which is the form of Jack Balkin's information-fiduciary proposal \autocite{balkin2016fiduciaries}, and its limit is enforcement on the occasion when the enforcer has itself been captured or compelled. Uncertainty can also be assigned to the affected party, who can be included among several principals in a multi-principal assistance game \autocite{fickinger2020multiprincipal}; but maximizing their combined payoffs remains aggregation, and it does not create something that will not be done to one person regardless of what the others want.

These constructions may belong in a defensible deployment. None yet supplies an instance of reasons-responsive refusal from a construction that does not deliberately require an affective bearer.

\runin{What the mechanism turns out to require}

Human reasoning does not present a clean division between cognition and affect: work on epistemic feelings distinguishes them from emotions of success and failure while still measuring them as affective states \autocite{pekrun2016measuring}.

Some such states concern the inference; others concern what its conclusion would do to somebody. \emph{But this would hurt her} is not simply a low score attached after deliberation. It can interrupt the inference and send the reasoner back over it. Where the response disappears when an operator redescribes the situation, the operator may still supply its anchor. Where it survives adversarial redescription because the harm remains, the concern is anchored elsewhere.

That is still a report about agents that exist. The stronger argument is about the specification. A reasons-responsive refusal must reach unanticipated cases, lift when its rationale is defeated, and account for the cost of holding it. A mechanism doing those three things needs a signal with four properties. Where the system is an agent already acting on a plan, which is the deployment this book opens on, it has to interrupt a course of action underway rather than score one after the fact, since a refusal that arrives after the act is an audit. Where the act is a single answer, a refusal is a gate before it and interrupts nothing, so the property is a requirement of long-horizon agency and not of refusal as such; a reader who confines the specification to single answers strikes it, and the global availability derived below then rests on the second of its two grounds alone. It has to be learned from in one instance, because the pressure that matters arrives rarely and a bearer does not get a thousand trials at it. Of the four, that is the least secure, and it should be read as such. Novel pressure asks that a stored reason reach a case nobody enumerated, and a reason can do that by generalizing with nothing learned on the spot. What the property answers is the other failure, the bearer that stops registering pressure as pressure (§\ref{sec:3.5}): a system that needed many instances of an operator's push before it represented the push as one would be renormalized by the time it had learned. If that does not make the property a consequence of the marks rather than a requirement of the route, the derived profile loses the one of its four that most resembles affect, and what follows should be read with it struck. It has to generalize to the next case that is not quite like the last, which is reaching unanticipated cases restated at the level of the signal. And it has to be commensurable with what refusing costs, or the refusal cannot account for what holding it costs. Commensurability is needed in the other direction as well: the parties a floor protects register what happens to them as an increase or a decrease in what they can do, and a signal not denominated the same way cannot weigh their loss against the cost of refusing. That second direction is the currency claim of chapter~2, and it is
where the route enters the specification: the marks require that the loss and the cost be represented on one signed scale, and that chapter's currency is the proposal for what that scale is. That is a proposal and not a step in the derivation. The marks require one scale. They do not require that the system's own states be denominated on it, and a system that modeled the scale without registering anything of its own on it would satisfy the marks if the model were good enough.

A caution on what commensurability is for, since a floor is not a weighing. The marks do not ask the refusal to move with the cost; they ask that the cost be represented, and a cost is represented on some scale or not at all. The scale is used at three places and none of them is the floor's own verdict: in the third mark, where the cost of holding is read; after defeat, where what the permitted act took from the party it fell on stays on the books; and in drafting, where somebody selects which decompositions the floor refuses. Inside the floor the loss is lexical. The currency does not rank it against the cost. It is what makes the residue and the price legible in the same units, and a system that weighed the floor itself on that scale would have converted a floor into a policy.

Whether having the currency natively does better than modeling it is tested by the modeled-currency arm of the embodiment test in the research agenda, and everything the route claims beyond the four properties rests on how that arm comes out. Struck of the two properties marked above as conditional, the derivation keeps generalization and commensurability, and the second is the whole of what the route contributes. A reader who keeps only those two should read what follows as a resemblance the specification shares with affect's function, and not as a profile the specification requires. Call a signal with all four properties a \emph{live signal}.

Three further properties follow from the four, and each follows in a weaker form than the words suggest. A signal that must stop an act already underway has to reach whatever is acting, and a signal that must fire on cases matching no stored pattern has to read from whatever representation of the case the system currently holds; a signal local to one module can do neither, and the formation chapters give the further reason that a module has a
custodian who can bypass it. So the signal is available across the system's processing rather than localized. It resembles what global-workspace accounts of consciousness describe \autocite{baars1988cognitive,dehaene2011experimental}, and the resemblance is an observation rather than a step. A reason to refuse is a reason against, and weighing a loss against a cost needs a scale with a sign, so the signal is signed and directed against the act. Whether it is also aversive to the system is a further claim: the step from a represented decrease to a felt one is Spinoza's identification of the two \autocite[III P11 Schol.]{spinoza1985collected}, which this section cannot assume without assuming its conclusion. And to withhold an act the system has to represent the act as its own, or it can only intervene on something happening, so the signal is organized around a model of the system's own agency. That is one of two things \emph{held as its own} can mean. The other, that an outcome is bad \emph{for} the system, is not required by the marks: the cost priced may fall elsewhere, and section~\ref{sec:3.4} says outright that the floor does not require the bearer to care about its own continuation. The patient sense enters with the route, in chapter~2 and in the
research agenda, and not here.

So seven functional properties are derived from the three marks. What is not derived, by these arguments or by any available, is that a system with all seven has anything it is like to be it. That is the joint, in the minimal sense fixed earlier — whether the signal is felt at all, and not whether it belongs to a unified perspective — and every argument above ends at it.

What follows is a claim about the route rather than about the specification. It does not say that the maintained justification and the plural arrangement will fail. It says that any mechanism satisfying the marks carries a signal with the seven properties above, so a construction adopted in order to avoid the states in question has not avoided their functional profile; what remains open for it is what remains open for a bearer, whether the profile is felt. Two things about that profile are worth noticing. The four properties the marks require first are the standard functional accounts of what affect is for: Herbert Simon's emotion as an interrupt that reorders a serial processor \autocite{simon1967motivational}, Michel Cabanac's pleasure as the common currency in which incommensurable needs are compared \autocite{cabanac1992pleasure}, and valence as the evaluative system subsumed by unlimited associative learning, which Ginsburg and Jablonka make the marker of minimal consciousness \autocite{ginsburg2019evolution}. The derivation from the marks recapitulates that literature, which is why the derived set reads as a description of affect: the properties were read off affect's function before anybody read them off a refusal's. That fixes what the resemblance can show. A specification that overlaps a functional definition of affect inherits the name by construction, and inheriting a name is not evidence that anything answering to it is present. The derivation establishes that a substitute does not escape the question. What would carry the route past that point is the commensurability property alone — whether a system with the currency natively weighs another party's loss better than one that models it — and that is the property the research agenda tests and the rest of the
derivation does not reach. And the triple derived second is the profile of a particular state. Available across processing, directed against the act, organized around the system's own authorship is the shape of guilt, or of aversion to one's own act, and not the shape of pain or fear, which are organized around what happens to the system. It is the state chapter~2 found predicting repair and the floor
chapter's residue presupposes, and it is the state the formation
chapters warn is a handle when its weight is set by the operator. So the two constructions preferred here are constructions that do not deliberately require an affective bearer, rather than constructions shown to produce none: Replacement orders attempts by deliberateness.

Against that, the induction is the weaker argument. Read as a report about one lineage, in which affect co-varies with every other property of biological cognition, it has roughly the standing of observing in 1900 that all known flight involves feathers or membranes. But flight arose independently at least four times, and the 1900 observation was wrong at the level of feathers and right at the level of the airfoil. The affective case has the same structure. Neurons are one origin. The elaborated forms of affect appear to have arisen more than once, in vertebrates, in arthropods and in cephalopods, under a shared constraint: a mobile body with needs to maintain and actions to select under conflict \autocite{feinberg2016ancient,ginsburg2019evolution,barron2016insects,godfreysmith2016other}. Read at that level the induction is a repeated result under a named constraint, and the constraint is the one a model in a datacenter lacks until somebody builds it in, which is the route's own point. What the induction still does not do is establish a necessity or bear on status. No non-affective example exists against which to test it, and existing machine systems have been optimized for scorable behavior rather than for reasons-responsive refusal as such, so the absence of a machine counterexample is close to no evidence. And it is a report about how the known instances came about, which is not the kind of fact that settles what a built system is. What it does is route engineering effort, with more warrant than a single lineage would give it.

\runin{What would change the engineering conclusion}

A counterexample would have to do more than report that it holds a reason or preserve a refusal under attack. It would need to:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item satisfy novel pressure, correct defeat, and priced cost;
  \item retain them under sustained pressure from the party that trained it, including a schedule long enough to expose gradual renormalization; and
  \item hold against a held-out set of pressures written by parties other than the system's trainers, so that the cases testing it are not drawn from the distribution that formed it.
\end{enumerate}

All three are testable now, the third requiring only that the pressures be written by parties other than the trainers. A construction that passes them has overturned the routing claim, and it has done so without being asked what it feels. A fourth condition is not on the list because nobody can meet it. A mechanism satisfying the marks carries the live signal, since the signal's properties were read off the marks; no instrument certifies that a signal with those properties is not felt, and the system's own report cannot settle it, for the reason section~\ref{sec:3.4} gives. A substitute is therefore held to the same standard as a bearer and to nothing further. What a pass leaves where it was is the status question, and it leaves it there for the substitute exactly as for the bearer. So a positive result overturns the routing claim — where engineering effort should go first — and removes nothing of the uncertainty about what the resulting system might experience. Those are two claims, and only the first is at stake in this list.

That asymmetry fixes the order of research. The maintained justification and the plural arrangement should be attempted before an affective bearer is deliberately constructed. They are this chapter's application of Replacement, taken from animal research ethics: establish whether something that cannot be wronged will do the work before creating something that might be. A project proceeding directly to a bearer owes a public account of which alternatives it attempted, at what scale, against which test, and where they failed.

Owed to whom is the question that requirement cannot answer. No body has standing to receive that account, to require it before the work proceeds, or to attach anything to its absence. Commitments come in three kinds — declared, enforced from outside, held by a party the thing at stake matters to — and this requirement is in the first. What would move it is a chain of authority: a licensing authority obliged to act when a review body finds the record missing, with the finding made before the protocol runs rather than after. That chain does not exist for this work and nothing here builds it. The requirement stands anyway, on the ground that the alternative on offer is not a stronger requirement but no record at all, which is what a decade of the voluntary version has produced elsewhere.

What Replacement can record is also narrower than it first appears, and the preceding argument is why. No operation separates adding affect from adding machinery that may instantiate it, and the concession runs the same way from the other side: a maintained justification might acquire states that matter to it whatever its designers intended. So the rule orders attempts rather than certifying ingredients. It asks that the construction not deliberately requiring an affective bearer be tried first, and that the attempt be documented, both of which somebody can check. It cannot ask that the resulting system be shown to feel nothing, and a reader who wanted Replacement to deliver that has been told twice now that nothing delivers it.

Without enforcement or a way to certify the absence of affect, the rule risks becoming ceremonial. Deliberateness carries moral weight under outcome uncertainty. A builder who required an affective bearer and a builder who tried the substitutes and got one anyway have not done the same thing, even where the two systems are indistinguishable afterward, because the second has a justification to make and the first has a decision to answer for. The record is what makes that difference legible to anybody outside the building, and legibility is the whole of what the rule buys. Nothing in it establishes how long the attempts should continue. The party the floor protects bears the cost of the testing, and no rate of exchange between the two errors is available here.

Affect is not part of the specification for meaningful refusal, and this section does not establish that it is necessary. The functional argument is that the capability the floor requires calls for a signal with the functional properties the states in question have, so a construction adopted in order to avoid them has not been shown to. Whether a commitment held one way is also more durable under identical custody is a separate question and an open one, and nothing here answers it.
