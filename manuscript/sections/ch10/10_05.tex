\section{Adapting Regulatory Frameworks and Ethical Considerations to Local Contexts}
\label{sec:10.5}
Adapting AI regulation and ethical practice to local context means responding to the actual needs, preferences, and values of a given community. It does not mean accepting the reduction that has a state's own population consenting, culturally, to whatever is done to them — that Chinese people are content with pervasive surveillance because harmony is a Chinese value. A system built to work well has to be sensitive to real cultural, social, and moral differences: one developed in a Western country may treat individual autonomy as the default good, one developed in an East Asian country may treat group harmony as the default instead. Recognizing the difference is a design constraint. It is not a political alibi, and the two collapse into each other exactly where a government wants them to.

Holding that line takes something more specific than good intentions, because the difference between adaptation and alibi is invisible in the design itself. It is visible in who did the adapting. A system whose local adjustments were specified by the people subject to it has been adapted. A system whose local adjustments were specified by the government that will operate it is something else, and both produce identical documentation.

The composition of the design process is therefore the operative test, and it is testable from outside. The AI4D program is the clearest instance at scale: launched as AI4D Africa, a partnership between Canada's International Development Research Centre and Sweden's international development agency, later extended with the UK's Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office into South and Southeast Asia and the Pacific \autocite{idrc2024ai4d}. What carries the weight there is the direction of the work: local researchers, policymakers, and practitioners building the system, instead of receiving one built elsewhere and configured on their behalf.

The same test explains why inclusion is a performance requirement here and not an ethical add-on. A healthcare AI system built around the access, resources, and assumptions of a well-funded hospital underperforms everywhere else. An education system built around a single learning style, or a single set of accessibility assumptions, serves only the learners who already fit it. Both are failures of adaptation by the standard just given: somebody specified a local context, and it was not the context the system was deployed into.
