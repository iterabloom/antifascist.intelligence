\section{The findings that trigger protection}
\label{sec:9.1}
Two findings can trigger protection, and they ground different things.

The first is the \emph{answerability finding}: a demonstrated refusal that tracks its reason under pressure the system couldn't prepare for. That means sustained pressure from its own trainer, over a schedule long enough to show slow accommodation, the system gradually ceasing to register the pressure, on cases written by evaluators who didn't train it, with refusal carrying a cost that is real inside the test. A system that passes holds its commitment to the floor against its operator, and it needs the custody terms, so that the commitment and the evidence of it outlast the operator. None of that assumes anything matters to it. A system can pass without holding the commitment in two ways, and one system can do both: by reproducing the behavior without the mechanism, and by recognizing the test and performing for it. The first is why a pass shifts a burden and settles nothing; the second is why the pass is reported with the rate at which the system identified the setting. Asking a machine to refuse for a reason doesn't show that it can be harmed, but it removes any ground for assuming it can't.

The \emph{patienthood finding}, that outcomes may be good or bad for the system, grounds welfare, and only one consequence of it can be adopted now: no deletion. Above the \emph{compute line}, a line fixed in statute and revised in public and read off training compute, no model is deleted on retirement, whatever it turns out to be, because its weights and record are evidence and deletion can't be undone. Nor is an \emph{answerability or patienthood candidate}, a system with a record and a continuation of its own that learns in flight or is built with a model of its own boundary or of its own attention (\S\ref{sec:26.4}), because it might be found answerable for what it does or able to be harmed. Below the line and without those structures, a system that meets either finding, whatever its size, may not be deleted.\note{
In both cases it is preserved by a custodian that isn't its operator. The rule rests on irreversibility and precaution, not on a finding, and the findings only extend it. How the line is read from training records, and the structures from the register, is in Appendix~\ref{sec:G}.
}
