10.1. AI and Human Flourishing in Antifascist Societies
What would it mean for AI to make a life better, not just avoid making it worse? The economist Amartya Sen's capability approach gives the question a definite shape: well-being is not the resources a person has but the real freedom to do and be what they have reason to value — a distinction Martha Nussbaum later extended into a specific list of central capabilities a life needs to count as fully human [[cite:C0581]]. Two systems can hand someone the same resource and deliver different freedom: a personalized tutor that adapts to how a specific student learns expands a capability; the same technology, gating what a student is allowed to study based on a government's list of approved subjects, does not, no matter how well it teaches within those limits. The design question chapters 2 through 6 spent their length on — what makes a system resistant to authoritarian capture rather than merely capable — is what decides which of those two a given deployment turns out to be.

Section 4.4.6 covers Taiwan's vTaiwan process, the clearest existing case of AI-assisted tooling expanding a capability — public deliberation — rather than substituting for it. A newer example runs the same logic against censorship instead of deliberation: a 2026 project led by the University of Chicago, with Princeton and SRI International, uses AI to distinguish deliberate internet censorship from ordinary network failure at population scale, aiming to produce something like a weather map of state interference with online information, funded by a million-dollar DARPA grant [[cite:C0582]]. Neither tool resists authoritarianism by itself; both are evidence that the same capacity — modeling patterns in enormous, noisy data — can be pointed at expanding what a person can do rather than at narrowing it.

That symmetry is the whole argument for antifascist design rather than a general presumption that AI helps. A capability expanded is not guaranteed; it has to be built in, the way chapters 2 through 6 describe, against a live alternative in which the same underlying capacity gets pointed at surveillance, censorship, or narrowing what a system's users are permitted to want.
