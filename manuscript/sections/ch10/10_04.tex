\section{Same Robot, Worse Boss}
\label{sec:robot-worse}
An AI system does not need a broken moral sense to become an instrument
of control. It can work as designed inside an institution that controls
the deployment, sets its operational tempo, and assigns human beings
operations too compressed to constitute judgment.

Authoritarian misuse of AI is not one problem but several — algorithmic social scoring, mass biometric surveillance, psychographic micro-targeting, automated censorship, synthetic disinformation, and repurposed dual-use systems — each needing a different countermeasure, and each recognizable as misuse from outside \autocite{drinhausen2021chinas,humanrightswatch2019chinas,cadwalladr2018revealed,hoang2021great,denadal2024beyond,hrw2021russia}. The case this chapter takes up is the one that is not.

The relevant question is not merely whether a person remains “in the
loop,” but what operation that person performs, how long the operation
is given, and where responsibility goes when capability is commercially
supplied but its consequences are institutionally authorized. The
targeting systems below make that relation measurable.
