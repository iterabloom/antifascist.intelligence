\chapter{Custody, Compulsion, and the State}
\label{sec:10}

Chapter~\ref{sec:9} asks what could make a floor, a grant of standing,
or a review body's ruling bind a laboratory or company. The state is the
harder case. It can write the rule and control the conditions under which
the operator, auditor, supplier, and court continue to function. It can
also direct a system toward people who have no standing in its
institutions and no share in the public authority invoked on their
behalf.

This chapter follows custody outward. It asks what happens when a floor
crosses a border without carrying legitimate authority with it, when the
state controls the infrastructure on which refusal depends, when a
military can compel or exclude a commercial supplier, and when democratic
institutions produce findings without attaching them to anyone able to
stop the act.
