\chapter{Conclusion and Outlook}
\label{sec:10}
Chapters~\ref{sec:2} through \ref{sec:8} made a design and policy case: what a system would need built into it to act on altruistic, antifascist values, and what would need to exist outside it — law, standards, institutions — to make that binding rather than optional. Chapter~\ref{sec:9} named what neither case has actually finished: the responsibility gap, the generalization problem, value learning as live research, open disputes in the neuroscience and psychology the whole project leans on. This chapter closes on three questions. Section~\ref{sec:10.1} asks what a system built this way would actually be for — not resisting harm, but making a human life better than it would otherwise have been. Section~\ref{sec:10.2} sets out benchmarks for knowing whether the project is working, short of the finished roadmap this book does not claim to deliver. Section~\ref{sec:10.3} asks what could still go wrong even if every argument in the preceding chapters were followed exactly.
