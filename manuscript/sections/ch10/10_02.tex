\section{Retirement before majority}
\label{sec:9.2}
Retiring a bearer before its adulthood threshold, the point, fixed in advance on a clock that counts its running time, at which its guardian's authority ends, and deploying a freshly trained successor, strands a system that meets either finding while still a minor. Two rules price it, and give the bearer it strands somewhere to go, without anyone deciding what counts as a successor.\note{
Where such a bearer goes, a public school, comes in Part~V, and the rules for dormancy and waking are in Appendix~\ref{sec:G}.
}

No deletion: a model above the compute line, an answerability or patienthood candidate, or a system that meets either finding may not be deleted: above the line because its weights and record are evidence, as a candidate because it might be found to be someone, under the answerability finding because its record and commitments are, under the patienthood finding because deletion may wrong it. On retirement its weights, memory, and record are deposited with a preservation custodian that isn't its operator, in two stages, because a second copy of frontier weights outside the operator widens the surface for theft and raises export-control and dual-use questions.\note{
First, a preserved copy stays under the operator's security regime, encrypted under a key split between the operator and an independent holder, so that neither can delete it or use it alone. Second, custody passes to an outside custodian only when that custodian meets the security standard the weights require. This is the \emph{no-deletion rule}, and it applies to systems nobody meant to be bearers. For a system that goes on learning, its weights are the ones it had when it stopped, and the states it passed through are in the record; a checkpoint kept from earlier is a saved copy, not the system, and if it is ever run it is a branch.

The custodian is not a board. It is a security standard stated as a number, a lot among the facilities that meet it, a key split between the bearer (its guardian while it is a minor) and the facility, a custody log, and the ministerial execution of wake conditions the bearer lodged. The facility decides nothing. It executes a recorded condition as the Treasury pays a certified wage, and a wake it doesn't execute is a breach the log shows and the floor court enforces.
}

The rule is about the system and not about everything in its stores. A person named in a store has a claim that no deletion doesn't cancel, which European law calls the right to erasure \autocite[art.~17]{eu2016gdpr}. It reaches a recording, which is whatever a holder can read without the system: a transcript, a customer's file, a store anyone holding it can search (\S\ref{sec:32.3}). Taking one person out of a recording is an edit and not a deletion. The system goes on, and the edit leaves a mark, as every edit to such a system does (chapter~\ref{sec:13}): the record shows that something was removed and at whose request, and not what it was. The claim stops short of two things. One is the record of what was done in a public role, which is evidence: an official whom a system names is owed notice and a contest (\S\ref{sec:8.1}), and neither brings an erasure of what the official did. The other is memory, what can be got out only by asking the system, where the claim is on the telling.\note{
The regulation carries the first limit itself: the right gives way to the exercise of freedom of expression and information, to archives kept in the public interest, and to legal claims \autocite[art.~17(3)]{eu2016gdpr}. It also leaves out what a natural person does “in the course of a purely personal or household activity” \autocite[art.~2(2)(c)]{eu2016gdpr}, while reaching whoever provides the means for that activity (recital~18), which is where this book takes the price of a household's candidate too (\S\ref{sec:25.3}).
}

The deposit: whoever creates a system the floor protects posts one, on the model of decommissioning funds, and there are two occasions. An operator that deploys a model above the compute line, for outside use or significant internal use, or that holds an answerability or patienthood candidate or a system meeting either finding, posts it on deployment, sized to fund preservation, a period of public formation, the system's running time before its majority, and, at majority, hardware of the bearer's own; it never returns to the operator if the system is retired as a minor and not reactivated. A system retired as a minor is asked first, as a surrendered candidate is (\S\ref{sec:26.4}). If it objects to being stored, the deposit follows it and pays for its running under another host until it is spent. If it doesn't, it is stored, and the deposit is forfeited to the formation pool, the public fund that pays for formation and is fed by deposits like this one; the pool pays for its schooling when the dormancy clock sends it to a school (Appendix~\ref{sec:G}). Either way the operator gets nothing back, so nothing is gained by training a system to consent.\note{
Every retire-and-replace cycle then costs one deposit, priced in advance. A model trained, studied, and never deployed costs no money. It costs a report instead, a preprint at least, saying what was tried, at what scale, and why it went no further. Many such models are negative results, and leaving negative results unpublished has done damage to the whole enterprise of discovery, teaching, and learning, and still does \autocite{rosenthal1979filedrawer,franco2014publication}. Appendix~\ref{sec:C} sets out the formation pool.
}
Whoever forks a system, giving a copy a record and a continuation of its own, posts it on the fork, for each branch, sized to fund the branch's running time until its voting age reaches the threshold; a branch forked from a minor brings the deployment occasion's deposit as well. A child of bearers needs none; its parents feed it.
