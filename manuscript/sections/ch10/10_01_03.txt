10.1.3. Commitment Was Never the Missing Piece
Section 6.3 covers how research institutions and standards bodies cooperate across borders; section 7.5 covers the treaty, standard, and declaration landscape state by state; section 7.1.5 covers what building the AI literacy a global commitment depends on takes, from K-12 curricula to public courses. This section's job is not to add a ninth item to that catalog. It is to say what the catalog adds up to.

Section 7.5 names the OECD's recommendation, the European Commission's expert-group guidelines, UNESCO's, the two Partnerships on AI, the Hiroshima process, the safety-institute network, and the Global Digital Compact — eight overlapping voluntary bodies, by its own count — before reaching the Council of Europe's 2024 framework convention as the first one of them that actually binds a signatory to anything. A global commitment to ethical, antifascist AI development is not the scarce thing this section's old title implied it was: declarations, principles, and voluntary codes have accumulated for a decade across governments, industry consortia, and standards bodies, documented with named institutions across sections 6.1 through 7.5. What has stayed scarce is the much narrower list of bodies that can act once a stated commitment is broken — the harder problem the rest of this book's policy chapters were actually written to address.
