Chapter 10: Conclusion and Outlook
Chapters 2 through 7 made a design and policy case: what a system would need built into it to act on altruistic, anti-authoritarian values, and what would need to exist outside it — law, standards, institutions — to make that binding rather than optional. Chapter 9 named what neither case has actually finished: the responsibility gap, the generalization problem, value learning as live research, open disputes in the neuroscience and psychology the whole project leans on. This chapter closes on three questions rather than a summary of the seven before it. Section 10.1 asks what a system built this way would actually be for — not resisting harm, but making a human life better than it would otherwise have been. Section 10.2 sets out benchmarks for knowing whether the project is working, short of the finished roadmap the introduction gestures at and this book does not deliver. Section 10.3 asks what could still go wrong even if every argument in the preceding chapters were followed exactly.
