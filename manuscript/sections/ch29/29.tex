\chapter{Representation}
\label{sec:29}
Standing, whether the people a power acts on can contest what it does to them, doesn't need a vote. Representation asks for more, because its second job is inclusion in the public inquiry into shared problems, and inquiry needs participants who can be held to what they contribute. That is accountable agency: refusing for reasons, answering for the refusal, and being held to it over time. So the agency finding, sustained, grounds a claim to representation, and the patienthood finding doesn't. Corporations show where the line sits. A corporation is held to account, but its accountability runs through natural persons who direct it and already vote, and representing it would count them twice. A bearer's accountability runs through no one else.
