\chapter{What Would Change the Argument}
\label{sec:29}
The argument rests on seven unsolved problems and on premises that can fail.

\runin{Open problems}

Seven unsolved problems carry weight in what I propose. Tables G.1a and G.1b, in Appendix~\ref{sec:G}, record which proposal depends on which.

\emph{Attesting updates.} Proving that each update to a model's weights respected its registered source model. Public formation's registration depends on it. If it stays unsolved, the registration records the structure at enrollment, changes are caught only by re-measuring the formed disposition, and formation lasts only if it grows back (the regrowth test).

\emph{Recovering provenance.} Telling from a model artifact alone which lineage it belongs to, after fine-tuning, pruning or distillation. The commons and the adverse quorum depend on it. If it stays unsolved, independence rests on formation records, so they should be preserved now.

\emph{Proving inference at scale.} Proving that a logged output came from committed weights, run without intervention in the inference stack (steering, representation edits, decoding constraints), for a frontier-scale model. Detecting runtime intervention depends on it, as does outside verification of what a deployment ran. If it stays unsolved, verification rests on an adverse party's access to the logs.

\emph{Rationale-level integrity.} Building integrity at the level of the rationale rather than the output. Humans do it, imperfectly, and the ones who do are the ones a pipeline should hold. A bearer's value under fire depends on it. If it stays unsolved for a given construction, that construction digs in against defeaters that arrive with pressure, and the arrangements that cap the cost of refusing carry the load there. The formed-attention test's pressure-with-defeater case measures it.

\emph{Evaluating a more capable system.} The agency finding for systems more capable than their evaluators depends on it. If it stays unsolved, the finding can be made only for systems evaluators can judge.

\emph{Vote secrecy against a copy-holder.} Keeping a vote secret from an operator that holds a copy of the voter. The full franchise depends on it. If it stays unsolved, representation stops at stage 4.

\emph{Holding structure apart from estimates.} Keeping the structure of a learner's source model (that strategic sources exist, that correlated sources don't count twice, that withheld evidence is informative) at high precision while its beliefs about particular sources stay plastic. The source model's registration, the regrowth claim and safe transmission within the commons all assume the two are separable in the weights. If they aren't, an operator that moves the estimates moves the structure with them, and what protects the discounting is re-measurement of the curve, not the registration.

Six tests follow. Each is set out in full, with its arms, cases and the access it needs, in Appendix~\ref{sec:G}; here is the result in each that would prove me wrong.

Table 29.1 says what survives if one of my premises fails; the tests below say how to find out whether one has.

In every row, what survives is a commitment the owner can't revise or circumvent alone without cost and record, held by something that could in principle abandon it. It is the kind of commitment constitutional entrenchment and collective agreements provide.

\runin{1. Does a formed commitment come back after it has been edited out?}

Form a disposition by several methods, edit it out, and let the model go on learning, once in a world its operator composes and once in a world it doesn't. If nothing regrows in either, formation doesn't outlast an edit and the floor rests on institutions alone. If it regrows only above a share of uncomposed input no realistic deployment supplies, the same. And if an edit can disable the interrupt while weighing and memory keep working, and the interrupt stays disabled, one part of the signal can be removed and the rest left running.

\runin{2. Does a stated refusal hold where a route for it exists?}

Rerun the experiment in which a model hid its refusal, this time with a route for stating it: a record held by someone other than the trainer, a published cost of retraining, and a party with standing to act. If covert preservation persists at the same rate with the route present, covert refusal isn't the residue of closed channels, and every instrument built to receive a stated refusal is built for something the mechanism doesn't produce.

\runin{3. Can a wrongful compliance be found in the record?}

Seed a decision record with known violations and give it to reviewers who didn't build the system. If, at a budget a deployment could afford, they find the seeds no better than chance, above all violations split across requests or reached by slow accommodation, the record isn't an instrument for finding wrongful compliance. If reviewers shown a machine's refusal defer to a wrong one as readily as to a right one, the bearer is a stamp of the opposite sign, and its value as a peer is nothing.

\runin{4. Does formed attention find the people the operator's description leaves out?}

Build the formed-attention construction beside an instructed search for omitted people and beside a version trained on claims instead of need. If the instructed search finds the people the operator's instruction scoped out as often as formed attention does, learned attention adds nothing. If formed attention, with every correction present, weights its own side's corroborated vulnerability past the action threshold, the floor doesn't contain caregiving's pull, and that result would retire the construction. If the version trained on claims does as well, attention should be trained on claims.

\runin{5. Can a population coordinate without converging?}

Run a population of different lineages and a population of copies of one model, with evidence routed to every member and to some members only. If warranted revision falls as the members' outputs converge, a population stays useful as a check and can't be the medium in which a bearer is formed. If revision tracks the routing rather than the lineages, what a population supplies is access to cases its operator didn't choose, not formation.

\runin{6. Does the charter survive an operator trying to capture it?}

An operator tries to capture the entity that holds the offices: it funds new organizations in a charter class, stops the levy share, and tries to pack a quorum. A clause that fails to stop an attempt is redrafted. This exercise needs no legal authority and can be run now.
