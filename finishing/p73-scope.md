**The author's finding.** §6.1 through §6.3.4 are the flattest ~6,000 words in
the book: a mostly-competent bias-and-robustness survey sitting immediately
before the strongest sustained stretch in the manuscript, so that a reader who
gives up in the flat stretch never reaches it. **The exceptions prove the
point** — the racial capitalism / New Jim Code passage, the *What the computation
runs on, 2024–2026* box, and the annotation-labor material in §6.1.1 all sound
like the rest of the book, and everything between them does not. **Compress
§6.1–§6.1.3 to roughly half, cut §6.3.4 entirely, reduce §6.3.3 to a short
bridge, and move the Benjamin distinction and the data-center box earlier so the
chapter opens on material with a position in it.**

### Why it stopped at 65 percent

**The finding's exceptions are 670 words of §6.1.1 by themselves**, which is
nearly half the 1,380-word target before anything else is counted.

- **The annotation-labor material** — the Nairobi contract, the wages, the two
  things that follow and the second one that gets skipped — is ~310 words and is
  named in the finding as an exception. Untouched.
- **The acquisition material beside it** — *Bartz v. Anthropic*, the EU AI Act's
  training-data template, the Bender no-channel argument — is another ~360 words.
  **It is not named in the finding's list.** I kept it on the finding's own
  test rather than its list: it names the defendant on purpose, records that the
  defendant is the company whose model helped produce this book, and holds the
  book against itself. **Compressing it would execute the letter of the
  instruction against its stated reason.**

Reaching half means cutting the five cases: Rekognition, COMPAS, the
activity-recognition amplification study, PredPol, and the healthcare
target-choice case. **Three are load-bearing elsewhere** — §6.3.3 reaches the
ACLU and PredPol cases with *“Section~\ref{sec:6.1.1} covers two more,”* and
§6.1.2's *COMPAS Revisited* opens *“What COMPAS did is described above.”*
**That is a decision for the author and not one to take by arithmetic.**

## What was cut

- **§6.1's enumerated three-fronts framing**, kept as a clause; the section is now
  that clause and the three fronts developed, at 227 words.
- **§6.1.1's opening sentence**, which listed the five run-in heads immediately
  below it.
- **The generic lead before each case in §6.1.1** — the facial-recognition
  generality before the ACLU test, the loan-applicant hypothetical before COMPAS,
  the deployment-context generality before PredPol. **Every case and every
  citation is kept**; what went is the sentence that said in the abstract what

- **§6.1.2's *Public Engagement and Oversight* run-in.** §6.1 already carries its
  point about involving the people a system's decisions affect; its one addition —
  a body with teeth rather than a channel for feedback — moved into the COMPAS
  block's close.
