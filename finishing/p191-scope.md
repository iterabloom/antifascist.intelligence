# P191 — the damaged-weights construction, in the body rather than a footnote

Author instruction: give the deliberately-damaged-weights refinement "a lil
footnote." **It went into the body instead, and the reason is a rule in
`style.md`.**

## Why not a footnote

**The manuscript contains no footnotes.** Zero `\footnote` across 90 sections and
144 pages. One would be the only one in the book.

**And `style.md` §2a names the move it would be.** The rule is that a passage must
finish the sentence *the argument I am making would be harder to understand or
believe without this, because…*, and then: "A passage that cannot finish it is
cut. **Not compressed, not moved to a footnote** — cut, because a definition
serving no claim is a definition the book does not need at any length."

So the style sheet offers two destinations and a footnote is neither.

**The refinement finishes §2a's sentence, so it earns the body.** §3.3's
precommitment paragraph claims that precommitment "changes who can edit the floor
and what editing costs." Every other instrument named there makes the cost
one-time. This is the only one that makes it **recurring**, and recurring cost is
what converts an attack into a decision somebody has to keep making — which is
the attribution property the rest of the chapter asks for. Without it the
paragraph's second sentence is weaker than it reads.

## What went in

75 words, after the precommitment paragraph and before attestation. Plain, no
cross-references, no citation — the construction is the book's own and was never
sourced.

What was **not** restored from the original passage: the framing sentences about
raising the price of an attack and making the attempt visible, which §3.8 already
states directly and §3.3's precommitment paragraph already implies. Only the
refinement P190 recorded as genuinely lost.
