# P60 — chapter 3's three arrivals, stated as arguments instead of citations

**The author's finding.** Chapter~3 opened *"Three times in this book, working on
unrelated problems, I arrive at the same requirement and do not stop to notice
it."* That is a claim about the order the book was drafted in, not the order
anyone reads it in. A first-time reader met citations to sections 5.6.2, 10.3,
10.6 and 12.1.2 — material 150 pages ahead — and had to take all three arrivals
on trust.

**The instruction.** State the three arrivals as compressed arguments a reader can
evaluate on the spot, with the section numbers as pointers to where each gets
worked out rather than as the evidence. Two sentences each.

## What each arrival now argues

Each was compressed from the section it points at, not from its own former
one-line summary. The step chosen in each case is the one a reader can weigh
without having read ahead, which is not always the section's stated conclusion.

| Arrival | The step now on the page | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Autonomy | Every safeguard is external — it works by giving an outside body standing and information to intervene — and autonomy is the property that puts distance between the action and that body's attention. An external check establishes what a system did after it did it; declining in the first place is available only to something inside the system | section 5.6.2, paragraphs 5–6 |
| Accountability | Command responsibility does not reach a model vendor, who can neither order a strike nor prevent one, and product liability does not either, the model having done what it was built to do. Democratic deployment does not close it, every mechanism holding a state to account being an aggregation, and nothing in an aggregation protects anyone from the aggregate | sections 10.3 and 10.6 |
| Enforcement | A decade of declarations has produced eight overlapping voluntary bodies and one instrument that would bind, not yet in force. A commitment nobody can enforce is a different kind of object from one somebody can, and counting the two together is what makes the landscape look fuller than it is | section 12.1.2 |

**The first arrival changed shape and that was deliberate.** Section 5.6.2's
*stated* conclusion is that nothing substitutes for a genuine override, which is
what the old opener reported. A reader cannot evaluate that conclusion without the
reasoning under it, so what stands here is the reasoning: the external/internal
split two paragraphs earlier. It also lands closer to the floor — *something
inside the system that can decline* is what the chapter goes on to require —
where the override framing needs section 3.5 to invert it first.

## What did not change

- **The closing sentence is untouched.** *"Those are three descriptions of one
  thing: a constraint that does not bend to instruction, to aggregate preference,
  or to the party holding the system. Call it a floor."* Its three terms still map
  onto the three arrivals in order.
- **The count of three.** Two other sections depend on it — section 5.6.2's *"That
  requirement turns up in three places"* and section 12.1.2's *"This is one of
  three routes to the same requirement"* — and both were read and still hold.
- **The same four references**, so the body count does not move. They are
  parenthetical pointers now rather than the subject of their sentences, which is
  a form the book already uses three times.

## Two wordings corrected on a re-read, before the build

Both were compressions that had drifted past their sources.

- *"autonomy is exactly the distance between the system's action and that body's
  attention"* is an identity claim. Section 5.6.2 says autonomy is the property
  that **puts** distance there, which is what the argument needs and all it
  supports.
- *"makes the landscape look full"* → *"look fuller than it is,"* which is section
  12.1.2's own phrasing and sharper than the paraphrase.

## Form

Three paragraphs of two sentences rather than one block of six, so the three are
visible as three. **No em dash was added**: the appositive in the first arrival is
a colon and the turn in the second is a semicolon, style.md section 3a's test
applied before the fact rather than after. Two contrastive negations survive, in
the second and third arrivals, both being D-025's permitted case — the negated
alternative is the position the book argues against, preference-tracking and
declaration.
