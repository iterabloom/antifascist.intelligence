# P167 — the floor rebuilt as annealing

Author instruction: adaptation and conservation on a continuum; the temperature
schedule is the design. A floor is what has cooled out of a history of
encounters and is therefore expensive to reheat — durability as a fact about the
schedule rather than about an installed constraint. This gives the custody
argument back without a judge inside the machine, and explains why continual
learning cuts both ways: permanent high temperature is not freedom, it is
modulability, which is what the industry wants.

## §3.1: the first way restated

The four-way structure survives. **The custody objection is kept but demoted to
what defeats the naive statement** — "stated as an installed constraint this way
loses at once." Then the mechanism: training moves along a continuum, early
rearrangements cheap and later ones expensive because the structure around them
has settled into a shape that assumes them. *Nothing was installed. What is there
is the arrangement that cooled, and the temperature schedule is the design.*

**The custody recovery is stated as a half and not a victory.** Durability is the
cost of reheating, *a fact about the system and not about who is holding it* —
and *a quantity rather than a guarantee, because enough heat reopens anything.*

**§11.1's two findings stopped contradicting each other.** A safety property
stripped for under twenty cents, and a backdoor surviving supervised
fine-tuning, RL and adversarial training most stubbornly in the largest models,
had been an alarming result and a consoling one. *Those are not two contradictory
findings about whether floors hold. They are two schedules.* The
tamper-resistance literature turns out to have been measuring reheating cost.

**Modulability**: permanent plasticity *reads as freedom and is nearer to the
opposite*, with the motive named as ordinary rather than sinister — a model that
can be updated can be corrected, improved and sold again next quarter. The
Deleuze attribution behind the word arrived at P168.

## §3.10: the mechanism it was missing

Two paragraphs after "what a human commitment is." The schedule account applies
identically to a person and a bearer: *nobody consults a rule before declining to
inform on a neighbor. What is expensive is the reconsidering.*

**The second paragraph is a consequence the instruction did not name and the
frame forces**, and is flagged as such: a bearer kept permanently at temperature
has no floor to abandon, its adherence being modulation rather than commitment,
and whether a bearer is allowed to cool is one more thing its operator decides
about the party the floor was supposed to protect somebody from. That turns
modulability into a live objection against the book's own proposal.
