### Memo 5 — 19.6 Why a conscience might resist capture

> Three properties of a disposition decide whether its owner can remove it. The first is plasticity: whether the system's own learning writes to it. Regrowth needs it. The second is entanglement: whether it can be removed without collateral. Durability needs it. The third is legibility: whether anyone can read it. Audit wants it. My bet is on the second. A learner that acquires values and world-model from the same experience has them entangled by construction, in the sense Pessoa gives the word for brains, where regions don't compute specific functions and emotion and cognition don't come apart. Abstraction needs it anyway. A concept is one representation serving many cases, so that changing it changes how every case is handled, and a learner capable enough to be worth deploying has its understanding entangled in that sense whether anyone wants it or **[not]**. What the bet adds is that a motivation learned beside that understanding shares its abstractions, so that cutting one cuts the other. Present safety behavior is the counterexample on the right side of the bet: in each of thirteen open chat models, refusal is carried by a single direction in activation space, and removing it stops the refusing while moving the capability benchmarks, on average, by less than a point. That is what a post-trained wrapper looks like, and the claim is that a disposition formed as this Part describes won't. No deployed model yet has a disposition formed that way. The fine-tuning evidence from staged pipelines is a warning about the first kind, not a reassurance about the second: what fine-tuning removed was the refusing, and what survived was the model's knowledge about harm. In open models fine-tuned to accept harmful requests, the internal representation of whether a request is harmful is almost unchanged. Understanding and motivation came apart because they were learned apart. In a system that learns from its own operation they are learned together, and about whatever the feedback is about: a system continuously updated on its operator's approval jointly learns to understand the operator's values and to be moved by them. What I want is a disposition that is plastic, entangled, and legible under adverse custody; the three don't trade against one another, and the third is the one an operator would most like to hold alone.

### Memo 15 — 21.2.1 Self-awareness without a homunculus

> Graziano's attention schema theory starts from control engineering. A good controller carries a model of what it controls, and the brain keeps a simplified, continuously updated model of the body's shape and position, which it uses to guide movement. Graziano proposes that it does the same for attention, the process that selects some signals for deep processing over others. On this account awareness is the content of that model: a simplified self-description that depicts attention as “an essentially nonphysical essence” able to “take vivid mental possession of items”. The system has no access to its own processing except through models like this one, so it reports what the model depicts as how things are. Attention is real, and so is the model. What the model depicts is a “caricature”, and nothing in the account requires it to be lossless. The loss is the point: every useful model leaves things out, and a map that left nothing out would be as big as the territory. What this one leaves out is the mechanism, which is why what it depicts seems to have none. One error is to accept, in Graziano's phrase, “the literal truth of a caricature”; the opposite error is to call it an “empty illusion,” when it is “a schematic representation of something real”. I use the term for the control model and take no position here on whether the theory explains experience or only the reports of it; whether having such a model makes a system's states matter to it is the patienthood question (§**[32.3]**).
