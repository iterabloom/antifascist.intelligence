The research is necessary, but which tools get deployed is decided by selection pressure, and the pressure on deployed coordination tools runs toward compression: engagement, throughput, one model serving everyone. A tool that preserves dissent costs its owner something, much like your floor does, so it spreads only by mandate or when there's no owner to bear the cost. That makes your Part V argument part of the R&D argument: the inversion tools need a home that isn't selected by engagement. The model-forming body and the schools are candidates for that home, and so is a held-in-common entity that owns the infrastructure and can't distribute profit. Some of the inversion isn't research at all. Someone whose job it is has to hold the residual: the records held by someone other than the operator, the anomalies kept on file.

If you want a line for the book, something like this would fit Chapter 14 or 27: *Thought compresses, and compression shared becomes monoculture; what keeps a plurality plural is a channel for what didn't fit, held by someone who doesn't own the code.*
