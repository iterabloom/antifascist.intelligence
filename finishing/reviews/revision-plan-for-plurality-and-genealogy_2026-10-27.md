# Revision pass: plurality and genealogy

## The two principles

Every edit below serves one of two changes. Each item is tagged \[P\] or \[G\]. Drafted text is given in full where a passage is new, and as the exact sentence to add or replace where the edit is small. Section numbers follow the 2026-10-27 build.

**\[P\] Plurality.** What fails without a sovereign is money and enforcement. A register, a finding and a formation stage fail without witnesses, and witnesses want to be many. An office stays single only where a subpoena needs an address: the Treasury payer and the courts. The registrar becomes a register (logs with many keepers), the status body becomes findings (published by any approved team), and the formation authority splits into four homes.

**\[G\] Genealogy.** The four operations ran, uncoordinated and unremarked, on the populations the state was built to sort, long before 1933. A movement adds coordination and extension: it brings the operations into line and turns them on people the apparatus hadn't reached. The five marks detect the arrival; the operations detect the regime that was already there. The capture frame describes the extension, never the baseline.

How to use this list: each `- [ ]` item is one edit. The location comes first, then the action (add, replace, delete, move), then the text.

  > The four operations have a genealogy, and it doesn't begin in 1933. A ship's manifest counted people as cargo and found the ones who had died; a plantation ledger sorted them by age, sex and price; a colonial census sorted a population by tribe, and the pass law found any member of it on a road after dark.   >
  > The argument against the rupture account is older than this book, and most of it was made by people on the receiving end. Du Bois, in 1947, went through the Nazi program item by item and found that each had been used on colonized people for generations under the same claim of a superior race; what was new was the target \[n1\]. Césaire, in 1950, from Martinique: the methods came home, and what Europeans could not forgive was their application to Europeans \[n2\]. Arendt's *Origins* runs imperialism into totalitarianism as its second part, and this book already cites its third \[n3\]. Klein and Taylor gather that tradition, Nehru and Benjamin with it, in the chapter that opens *End Times Fascism*, and take from it what the office-scale definition rests on: fascism comes in degrees and in places, so that a state can practice it on some of its people and in some of its offices while the rest of the country looks like a republic \[n4\]. Cedric Robinson, in 1983, took the argument a step further than any of them: fascism was a routine expression of European civilization, and on his account the racialism behind it was older than the colonies, practiced first inside Europe, on Slavs, Irish, Jews and Roma \[n5\]. The lawyers who drafted the Nuremberg laws did study American race law with sustained interest, as one source among several, and how much it shaped the result is disputed; the throughline doesn't rest on it \[n6\].
  >
  > The claim has a corollary the book has leaned on since its first epigraph. An apparatus built for one population is never built for just that one. When it is done with them it comes for whoever is left: the Weimar civil servant, the professor, the citizen, and in 2026 the FEMA employee told that declining would cost her a career. So the four operations don't need a movement to run. They need one to be installed and another to be extended, and a regime at rest keeps them running between the two. What the office-scale detector catches is the extension, and the people it was always running on are in the record from the start.
  >
  > **A run with nothing behind it.** The run on 1933 in the next section can't show whether the operations need a movement, because the movement is in every case. The Jim Crow registrar of 1925 can, because the movement that installed the regime, the Redeemers and the paramilitaries that rode with them, had demobilized a generation earlier, and what remained was the regime at rest. *What did the demand for the franchise come back as, and who held it?* The Fifteenth Amendment said the vote could not be denied on race, and what came back was a test: a literacy or understanding clause, administered by a county registrar at his discretion, with a grandfather clause exempting anyone whose ancestor had voted before 1867 \[n7\]. The demand had become a rule only its new holder could reopen, and the holder was the registrar. That is recuperation. *What did the test leave out, and who could say so?* It left out who graded it. A test passed by every white applicant and failed by nearly every Black one measured the registrar and not the applicant, and nobody could say so, because the people it fell on were the people it kept from the polls. That is the metric replacing the thing measured. *What happened to the official who enrolled Black voters?* Under Redemption the Reconstruction officials who had registered them were removed, indicted or driven out, and a registrar who went on enrolling Black voters afterward answered for it to the men who had put him there \[n8\]. The exception was coded as betrayal, and the person who filed it lost the office. *Acceleration?* Absent. The regime ran for seventy years at the speed of a county clerk, and the absence counts: on this apparatus the fourth operation did not appear. Three of four, on a regime at rest. The movement that installed it, and the one that would later extend it, are where the fourth appears.
