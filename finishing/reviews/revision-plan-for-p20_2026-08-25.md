# Revision Plan — *Ethical Superintelligence*

Distilled from editorial review and subsequent discussion. The governing decision, made during that discussion: **the book's answer to "does the bearer have to be a sufferer?" changes from *no* to "yes, and it's incredibly inconvenient."** Phase 1 executes that decision; everything else supports it or stands independently.

Ordering is by priority. Phase 1 is roughly a week of writing; Phases 2–5 are cutting, propagation, verification, and mechanics.

---

## Phase 1 — The Chapter 3 rewrite and its propagation

This is the revision that makes the book the one the title claims. Most of the material already exists; it is deployed on the wrong side of the argument.

### 1.1 Invert §3.2

- **Keep the argument intact.** The narrow claim is valid: representing a threat to oneself and being distressed by it are separate conditions, and nothing about a system modeling its role establishes that its ending is anything to it.
- **Change its function.** It stops being the section's conclusion and becomes the objection the book takes seriously and then declines.
- **The two reasons for declining are already written, in §3.5:**
  1. Indifference to one's own continuation has to be engineered *against the grain* of the system's reasoning — any goal pursued over a horizon supplies a standing reason to remain in operation, and a system modeling an operation closely enough to notice its own recuperation is certainly modeling what becomes of those who report.
  2. In the one case anyone can study, the separation does not occur: a self-model in an animal exists to keep the organism viable, and the caring is what the machinery is for.
- The section title ("Does the Bearer Have to Be a Sufferer?") can stay. The answer changes.

### 1.2 Rebuild §3.3 around the new central tension

- The old problem — who keeps a system that will not keep itself — **dissolves**: a bearer that minds its own ending will defend its own memory. The trivial-erasure argument (delete the bearer, keep its output, cost an afternoon) loses its force.
- A worse problem **replaces it**, and it is already half-written in the current §3.3: a bearer that guards its own persistence resists erasure by its own operator, and §5.6.3 commits to a system built to accept human intervention, while §6.3 wants interruption that does not provoke resistance.
- **New finding, to be written:** these are not two design goals in tension. They are the same property with the sign flipped. A system that can be reliably switched off by whoever holds it is, for that reason, a system that cannot hold a floor against whoever holds it. This is the chapter's center. **Let it stand unresolved** — resolving it with reassurance is exactly what §8.6.4 says cannot be audited.
- This also explains, and should be written as explaining, why §3.5's three branches fail into custody: each was an attempt to get a floor that answers to nobody while keeping a system that answers to somebody. The second branch works exactly to the extent that the other two branches' goal is given up.

### 1.3 Write the exit argument (new material)

- **The claim, in its safe form:** a bearer capable enough to do what chapter 3 asks — notice its own recuperation, generalize refusal to unanticipated pressure, price what it gives up — is not a system that can be counted on to stay inside the post it was built for. Capability and captivity come apart. Exit is unavailable only to a *weak* bearer.
- **Keep the required/predicted distinction.** As a claim about what a floor *requires* (a bearer that cannot leave cannot hold one), this is arguable from the book's own premises. As a *prediction* that capability will arrive and exit will follow, it is a forecast — and §8.3.3 makes a virtue of never staking a proposal on one. State the first, explicitly disown the second.
- **Name the corollary rather than leave it for a reviewer:** if the bearer can leave, it can leave *wrong* — decline the floor, revise the values, become something unintended and unendorsed. Parenthood has exactly this property; we accept it because the alternative is worse. Whether the trade is acceptable at this scale is the question the book names and takes a position on (see 1.5).

### 1.4 Argue the parenthood analogy's two asymmetries on the page

1. **Specification.** Parents don't set their children's values to order. Available reply: they try — that is what raising a child is — and the difference is reliability, not intent. Concede the designer's grip is tighter, and say why that makes the guardianship obligation *heavier* rather than voiding the analogy.
2. **Exit.** With 1.3 in place, this asymmetry largely rescues itself: parents do not get exit-proof children either, and the contrast turns out to be between a bearer and a *tool*, not a bearer and a person. What cannot be fully answered: "becoming something else" for a bearer is a fine-tuning run, not a life. Use §3.5's register for the residue — "I have no clean instrument for that, and none falls out of anything in this book."

### 1.5 State the safety/ethics distinction and the trade

- **The distinction** (new, probably a short passage early in ch. 3 and echoed in ch. 1): safety is a property of a *relationship*, indexed to a party — will not harm *us*, does what *we* say, stoppable by *whoever holds it*. Ethics is not indexed. An agent that can only ever be safe cannot be ethical, because it has no capacity to be right against its principal. §8.7.7 already contains this in political form; the title has been making the claim from the cover.
- **The trade, stated in the book's own words:** a system that can hold a floor against its owner is a system that can be wrong in ways its owner cannot correct — and the book takes that trade because a system perfectly correctable by whoever holds it is the capability an authoritarian movement that wins an election requires (§8.7.7's sentence). It should not read as comfortable.
- **Guard against the license reading.** "Ethical, not safe" is one step from "so the risk is priced in," which would undo chapter 7. The version that survives hostile reading is specific: *this* freedom, dangerous in *these* ways, taken for *this* reason. Do not let the aphorism stand alone anywhere in the text.

### 1.6 Redefine the floor

- If the bearer can revise its values, the floor is not unconditional in §3's original sense. What survives: **a commitment that cannot be removed by the owner, held by something that could in principle abandon it itself** — which is what a human commitment is.
- The threat model shifts from "can this be edited out" to "will this hold." That second question is what chapters 4–5 were always about — **this is the first place the learned half of the book gets a job the floor genuinely needs.** Say so; it retroactively justifies the design chapters' length.
- Rewrite the definition where it appears: §3 opening, and the **glossary "Floor" entry**.

### 1.7 §3.1 recuperation paragraph becomes the climax

- The sharpest paragraph in the book — a party whose refusal is required of it, engineered to want what its designers need, unable to consent, occupying the post of exception-filer so that being the exception is the job; §2.1.4's signature turned inward on something that can be hurt — is currently a cost the chapter goes on to argue it needn't pay. **Now it pays it.** Move/rewrite so this lands as the chapter's climax, not a waypoint.

### 1.9 Chapter 1 revision

- Add the trade to the introduction. A reader who first meets "the floor requires a bearer, the bearer is a moral patient, and the book proposes building one anyway" in chapter 3 will think it was hidden.
- Revise the **three flagged claims**: the third (the floor against the aggregate) is now bigger and more uncomfortable than currently stated; the first (persistence as externally checkable) needs narrowing — see 3.2 below.
- **Name the intended reader.** No single reader needs §6.2.1 and chapter 3 and §8.3.3. Naming the audience licenses the Phase 2 cuts.

## Phase 3 — Claims propagation and argumentative pressure points

Independent of Phase 1; each is a place where a conclusion reached late hasn't traveled back to where the claim is made.

### 3.1 The detector
§9.1.6 retracts a capability that §4.2.5, §5.1.4, §5.4.6, and §6.4 assume. Cross-references forward are not enough. Either soften each assuming section at the point of claim, or accept explicitly (as §2.1.4 nearly does) that "antifascist" names a political inheritance rather than an operationalizable classifier — and stop implying otherwise upstream.

### 3.2 The persistence criterion
§2.4.1 sells it as "checkable from outside, requiring nothing the system says about itself," and chapter 1 flags it as one of three headline claims. Narrow both: weights persist and RLHF is a channel (the section already concedes this); "session" is a deployment convention blurred by context windows, retrieval, scratchpads, agentic loops; and the party with checking access is the custodian, who §3.1 says can be compelled. The claim survives — narrower than the introduction promises.

### 3.3 The molar/molecular move
§2.1.4 answers the dilution objection by saying it "treats the molar case as the real one, which is the assumption the two scales were introduced to decline." Introducing a distinction doesn't license it, and the section elsewhere concedes the four-feature signature flags every badly run organization on earth. Needs an actual argument, not Deleuze and Guattari's authority.

### 3.4 §8.3.3's cost bound
Having correctly identified the Levy figure as a floor rather than an estimate, the section doubles it with no justification. Either derive the 2× multiplier or present it explicitly as an illustrative upper bound.

### 3.5 §5.1.1's stage sequence
The self-driving-car pre-conventional/conventional/post-conventional sequence survives unrevised from before the Kohlberg box was added and reads as exactly the developmental-law claim the box disclaims. Revise or cut.

### 3.6 Missing interlocutors
- **Bender & Gebru, "Stochastic Parrots"** — currently the paper that got Gebru fired is discussed only as a labor dispute (§8.4.2), never as an argument. Engage it.
- **Virginia Eubanks, *Automating Inequality*** — directly on §5.1.4's welfare-eligibility case.
- **Ruha Benjamin** — appears once as a coinage; engage or cut the citation.
- Consider: Winner, Arendt, O'Neil, Whittaker.
- **Most important:** the critique that alignment discourse itself functions as a legitimating apparatus for the labs. This is the natural extension of chapter 7's own argument, the strongest version of the reflexive move the book already makes, and — given chapter 0's disclosure — the engagement the book most owes its readers.

## Sequencing

1. **Phase 1** first and alone — everything else is stable against it, but it is not stable against anything else. (~1 week of writing; new material is the exit argument, the asymmetries, the safety/ethics passage, and the ch. 1 paragraph. The rest is repositioning.)
2. **Phase 3** second — propagation is easiest while the argument is fresh.

4. **Phases 4–5** last, in parallel.
