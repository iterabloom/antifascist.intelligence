# P171 — the Nietzsche edition verified, and bad conscience attributed

Author instruction: verify the edition and translation, then add the citation
P170 had deliberately left out.

**`nietzsche1998genealogy`**: *On the Genealogy of Morality*, trans. Maudemarie
Clark and Alan J. Swensen, intro. Clark, Indianapolis: Hackett, 1998, ISBN
978-0-87220-283-2. Original *Zur Genealogie der Moral: Eine Streitschrift*,
Leipzig: C. G. Naumann, 1887.

**Three editions were compared.** Kaufmann/Hollingdale (Vintage, 1967) is the
classic and the one most readers own; Diethe (Cambridge Texts, 1994) is more
literal; Clark and Swensen is the scholarly default, a Nietzsche specialist
working with a Germanist.

**The choice barely matters, because the locator is edition-independent.** The
book already cites Kant by Akademie pagination, and the equivalent here is
treatise and section: `[II:16–17]`, which holds against the other two
translations. The entry says so, so nobody has to re-verify if the edition ever
changes.

## Where it landed

§5.2.1's second reason, after the sentence about arranging for a system to hurt
itself on cue. Nietzsche *arrives at the same object from the other end*:
instincts that cannot discharge outward turn inward, and a creature denied an
outlet learns to do to itself what it could not do to the world. **The load-bearing
sentence is the next one** — he was describing something that happened to people
over centuries, under a compulsion nobody designed, and *it is a different
proposition written into a specification.* That gap between description and
design is the author's objection, stated.

**Paraphrased and not quoted.** The mechanism was verified across sources; no
sentence was checked verbatim against the Clark and Swensen text, so nothing is
in quotation marks. Recorded rather than glossed over.
