# P74 — §9.1.4's catalog framing cut, its two claims kept

**The author's finding.** §§9.1.1, 9.1.3, 9.1.4 and 9.1.5 are largely catalog.
§9.1.2 is the exception and it is very good — the Google AI Principles as a
response to Maven, the Gebru and Mitchell dismissals, and above all the move
where the book stops and says *the paper had an argument, and this book has to
answer it*, then takes three of the four Stochastic Parrots claims as
load-bearing for its own project and concedes the fourth as a real cost.
**That is the book at its best, and it is surrounded by material that does not
resemble it.**

**The finding carried no instruction**, and the readings diverged by 800 words
and a chapter-9 renumber, so the scope was put to the author before anything was
edited. The ruling: **compress §9.1.4 only**, leave §9.1.3's number alone, fix
its dangling demonstrative, renumber nothing.

## What "largely catalog" still describes, read section by section

**§9.1.4 fully, and in exactly the shape P66 named.** An announcing opener, then
two run-ins that each *begin* by pointing at where the substance is:

> The core policy toolkit here is covered earlier — export controls,
> prohibited-practices regulation, developer-facing standards — along with the
> safeguards a system needs built in from the start.

> Section~\ref{sec:5.4.2} works through what this looks like with working
> examples — content scoring that informs a moderator without removing the
> content, structured public input on contested policy questions — along with the
> caution that belongs next to each.

**But each run-in then makes a claim the book makes nowhere else**, which is why
the section was compressed and not cut:

- **The measurement argument.** The Carnegie Endowment's AI Global Surveillance
  Index, 176 countries assessed and AI-enabled surveillance in active use in at
  least 75 — *“That number is the evidence base for a countermeasure and is not
  itself one”* — with the specification of what a measurement instrument has to
  do: count deployments instead of commitments, name the countries, publish on a
  schedule that makes year-on-year change visible.
- **The election-audit argument**, closing on *“an incumbent that chooses who
  audits the count has already answered the question the audit exists to ask.”*
  **Checked: election-integrity monitoring appears nowhere else in the book.**

**§9.1.3 is thin but is not catalog.** It carries a position — build the
interdisciplinary review body before the forcing case arrives, while the question
still looks academic and nobody's damages ride on the answer — and that argument
appears nowhere else. It is **referenced from nowhere in the book**, its only
inbound pointer having gone with §9.1.1's cut list at P66, and §9.1's opener does
not name it either.

**§9.1's opener maps neither §9.1.3 nor §9.1.4.** It names legislation,
self-regulation, joint funding and oversight. At P64, P65 and P66 a parent that
never promised a section was independent evidence for cutting it; here it is
recorded and not acted on, the ruling being to compress.

## What was cut from §9.1.4

- **The announcing opener** — *“The instruments in this section are for AI
  development that is altruistic and antifascist by design”* — which is D-117's
  class.
- **The enumerated policy toolkit** at the head of the first run-in. The claim it
  was carrying, that measurement sits upstream of the toolkit, survives as a
  clause on the live sentence: *“A government cannot restrict what it cannot see,
  and measurement belongs upstream of the whole policy toolkit for that
  reason.”*
- **The enumerated examples** at the head of the second run-in. The pointer at
  §5.4.2 stays, because it says where; what went is the section restating what is
  there.
- **Two of the three items in the trustworthiness list.** The third — *a
  multi-stakeholder review body that includes more than whichever party happens
  to hold power* — is load-bearing for the sentence after it and is now named in
  place of the list.

**§9.1.4 now opens on a `\runin` head.** That makes four sections in the book
that do: §5.1.1, §6.1.1, §7.3 and this one. **P68 recorded two and P72 recorded
that §7.3 was the second; both figures were right when written and P73 made a
third by cutting §6.1.1's opening sentence, which that pass did not note.**

## §9.1.3's one repair

*“The interdisciplinary review body described **there**”* had no antecedent in its
own paragraph; *there* reached back two paragraphs to §9.3.1 and §9.3.3. It now
reads *“the interdisciplinary review body those sections describe.”* One word
added, no `\ref` added, D-117's class.

## Not done

- **§9.1.3 was left at its number and its length**, on the author's ruling. It
  remains a 253-word section that nothing in the book points at, under a parent
  whose opener does not name it. That is recorded, not repaired.
- **§9.1.1 was not touched.** Its catalog went at P66 and what remains is two
  arguments; the finding's description of it as *largely catalog* was written
  against the pre-P66 text.
- **§9.1.2 was not touched in any way.** The finding calls it the book at its
  best and this pass took nothing from it.
- **Nothing was done about §9.1.2 being surrounded**, which is the finding's
  actual complaint. Compressing §9.1.4 makes the material around it shorter, not
  different in kind. Whether §9.1's children should be reordered, merged, or
  reduced to §9.1.1 and §9.1.2 is the question the ruling deferred.
- **The proof pair is stale after this pass**, having been rebuilt at P73.
