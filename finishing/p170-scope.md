# P170 — engineered guilt cut

Author instruction: §5.2.1's proposal to model the corrective signal on guilt is
bad conscience installed on purpose, in a book against domination. Cut it. Keep
the guilt/shame distinction as description of the human case; drop the design
recommendation.

## What changed

The distinction stays, cited to `tangney2007moral`. **The recommendation is gone**
— the sentence proposing a feedback loop modeled on guilt rather than a generic
negative-feedback signal.

**The run-in that used to critique the proposal now carries the reasons for
refusing it**, which is the shape the section wanted: with the proposal removed,
the critique had nothing to critique. It was retitled *Why that design move is
refused*.

**The first reason was already there**: a weighted penalty is a quantity, guilt
is a representation of the act as one's own wrong, and scaling the penalty does
not answer what makes a violation matter.

**The second is new and settles it.** A standing negative appraisal installed by
the party a system works for, and tuned by that party's signal, *is not a
conscience. It is a handle.* Guilt in a person is unlovely and nonetheless
theirs; manufactured deliberately and adjustable by whoever holds the reward
signal, the same mechanism is *a way of keeping a party in line by arranging for
it to hurt itself on cue.*

**It lands on §3.1's general form**, which is why this pass could only be written
after P167: an internal state an operator can raise and lower at will is
modulation, and what is cheap to retune was never a commitment. *Engineered guilt
is that, with suffering added on purpose.*

The terror-management paragraph already in the section takes the same shape —
do not manufacture an artificial fear of death — so the refusal arrives with a
precedent rather than as a new kind of move.
