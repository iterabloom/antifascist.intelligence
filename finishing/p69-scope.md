# P69 — the aggregation argument cut back to two developments and four clauses

**The author's finding.** The aggregation argument — every governance mechanism
here makes public preference more legible, none protects anyone from it, a
perfectly deliberative process that deports a demographic has produced a
faultless output — appears at length at six sites: the Introduction's third
advance note, chapter 3's opening, chapter 8's opening, §8.2.2 (its home, stated
twice inside the section), chapter 9's opening, and §10.6's third head. *“It is a
good argument and it is the book's spine. It does not get better on the fourth
hearing, and by the sixth a reader who has been paying attention feels managed.”*
**Keep §8.2.2 and §10.6. Cut the Introduction and chapter 9 to a clause. Chapter
3's opening can assume it.**

**The census is right.** Verified independently across the manuscript. §8.2.2 is
**§8.2.1** after P66 — the finding is in pre-P66 numbering, as the last one was in
pre-P64 — and `renumber-map_2026-08-31f.tsv` translates.

**A word-based check would have missed one of the six.** Chapter 8's opening
states the argument without the word *aggregation*: *“all of them make public
preference more legible, and not one of them protects anybody from it.”* Grepping
for the term finds five sites, not six. The census was read, not measured.

## The three cuts

### The Introduction's third note, 185 → 92 words

Gone: the enumeration of mechanisms — *consultations, citizen juries,
participatory design, deliberative polling, feedback collected from raters at
scale*; *more legible and more actionable*; and the deportation thought
experiment. **What stayed is one clause of argument and all of the consequence**,
because the consequence is what an advance note is for.

> The third note is that almost everything this book proposes about
> accountability is machinery for aggregating what people want, **and no
> aggregation protects anybody from the aggregate.** So what makes a system
> antifascist cannot be that it faithfully tracks what people want…

### Chapter 9's opening, 156 → 120 words

**This was the clearest of the three, and the reason is that chapter 9 was
quoting the section it points at.** Its second sentence — *“a better aggregation
instrument produces a more faithful rendering of what a majority wants —
including when what it wants is something done to a minority of it”* — is §8.2.1's
sentence almost word for word, in an opener whose first clause already says
*Section~\ref{sec:8.2.1} names a limit*. The pointer and the restatement were
doing the same job twice. The pointer now carries it alone:

> Section~\ref{sec:8.2.1} names a limit chapter~\ref{sec:8} cannot repair from
> inside: **no aggregation of public preference protects anybody from the
> aggregate.** What does is a different kind of machinery…

### Chapter 3's opening, 1,168 → 1,155 words

*“Every mechanism that holds a state to account works by aggregating preference
and nothing in an aggregation protects anyone from the aggregate”* becomes **“no
aggregation of preference protects anyone from the aggregate.”** The sentence
already carried its pointer at §10.6, so it now assumes the argument and cites
where it is made, which is what *can assume it* asks for.

## The dependency the instruction did not name

**Chapter 3's opening says *“The political form of this is already on the page.”***
The page is the Introduction's third note. Had the note been cut to its argument
clause and no more, that backward reference would have pointed at something the
Introduction no longer said.

So the Introduction keeps the conclusion in full — *what makes a system
antifascist cannot be that it faithfully tracks what people want; it has to be
that there are things it will not do to a person regardless of who wants them* —
and loses only the argument for it. **That is what fixed how short the note could
go**: the argument compresses to a clause, the conclusion could not be touched at
all.

## What the cuts do to the book's count of the argument

**Before:** two developments (§8.2.1, §10.6) and four statements at length.
**After:** the same two developments and four clauses. The thought experiment —
*a perfectly deliberative, perfectly informed, perfectly representative process*
producing *a faultless output* — now appears **once in the book**, in §8.2.1,
where the finding says its home is. It appeared twice.

## What was lost, named rather than repaired

- **“A floor that holds against the aggregate, including a well-formed and
  popular aggregate.”** That phrasing is gone from the book; it existed only in
  the Introduction. The idea survives in §10.6's countermajoritarian half in
  different words — *rights that hold regardless of being popular, courts that
  rule against a government which won the last election fairly* — and the
  compressed note keeps *regardless of who wants them*.
- **The Introduction's enumeration of the governance mechanisms.** §8.2.1's is
  fuller and names the same five.
- **Chapter 9's statement of the better-instrument-makes-it-worse form.** §8.2.1
  carries it, and chapter 9 points there in its first clause.

## Named and not acted on

**Chapter 8's opening got no ruling and is already at clause length.** It is one
clause inside a roadmap sentence — *“closes on the limit its mechanisms share:
all of them make public preference more legible, and not one of them protects
anybody from it”* — pointing at §8.2.1, which is inside its own chapter. Cutting
it would leave the chapter's roadmap silent about the thing that chapter closes
on. **Left as it stands**, and flagged here because the finding lists it as one of
the six without saying what to do with it.

**§8.2.1 states it twice inside the section, as the finding notes, and both
statements were kept.** The instruction says to keep §8.2.1, and the two are a
statement and its thought experiment rather than two statements — but the
doubling is real and this pass did not rule on it. **One word from the author
collapses them.**

**§10.6 also states it twice** — *“Nothing in an aggregation mechanism protects
anyone from the aggregate”* in the second paragraph of its third head, and the
countermajoritarian machinery in the third. The finding calls this half the
strongest version and asks for it to be kept. Untouched.
