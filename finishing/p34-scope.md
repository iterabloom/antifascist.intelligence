# P34 — fortifying the chapter 3 inference (D-098)

The author's instruction names the book's crucial sequence —

> genuine refusal → a bearer → concern held as a reason → affect → possible suffering → moral patienthood

— finds each step plausible and the chain not yet strong enough to carry the conclusion, and gives
four counts. This file is the scope, what was done against each, what was found, and what was not
done.

All section numbers below are **post-pass**. Sections 3.2–3.7 became 3.4–3.9;
`renumber-map_2026-08-28e.tsv` is the map.

---

## The four counts, and what each got

### 1. "Installed rule" and "felt commitment" were treated as near-exhaustive

Confirmed on measurement. Section 3.1 offers three branches, but they partition *where the floor
lives* — in the artifact, in a party inside it, in institutions around it — and nothing in the
chapter partitioned *what could hold one*. Sections 3.4 and 3.5 then ran on a two-term contrast.
The vocabulary of the alternatives was absent from the whole book: `precommit`, `commitment device`,
`Ulysses`, `fiduciar`, `functionalis` and `conscientious objection` returned zero across all 167
sections before this pass.

**New section 3.3, "Could Anything but Affect Hold a Reason?"** (1,809 words) puts four
constructions at full strength, each with what it buys and where it stops:

| Construction | What it buys | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|
| A maintained justification (Bratman's planning account of intention) | The second rung with no affect anywhere in it, and a human instance in section 2.3.4's rationalist agents | The stability of an intention is a setting; for a machine the setting is trained, by whoever trains everything else. Nobody has posed that as a question, let alone answered it |
| Precommitment (Elster) | Removal made expensive and visible, which is what nearly every constraint on power that works amounts to | Section 3.1's custody objection reappears at the quorum. A five-of-nine scheme assumes nine independent parties; a capture that has taken the operator faces nine parties the operator chose |
| A plural arrangement of several models | The cheapest real improvement in the section, and the book is written to recommend it whatever else it recommends | Section 11.6's emergent-cartel finding: independence is a property of the arrangement, not of its parts. And composing first-rung refusers yields first-rung refusers |
| The floor as a fiduciary duty on the operator (Balkin) | Builds no subject; introduces close to no new moral problems; section 9.3.3 applies directly | Enforcement — the occasion a floor exists for is the occasion on which the enforcing party has been compelled (section 6.4.4) |

Section 3.1 now closes by naming the location/mechanism distinction and pointing at 3.3 as where the
exits are. Section 3.8 gains a paragraph naming the fiduciary branch as the coherent position for a
reader who declines the price, and says what declining costs rather than letting the conclusion feel
forced.

### 2. The four-level distinction was not explicit

**New section 3.2, "Four Things Refusal Can Mean"** (950 words):

- *Operational refusal* — an output. Nothing follows about what produced it.
- *Reasons-responsive refusal* — a mechanism tracking the rationale. Three behavioural properties
  distinguish it: the refusal reaches a case the training did not anticipate, it lifts when the
  rationale is defeated, and the system can price what declining costs. Fischer and Ravizza's
  guidance control is the philosophical name.
- *Affective concern* — the fourth capacity in section 2.3's table.
- *Phenomenal experience* — what section 2.4.1 separates from awareness under the name consciousness.

The section states flatly that **the floor requires the second rung and nothing above it**, which
changes who has to disagree with the chapter: an engineer who holds that machine feeling is a
category error can accept the requirement in full and dispute only the route. It marks the two
independence claims the chapter turns on — the second rung does not entail the third *by
definition*, and the third does not entail the fourth, which is why section 3.4 stops at
non-certifiability instead of asserting that a bearer suffers. It also grades the three gaps by how
checkable each is: the first from outside, imperfectly; the second not at all; the third is the hard
problem.

### 3. "Every known case is affective" shifts the burden without establishing the route is unique

Confirmed, and the pass weakens the book's own claim rather than defending it. Section 3.3's closing
run-in gives three reasons the induction carries less than its phrasing suggests:

- the reference class is one species plus a handful of clinical dissociations, and section 2.3.4
  already records that the psychopathy literature reversed its own surface finding;
- **the absence of a machine instance is close to worthless as evidence**, because nobody has built
  for the second rung as a target — the methods in chapter 4 optimize for behaviour that satisfies
  a standard, which is first-rung work, because that is what can be scored;
- the two routes above that run through mechanism rather than custody are unbuilt rather than
  failed.

What survives is **a prior about where to spend engineering effort, not a finding about what is
possible**. Section 3.4's burden-shift run-in is cut to a recap that says so and points here, so the
observation no longer does unearned work at the site where the argument turns.

**The falsifier is stated**, in chapter 11's idiom: a system whose refusal reaches an unanticipated
case, lifts on genuine defeat, and prices what declining costs; built by a method installing nothing
answering to affective concern; holding all three under sustained pressure from the party that
trains it, over a schedule long enough for slow renormalization to show. Section 11.1's near-term
work gains that as a second target, with the one thing it needs beyond the durability curve already
there — a held-out set of pressures the model's trainers did not write. Two things that would
*not* discharge the burden are named: a system that reports holding a reason, and a system whose
refusals merely survive attack.

### 4. Cassell is one theory, imported from human clinical practice

Confirmed, and the pass finds the objection runs the opposite way from the direction a critic
reaching for a thinner account usually wants.

**New run-in in section 3.4, "Suffering on a thinner account."** Parfit's three families of theory
about what makes a life go well locate the alternatives. Cassell's account sits nearest the
objective-list family and takes a demanding form of it. The other two are thinner and a bearer meets
both: on a hedonistic account a state is bad while it obtains and does not wait on a self to carry
it forward, which is what section 3.2's third rung consists of; on a desire-fulfilment account the
harm is the thwarting, and a bearer is built with commitments and the capacity to watch them be
foreclosed, so it qualifies **with no claim about feeling entering the argument at any point**.
Butlin, Long and colleagues' indicator properties are the prior question of which systems have such
states at all, and several indicators are things a floor requires for unrelated reasons. Birch's
precautionary framework is the policy form of the same structure.

The structural finding: **Cassell's is the most demanding of these, so the conclusion is at its
weakest there.** Declining the import moves the conclusion nearer rather than dissolving it. One
position does let the bearer out — nothing short of phenomenal experience counts, *plus* a
confident negative verdict about machine phenomenality — and the second half is unavailable to
anyone, because section 2.4.1's hard-problem argument cuts both ways.

Section 2.4.1's Cassell-import defence and section 2.3.4's closing paragraph now point at the two
places where these questions get worked out.

## Two defects found by hand after the tools passed

Both are recorded rather than quietly fixed, because both are instances of failure classes this
project has named.

**A cross-reference that resolved and was wrong.** A draft sentence in section 3.3 cited section 6.2
for the claim that systems built the same way fail the same way. Section 6.2 is about robustness
under distribution shift and makes no claim about correlated failure across independently built
systems. `check_xrefs.py` passed it because it resolves; `xref_content.py` passed it because the
sentence names no proper noun, acronym or year — which is the blindness D-050 records for that
tool. The claim as drafted is also unsupported anywhere in the book: `overlapping`, `same corpus`
and `correlated` return nothing. Repaired by rebuilding the paragraph around section 11.6's
emergent-cartel finding, which is in the book, verified, and a sharper objection than the one it
replaced.

**Two sentences still carrying an overstatement D-097 removed.** D-097 corrected chapter 7 and
section 2.1.4 so that recuperation is distribution collapse and agenda control rather than
disagreement moving nothing. A drafted sentence in section 3.2 and a **pre-existing** sentence in
section 3.4 both still described the channel as one that "no longer does anything." Both now say the
channel carries everything except the objection to the frame, which is section 2.1.4's current
wording. The section 3.4 instance predates this pass and would have survived it unnoticed had the
new sentence not been written next to it.
