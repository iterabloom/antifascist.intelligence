# P63 — what survives if the two mechanism routes work

**The author's finding.** Section 3.3 ends *"I would like to be wrong here,"* and
puts the two mechanism routes ahead of the bearer under the Three Rs. Both are
right, and **the book never budgets for those routes succeeding.** There is no
passage on what survives if a non-affective bearer works, and quite a lot does:
the custody analysis, the enumeration procedure in 3.6, the whole of chapter 7,
the exit argument in 3.8, which turns on capability rather than affect. A reader
who rejects the induction over one species has to reconstruct that alone. Half a
page gathering it would make the argument harder to dismiss wholesale.

## Where it went, and why not at 3.3

The passage is 318 words in **section 3.9**, between the two carry-forward items
and the chapter's proposition. Section 3.3 is where the reader who rejects the
induction stops, and it was the obvious home until the material was listed:
**three of the four items the author names are downstream of 3.3.** Gathering
them there would have rebuilt exactly the defect P60 removed from the chapter's
opener — a passage whose evidence sits a hundred pages further on than the reader
is. Section 3.9 has all of it behind the reader, and it is already the chapter's
what-survives section, with two reader-cases in place: the one who finds the
price too high, and the one who accepts it and owes the attempt first. This is a
third.

**Section 3.3 gets a six-word pointer instead**, at the end of the line the
author quotes: *"…would go with it. Section 3.9 says what would not."* That is
where the reader who is going to bounce actually bounces, and the pointer says
the loss is bounded without arguing it there. It is the one addition here that
touches an author-written close, so it is the one to strike if it dilutes the
line.

**What goes is stated too, because a budget that only counts what survives is not
a budget.** The price, section 3.4's patienthood conclusion, and the claim at 3.9
that the learned half is the material the floor is made of.

**One thing was qualified rather than listed on either side.** Section 3.3 says
the obligations handed to chapters 4 and 5 come off with the inference. Section
3.7 hands them the witness problem, and that does not come off: a mechanism's
account of its own internals is no more auditable than a bearer's. So the passage
says the obligation *changes rather than lifting*, which is narrower than 3.3's
sentence and is the accurate version.
