### The best catch, and the one that drove the rebuild

**The WIOA reversal.** Old §6.3.3 defended the guarantee by disclaiming it as a
retraining program, citing old §8.3.2 as discouraging. That section's actual
closing finding is: *"What performed better, in the same data, was not classroom
retraining but employer-led apprenticeship."* The section cited the evidence and
dropped the half pointing at its own mechanism — since the guarantee's own
definition, three paragraphs earlier, is "paid on-the-job training built into the
position." That is employer-led apprenticeship, publicly employed.

It also made §1.2's promise false as written: the book promised "its strongest
objection" and delivered one its own framework defeats.

## 3. The jobs-guarantee rebuild — old §6.3.3, now §8.3.3

Retitled from "AI's Impact on Labor Markets and Income Distribution" to
**"A Jobs Guarantee, and the Objection It Has to Answer."** 950 → 3,157 words.
Every item the review specified, in the sequence it specified:

1. **Uncoupled from the displacement forecast.** The lump-of-labor assumption
   under both the maximal-displacement scenario and the standard UBI case is
   named and rejected. The disciplined version concedes task-level automation and
   claims only that the binding constraint on care and remediation employment is
   what society will pay — which holds at any capability level.
2. **Costing resized and resequenced**, in the review's order: residual cost →
   why the competitive version costs more → why the increase is the design
   working. The Levy figure is dated (April 2018) and indexed. A bound is given:
   4% of GDP would sit between Medicare and Social Security. Administrative
   capacity and the private-sector wage response are named as the two things that
   scale with participation and are not in the model.
3. **The fiscal framing asserted, not hedged.** The old sentence — "I am not
   competent to adjudicate a macroeconomic dispute" — was, by chapter 0's
   disclosure standard, a false report of the author's epistemic state. The
   position is now stated, the narrow premise the argument actually uses is
   separated out, and the interest-income channel is named as a real objection
   without a settled answer. Dissenters are not characterized by motive; the
   section says why not.
4. **Skill mismatch argued, not conceded**, via §2.1.4's own diagnosis: what gets
   automated is the legible part, and legibility is a poor proxy for an
   occupation. "Public job" removed.
5. **The training disclaimer reversed** on the WIOA evidence, which converts the
   guarantee from a bare floor to a competitive one and dissolves skill mismatch
   a second way.
6. **The status concession corrected for baseline** (unemployment, not the prior
   job), with C0725 and C0726, linked to §5.1.4's account of what authoritarian
   movements recruit from. The residual is stated narrowly: relative standing
   within a profession at the top of the wage distribution.
7. **The objection the section actually needed** — a guarantee under a hostile
   administration — supplied, with a three-part design specification (appealable
   placements on a surviving record; refusal rights priced by the refuser, per
   §8.6.4; auditors separate from placers), the redundancy rationale for
   federally-funded/locally-administered, an explicit statement that it cuts both
   ways, and a checkable test separating it from devolution-to-shrink.

**§8.3.4** gains the rebuilt UBI comparison under a run-in head: the freedom
argument engaged on its merits, then answered — both proposals are conditional,
differently; a basic income is defundable by failure to index, which nobody has
to defend, while a placement decision leaves an artifact. §8.6.4's slope
instrument turned on the book's own proposal.

**§1.2** now names the strongest objection correctly.
