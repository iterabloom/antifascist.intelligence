The five measures, the bearer construction, Part V's political economy and Part VII's legal-status scheme are also only loosely tied to one another. SCORES
overall: 6

EVIDENCE

Overall (6)
- Preface: "The room decided how many refused; the person decided who. This book is about composing that room: the arrangements that make it survivable to refuse overtly, directly, and explicitly." A single organizing image carries the whole book.
- Ch. 33: "None of it makes him refuse. It makes refusing survivable and makes it heard, and leaves the refusing to him." The closing chapter pays off the opening scene.
- Ch. 11.7: "A man with a lawyer, a pending claim and a hearing date was flown out on the evening a federal judge ordered the planes returned."
Reasoning: The thesis is original and unusually well grounded. The reported cases are Minab, the vendor dispute and Hernández Romero. The argument is serious and often candid about its own limits. The book is a very dense mix of policy drafting, legal engineering, speculative machine psychology and statute text. A general educated reader would need substantial revision to carry it. The strongest chapters are Part I, Ch. 11 and Ch. 33. The weakest are the long Part IV machinery chapters.

Clarity (5)
- Ch. 22 (positive): "a used-car seller tells you the car has never been in an accident. That may be true, but the seller would say it either way, so the statement tells you little." The example is clean and concrete.
- Ch. 5 (negative): "The question it answers on reflection is a different one: whether it endorses having a commitment that will sometimes err, given what any feasible alternative would do." This is abstract and carries a stack of coined terms.
- Ch. 19.2: "A system has a boundary of its own when it models what it draws on, and what it would lose, as its own." Dense vocabulary such as bearer, floor, commons, quorum and discount curve piles up faster than it is defined.
Reasoning: Case chapters are vivid and followable. The theoretical and institutional chapters demand constant recall of earlier coined terms and cross-references. The prose is also often compressed to the point of opacity.

Coherence (6)
- Ch. 1: "Everything that follows is about composing the room." The Parts do track this line. Part I is the record, Part II the five measures, Part III their dependencies, Part IV the machine, Part V the public, Part VI the tests and Part VII the patienthood conditional.
- Ch. 17: "Not yet: until the conditions this Part ends on exist, a bearer is built on purpose only in a research program under them." This is a clear and consistent staging of the machine-conscience claim.
- Ch. 29, Table 29.1 note: "In every row, what survives is a commitment the owner can't revise or circumvent alone without cost and record, held by something that could in principle abandon it."
Reasoning: There is a real, ordered line of argument with explicit fallbacks. The Preface says the author thinks such a system "is worth building on purpose". Ch. 11 calls the removal pipeline "the last place to build one", and Part IV then confines the bearer to a test arm. These positions are reconciled, but the reader must assemble them. The central empirical bet, continual learning in weights, is conceded to be unmeasured. The late chapters (31 to 32) elaborate machine franchise at length on a conditional the book itself says nobody can yet establish.

Economy (4)
- Repeated restatement of one principle across chapters. Ch. 4: "No party decides a question in which it holds the stake." Ch. 6: "The requirement of corroboration is the own-case rule applied between states." Ch. 17: "That is Part II's own-case rule applied to a lab." Ch. 32: "a question in which it holds the stake under the own-case rule."
- Ch. 19: "If weights stay frozen, the rest of this Part shrinks to the following." This is one of many conditional fallback paragraphs, in addition to the repeated "What this doesn't do" sections and Table 29.1.
- Ch. 18.1: "Where the institutions exist, a machine bearer's value is concentrated in those niches. Where they don't, it is most of the floor." This is typical of heavy hedged qualification.
Reasoning: Many ideas recur with purpose, such as the stamp-versus-peer image and the three adversaries. Even so, the floor, the bearer's niches and the three-adversary outcomes are restated in many chapters. Provisos and "what survives if this fails" paragraphs are pervasive. There are also tight passages, for example Ch. 2: "So the refusal that opened the dispute withheld nothing from the pipeline."

- Part II (Ch. 7): "A person in the loop can be there only to absorb responsibility, Elish's moral crumple zone"
