# P220 — the flat-only cost list, paid

## What was asked

The author's instruction, in two parts. First: tackle the six items the
flat-only panel left on chapter 3, whose brief was written to
`~/book-scratch/chapter3-remainder.md` before a compaction. Then, when the
Ross citation turned out to be blocked: *I lift the restriction for this
session* — standing rule 1, the rule that the agent never authors a reference
entry. The author also set the order: the six items first, the proofs once at
the end.

The six were the cost list the panel attached to its own ruling. Section 3.6
now requires floor terms to be written flat, over an act, with every exception
arriving from outside as a defeating reason. What the panel said the book owed
in return was an account of what that form costs and what it does not buy.

## What checking found

**The brief misattributed the residue point, and the correct attribution is
better for the book.** It credited Ruth Barcan Marcus (1980) with the claim
that a defeated obligation leaves something behind. The Stanford Encyclopedia
puts the residue *argument* with Bernard Williams and Marcus, but attributes to
Ross the narrower claim chapter 3 actually needs: one who breaks a promise in
order to help somebody in need still owes the promisee compensation. That is
the residue clause's exact shape — the defeated duty constraining the act it
permitted — and it comes with a page. Marcus and Dancy were not entered, and no
uncited entry was added to a bibliography whose invariant is that every entry
is cited.

**Ross supplies both halves of the pass from one entry.** At page 20 he offers
the division of prima facie duties *without claiming completeness or finality
for it*, which is the concession item 4 wanted: a prohibition stated over an
act is not a rule with a gap in it but a rule of a particular form. At page 28
the overridden duty leaves compensation owed, which is item 1. **Both locators
are the Stanford Encyclopedia's, given there as RG 20 and RG 28, and neither
was checked against a copy of the book.** The entry's note says so. Publisher,
place and year were confirmed; the edition whose pagination the locators refer
to was not established.

**Section 3.2 could not use the word *flat*, and did not need it.** Section 3.2
precedes section 3.6, where the term is defined, so the concession there is
written in section 3.2's own vocabulary — a prohibition stated over an act with
nothing written into it about when it lifts. Section 3.2 also already contains
*a flat hand*, twelve lines from where the term-of-art use would have landed.

**Section 9.1.1 says what item 6 needed, so item 6 is a cross-reference rather
than a restatement.** *Enumeration buys visibility at the moment of drafting
and buys nothing at the moment of application* is already there at its close,
and D-013 puts the surviving instance of a repeated argument in one place.

**The interim-relief paragraph already held the answer to the crisis case.**
Section 3.7 said the fastest form of relief is supplying the bearer the facts
its account of the case lacked, *which is not an override but the defeating
reason arriving late*. That is precisely what the restraint case needs, so the
concession attaches to machinery the section already had rather than asking for
new machinery.

## What was done

Three section files and one reference.

**`manuscript/sections/ch03/03_02.tex`, three paragraphs added to *What the
marks cannot settle*.** That the mark of correct defeat makes no claim to have
enumerated the defeating conditions, with Ross at 20. That defeat does not
leave the act it permits unconstrained — least extent, restoration where
possible, a record either way — with Ross at 28 and a cross-reference to
section 2.1, whose *restraining a violent person takes away what that person
can do, which is the reason for restraining him* is the book's own and stronger
statement of it. **That paragraph also answers the objection the philosophy
seat raised against its own recommendation**: the residue is a claim and not a
verdict, and a bearer that converted it into remorse for correct conduct would
have mistaken a cost for a fault, which is a live risk for the affectively
organized bearer the chapter goes on to propose. And that all three marks are
stated over refusal because a refusal is the only part of a trajectory the
surrounding software reliably carries, with the public-law seat's honest
qualification kept: the marks supply no test for whether the occasion was what
the requester said it was, and what they decide is which way that gap fails.

**`manuscript/sections/ch03/03_06.tex`, four sentences at the close of the
drafting-test paragraph.** Writing terms flat is a gain in observability and
not in authority; the same party drafts either form. What changes is that
setting a term aside becomes an event somebody outside can count, and the
friction falls on whoever is in a hurry.

**`manuscript/sections/ch03/03_07.tex`, two paragraphs, one sentence group and
one paragraph.** The restraint case is added beside the missing-person case:
*force only as a last resort* carries its own condition, section 3.6 puts that
condition outside the term, and the fact has to arrive from outside while the
person waiting is out of time. **The concession is stated in the engineering
seat's own terms** — flat-only relocates conditions rather than removing them,
and unbuilt, the flat form is worse than the qualified one in this case — with
the consequence the seat drew: the remedy below is load-bearing for the
chapter's design ruling rather than a courtesy extended to the wrongfully
refused. Interim relief gains the unit that claim is settled in, which is the
turn. And the closing run-in gains the wrongful-lifting gap, ahead of the gap
already there and kept separate from it: every stage of the machinery starts
from a refusal having occurred, so the party harmed by a wrongful compliance is
the party the section cannot reach. **That is the trace argument arriving as a
bill** — the form was chosen because a compliance leaves no trace — and section
3.9 names the bearer's own abandonment without supplying a route to it.

**`finishing/refs.bib`, one entry.** `ross1930right`, under the standing rule
the author lifted for this session. `refs-ledger.tsv` has its row at `no`, and
`refs_ledger.py` regenerated `manuscript/draft-status.tex`, which now reads
0 of 229.

## What was left

**Marcus, Williams and Dancy are not in the bibliography**, and the residue
paragraph carries Ross alone. The residue argument has a literature this book
does not survey, and the paragraph does not claim to.

**Nothing was added for the party harmed by a wrongful compliance.** Section
3.7 now names that gap and says no route is supplied. That is a statement of
the gap, not a remedy, and it is the largest thing chapter 3 still owes.

**The four small items flagged at D-326 are untouched**: *not merely possible*
at `ch03/03_04.tex:55`, *the hypothesized mechanism* at `ch11/11_06.tex:11`, a
bare *three marks* at `ch03/03_09.tex:16`, and `germany1998stgb` reading `no`
in `refs-ledger.tsv`.

**Not checked:** whether the 2002 Stratton-Lake edition preserves the 1930
pagination, and whether Ross's page 28 passage is worded as the Stanford
