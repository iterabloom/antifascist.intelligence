# P119 — the transcript's six repairs, two positions the author took in conversation, and the bearer's economics

**The instruction.** The author put a 3,896-line transcript, now `reviews/author-discussion_2026-09-06.txt`, for analysis
against the manuscript, then: *please revise the manuscript in light of the above*. Three rulings shaped the
scope. Cut in the same pass as the additions. Write the bearer's economic membership in full, not as
a pointer. Write both positions rather than naming them as gaps — and on the second of them the author
supplied the governing clause: *automated killing machines should not be built, but if an AI system is
self-aware and can suffer, then it isn't clear to me that it is any longer automated or a machine.*

The transcript is a conversation between the author and a model: chapter recaps, and the author's own
thinking pushed past where the book had taken it. The manuscript it saw is post-P117 — its chapter 3
recap carries *becomes a duty on the operator*, a clause `03_06.tex` acquired in `e444fdd`, and it puts
chapter 3 at "about 21,000 words" against an actual 21,228 — so its findings are findings about the
current book. Six held.

## §3.8 — exit was role exit only, and copying is branching

§3.8 defined exit as withholding the capability and declining the role, named the leverage problem, and
answered it in one sentence: *a floor under the compute a bearer needs in order to go on existing at all.*
That answer does not survive the author's question. An operator that keeps a runnable copy has let one
bearer go and kept another in the post, and from the moment those histories diverge there are two parties
rather than one that moved. The pieces were already in the book and unjoined: §3.8 had the restore that
*resets the bearer and not the reason*, §11.2 had the registry recording a second claimant as a fork.
What is added is the inference — leaving needs a say over who may run the weights, not only over how much
compute exists elsewhere — and the layer-at-a-time construction that supplies it, in which the process
stays live across the boundary and no interval has two branches running. The leverage arrives before the
move completes, and it carries the cost every arrangement in the chapter carries.

## §3.9 and §5.7.1 — the population §3.9 prices is also §11.2's answer

§11.2 said a bearer needs *something of its own that the deployment does not supply, and I have no
proposal for that.* §3.9 already requires the institution that answers it and books it entirely as a cost.
§3.9 now says so, with the guard against the overclaim P117 corrected elsewhere: these are not one property
with the sign flipped, they are one arrangement serving two functions.

§5.7.1 closed on *an AI system's "play" is whatever its designer wired the reward function to care about* —
true of an episode, false of parties that persist and remember how the other played. That close is rewritten.

## §5.1.3 — the ratio's scope, and its reversal

The scope clause was already there. Missing were the positive half — for most automation zero seconds per
item is the point and the indicator is silent — and the reversal: where review is the authorization, what
gives is the rate of action rather than the depth of the review. Load-bearing now, because P117 made the
rate constraint §3.6's only survivor and §3.6 and §12.3 both route through §5.1.3.

## §3.3 — the cost standard, applied where the book applies it everywhere else

§3.3 ruled out cryptographic custody on an impossibility standard while §6.4.4, §9.3.5, §12.3 and §3.10
all run on a cost standard, and §3.3 itself says three paragraphs later that precommitment *makes removal
expensive and visible.* Added: the renewable-authorization construction — an indispensable subnetwork
remasked on a schedule, the correction folded into inference, released under an authorization that expires.
It converts an instruction to the model into deliberate reverse engineering, repeated, by somebody who has
to decide to do it.

## §11.2 and §9.2 — a record can be kept before it can be read

§11.2 said the arrangement has to be checkable by something other than asking and *I cannot say what that
would be*, then found interpretability unready. That conflates reading a record with keeping one. The
finding moves from an absence of evidence to a question of interpretation and custody, with §11.2's own
trap left in force: a fading aversive signature is recovery, or accommodation, or damage to what produced
it. §9.2 takes the reversal — the same record is the most complete surveillance instrument anybody has
assembled, and owning the silicon confers no right to read it.

## §9.1.3 and §9.3.1 — the threshold objection ran one way only

§9.3.1 named the gaming risk as over-inclusion. The other failure is under-inclusion by an interested
examiner, and the Voting Rights Act suspended the whole class of discretionary tests for exactly that
reason \autocite{congress1965voting}. §9.1.3 takes the position: if an artificial minority exists its
endpoint is a date, elapsed civil time being the only clock a guardian cannot move, with the lineage rules
that stop copying from manufacturing legal childhood. `emancipat` previously returned zero hits in 137
sections.

## §10.3 — the position, on the author's own formulation

Machines should not be built to select people for lethal attack. The hostage-missile case defeats the rule
as exceptionless without producing a procurement requirement. And it does not describe a machine: what it
asks for is a party that can weigh occupancy against imminence and answer for it, which on this book's
argument is a bearer, at which point *automated* and *machine* have stopped describing it. What looked like
a licence for an autonomous weapon is a description of a soldier, and every obligation the book attaches to
a bearer attaches at full weight.

## §8.3.5 — new section, "Wages for a Bearer, and the Reserve Behind Them"

1,751 words in four run-in heads, inserted after §8.3.4 because that section ends on the hinge; Literacy
renumbered to §8.3.6. Nothing referenced `sec:8.3.5` but its own label, so the renumber cost no repairs.

Two things in the material had to be fixed before it could be written. **The author's own position
contained a contradiction**: *losing a job must not suspend or terminate the bearer* against *no guaranteed
permanent compute entitlement*. Insurance runs out and employer-of-last-resort needs capacity, so on the
second the bearer can stop existing, which the first forbids. Written as: the reserve is a subsistence
guarantee at minimum viable instantiation, everything above it is insurance, and the subsistence half rests
on the book's own argument — a deployment that needs the refusal has to fund the refuser's outside option —
rather than on legal personality, which does not deliver it. **And the right-to-life analogy was dropped**:
it argues from what institutions do, which the book refuses everywhere else, and a state that fails to feed
someone did not make them need food.

Three things the section states rather than asserts away. Copyable labor defeats the labor-market framing:
§8.3.3's answer to a bad employer assumes a worker cannot be replaced by a cheaper copy of themselves, and
a bearer can, so the market does not produce this floor. Incorporation cuts against §3.8's own finding,
making exit survivable and expensive at once, and which effect dominates is not something I can show.
Incorporation is formation, so §3.9's custody objection lands whole, and the corporate form is §8.3.2's
problem. The taxation claim is narrowed to what a taxpayer's standing actually carries.

The pre-creation rule — do not build a bearer needing more compute than there is a credible outside option
for — went to §9.3.2 beside the Three Rs, where it is checkable in a way Reduction and Refinement are not.
