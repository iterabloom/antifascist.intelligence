# P90 — the book's six orderings mapped, and one conflation repaired

**The instruction.** Out of P89's check of the surviving `rung`: *might as well look at it
properly*, then *run it* on the three things the map recommended. The map was the point; the pass
that followed is one sentence, and **two of the three recommendations were withdrawn on closer
reading rather than executed**.

**Three of the six are already tied together properly, and none of that was known when D-182 was
run.** C and E are one figure on one axis, joined deliberately — *Laid against the account above
they are three depths in the same water, ordered by how much of a self each one asks for* — with
§3.4 citing §2.3.1 three times. A and B are joined by §2.2's own bridge, *That last sentence needs
a vocabulary*: B is the vocabulary for the claim A's table makes. And D's third item is already
placed on E's scale, at §3.4's *affective concern … is a state of the shallowest kind by
construction*.

**Which terms actually carry**, measured across the manuscript: A's *recognizing emotion* and
*simulating emotion* appear **once each, in the table itself**; *instrumental valuation* twice.
B's three run 6, 7 and 5 across chapters 2, 3, 4, 5 and 13. D is almost entirely chapter 3 —
*operational refusal* reaches only §3.2 and §3.3. *affective concern* is 18 and belongs to two
sets at once.

## What was repaired

**§2.2's *moral agency* conflates exactly what chapter 3 prises apart.** It was defined as
*treating those considerations as reasons that matter, **and** being capable of acting from them*
— which is D3 **and** D2, joined. Chapter 3's load-bearing claim is that those come apart:
*Reasons-responsive refusal does not entail affective concern by definition*. Nothing said so, so
a reader learned a joined term in chapter 2 and carried it into a chapter built on splitting it.
One sentence added at the definition: *Those two halves come apart, and section~\ref{sec:3.2}
takes them apart: a mechanism can act from a reason with nothing about the outcome registering as
mattering to it.* +28 words, one new cross-reference.

## What was withdrawn, and why

**The "same water" seam was reported to the author as a defect D-182 had introduced, and it is
weaker than that.** The claim was that §3.4's run-in *The same water, shallower* had acquired a
nearer antecedent, because before D-182 chapter 3 contained no water outside §3.4 and now §3.2
carries seabed, anchorage, anchor and drag. **Checking the reading order withdraws most of it.**
§3.4 invokes §2.3.1's suffering account **8,500 characters before** the run-in and develops it
continuously in between, and the sentence that actually uses the figure names its owner in the
same breath — *Laid against the account above*. What moved is the nearest prior *token* `water`,
not the nearest prior *account*. **§3.2 also marks itself as a different question in its own
words** — *what the seabed beneath it is made of*, a matter of composition, tested by load rather
than measured for depth. No edit. Recorded because the stronger claim was put to the author and
should not stand in the record uncorrected.

**§2.2's table keeps four rows.** The measurement holds — two of its four terms are never spoken
again — but the terms' function is local discrimination, and both available cuts damage
something. Cutting rows 1 and 2 breaks the two sentences that follow, which depend on there being
four (*Only the fourth is relevant to genuine caring*; *The first three can support morally
competent behavior*). Trimming the duplication in the prose above the table instead would gut a
sentence doing its own work, on what the field mostly builds and why that is worth building. **A
term introduced once so that it can be set aside is not a dead term**, and this is the shape
D-117's reader tax does not reach.

**§2.2 gets no figure, which was the question P89 left open.** B works as a plain vocabulary and
is used across five chapters; A is a one-time discrimination. A fourth figure, in a book that now
carries depth, an anchorage and a rejected ladder, would cost more than it returns.
