# P129 — the ledger of what survives had no moral entry, and the officer's sentence had been read as a gauge

## Instruction

*"What do you think of the following feedback?"* on a note titled **Add a consequentialist
second wall**, then *"Please first check your assumptions and then make the needed
revisions."* The note asked for a new section: build systems that present as suffering,
deploy them at scale, train people to override the presentation as routine, and the
practice of overriding is a harm to those people whatever is or is not behind the
presentation. Its case for a section was that the argument *survives a reader who holds
that machine feeling is a category error — a reader who currently keeps the specification
and loses the whole moral argument.* Two guardrails were asked for.

## The premise failed on text three sections past where the note was looking

§3.10's closing movement already supposes the induction fails and enumerates what that
reader keeps: *The requirement survives… Custody survives… The price on the halt
survives… Both enumerations of the worked floor survive… And exit survives.* It then
states what goes and closes *a reader who rejects the route is left with most of a chapter
rather than none of it.* **§3.10:37 answers the note's grievance in its own words** —
*A reader who declines a generalization over one species should not have to assemble the
remainder alone.* §3.2's last paragraph does the same from the other end: *an engineer can
understand this requirement even if they believe that machine feeling is a category error.*

**What is true is narrower and it is one entry wide.** Six things survive on that ledger
and every one is a specification. Chapter 3 draws every obligation it has from patienthood,
which is exactly what that reader has declined, so the ledger hands them an engineering
programme and nothing else.

## Two drafts of the fix were wrong, and the sections they were about are why

1. **That routine override is a channel whose contents reach nothing.** §3.5:18 closed that
   door before this pass started: *A bearer that yields to every halt, leaves a record of
   it, declines to resume until it has been told what filled the gap, and refuses the
   covered act when it does resume has conceded the switch entirely and given up nothing
   the floor needs.* The override the book specifies moves a record and an account.
2. **That the operator prices that record**, applying §11.3:13's who-prices-the-dissent
   test. **§3.6:20 is already that sentence**: *A refusal that requires a person to read it
   before anything happens is a refusal priced at whatever that person's attention costs,
   and the deployment sets that price by setting the tempo.*

Both were caught by reading the section the draft was about rather than by reasoning about
it. Neither would have been caught by the suite or by the build.

## What was missing was one step, and its evidence was already in the book

Three passages stand in a line and stop short of each other.

- **§3.6:20** names the review step among the positions where a refusal can be stopped.
- **§5.1.3:8** measures it — *past a certain ratio the reviewer's only available behavior is
  approval* — on a ratio that *requires no access to the model and no judgment about
  intent.*
- **§6.4.1's box** carries the testimony, verified and cited at P37 to
  `abraham2024lavender`: an officer reviewing machine-generated target lists at twenty
  seconds each, *"I had zero added value as a human, apart from being a stamp of
  approval."*

**The book reads that sentence as a measurement of the safeguard.** §6.4.1:34 — *Twenty
seconds is what "a human makes the final call" measures out to under operational tempo. It
is the whole of the safeguard.* §5.1.3:8 cites it for *whether the human check is a check
or a formality*. A man said what the position had made of him and the book took the reading
off him like a gauge. **Nobody in 133 sections says that putting him there was something
done to him.**

That step needs no verdict on machine phenomenality. The officer's position did not depend
on what Lavender was, and an operator's does not depend on what a bearer is: ranking
machinery elaborate enough to pass for concern seats the same person at the same tempo with
the same thing to do.

**It clears §7.3:28**, which is the book's own standing objection to arguments of this kind
— *A defect of character has to be fixed one organization at a time by persuading people to
be better. A defect of structure can be specified against.* Nothing here is a claim about
anyone's character. §5.1.3 supplies the structural form and §2.1.2 makes the general
commitment: *an institution can display the whole structure with nobody in it who thinks of
themselves as anything of the kind.*

## The note's two guardrails

**The first is wrong by the book's own definition.** It calls *train the system not to
report distress* recuperation, *a channel built so that what it collects reaches nothing.*
§2.1.2 item 1 opens *The institution does not suppress disagreement; it metabolizes it*,
and §7.2:13 is *Recuperation was never the claim that a mechanism does nothing.* Training a
system not to report distress builds no channel. Adopting the wording would have been the
first use of the term against its own definition in a book that runs the definition as a
detector in chapters 7, 9 and 11. **The cheap fix in the reframed argument is different and
sharper**, and it is what went in: take the person out of the review step, and the
safeguard goes with the harm.

**The second needed no argument.** §2.3.2:16 says the Three Rs and the pet-trust form *were
built for exactly a subject that cannot consent and can still be wronged*; §9.3.3:13 defines
the instrument as *an interest protected by an obligation running to a third party*. One
clause and a cross-reference.

## The literature the note named will not hold the wall, and both of its sources say so

Verified before drafting, per D-030.

- **Darling states the desensitization premise conditionally throughout** and argues on
  precautionary and analogical grounds: *if more research shows*; *further research may*;
  *even without scientific evidence*; *further research will be important in helping to
  confirm or debunk our hunches.* Her one demonstration was an uncontrolled conference
  workshop she declines to draw conclusions from, and her actual study measures hesitation
  to strike a robot as a function of the subject's **pre-existing** empathy — the causal
  direction opposite to the erosion hypothesis.
- **Sparrow's 2021 asymmetry paper rejects the causal version by name.** He calls it the
  *cruel habits* argument, attributes it to Kant, and says its reliance on a contested
  empirical claim gives it *limited utility*. His argument is that mistreatment **reveals**
  a vice, not that it **causes** one, and he is explicit that this is the point.
- **No study measures empathy erosion from robot mistreatment.** What exists is small-N
  single-session lab work whose dependent variable is reluctance to harm the robot, and
  field observation of children's motives with no outcome measure.
- **The video-game literature it would borrow from is contested and shrinking**: publication-bias
  reanalysis, a pre-registered null at N = 1,004, a four-wave longitudinal null on the
  desensitization sub-claim specifically, and the APA's own 2020 revision stating that
  attributing violence to violent video gaming *is not scientifically sound*.

**Neither Darling nor Sparrow is cited and no empirical erosion claim is made.** D-137's
rule is to cut rather than hedge, and D-030's is to drop what does not check out and write
the argument without it.

## Kant enters for one thing and is marked as not carrying the other

`kant1996metaphysics`, the Gregor Cambridge edition, cited at 6:442–43 for the distinction
between a duty **with regard to** a being and a duty **owed to** it. The manuscript says in
terms that nothing here uses his mechanism — that cruelty dulls fellow-feeling and uproots
it by degrees — because the evidence does not settle it, which is the same sentence doing
guardrail 2's work.

**Two verification limits, recorded at C0762 and deliberately not in the bibliography note**
(D-095: a note states facts about the source and never narrates the search for it). The
6:442 wording was checked against the Cambridge text. **§17's wording at 6:443 was checked
against scholarly reproductions and not against the printed page, so the manuscript
paraphrases it and does not quote it.** The widely circulated *he who is cruel to animals
becomes hard also in his dealings with men* is **Infield 1963, p. 240**, a different
translation, and is deliberately unused; it is mis-cited to the Cambridge edition in at
least one of the works the note pointed at.

A dissent is on the record and not adopted: Patrick Kain argues Kant's account involves
direct concern for animals, against the standard indirect reading the Stanford Encyclopedia
and Camenzind both give.
