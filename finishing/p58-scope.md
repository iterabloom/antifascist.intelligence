## What the section becomes, and it is a weaker claim

The section stops having any case of affect removed. What remains is Nichols on
rules bound to an affective response, the prosocial-emotion findings, the two
senses of motivation with the language-model existence proof, the Frankfurt and
Watson specification of what a non-affective candidate would look like, and the
close. That is an argument about where the burden sits rather than a
demonstration, **and the replacement prose says so rather than letting the
opener's promise stand unmet.**

**L5, replacing "Five lines of evidence bear on the stronger claim, and one
serious argument runs the other way":**

> Two lines of evidence bear on the stronger claim. Neither shows what happens
> when affect is taken away, which is what would settle it, so what follows
> argues about where the burden sits rather than claiming the question closed.

**L3 is unchanged.** Its closing sentence — that the gap between "affect changes
particular judgments" and "affect is what makes anything matter" is where an
argument has to be made rather than gestured at — now pairs with the new L5
instead of promising evidence the section no longer has.

**L26, run-in retitled:** *Two senses of motivation.* **L27 unchanged**, and
section 5.6.1's citation of "section 2.3.3's sense" still resolves.

**L31, opening rewritten** to drop the concession to Kennett and the sentence
that made the machine case continuous with the human one:

> The first sense is not in dispute and there is an existence proof close to
> hand. Language models running as agents pursue objectives across long horizons,
> revise plans when obstructed, and hold to an instruction against conflicting
> local pressure, and nothing in this section's framework says that anything
> matters to them while they do it. Action-producing motivation without
> experienced motivation is the ordinary case in the systems this book is about,
> and a section arguing otherwise would be arguing against the reader's own week.

**L34, the Kennett clause cut:**

> So the open question is not whether reason can produce goal-directed behavior.
> It demonstrably can. It is whether an outcome can \emph{matter to} the agent
> rather than merely govern what the agent does. Producing a verbal justification
> or computing a normative relation is not evidence that the consideration
> matters to the system. The evidence assembled above bears on the second
> question, and every item in it is about systems in which something does.

**L36–37 and L40–42 unchanged.** *The generalization survives in the only form it
can* is more accurate after the cuts than before.
