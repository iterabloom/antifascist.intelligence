# P216 — four self-contradictions repaired

The author asked for four rows of an analysis table run against
`~/book-scratch/argument-changes`, a nine-file revision set produced by another
model: the four places where that set finds the book contradicting itself, and
nothing else from it. Those four rows are its findings 9a, 10a, 11a and 38c.

**Each is a case where the book already states the right thing in one place and
its opposite in another.** That is why these four and not the other thirty-six:
the repair is settled by the book's own argument rather than by a position
somebody has to take first.

**Nothing else from the set was applied.** The rights foundation (the
Declaration's Arts. 3–21 taken entire), the hours-of-operation adulthood clock,
the replacement of "Keep the floor narrow", the register and cross-reference
rulings and the remaining findings are untouched. **Finding 26a is not one of
the four rows and was left** — the §3.8 repair of *several instances of one
model are one party with more instances* was named in the same analysis as
worth taking, and it is a looseness rather than a contradiction.

## The four

**§2.4.1 — nociception is not itself bad (9a).** The section defines
nociception as "peripheral, present without any experience attached, and
dissociable from pain" and then, five paragraphs later, said "nociception and
pain are bad while they obtain." The badness now belongs to pain alone, and
nociception's significance is made to depend on what it indicates and how it
functions in the system. The amnesiac who is mistreated and cannot afterward
recall it — which is what the sentence was for — is kept.

**§9.1.1 — what §86a leaves unassessed (38c).** The section's two-setting
argument said "At the second setting nothing is assessed" of the German symbol
prohibition. §3.2 spends six paragraphs showing the courts assessing exactly
that statute: whether a casual observer would take a variant for the original,
whether displaying it endangers political peace as the original would, and, in
the 2007 crossed-out-swastika case, how thick the strike-through has to be. So
"nothing is assessed" was contradicted by the book's own worked case. What §86a
leaves unassessed is **the defendant**, and that is the sharper contrast,
because the paragraph two below already frames the decree failures as "an
official deciding whether a person or a movement threatened the order." The
sentence now reads "At the second setting nobody's dangerousness is assessed,"
and *The symbol is on the list or it is not* — which was the false half — is
replaced by what the courts do assess. **No cross-reference was added**; §3.2's
content is restated, per the standing ruling on references.

**§10.3 — what defeats the bearer's consent (10a).** The military-custody
section said "Consent is unavailable to a party made for its role." §2.4.2 had
already argued the opposite in terms: "Being made for a role is not the defect.
Every will was built by somebody… What spoils it is the power that outlasts the
making, and here it comes in three parts." The summary now relocates the defect
to those three conditions — the maker can rebuild the bearer, is also the party
asking, and controls every viable outside option — names the two remedies
§2.4.2 reaches, and keeps the burden-of-justification clause the original
sentence carried. The set's supplied prose dropped that clause; it was kept
because §2.4.2 affirms it.

**§3.3 — the signal's second step (11a).** The section derives four properties
a refusal signal must have and then said a signal answering to all four is
"organized around the system's own continuation." §3.4 says, in as many words,
"The floor does not require the bearer to care about its own continuation." The
clause is now "organized around something the system holds as its own."

**Only the clause changed.** The set supplied a four-sentence replacement whose
first sentence restates the paragraph above it and whose second imports §3.4's
claim into §3.3; the set's own index calls the clause swap "the single
operative edit," and its own disposition rule allows a redundant sentence to be
dropped from a replacement. Three passes and 217 author items (P213–P215) went
to removing sentences of exactly that first kind.

## What the swap costs, stated rather than settled

The clause that left was unargued. §3.3's four properties concern interruption,
one-encounter learning, generalization, and commensurability with what refusing
costs; none of them mentions continuation, whose or otherwise. **The clause that
replaced it is unargued in the same way.** It is weaker, which is what removes
the contradiction, and the weakening lands on the sentence immediately after
it: "That is a functional description of the states this section set out to find
a way around."

A signal organized around *something the system holds as its own* is satisfiable
by a maintained goal representation with nothing felt about it — which is the
construction §3.3 is arguing does not escape the candidate states. So the repair
may hand that construction a pass the old clause denied it, and §3.4's "a
designer cannot take the first and decline the second by intending to" now rests
on less than it did. **The instruction set asserts that the identification with
the candidate states survives and does not argue it. Neither does this pass.**
The contradiction is gone and the gap it was covering is now visible, which is
the honest state of the section and is the author's to close.

## What the set asked for at these sites and did not get

**38b, the companion to 38c, was not applied.** It rewrites §9.1.1's "Enumeration
removes discretion at the point of application," and it is entangled with the
author's separate decision to replace "Keep the floor narrow" — which this pass
was not asked to run, and which would orphan the next paragraph's "with how much
the floor covers, **which the paragraph above prices**." So §9.1.1 now repairs
"nothing is assessed" while leaving that sentence standing. **The two do not
contradict each other**: its subordinate clause bounds the discretion it claims
to remove to the emergency judgment ("so that nobody applying the floor has to
decide whether the present case is the emergency"), and interpreting a listed
term is a different thing. The main clause reads more loosely than before.

Also not applied, at the same four sites: 11a's fuller prose, 10a's dropped
burden clause, and 9a's version of the sentence without the amnesiac.
