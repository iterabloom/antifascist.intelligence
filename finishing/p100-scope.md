## Q-078 — *specifies* stays and the prepositions go

The sentence at §3:15 read *it specifies the antifascist question from the matter of what a
system values into ⟨what comprises the floor, and who can edit it?⟩*. The verb is the
author's and stays. *From … into* belonged to *converts*, the verb it replaced, and
*specifies* has no preposition that carries the same movement: *specifies X as Y* names a
value and not a change. So the movement is carried by a relative clause on the question
itself:

> And it specifies the antifascist question, which stops being what a system values and
> becomes *what comprises the floor, and who can edit it?*

*The question … stops being … and becomes* keeps *question* as the subject of both verbs,
which is what rules out the misreading the shorter forms invite — *what a system values
becomes what comprises the floor* reads, at speed, as the values turning into the floor's
contents. Nine words for nine; the word count did not move. Two alternatives were declined:
*no longer what a system values, but …*, which is the contrastive frame D-025 counts, and a
two-sentence version whose second sentence opened on a bare *It*, which D-055 counts.

## Q-080 — the bridge stays down

The ruling offered two ends and left the choice here: a bridge a reader could cross, or
none. None, on three checks.

**What the cut bridge carried is on the page in four other places.** Its second sentence
stated the chapter's stance toward the bearer. Chapter 1 states it — *The only version
that holds against the party operating the system runs through the capacities by which
things come to matter to a system*; §3's opener states it twice, as the *mattering*
question at §3:21 and as *held by something that could in principle abandon it itself* at
§3:23; §3.9's opener says *the chapter still inclines toward the second*, and that *still*
has its antecedent in the opener; §3.10 works it through. Nothing dangles.

**Its first sentence's work is §3.2's own first paragraph.** *“Refusal” covers two
different capacities* is §3.2's third sentence, which stands about a hundred words from
where the bridge stood. One bridge was drafted for this pass — *What could hold one turns
on what refusing is, and the word covers more than one thing* — and it put that claim on
the page twice within a paragraph's distance, so it was not used.

**A bridge that did not repeat it would have to announce §3.2 instead**, which `style.md`
§2 deletes on sight, and which P96's sixth class extends to a closer that makes the next
section's point. The author's test for the cut bridge — a reader falls through it — is a
test of whether the sentence can be read at speed, and *the next two sections ask what
could hold one* passes it by saying nothing.

§3.1 therefore ends on its hybrids sentence, which reads as an ending: it says what the
enumeration divides and what it does not. Closed on the author's second option.
