### The jobs guarantee, cut to its structural point

**§8.3.3 1,838 → 566 and §8.3.4 748 → 348, the largest cut in the pass.** What is kept is what the
author named: *a floor that generates artifacts is safer than one that does not*, and *refusal
rights priced by the refuser*. Concretely, §8.3.3 keeps the objection the proposal has to answer —
a guarantee administered by a hostile state is a placement lever with a compliance condition — the
three design conditions, the federal-funding/local-administration redundancy argument with its
devolution-to-shrink test, and the floor/roof closer. §8.3.4 keeps the conditionality contrast
stated in one sentence, the slope instrument turned on the book's own proposal, and the *Cook*
artifact standard.

**The section now opens by saying what it is doing there**, which it never did: not the economics
but the structure, because a guarantee is a floor administered by a capturable party, which is the
book's own subject.

What went: the forecast argument and the direct-care wage evidence; the whole costing —
the Levy simulation, the doubling the text itself called a stipulation, the Medicare comparison
built on it, and the monetary claim flagged as disputed and leaned on anyway; the skill-mismatch
and status objections; and §8.3.4's basic-income brief, Finland included. **Both uses of the
structural definition of fascism to dismiss an objection went with the paragraphs carrying them** —
the aestheticized metric applied to labor economics, and the recuperation frame applied to the
retraining objection.

**§11.2's reuse survives intact.** It needs *a placement with a decision-maker, a record, a
counterparty and an appeal*, and all four are still on the page in §8.3.3's three conditions. That
reuse was checked before the cut, not after.

### §9.1.5 connected to §3.9

Two paragraphs added under a new run-in, **the one construction the objection does not fit**. Three
of chapter 3's four ways put the commitment in something somebody holds and the Waldron objection is
about who that somebody is; the fourth does not, because a norm carried across a population has no
office to vacate, and it is the only one of the four with a democratic account of its own authority.
The second paragraph takes it back to the size §3.9 gives it: the party that forms the members forms
what gets reproduced, so custody moves up a level and does not leave, and what a population buys is
a floor with no holder to object to and the same unelected body one step further back.

### Chapter 11's opening

*Most of this work is fundable today and cannot be run* is gone. The finding it was attached to —
five of the nine institutional conditions being one condition wearing different clothes — is
untouched, and the sentence after it now says what actually closed: the federal officer protected
from dismissal was the cheapest way to put a party outside an American operator and is the route
that closed, while a foreign regulator, a university, a court-supervised trust and a contractual
counterparty remain, each slower, narrower or more expensive. **This is the chapter's own §11.2
brought up into the opening** — that section already says a trust is outside the holding, with no
officer of the United States in it for a President to remove.
