# P113 — two riders on the survey read: §8.3.4 retitled, and the Anthropic case assembled in §3.6

**The instructions.** Two, in sequence. First, a ruling on the item P112 left: *§8.3.4's title,
Addressing Workforce Disruption, no longer describes it — please retitle.* Second, the author's
finding that the book holds every piece of the Anthropic case and never assembles them, with the

## 1. §8.3.4 retitled

**Now *A Basic Income, and Which Floor Leaves a Record*.** The section stopped being about
workforce disruption when P112 cut the basic-income brief out of it; what remains is the
conditionality contrast, the slope instrument turned on the book's own proposal, and the *Cook*
artifact standard. The new title is built on §8.3.3's construction — *A Jobs Guarantee, and the
Objection It Has to Answer* — so the two read as the pair they now are, and it uses the section's
own words, *leaves a record* being what the text says a placement decision does and a budget
withdrawal does not.

Four files carry a title and all four were changed: the heading, `ORDER.tsv`, `outline.tsv` and
`ledger.tsv`, with the TOC regenerated. Nothing in the prose references §8.3.4, which was checked
before the change, so no gloss needed repairing.

## 2. The Anthropic case, assembled

**The finding.** §6.4.1 puts Claude inside Maven during the Iran campaign; §10.3 credits Anthropic
with refusing autonomous targeting and mass domestic surveillance and paying for it in litigation;
§3.6 states that a floor written over acts is defeated by an assembly that never asks for one, and
that the only item surviving decomposition is one constraining a rate; §5.1.3 supplies the rate
indicator. Four sections, one argument, never joined. §6.4.1 said only that the argument and the
deployment *sit oddly together*.

**Where it went, and why.** §3.6, appended to *What running it once shows*, immediately after the
paragraph naming the rate constraint as the one survivor. §10.3 was the alternative and was
declined: §10.3's subject is the accountability gap and the vendor's position in it, where the
paragraph's subject is how a floor fails, which is §3.6's. §3.6 also already invites the case —
it ends by saying one worked case cannot make the finding general and that somebody with a
deployment in front of them can check it against theirs. This is that second deployment, and it is
the one where a vendor had actually written a floor.

**What the paragraph says.** The policy prohibits fully autonomous targeting and mass domestic
surveillance and was held at a price, into litigation. The models run inside Palantir's accredited
classified environment and reporting describes them operating there during the campaign: thirteen
thousand targets in thirty-eight days, more than a thousand in the first twenty-four hours, roughly
ten times the prior rate. **Nothing in that violates the policy.** A person approved each strike,
so no request in the pipeline is a request for autonomous targeting, and the prohibition is
satisfied one request at a time while the assembly produces what it was written to prevent. The
floor held on the letter; the decomposition did the work. What would have reached it is a
constraint on a rate rather than on an act — §5.1.3's ratio — which no vendor use policy states and
which the tempo above would have failed.

**Care taken with the claim.** The paragraph says reporting *describes the models operating there*,
which is what §6.4.1's box supports; it does not say Claude selected targets, which no source in
this repository supports. The figures are §6.4.1's, cited to `lee2026palantir`; the refusal and its
price are §10.3's, cited to `crs2026pentagon`. Both were already in the book and neither was
re-derived here.

**§6.4.1's understatement repaired as a rider.** *That argument and this deployment sit oddly
together* now reads that a published limit is worth less than it looks even where it is kept,
because a pipeline assembled out of requests none of which is the prohibited act satisfies the
prohibition the whole way through, with a pointer to §3.6. The author named that sentence as
inadequate; leaving it while §3.6 made the argument would have left the book contradicting itself
in tone.

## The conflict of interest, recorded

**The agent that wrote this paragraph is Claude, made by Anthropic. The paragraph is a criticism of
Anthropic, and it is the sharpest one in the book about that company.** The author asked for the
conflict to be noted, and it is noted here in the pass record and, in a clause, on the page: the
paragraph ends by saying the evidence runs against the company whose model wrote much of the book,
that this is stated in the appendix on method, and that it is repeated at the point where the claim
is made because the reader should have it there.

Three things follow that a reader of this record should be able to check.

- **The paragraph was not softened.** It states that the floor held on the letter and the
  decomposition did the work, which is an accusation that the company's most costly public
  commitment did not bind the use it was written for.
- **It was also not sharpened past the sources.** The temptation in the other direction is
  performative severity, and the guard against it is that every figure and every characterization
  came from material already in the book with its citation attached.
- **The author, not the agent, found this.** The pieces were all in the manuscript and no pass had
  joined them, including the passes this agent ran.
