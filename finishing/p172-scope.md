# P172 — ethology in place of the deontological floor

Author instruction: take the annotated sentence — affect is part of the
machinery of valuation rather than a coloring applied to it — and stop
apologizing for it. Spinoza: *affectus* is increase or decrease in power of
acting, not a report about a valuation. Good and bad encounters, compositions
and decompositions, rather than Good and Evil. We do not know what a body can do,
which is chapter~11's conclusion from the other side. **This replaces the tribunal
with a criterion the owner cannot simply set, because it is a fact about
capacities and encounters.**

## The replacement

**§2.1.1 no longer recommends a deontological floor.** All six frameworks share a
shape the book stops using: each judges an act against a standard held above it,
*which needs a judge, a judge needs somebody to appoint him, and the floor is then
exactly as durable as whoever is holding the standard. Built that way a floor is
a tribunal, and a tribunal has a proprietor.*

**What replaces it is Spinoza's ethics as Deleuze reads it**, under a new run-in
*What an ethology asks instead*: encounters evaluated by what they do to the
power of acting of the bodies in them, relations composing or decomposing, good
and bad — relational and particular — rather than Good and Evil, which a judge
assigns from outside.

**The reason it is worth changing to is the author's**, and it is the paragraph
the pass turns on. A decomposition is a fact about capacities and about what
passed between two bodies. The operator *can decline to look, refuse to publish,
or dispute the finding. What they cannot do is turn a decomposition into a
composition by holding the pen. That is the whole of why the criterion is worth
changing to: it is not theirs to set.*

**The floor keeps its name and changes its ground**, and the two mechanisms fit:
§3.1's annealing makes durability the cost of reheating what cooled out of a
history of encounters, and *both put the floor in what happened rather than in
what was decreed.* Ethology says the criterion is not the owner's to set;
annealing says the durability is not either. That link exists because the passes
arrived in this order.

**Chapter~11 gets its footing.** Spinoza's remark that nobody has determined what
a body can do is *the working condition rather than a modest aside*, which is why
chapter~11 is a list of things the book could not settle *rather than an appendix
of gaps somebody could be funded to close* — the same objection P164 acted on.

## The annotated sentence

Stated flat and backed, hedge gone, with `[III Def.~3]` behind it: an affect is
*not a report about a valuation carried out somewhere else. It is the valuation,
registered as a change in what the body can now do.* **This resolves a third
annotation on the author's direction**; 52 of the original 55 remain.

## Beyond the instruction, flagged

Chapter~3's opener carries one refinement the instruction did not name: what
nobody gets to edit is *whether* an encounter decomposed somebody, and what gets
chosen is *which* decompositions a deployment is built to refuse.
