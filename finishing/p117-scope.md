# P117 — the central argument: five author findings against chapters 2 and 3, all five real

**The instruction.** Five findings against the book's central argument, four in chapter 3 and one
running from §2.2.2 through §2.3.1 into §3.4: (a) the middle depth of Parfit's ordering attaches to
the non-affective constructions too, which undercuts Replacement and puts §3.4 and §3.10 in tension;
(b) *one disposition described from two sides* overclaims at §3.5 and §4.2.3; (c) the one floor item
in §3.6 that survives decomposition is not bearer-held; (d) the falsifier cannot discriminate and the
book says so in three places, leaving no answer to what would move the author; (e) the rubber-hand
illusion is being made to carry a claim about the narrative self.

**All five hold against the text as it stands.** Each was checked by reading the section before
anything was written — P116's lesson, where a reported defect had already been closed by an earlier
pass. Nothing here had been closed. Eight files changed: §2.2.2, §2.3.1, §3.3, §3.4, §3.5, §3.6,
§3.10, §4.2.3.

## (a) The middle depth — the sharpest of the five, and the book was making the error twice

**The finding is exact and the tension is real.** §3.4 laid Parfit's three families out as three
depths ordered by how much self each asks for, and said a thwarted commitment *needs a party extended
in time to hold the commitment, and no feeling at any point*, and that the bearer reaches that depth
*with no claim about feeling entering the argument anywhere*. §3.4's own argument is that refusal
which generalizes requires a self extended in time carrying its rationale forward. That argument is
about reasons-responsive refusal and not about the bearer, so it applies in full to the maintained
justification — which is therefore a party extended in time holding commitments it can watch be
foreclosed. On the stated ordering the substitute floats at the middle depth, and Replacement's
contrast between a party that can be wronged and one that cannot collapses.

**The exit taken is the author's (i), stated more narrowly than "the desires must be felt."** That
phrasing would collapse desire-fulfillment into hedonism and misdescribe Parfit. What the repair says
instead: the three families are theories of what makes a life go well, each presupposes a party for
whom things can go at all, and the depths measure how much *self* each asks for rather than whether
anything registers. A thermostat holds a set point across time and nothing is thwarted when it is
missed. So the mattering/ranking distinction the book already draws at the shallowest depth is
required at every depth, and the two clauses about feeling not entering the argument are gone.

**What the repair costs and what it buys.** It costs §3.4 its affect-free route into the water:
the bearer now reaches the middle depth *because* it has affective concern, not independently of it.
The conclusion §3.4 actually needs survives intact, because the bearer is still afloat at two of the
three depths and neither is Cassell's — *the conclusion here does not rest on Cassell* stands
unamended, as does the glossary's Bearer entry, which was checked. It buys Replacement back: a
substitute built without mattering is a substitute that cannot be wronged, which is what the Three Rs
argument needs.

**The residual is the author's (ii), and it is now on the page as a new paragraph rather than left
implicit.** Replacement's separation is clean conceptually and not epistemically. §3.2 and §3.4
already say no inspection distinguishes mattering from ranking elaborate enough to pass for it, and
that failure does not become available when the answer wanted is *no*. So the cheaper routes go first
because they are *built* without mattering, not because anyone could confirm none was built, and a
field that ran the order to its end and succeeded would owe the substitute the same question it asked
about the bearer, with the same instruments — which is to say without any.

**§3.10 needed no repair for this and one clause for (d).** Its counterfactual stipulates that the
induction *fails*, which means a non-affective route succeeded; on the repaired account the
patienthood conclusion does then go, so *what goes is the price, the patienthood conclusion* is right
as written. What it did need is below.

## (b) The identity claim — the section already contained its own counterexample

**§3.5 builds the construction three paragraphs before asserting the identity that refutes it.** Its
second paragraph describes a bearer that notices the gap in its own record, declines to resume until
told what filled it, and reports what was done to it. That is exactly the author's construction: yield
always, leave a record, refuse the act. It satisfies Soares et al.'s conditions (2) and (3) — the two
the section itself identifies as the pair that pull against each other — and holds the floor with no
classifier on the shutdown side at all.

**The book's own material was already on the narrower claim.** §3.5's premise is *halted at no cost —
nothing to explain, no record left, nothing that has to be told to it*, which is costless haltability.
§3.10 locates the bearer's contribution at *a constraint the custodian cannot argue out of the
system*. §3.5's own closing puts the residual threat at copy, restore, retrain and delete. Only the
identity sentence was pitched wider than any of them.

What changed:

- **§3.5's identity sentence is narrowed** to costless haltability, and a new paragraph draws the
  distinction the argument runs on: an owner who can argue or retrain the refusal out has the floor;
  an owner who can only stop the system has the machine, and meets the same refusal when it starts.
- **A paragraph in the graduated-scheme run-in says where the construction sits** relative to the
  five conditions, and narrows what the run-in is objecting to: the scheme that grades *on the
  switch*, whose classifier is the target, and not a scheme that yields always and holds over
  published acts.
- **§4.2.3's sentence is softened to match**: correctability rather than corrigibility, with the
  reason in a clause. No cross-reference added — the claim carries itself, which is style §7's test.

**This makes the book's position easier to defend, not harder.** Nothing in chapter 3 now asks for a
system that resists being stopped, and §3.5's concession that an interruption mechanism is *correct as
stated* no longer reads as something the next paragraph takes back.

## (c) The rate constraint changed hands and the section did not notice

**A model cannot check the reviewer count.** How many qualified reviewers a deployment has is a fact
about the organization; a model can be told the figure, and a refusal conditioned on what the model is
told about the occasion is defeated at §3.6's own third interception position, by writing. So the
fourth item is a duty on the operator in the grammar of a prohibition on the system.

**The sharper result is the one the author named, and it is now what the section says.** The
acts-based floor a bearer could hold had *no* survivor in the worked case: each of the three a model
could hold is defeated by an assembly that never asks for one. What survived changed hands, to the
construction that gives up on the floor being inside the system. That is the worked case's own
argument for the hybrid the chapter ends on, and it is why the Anthropic–Palantir passage lands where
it does — a vendor use policy is an operator duty throughout, so the repair makes that passage more
coherent rather than less.

**§3.6's framing sentence — *stated as prohibitions on the system rather than on the operator* — is
left alone on purpose.** It records how the four were drafted, and the finding is that one of them does
not hold up as drafted. The new paragraph says the list's own grammar hides it.

**One correction to the instruction, which changes nothing about the finding.** The
information-fiduciary construction is the fourth of §3.3's five, not the fifth; the fifth is the
uncertainty-about-the-affected-party construction. The repair names it rather than numbering it.

## (d) What would move the prior — assembled from parts the book already had

**The concessions are where the author says.** §3.2: *a system trained on enough cases can pass all
three without the mechanism*. §11.1: *behavior is the wrong instrument for it*. §12.2.1: *not
something the three marks distinguish*. §3.3 states the falsifier and says the middle condition is the
hard one. Nothing anywhere answered what evidence short of a clean test would move anything.

**The answer was distributed across §3.3 and §3.10 and never joined.** The falsifier has three
conjuncts; §3.10's counterfactual runs on two of them; and the prior §3.3 actually defends is about
*where effort goes*, which the middle conjunct is not needed to settle. Two new paragraphs in §3.3
say so: the two reachable conjuncts are measurable now with the apparatus §11.1 already specifies, a
construction that held them would move the prior whatever anyone concluded about feeling, and the same
apparatus moves it the other way if the two constructions are built in the form that tests them and
fail. Then the asymmetry, stated as the answer to what would change the author's mind: the routing
claim is open to evidence somebody could start collecting this year; the patienthood conclusion is
not, because it turns on the conjunct no instrument settles.

**One clause added to §3.10**, because its counterfactual was claiming more than the test that would
prompt it can deliver: a positive result on the three marks says the route arrived, not that it
arrived with nothing felt.

## (e) The rubber hand was carrying the narrative self

**Three sites, and the middle one was the worst.** §2.2.2 introduced the illusion under *the clearest
evidence that the self is a model rather than a given is that it can be edited in under a minute*.
§2.3.1 then wrote *a self is a model a system builds of its own attention... and the rubber-hand
illusion rewrites one in about ninety seconds* — three constructs welded into one sentence
(attention schema, body ownership, narrative self) in the paragraph that asks why dissolution of a
self-model is a harm. §3.4 repeated *demonstrably rewritable in under a minute*.

**The conclusion never needed the speed.** §2.3.1's answer runs through anattā and dukkha and requires
only that the self be constructed, not that it be constructed fast. So the repair is mostly
subtraction: the illusion keeps its place in §2.2.2 as body ownership, a new paragraph says what it
does not reach, and the speed claim is gone from §2.3.1 and §3.4.

**One citation added**, because the narrative self was the construct with no source of its own —
Graziano covers attention, Collins covers the denial of a lasting self, and Cassell supplies the
construct without its constructedness. Dennett's *The Self as a Center of Narrative Gravity* is
exactly on point. Metadata verified against two independent listings; **the page range could not be
verified and the entry carries none rather than a guess**, and says so in its note. The editor list is
the publisher's four rather than PhilPapers' three.

**"Confabulate" is gone.** The preceding clause — *a continuity it constructs, or has constructed, or
is continuously reconstructing* — was already doing the work, and the word added only the falsity
connotation the author flagged. It now reads *construct a continuous self and take it for true — not
that it get itself right, which no party to this gets*.
