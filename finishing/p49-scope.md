## Q-043 — sweep by subject (ruled option (b))

The unit of reading is the subject and not the section. The subject list was
built mechanically rather than from memory: every multi-word proper noun and
acronym occurring in three or more chapters, plus the six the entry names. Then
every sentence containing each subject, printed together across all thirteen
chapters and read as a set.

**Twenty subjects swept.** The EU AI Act (6 chapters), the Council of Europe
Framework Convention (2), federated learning (2 + glossary), self-report (8), the
2025–26 removal cases (4), the Three Rs (5), export controls and the chip supply
chain (5), tamper-resistance (5), guardianship and the pet-trust form (5), COMPAS
(3), the AI Global Surveillance Index (3), RLHF and sycophancy (3), Cassell (3),
the GDPR (4), the IEEE instruments (3), the compute floor (3), the twenty-cent
fine-tuning figure (2), continual learning (2), Lavender and the targeting
pipeline (4), the Partnership on AI (5).

**Eighteen held.** Named so a later session does not re-open them. Self-report is
the strongest case: thirteen sentences across eight chapters, every one saying
that a system's account of itself is not evidence about itself, with section 11.1
carrying the finding and sections 3.4, 3.6, 5.2.3, 9.3.4 and 12.2.2 all citing it
correctly. Cassell holds across seventeen sentences in three chapters, including
the delicate part — sections 2.4.1, 3.4 and 3.8 agree that the thinner accounts
of suffering admit the bearer *sooner*, which is the counterintuitive direction
and the one an inconsistency would show up in. Federated learning holds because
section 6.4.4 explicitly reconciles section 9.2.1's "a government cannot simply
seize" with section 6.4.2's "an update is not nothing." P45's compute-floor
repair holds; P48's Partnership on AI repair holds.

**Two did not.**

**1. Three sections cite *Trump v. Cook* and two of them say it does not
generalize.** Section 3.1 has the Court keeping the Federal Reserve's protection
"on grounds that cannot be extended by construction"; chapter 11's opening has it
kept "for one body on grounds no new body can have." Section 8.3.4 then uses the
same decision as general authority — "That standard now has a court behind it" —
for what a removal must produce procedurally. The two readings are compatible,
because they take different halves of the case, and **no sentence anywhere said
so**. A reader tracing the case across chapters meets the third use immediately
after being told twice that the holding is not portable. Section 8.3.4 now names
which half it is taking and points at section 3.1 for the half that is not.

**2. The glossary attributes the sycophancy finding to three sections and one of
them has it.** The RLHF entry read "The book flags sycophancy ... as RLHF's
documented failure mode (§11.5, §7.2, §6.3.5)." Section 11.5 has it. Section 7.2
documents a *different* RLHF failure — persistent rater disagreement entering the
fit as variance around a mean — and never mentions sycophancy. Section 6.3.5 is
red-teaming and pre-release evaluation; it mentions RLHF once, on attack
resistance scaling, and has nothing on either failure. The entry now credits
section 11.5 with sycophancy and section 7.2 with the averaging failure by name,
and the section 6.3.5 pointer is dropped.

Both are the class the entry describes: a claim the book itself could have
corrected, found by following it across chapters rather than by a reference check.
**Nine instances now, across five passes.**

## Q-038 — the "rather than" sweep of chapters 2, 3 and 4 (ruled option (c))

**The re-count the ruling asked for first.** Book-wide the count is **317**, and
chapters 2, 3 and 4 carry **112** of it — 43, 39 and 30. The entry's figure was
141 of 340. So the three chapters' share has fallen from 41.5 percent to **35.3
percent**, and the sweep is a third smaller than the ruling was priced at.

**A negative result worth recording before the sweep.** No paragraph in the three
chapters carries three or more instances. P35 repaired the pile-ups at sentence
level; at paragraph level they are gone too, and nothing has rebuilt them across
thirteen passes.

**110 sentences read one at a time**, in their paragraphs. The instrument that
found the repairs was not the reading alone but the per-section rate combined
with it: the tic is audible where two instances sit inside about 120 words, and
seven paragraphs in the three chapters do that. **Three repairs**, a rate of 1 in
37 — below chapter 6's 1 in 25, which is what the entry predicted would happen as
the surviving instances become the ones a reading keeps.

**1. Section 2.4.2 was contradicting its own title.** The tightest paragraph in
chapter 2 ended: "A system that develops new capacities during the research it
did not have going in should trigger re-review rather than ride on its original
consent. And review without enforcement is a promise rather than a safeguard."
Section 2.4.2 is titled *When Consent Is Inapplicable* and spends five paragraphs
establishing that consent is not merely hard to obtain from these systems but
unavailable — "there is no continuous consent to be had, and no better
measurement instrument would produce one." Its last paragraph then referred to
"its original consent." The sentence now runs in the guardianship vocabulary the
section has just established: what lapses is **the board's approval**, not the
subject's consent. This is a Q-043-class defect surfaced by a Q-038 instrument,
and it is the most substantive thing the sweep found.

**2. Section 3.3's run-in head echoed the sentence beneath it.** "A floor held by
several systems rather than one," immediately above "the reply to an unreliable
witness is corroboration rather than a better witness." The head is a label and
carries no argument in the construction; it is now "A floor held by several
systems, not one."

**3. Section 4.2.5 followed the book's own formula with a throwaway antithesis.**
"A floor has to be a reason held rather than a result produced" — which must
stay, it is the book's formula — was followed one sentence later by "the
distinction is engineering rather than philosophy." The second now reads "the
distinction is buildable and not only arguable," which says the same thing and
drops the echo.

**What was read and left, and why.** The other 107 are load-bearing contrast, and
four cases were considered at length and declined. Section 3.4's four-item
parallel ("affect is part of the machinery of valuation rather than a coloring
applied to it; the prosocial emotions are motivational states rather than
readouts; ...") is anaphora and not a tic. Section 2.4.2's other pair sits three
sentences apart and both instances carry the argument. Section 3.3's other pair
is two paragraphs apart. **Chapter 3's closing paragraph was the closest call and
is left intact**: its two instances are three sentences apart, the second is the
chapter's final clause, and substituting "and not" for "rather than" in the last
sentence of chapter 3 would be the cosmetic swap P35 declined to make, with more
downside than the echo costs.

**Where the density is, for whoever takes the remaining nine chapters.** Highest
per-1,000 rates in the three chapters swept: section 4.2.3 at 9.09, section 2.4.3
at 8.20, section 4.1.3 at 7.21, section 4.2.5 at 6.92, section 4.2.2 at 6.61,
section 3.6 at 6.35, section 2.4.2 at 6.28. Against a calibration set that runs
at 2.90 per 1,000 for *all* contrastive constructions.

## Q-035 — the vocabulary measurement over chapter 5 (ruled: run it)

Run the way chapter 4's was run before P33, and it **finds the same defect
chapter 4 had**. This is a finding to rule on and not a pass; no chapter 5 file
was touched.

**The citation vintage.** Chapter 5's 36 citations have a **median year of 2009**.
Four are from 2020 or later (11.1 percent); eighteen are from before 2010 (50
percent). **Its newest citation is 2021, and it has none from 2022 or later.**
Chapter 5 is the only substantive chapter in the book for which that is true —
chapter 2 also has none, and chapter 2's subject is foundational moral philosophy
and the psychology of affect, where 1890 and 1982 are the right dates. Chapter
4's median after P33 is 2018 with 41.9 percent from 2020 or later, which is what
that rebuild bought. The book's other chapters run 2016 to 2023.

**The absent vocabulary, by the subsection whose subject it is.** Each of these
strings occurs **zero times in chapter 5**:

| section | its subject | absent |
|---|---|---|
| 5.3.3 | *AI Systems Learning from Moral Disagreements and Conflicts*, 386 words | annotator disagreement, jury learning, pluralistic alignment, value pluralism, Delphi, bridging-based ranking |
| 5.6.1 | *Intrinsic and Extrinsic Motivation* | intrinsic reward, empowerment, open-endedness as a named program (curiosity is present, via Pathak 2017) |
| 5.7.1 | *The Role of Play in Moral Learning* | self-play, open-ended learning, generative agents |
| 5.5.1 | *Cooperative AI Systems and Multi-Agent Moral Development* | social dilemma, emergent cooperation, opponent shaping — though this is the best-covered of the four, citing Leibo 2017 and 2021 |
| 5.2.3 | *The Role of Introspection for AI Systems*, 344 words | see below |

**Section 5.2.3 is the cheapest instance and the most telling.** Its argument is
that "a fluent reconstruction after the fact is indistinguishable from the real
thing at the point of use," and its two instances are a 2011 tutoring system and
DARPA's 2019 XAI program. The book's own empirical finding on exactly this claim
is in section 11.1 — concept injection, no tested model's self-report trustworthy
as a readout of its internal state — and sections 3.4, 3.6, 9.3.4 and 12.2.2 all
cite it. **Section 5.2.3 does not.** So the gap is not principally a literature
chapter 5 would have to go and find. It is the literature the book already
contains, two chapters over.

**A structural measurement that goes with it.** Chapter 5 makes 65 cross-chapter
references and **16.9 percent of them point forward**, against 37.5 percent for
chapter 4 and 38.6 percent for chapter 6. The share falls monotonically through
the book as position would predict, and chapter 5 is a trough between the two
chapters on either side of it. Fifteen of its references go to chapter 2 and
eighteen to chapter 3; chapters 6 through 12 take eleven between them.

**What this does not establish.** It does not establish that chapter 5 is wrong,
or that any specific sentence in it is out of date. Chapter 5's human moral
psychology is a mature literature and Piaget, Kohlberg, Gilligan and Dweck are
not stale for being old. What is measured is that the chapter's machine-facing
half — five subsections that are about systems — stops in 2021, and that the
subsections with the largest contemporary literatures are the shortest in the
chapter. **Whether to act on it is the author's to rule.** Q-035 records the
finding and the options.

## Q-045 — chapter 3 and section 11.2 read whole (ruled: read and report)

About 16,000 words, read straight through in order, and reported as prose. **No
repair was made under this item**, which is what the ruling asked for; one defect
the reading found is repaired above under Q-043, and the rest is reported for the
author.

**The seams are visible, and they are visible as a specific verbal habit.**
Sentences in which the book reports on its own earlier state occur **seven times
in the manuscript, and all seven are in chapter 3 and section 11.2**. Nowhere
else in 94,000 words. They are: "the two answers I have given it before" and
"That is weaker than the answer this section gave before" (3.4); "recorded
earlier as a coincidence" and "which is more than this section had a paragraph
ago" (3.5); "has been carrying more weight in this book than it can take" (3.3);
"a further consequence I did not see for a long time" (3.7); "The third question
has one experiment after all" (11.2). That is what seven conversation-driven
passes concentrated in two places did to the prose: the chapter narrates its own
revision history. Some of it is honest and earns its place — 3.4's is a
correction the author should see. The aggregate is a register no other chapter
has.

**Chapter 3's opening map omits two of its eight sections.** The opener says "The
chapter's argument runs in five steps" and names sections 3.1, 3.2, 3.3, 3.8 and
then 3.4 and 3.7. **Sections 3.5 and 3.6 appear nowhere in it.** Section 3.5 is
the chapter's most-cited section from inside — eight inbound references, three
from 3.7 and three from 3.8 — and it carries the claim the last three sections
lean on: that resistance to shutdown and the capacity to refuse an owner are one
property with the sign flipped, and that the fork was never three options. A
reader is told the chapter has five steps and given a map with no place for its
pivot. The map is internally coherent as written, because 3.5 and 3.6 run out
consequences rather than advancing steps. It is still a gap. **Left for the
author**, because closing it means adding words to chapter 3, which is the thing
this question exists to watch.

**Section 11.2 is now 2,599 words in a 9,000-word chapter of ten sections.** It
is twice the chapter opener and two and a half times the next-longest section.
Book-wide, 25 paragraphs exceed 250 words; section 11.2 holds three of them, at
439, 424 and 257 words, in 2.75 percent of the book. Chapter 3 holds five more.
So chapter 3 and section 11.2 together are 17 percent of the book's words and 32
percent of its longest paragraphs. The 439-word paragraph on identity and
registration and the 424-word paragraph on the compute floor are each a section's
worth of argument set as one block.

**Two smaller things the reading found in section 11.2.** The first is repaired
above: the *Near-term work* head opened "The third question has no experiment,
and that is the finding" and closed, 400 words later in the same block, "The
third question has one experiment after all." The closing sentence already says
the experiment is not a welfare experiment, so the opening claim is now "no
welfare experiment" and the reversal goes. The second is left: the five-feature
list has two consecutive sentences beginning "And," which is where a later pass
added the fifth feature and did not reflow the fourth.

**Does it compose?** Chapter 3 reads as one argument and not as five repairs.
Every seam named above is a sentence about the book rather than a break in the
argument, and the argument itself runs: fork, capacities, rivals, the bearer, the
switch, the witness, exit, price. Section 11.2 composes less well. Its three
questions are announced and then the second one absorbs a 439-word excursion on
identity and registration that answers a different question, and its last head
carries the compute floor, the roof argument, a failure mode and an experiment in
two paragraphs. **The honest report is that chapter 3 survived the additions and
section 11.2 is carrying more than its structure declares.**

rule on, and the finding is above with its options; acting on it would be a pass

subject the book names once or twice.
