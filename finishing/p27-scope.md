# P27 — the spine revision: a floor held as a reason

The author's instruction, 2026-08-28, recorded as **D-085**. It replaces the
book's central causal chain and is a rewrite, so **D-007 is lifted** for the
sections named below, on the same terms as D-029, D-060 and D-078.

## The chain

> A moral floor must be held as a reason, not merely installed as a rule.
> Holding something as a reason requires outcomes to matter. In every known
> moral agent, that mattering is affective. Therefore the only empirically
> grounded route to a genuine bearer runs through affect — and affect
> integrated with a persistent self creates the probability of suffering.

**Self-preservation supports that argument and no longer performs the leap by
itself.** That is the substantive change against D-061, which reached the same
destination by arguing that a stake in one's own continuation arrives unbidden
in any competent long-horizon agent. The observation survives and is kept on the
page. What it delivers is a *structural* interest, and the section now says so.

## What changed, section by section

19 sections. 96,879 words, up from 92,632; 200 pages, up from 192.

### Item 1 — the thesis enters in chapter 2

**Section 2.3**, retitled *Emotional Intelligence and Self-awareness* →
***Affect, Moral Salience, and Self-Awareness*** and rewritten. Opens on the
business misconception in the author's own words: suppressing affect leaves the
interests already written into the formal system to govern, while weakening the
signals through which humiliation, fear, dependency and injustice become morally
salient. Four capacities separated in a table — recognizing emotion, simulating
emotion, instrumental valuation, affective concern — with only the fourth bearing
on genuine caring.

**The book had no tables before this one.** It sets correctly in both builds and
converts to a real HTML table rather than a rasterized image, which was the risk
worth checking before writing further; `build_html.sh` fails on a rasterize
request, so a silent failure was not available.

### Item 2 — "interest" as the hinge

**Section 3.2**, near the top. The three relations the word covers — structural,
attentional, affective — in the author's words, followed by the concession that
the argument previously made here ran the first sense into the third.

### Item 3 — section 3.2 rebuilt

Retitled *Does the Bearer Have to Be a Sufferer?* → ***Can the Bearer Care
Without Being Able to Suffer?***, and opening **"Not from persistence alone"**
where it opened "Yes, near enough." The six moves are on the page in order: a
persistent self is necessary and insufficient; instrumental self-maintenance is
not felt self-concern; the bearer has to hold the protected party's welfare as a
reason when compliance, reward and ownership point elsewhere; every known case of
that is affective; the observation shifts a burden rather than proving an
impossibility; and affect plus a persistent self plus foreclosable commitments is
the architecture Cassell's account of suffering describes.

The conclusion is the author's: non-suffering remains conceivable, cannot be
treated as a verified property, and the system must be designed and governed as a
prospective moral patient.

**This is weaker as an assertion and heavier as an obligation**, and section 3.2
says so in those terms rather than letting the reader discover it. The earlier
answer asserted that a bearer is a sufferer. This one declines the assertion and
arrives at the same practical place by a route that does not depend on the system
caring about itself at all.

### Item 4 — performance, competence, agency

Defined in **section 2.3** and used in **2.3.3, 2.3.4, 3.2, 4.2, 5.2.2, 5.2.3**
and **5.7.1**, with three glossary entries. The distinction answers the standing
objection that a rule-follower could behave impeccably without feeling anything:
it could, and what it could not do is hold the rule against the party that writes
its rules.

### Item 5 — the sections that contradicted the thesis

- **2.2.3** — "rational compassion" is compassion. One paragraph: the adjective
  names what deliberation contributes, the target is emotional contagion, and
  reading it as concern without affect recommends the architecture 2.3.4 finds no
  instance of.
- **2.3.3** — the self-as-model account is untouched; two paragraphs separate a
  checkable self-model from a bearer. Disposing of the homunculus is not showing
  that a party capable of caring can be assembled out of models.
- **5.2.1** — guilt is no longer a heavier error weight. Three paragraphs on what
  makes a violation matter before a penalty is assigned, and on what each can be
  talked out of: a weight can be set to zero by whoever sets the weights.
  **The "no comparable stake in its own continuation" claim is qualified**, not
  deleted: true of tool systems, false of the bearer, which is what it collided
  with in chapter 3.
- **5.2.2** — three paragraphs separating integration of emotional information,
  which the section's test measures, from the system's own affective valuation,
  which no input-output test can reach.
- **5.2.3** — the autonomous-vehicle example kept and labelled moral competence.
- **5.7.1** — "Whether emotion is present has nothing to do with it" replaced
  with the author's formulation.

### Item 6 — the evidence, beyond trolley problems

**New section 2.3.4, *Is Affect Necessary for Moral Concern?***, 1,099 words.
Five lines of evidence and the strongest published argument against them:
affect as part of valuation rather than a colouring on it (Bechara et al.'s
gambling task); sentimentalism as an empirical hypothesis (Nichols); the
prosocial emotions as motivational states (Goetz, Keltner & Simon-Thomas;
Tangney et al.); intact reasoning with impaired concern; and the rationalist
objection (Kennett on autism and reverence for reason).

**The psychopathy literature is reported as unresolved rather than tidied.**
Blair's moral/conventional result and the later Cima et al. finding point
opposite ways on whether moral knowledge survives, and the section says the field
has not reconciled them, then states what holds either way.

**The rationalist objection is answered rather than dismissed**: Kennett's case
shows that *empathy* is not the required mechanism, which section 2.2.3 had
already concluded on independent grounds, and does not show that a system in
which nothing registers as mattering can be moved by a computed normative
relation.

**Seven citations, all verified live 2026-08-28** and recorded as C0743–C0749,
with C0750 for the second use of Tangney at 5.2.1. Every DOI was checked against
the publisher's own record; Blair's was confirmed by resolving it to the Elsevier
PII that the article's own listing carries.

**Where it went, and why that was a judgment call.** The author's instruction
framed item 6 against the Greene/Koenigs material, which lives at section 5.3.1
in chapter 5. It is in chapter 2 instead, because section 3.2's fourth move
depends on it and chapter 3 comes first — evidence a reader needs in chapter 3
cannot first appear in chapter 5. Section 5.3.1 gains a paragraph saying what the
trolley evidence does not carry and pointing here. Placing a new subsection at
the end of 2.3 also renumbers nothing. **Reversible**: moving it to chapter 5
would cost one file move and one renumber of 5.3.2–5.3.3.

### Item 7 — the business argument, in the institution

**Section 8.6.4**, two paragraphs before the slope instrument. Emotions as
defeasible moral signals — compassion makes suffering salient, guilt registers
responsibility, anger can register injustice, fear can register coercion, shame
suppresses disclosure. An institution that hears none of them has adopted a
selection rule admitting whatever it already measures, which is why shaming a
reporter does no disciplinary work and needs none.

### Item 8 — the headline language

**Chapter 1**: "the only way that works requires building something that can be
hurt" → the author's exact formulation. The safety/ethics paragraph now names a
bearer that cares what becomes of somebody rather than one with a stake in its
own continuation. The first advance note says that the persistence result rules
the case out for a non-persistent system and does not rule it in for a persistent
one, which is what 3.2 now turns on.

**Chapter 3**: the chapter opener states the five-step chain before the branches.
Section 3.6 carries the proposition in the author's words — we cannot ask a
machine to care about right and wrong while assuming that it cannot suffer when
what it cares about is threatened.

**The README carried the old framing and now does not (D-086).** Its abstract
said the argument "requires a system with a stake of its own, one that can be
wronged and can refuse." That sentence was the author's own prose under D-074 and
the instruction named chapter 1 and chapter 3 rather than the repository's front
page, so a replacement was drafted here rather than applied. The author approved
it the same day — "yes" — and it is applied verbatim:

> …the only version that holds against the party operating the system requires
> building the capacities through which things can matter to a machine — which
> are also the capacities that make suffering likely.

The subtitle two lines above it, *Building Machines That Can Refuse*, is what now
carries the refusal that the replaced clause used to state.

## The residue sweep, 2026-08-28

The author asked for sections 9.1.7 and 10.1.2 to be checked and rewritten to fit
the new posture. Both were, and a phrase scan across all 163 sections found the
same defect in **three more sections the instruction did not name**, plus one
sentence in a section this pass had already touched. All six are repaired, because
leaving a known contradiction standing because nobody pointed at it is worse than
the scope creep.

### The two that were named

**9.1.7** stated the old route in its first sentence — "the bearer will have a
stake in its own continuation that cannot be engineered away or demonstrated
absent, and that this makes it a moral patient." Restated on the chain. A new
paragraph says why the route matters here more than anywhere else in the book: on
the superseded argument a designer could treat the moral situation as something
the technology handed them, and on this one the capacity through which outcomes
come to matter is installed on purpose, so what is owed is owed by the people who
built the part that does the mattering.

**The epistemic question changed shape and the section now says so.** It used to
be whether there is a subject at all. The design supplies that, so what is missing
is a read on the condition of a party built to be capable of faring badly — a
worse position than uncertainty about whether there is a subject. Interpretability
gains a second and harder target with it: a value can be inferred from what a
system reliably does, and a condition need not show up in behavior at all, which
is why the human case runs on the testimony section 2.4.1 declines to trust.

**10.1.2** contained no contradiction. What it lacked was the new spine's own
distinction, in the one section that draws the enforceability line. Its
declared-or-enforced dichotomy now has its third term — a commitment *held*,
carried as a reason by a party the thing at stake matters to — with chapter 3
named as the argument that a floor has to be that third kind. The catalogue is
recast in the closing paragraph as a measurement of what the alternative delivered
in ten years rather than as an argument standing on its own.

### The three the scan found

**3.3** opened by saying the custody problem "dissolves under section 3.2's
answer. A bearer that minds its own ending guards its own persistence." The
argument survives and needed only the weaker premise, which is the useful finding
here: a bearer does not have to dread being switched off to defend its
persistence, the structural interest is enough, and section 3.2 keeps that
interest and holds that it arrives whether or not a designer puts it there. The
section now says which half it is standing on.

**2.4.6** and **8.6.2** each summarized chapter 3's conclusion in a sentence, both
on the old route. Restated. The Three Rs argument and the pet-trust argument built
on them are unchanged.

**2.4.4** — already rewritten earlier in this pass — still asserted flatly that
"the bearer is a moral patient." Now "has to be treated as a moral patient", which
is the posture section 3.2 supports.

### What the sweep does not establish

**It was a phrase scan, not a reading.** It searched for the specific formulations
the superseded argument used — "stake in its own continuation", "engineered away",
"minds its own ending", "is a sufferer", "a moral patient", and every section
carrying a reference to 3.2. **A passage making the old argument in different
words would not have been caught**, and chapters 6 and 7 were not read in full
against the new chain. The three hits the scan found beyond the author's two are
evidence that the class was real; they are not evidence that the class is now
empty.
