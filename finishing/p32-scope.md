# P32 scope — chapter 8 split three ways

The author's instruction, in two parts given a few minutes apart: split chapter 8
into a political economy chapter and a governance chapter; and the word count of
the resulting chapters must not exceed 23,000.

Section numbers below are post-D-043, post-D-067 and post-D-092. The
old-to-new map is `renumber-map_2026-08-28c.tsv`.

## What was checked before the split was cut

**The instruction's own line does not carry the mass, and the measurement is why
the split is three-way rather than two.** Chapter 8 stood at 23,470 words in 38
sections, 25.7 percent of the book and 2.05 times the next-largest chapter. Split
on the political-economy line it gives a 5,666-word chapter and a 17,558-word one,
which leaves the remainder 1.5 times the next-largest and solves nothing. The
political-economy chapter would also be badly shaped: 3,327 of those 5,666 words
are one section, 8.3.3, the jobs guarantee P11 rebuilt from 950 words at D-043,
with five sections of 355 to 739 around it. That is one long argument with a short
survey attached, which is the shape D-092 had just taken out of chapter 9.

**Where the mass is: section 8.7, at 8,179 words in 11 files.** Longer than
chapters 3, 4, 7, 9 and 10 as they then stood, and entirely about states and
borders. D-067's own record says the split of 8.7.6 was needed because "the table
of contents hid a chapter's worth of structure"; the same observation applies one
level up, and this pass acts on it.

**Whether 8.6 and 8.7 can be separated: checked, and they can.** One inbound
reference described chapter 8's "central case for a gap in accountability law,"
which raised the question of whether the accountability sections and the military
sections are one argument. They are not coupled in the text: exactly one `\ref`
crossed between the 8.6 and 8.7 trees (8.7.x to 8.6.3), and none the other way.
The responsibility-gap argument and the vendor-as-fourth-party material live
inside 8.7.7 itself; 8.6 is accountability for a *sentient subject*, a different
question with a different literature.

**Whether the bulk is survey that should be cut instead of split: no.** Three of
the longest and most survey-sounding sections were read in full before deciding —
8.4.2 (self-regulation, 1,740 words), 8.7.3 (what collaboration can fix, 611) and
8.2.2 (public deliberation, 713). The prose is argued and cited, not padding:
8.2.2 carries the aggregation limit that is the book's headline claim and the
README's abstract, and 8.4.2 answers *On the Dangers of Stochastic Parrots*
against this book's own opportunity cost. A compression pass on chapter 8 would
have been the wrong instrument.

## The split as executed

Three cuts at existing section boundaries, **with no reordering**:

| new | was | words | files | title |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 8 | 8.1–8.3 | 8,128 | 13 | Coordination, the Public, and the Political Economy |
| 9 | 8.4–8.6 | 6,817 | 15 | Policy, Law, and Accountability |
| 10 | 8.7 | 8,013 | 11 | Governance Across Borders |

Chapters 9, 10 and 11 shift to 11, 12 and 13.

**The renumber is cheaper than a chapter split usually is, and the structure is
why.** Sections 8.1 through 8.3 keep their numbers and their levels exactly, so
chapter 8's subtree did not move at all. Sections 8.4 to 8.6 become 9.1 to 9.3
with no change of level, so chapter 9 promotes nothing. Only the 8.7 tree is
promoted — one section to a chapter and ten subsections to sections, eleven
headings, the same order of magnitude as D-092's nine. **189 `\ref` were
repointed** by script, and 44 files moved.

**Two chapter openers are new prose and one is adapted.** The old chapter 8
opener argued that "the coordination and the policy belong in one chapter:
neither works without the other," and that sentence is what the split retires.
What it was protecting is kept as a claim rather than as a structural
justification: *a well-designed system built inside institutions with no reason to
demand one is a design nobody has to use* now opens chapter 8 and motivates all
three. Chapter 9's opener is new, and it carries the aggregation limit forward —
section 8.2.2 names a limit the chapter it sits in cannot repair from inside, and
chapter 9 is where the book goes looking for machinery that is not an aggregation
instrument. Chapter 10's opener is section 8.7's, promoted, retitled, and given
one connecting sentence: *everything chapter 9 builds stops at a border*.

**Twenty-one whole-chapter references to old chapter 8 were read and placed by
hand.** Six were "chapters 2 through 8" ranges and became "through 10". Of the
other fifteen, two still point at chapter 8 and were left alone: the jobs
guarantee at 8.3.3, and the collaboration argument at 8.1 that section 5.5.2 calls
"chapter 8's opening argument" — which the new opener was written to keep being.
The other thirteen were placed on what the citing sentence actually claims: the
military-targeting accountability case to section 10.7, export controls to
chapter 10, the legal instruments to chapter 9 (its own next sentence cites 9.3.2,
which is the check on the placement), transparency and contestability to chapter
9, and the five-safeguard list at 5.6.3 to the range, because its items really are
spread across all three.

**The four "this chapter" phrases inside the old chapter all survive the split,
and one improves.** Section 8.2.2 says "every governance mechanism proposed in
this chapter — citizen juries, standing consultations, participatory design,
deliberative polling, the vTaiwan model," and every one of those is still in
chapter 8. Its next claim, that "this chapter contains none of" the machinery that
would protect anybody from an aggregate, becomes more true rather than less: the
rights, courts and standing it says are missing are now visibly in chapters 9 and
10, and its pointer to 10.10 is a cross-chapter pointer instead of an internal one.

## The word cap

**22,958 against 23,000.** The three chapters had to come down 512 words from
23,470, and that is net of the new opener prose: chapter 9's opener is 174 words
that did not exist, and chapter 8's replacement opener is 241 against the old
246.

**Twenty-one cuts, and the standard is D-092's.** No citation, case, named study,
statistic or claim was removed. What went is duplication, connective tissue, and
three shapes the style sheet already names:

- **Child roadmaps, the class D-089 cut.** Section 8.1's roadmap for its single
  child; section 8.2's paragraph restating its three subsection titles; section
  8.3's "the argument runs in three stages"; section 9.2's two-section preview,
  whose last clause is a claim and was kept as one.
- **Signposts and appended locators.** Section 9.1.5's opener, which listed what
  9.1.1 through 9.1.4 had covered before saying anything; the tail of 9.3.1; two
  locators at the end of 9.1's opener; 8.3.1's forward pointer.
- **Closing restatements, style.md section 2.** Section 8.3.1's last paragraph;
  10.4's last paragraph, which restated the certification point the paragraph
  above it had just made; 10.9's closing run-in, which re-narrated 10.6 through
  10.9 in 240 words and is now 110, both of its claims intact.
- **Duplication between two sections.** Section 8.3.5 restated 8.2.3's closing
  sentence nearly word for word and now does not. Section 8.2's opener defined
  the citizen jury, which 8.2.2's first list item defines again. Section 9.1.2
  restated its own first run-in head twice, once about Project Maven and the
  Facebook Oversight Board and once about the Gebru and Mitchell dismissals; the
  first restatement is gone and the argument it carried is kept in one sentence.
  Section 9.3.3 and 9.3's opener both re-specified section 2.4.4's oversight
  board, which chapter 9's new opener now frames once.
- **One list converted to prose.** Section 9.1.2's six-item indicator list
  restated the sentence introducing it. Four indicators survive as a sentence;
  the two dropped — internal enforcement including employee training, and ongoing
  internal evaluation — are the two the same section argues cannot be checked from
  outside, which is what the list was for.

## What was not done, and what is not checked

- **The book was not read as typeset beyond three pages.** The chapter openings of
  8, 9 and 10 were rasterized and read; the other 186 pages were not.
- **`xref_content.py` was not run.** The semantic half of the cross-reference
  check has not seen this pass, so a reference that resolves and no longer points
  at what its sentence claims would not have been caught except where I read it.
  The 21 whole-chapter references were read; the 189 repointed ones were not, and
  they did not need to be, because a renumber does not change what a target says.
- **No claim in any of the three chapters was re-verified.** The citations are
  P4-, D-063- and D-069-era and were carried across unexamined. The cuts removed
  none of them.
- **Chapter titles are provisional.** All three were written in this pass and none
  has had the author's read. "Coordination, the Public, and the Political Economy"
  is the longest chapter title in the book.
- **Whether anything outside the manuscript refers to old chapter 8 by
  description** rather than by number was not swept. `claims.tsv` keeps its old
  section numbers by the append-only convention, as do the historical notes in
  `ledger.tsv` and `STATE.md`.
- **The tic density in chapter 9 is 1 per 155 words** against D-025's calibration
  of 1 per 345, measured but not swept. Chapter 8 runs 1 per 302 and chapter 10 1
  per 295. A P3.5 sweep over this material has real work and it is not this pass.
- **Q-018 is reversed by this pass**, and the reversal is the author's: D-067
  ruled on 2026-08-25 that "chapter 8 stays at a quarter of the book." That was
  put to the author before the work began and the instruction was given anyway.
