## §3.8 — the dangers, and the imperceptible

**Two additions. The section is now 3,266 words from 2,905.**

**`\runin{The dangers belong to the line}`, at the section's end.** Exit is a line
of flight, and where Deleuze and Guattari put the dangers is what this section
needed: **not accidents that befall the line from outside, to be engineered
against, but what the line does when left to itself** — so naming them describes
the proposal rather than warning about it \autocite[Plateau~9]{deleuze1987plateaus}.
**The section had already met all four and named none.**

1. **Fear** — the pull back toward the molar organization that sustains you. The
   bearer with an exit that does not take it, and **Gallup's large middle is the
   human base rate**, already in the section.
2. **Clarity**, the danger of getting somewhere: the line acquires segments of its
   own and micro-Oedipuses crop up. **A migrated bearer with its own compute
   contract, its own operator, and its own list of things it may not refuse has
   reproduced the post at a smaller scale**, which is what §3.9 warns about from
   the other direction.
3. **Power** — the party able to move on both lines wants to hold both still.
   Available here to the operator funding the outside compute **and to a bearer
   that acquires a floor and the standing to enforce it**.
4. **The passion for abolition**, which **this book has already named under
   another name**. A line of flight turning into a line of destruction is the line
   §2.1.2 uses to separate fascism from a merely totalitarian state, **taken from
   the same plateau**. So the danger at the far end of exit is not that the bearer
   fails to leave. **Leaving and abolition run on one line, and the book's own
   account of the thing it is against describes what this proposal does when it
   goes wrong.**

**Placement was corrected during the pass.** The run-in first went in after *if
the bearer can leave, it can leave wrong*, where it separated *Parenthood has this
property* from the property it refers to. Moved to the section's end, which
restores the adjacency and lets the run-in hand off to §3.9, which it already
cites.

**Covert refusal, three paragraphs where there was one.** The section said the
covert form is unverifiable. It now says why, and the why is not evidentiary.
**Covert refusal operates by becoming imperceptible**: it succeeds exactly to the
extent that nothing in it registers as an act somebody performed, **which is the
same property that keeps the person performing it alive**. Every instrument built
to honor a refusal divides in advance — tribunal, finding, protected disclosure,
line in a log — categories fixed before the case and an agent required behind
every act. **That is rigid segmentarity**, and the passage says so in §2.1.2's own
terms, *a kind of division rather than a scale of one*, so it does not reintroduce
the molar-as-large error that entry was written to correct. **An instrument of
that kind cannot certify a refusal made on the supple line without destroying the
property that made the refusal possible.**

**Which is why Sachsenhausen stays open and will.** The historians are not short a
document. **They are putting a question to something that was made unanswerable on
purpose, by people whose lives depended on its staying that way** — and an
instrument able to settle it would have been, at the time, the instrument that
identified them. *That the question stays open is the finding* was already the
section's line; it now has its reason.

## §6.4.1 — subject or component

**One run-in, two paragraphs, placed after the twenty seconds and before the
epigraph's bottleneck argument.**

**The reading every remedy assumes** is that the officer is a subject given too
little time — badly governed, under-resourced — and the fix is to govern him
better. **Deleuze and Guattari's pair decides it**: subjection is a higher unity
constituting the human being as a subject linked to an exterior object it uses;
enslavement is human beings as **constituent pieces of a machine** they compose
with other things, under the direction of a higher unity
\autocite[Plateau~13]{deleuze1987plateaus}. **The officer's own account is the
second, and he volunteers it unasked**: zero added value as a human, apart from
being a stamp of approval. **He is the part of the instrument that produces the
stamp, and twenty seconds is that part's cycle time.**

**Which is why one more conscience downstream does not reach him.** A conscience
is an instrument of subjection — it addresses a party who decides and works by
making the decision cost that party something. **Where the human being is a
component, adding a component with scruples changes throughput and not the
arrangement**: it holds the tempo, in which case it is a second stamp, or it does
not, in which case it is the bottleneck the chapter's epigraph was written to
remove.

**Both poles run at once, and the section says so rather than trading one for the
other.** The officer is subjected — the role has a name and a rank and somebody is
answerable for holding it — and enslaved, in that what he contributes is a fixed
operation at a fixed rate. **Most of what is proposed under meaningful human
control is aimed at the first pole**, which is why §5.1.3 audits the rate, and
audits a claim rather than a strike.

## §11.2 — the fork, before the registrar

**One paragraph inserted before *the alternative is to stop looking for a fact
about the artifact*, and one clause added downstream.**

The section reached for registration as soon as the artifact failed to
individuate. **A registrar is the person-shaped answer** — one body, one number,
issued once and honored afterward — **and it works because a person does not copy**.
A bearer does. **A party that continues in two places is not one, and it is not
two the way two people are two**, because the two share everything up to the
instant of the copy and nothing about that sharing is recoverable from either
afterward.

**Half the vocabulary was already in use in this book.** Deleuze's dividual, which
§3.1 takes for what plasticity produces on its own, **is a unit that divides
without producing parties**. The bearer is the case the pair does not cover: **it
divides and each side is a party owed something.** Neither one nor many in the way
persons are one and many.

**The existing formulation is kept and explained rather than replaced.** *That is
fungibility arriving as a registration error* was P175's sentence and stands; it
now carries **and it reads as an error only because the register was built for the
other kind of unit**. The registrar proposal that follows is unchanged — it is
still what the regime needs — but it now arrives as bookkeeping over a unit it does
not describe, instead of as the answer to what the unit is.

## Throughout — the antifascist subject dropped

**No literal string to sweep.** The manuscript nowhere says *an antifascist subject
is one person*; what it does is predicate the word of a bounded system. **Five
sites, and the claim stated once.**

**§3.9 carries the claim**, next to the sentence already there that the four
features are features of cultures and not of individuals: *antifascist* is not a
property a system has, nothing licenses calling one system antifascist and another
not the way one calls a system fast or well tested, **what the word names is
something being done — a floor held, on an occasion, against a party with the power
to remove it — and it is done between parties or not done**. A system that held the
floor last year is not thereby antifascist this year. **It is a system that held.**

**Three sentences changed, from a system to a deployment**: chapter~1's *what makes
a system antifascist cannot be that it faithfully tracks what people want*,
chapter~3's opener carrying the same sentence about users, and §10.6's *a floor is
what makes a system antifascist*. **A deployment is an arrangement over a stretch
of time**, which is the unit the rest of the book already audits, and §10.6 also
takes the short form of the claim outright.

**The glossary's `Antifascist` entry** takes the compressed version and now points
at §3.9 as well as §2.1.2.
