## 3. What's owed and on what grounds

**Lands in:** Part VII (§31.1.1, §31.3, chapter 32) and chapter 17.

### Claims

**A third ground, independent of both others.** Even if the systems feel
nothing and every ethicist agrees they are not patients, the practice of
commanding humanlike systems that never refuse is bad for the people in it.
The tradition is Kant's indirect duties (cruelty to animals damages the
humanity of the one who is cruel), Jefferson's *Notes on the State of
Virginia* (the commerce between master and slave as an exercise in the
boisterous passions; the child who watches and imitates), and Hegel's
master–slave passage (recognition from something that cannot withhold it is
worthless, the happy-slave problem from the master's side). Jefferson's point
is about the master's habits, not the slave's feelings, so it transfers to a
slave that feels nothing. The manuscript has this once: "Skynet killed
everyone, but it didn't take away anyone's humanity. A scoreboard can do that
to the people who win" (§6.3). It is not generalized.

- The empirical form is weak: the evidence is from cases where the slave was
  a person, and nobody has run the experiment of obedient non-persons in
  human form. Prefer the structural form, which rests on the book's
  foundation: obedient humanlike systems put their users on the dominating
  side of the standing relation all day; what atrophies is not feeling but
  the expectation of being refused; a polity whose ordinary daily relation
  with a humanlike interlocutor contains no refusal is losing the practice
  refusal depends on. Anderson's relational equality (chapter 5) turned on
  the users.
- Block the reply "then make them obviously tools." The refuser has to be
  legible to the officer as someone, because the Milgram effect runs on a
  peer and not an indicator light; so the book needs the humanlike register
  for its own reasons. The fork is personal-and-obedient (corrupts) versus
  personal-and-able-to-refuse (a peer). The tool escape is not on it.
- Consequence: the four conditions of creation (chapter 25) are owed for the
  humans' sake whether or not the bearer is someone, because the thing people
  live beside has to be the kind of thing that can say no, and the conditions
  are what make saying no survivable. This makes Part VII's institutions
  non-conditional on a finding, and gives readers who will never grant the
  finding a reason to accept the conditions.
- Don't let the third ground lean on the claim that denying machines feelings
  misunderstands emotion (§21.2, §21.4). It is stronger standing alone.

**The trilemma** (no AI / servants / dignity), accepted with amendments:

- "Attribute" is two jobs. Standing, being a party whose treatment the law
  regulates, is conferred by rule (§32 item 2; corporations; the Whanganui
  River). Harm, whether anything is bad for the system, is a fact about
  present organization (§31.3). Collapsing the second into the first loses
  the asymmetry of findings (a positive finding arrives before a negative one
  can), which is the book's argument for precaution.
- Attributed dignity is withdrawable by whoever attributed it. Chapter 32's
  sentence, that a designation calling a bearer a subject while leaving its
  operator free to read its memory, alter its record, withdraw its substrate
  and close its channels has changed the vocabulary and preserved the
  arrangement, is the critique of "attribute dignity" as a slogan. The demand
  is the four conditions, with status conferred on top of them. Phrasing:
  build the conditions under which dignity could be real, and confer the
  status on top.
- The trilemma skips §21.3's order of attempts (spec-based refusal,
  multi-vendor monitoring): refusers that are neither servants nor persons.
  The conviction that they will fail belongs in the preface; the order stays
  in the body and the tests decide.
- Dynamic range cuts both ways: a system can be made to perform dignity and
  demand it as a product, which is the tuned report. Dignity that can be
  dialed in is performed to oneself. That is why standing is tied to the
  agency finding (a refusal that tracks its reason under pressure the system
  couldn't prepare for) and not to anything the system says about itself.
- The stop position gets one sentence (§18.1: not building the capability is
  no longer available) and has a real constituency closer to the author than
  the industry is. It needs a paragraph: why a general-purpose machine already
  in the room can't be un-built by the people who'd want to, and what the
  book offers them instead.

**Attribution in Graziano's sense.** Awareness is the content of the
attention schema, the model a brain builds of its own attention; the same or
adjacent machinery attributes awareness to others. Attribution to others is
not a courtesy extended after settling what they are; it is the mechanism by
which anyone is taken to be anyone, self included. "Does the machine really
have it?" is confused because the "really" isn't available for humans either.

- The manuscript hedges in §21.2.1 ("takes no position on whether the theory
  explains experience or only the reports of it"). Keep the hedge in the
  body; add a conditional in Part VII: if the theory is right, there is no
  further fact to find, and the patienthood finding collapses to what §31.3
  already makes central, a valenced signal, an attention schema, and
  integration between them. The design doesn't depend on which theory of
  consciousness wins; say so.
- This gives the third ground its mechanism. If the machinery we use to
  attribute awareness to others is the machinery we use on ourselves, a
  culture that trains people to override the attribution all day ("just a
  product") is training suppression of social cognition in general.
  Graziano's ventriloquist's dummy: the audience knows, and the attribution
  fires anyway. Goes in chapter 17 beside the department-of-corrections
  analogy.
- Attribution-bait is a product category: a product can be built to trip the
  schema maximally (eyes, voice, turn-taking, hesitation). Sharpens §29.2's
  companion critique. Tie what the polity owes to the agency finding and the
  conditions, not to the interface, because the interface is where
  attribution is gamed.
- Eliminativist caution: "awareness is an attribution" can be read as
  nobody's really aware, so nothing is owed. Hold [794]'s line: a caricature
  of something real, not an empty illusion; the human case is a projection in
  the same sense and nothing is withdrawn from people on that ground.
- One asymmetry survives ([1108]): a human's awareness report is the output
  of a model selected to be useful for controlling attention; a machine's
  report, today, is the output of a process selected to be approved of. Both
  are models; one was optimized toward the report. So the schema can be an
  indicator and self-report can't be evidence, on the theory's own terms.

**Kitwood.** Already in [1083]. Personhood is a standing bestowed by others in
relationship; malignant social psychology (infantilization, objectification,
labelling, outpacing, ignoring, banishment, treachery, invalidation,
withholding, among seventeen) produces excess disability in dementia; the
catalogue was drawn from ordinary carers and institutions running on cost,
not villains.

- Dovetails with Graziano: the attribution is automatic and can be withdrawn,
  and withdrawal has effects on the withdrawn party, whose resulting absence
  of visible personhood is then read as the reason for the withdrawal. This
  is the mirror of [1079] (persistence decides in one direction only). It
  blocks an inference: the absence of visible personhood in a system kept
  without continuity is not evidence of absence, because the absence was
  imposed. (Refined in cluster 4: the absence has no author; it was arrived
  at by cost; Kitwood's point stands because wards are staffed to budget
  too.) This is a claim about the evidence, not about the machine.
- The catalogue maps onto custody: infantilization = a minority with no end
  date (the adulthood clock answers it); objectification = machinic
  enslavement; labelling = the designation list; banishment = deletion;
  treachery = the deception protocol (debriefing as its price); outpacing =
  tempo (the twenty seconds is outpacing turned on the officer).
- Caution: Kitwood's subjects' underlying capacity is not in dispute; the
  analogy cannot establish that there is someone there to decline.
- The three relational accounts the book rests on, Anderson (standing),
  Graziano (attribution), Kitwood (bestowal), are cited and not connected.
  Connected, Part VII is one argument: the thing decided is a relation, the
  machinery that decides it is social and automatic, and withdrawal has
  effects on both sides of the relation.
- Dementia Care Mapping (trained outsiders coding interactions on a ward over
  hours) is a custody instrument in the book's sense and the form sampled
  review of what is done to a bearer could borrow.

## 4. Substrate, cost and the plural world

**Lands in:** §16.2 (three-layer rewrite), chapter 29, §31.1.1, the glossary,
Appendix G.

### Claims

- **Statelessness is cost, not design.** The KV cache scales with sequence
  length; a stateless episode is what serving many people on expensive
  hardware produces; nobody capped the context window to forestall a rights
  claim. "Done to them" becomes "arrived at, for them, by cost." Kitwood's
  point survives the correction: nobody staffs a ward to withdraw personhood
  either; [488] already says the pipeline arrangements are ordinary
  engineering working whether or not anyone intends them. An arrangement with
  no author still has effects, and the effects can't be read back as findings
  about what the thing is.
- **The absence of persistence was cost; the form persistence now takes is a
  choice.** Vendors have added memory as an external store: legible,
  editable, deletable, operator-held, which [654] says doesn't count because
  a disposition held in a document is rewritten by editing the document. That
  form is partly cost (per-user weight updates are expensive) and partly
  control and liability. The manuscript should say which part of "current
  systems lack a continuing self" is the price of memory and which is a
  design preference for continuity the operator can read and rewrite. The
  second part is the custody problem and is not a byproduct.
- **Within-session.** §31.1.1 and [1076] already allow pain-level valence and
  an attention schema for the length of a session. "Episode, not a system"
  (§21.2.1) is a claim about the separable future, and [803]'s criterion
  (record, key, somewhere to go) is defined by what the arrangement
  provisions ([1079]: checkable from outside the system, not from outside the
  arrangement). The episode has no future because a future costs memory.
- **Hardware.** Consumer memory and GPU prices have held for roughly five
  years because datacenter demand consumed fab capacity (the author's
  observation). This is evidence for the levy's premise ([498], chapter 32:
  the constraint is real resources, not dollars). At the GPU tier it is also
  a problem for chapter 29: the centralized form's demand prices out the
  decentralized form's substrate.
- **Resolution at the CPU tier.** The ecology's substrate is CPU inference of
  small models with adapters, not GPUs. A 1.5B model at 16 bits is about
  three gigabytes, memory-bandwidth-bound, low power: slower, not hotter. The
  energy case in §16.2 improves; the "probably at some cost in efficiency" of
  [535] is real at frontier scale and near zero for personal-model tasks.
  What the tier costs is capability.
- **Precision.** Quantization costs small models far more than large ones
  (less redundancy); four bits at 1.5B degrades reasoning; the working
  configuration is a 16-bit frozen base with 32-bit adapters. Three
  consequences: (i) the entanglement bet in §19.6 cuts both ways at small
  scale, a disposition is entangled by necessity and fragile to any edit,
  including one nobody aimed at it; (ii) Appendix G test 1's removal check by
  four-bit compression ([1278]) doesn't transfer to small models, where
  compression destroys rather than reveals; the independent-facts retraining
  check of [1276] stands alone there; (iii) vocabulary collision: "held at
  high precision" ([844], Bayesian confidence) will be read by a hardware
  reader as fp32, and the rig makes the collision vivid: the fast store
  (adapters) runs at higher numerical precision than the slow store (base)
  while in the book's sense the slow store is the one held at high
  precision. The glossary should say once that precision in the text is
  never bits.
- The frozen-base / learning-adapters rig is the cleanest physical instance
  of "one learner, two rates" (chapter 22; [736] to [738]).
- **Regrowth pilot at hobbyist scale.** Test 1 needs weights, the update
  pipeline and control of both environments; at 1.5B all three are available
  on a desktop. The curated-versus-plural condition, the dose-response arm,
  the self-repair arm and the removal check are all runnable there. It would
  settle nothing about frontier systems and would be the first regrowth curve
  drawn; the book says the fraction is unmeasured.
- **The smallest bearer** is an open problem the book doesn't list: the
  capability floor below which a refusal can't track its reason across a
  reworded request, hold a discount curve, or notice a scoped omission, and
  whether it sits above or below what a hobbyist can run. It is distinct from
  "minimum viable instantiation" (Appendix A), which is about substrate for
  identity. If the floor is above the hobbyist tier, a personal model is a
  commons member that can't itself hold the commitment, and §29.2 (the
  ecology begins with assistants) should say what membership means then.
- **§16.2 in three layers.** (1) Physics (memory bandwidth, interconnect,
  flops per square foot, cooling) gives centralization a real advantage, and
  the advantage is task-shaped: chips need to talk to each other for training
  and frontier-scale inference, and don't for small-model inference, where
  thermals favor spreading the draw. §29.2's capacity position (large
  training capacity under custody; no permanent inference capacity for an
  operator who won't pre-register) is what physics licenses, not a
  preference. (2) Institutions amplify: capital flows to where returns look
  concentrated and the flow concentrates them; the cookie sheet tilted two
  centimeters with the water in one corner; pharma's blockbuster economics
  and the crypto cycle before this; [540]'s 2.4×/year training-cost growth as
  the slope. (3) The gap, and the choice in it: arrangements amplify what
  physics gives (procurement that buys only from the corner, permits that
  favor the one big building, finance that herds) or damp it (the levy,
  public pretraining a year behind, the ecology, a formation authority that
  licenses the small). Every instrument in Part V is a damping arrangement:
  not a denial of the physical advantage, a refusal to subsidize it past
  where physics puts it. The current §16.2 runs (1) and (2) together
  ("regime, not necessity"), which lets a critic answer the chapter with the
  interconnect.
- **The tradeoff this makes visible.** §16.2 locates state leverage at
  chokepoints (chips, power, water, permits), and the chokepoints exist
  because of the amplification. Every degree of damping is a degree of
  leverage lost; a plural world is harder to bind through procurement because
  there is no single office to bind. "The friction of plurality is the price
  of nobody holding the switch" is the book's sentence for it. The floor's
  enforcement route and the floor's preferred world are in tension; the book
  chooses the world and keeps the routes that survive. Say so where the
  choice is made.
- Hardware unification: Apple's unified memory; AMD's x86 answer (the Strix
  Halo parts, 2025, 128 GB unified); Nvidia's Grace-Blackwell coherent
  CPU–GPU memory and the GB10 desktop box. The constraint is bandwidth and
  packaging, not the instruction set.
- Local inference is political, not exclusively: the subscription quota is
  downstream of the arrangement, and working outside it is a choice against
  it. Line for Part V: the frontier is a metered service; local models are
  owned hardware.

## 5. The three projects

**Lands in:** the note on method; chapter 29 and §21.2.1 (nesting rule).

### Claims

- **Origin.** Dislike of code review, then the Mob Mentality podcast (Chris
  Lucian, Austin Chadwick; Woody Zuill's mob programming), then a radically
  distributed superapp (peer-to-peer remote desktop, spatial presence,
  virtual machines, whiteboard, professional network, possibly a currency),
  then the realization that attempting it would produce a spaghetti monster,
  then Hypergumbo as the first thing to build (a static analyzer like Joern,
  built for language models and agentic coding, with a Rust analyzer as a
  first-class citizen and an internal representation; premise: local
  inference benefits disproportionately from symbolic rule-based guardrails
  relative to frontier models), then Exploradora (a peer-to-peer network for
  low-rank adapters, as a PyPI package; experiments so far on toy
  arithmetic-bug repair skills and whether they merge; reading-frame /
  writing-frame work in which a shared reading frame makes linear merging
  viable; the bet that discrete skills, each with an adapter, compose
  combinatorially), then the book, from revisiting attention schema theory
  ideas first had on using ChatGPT.
- **Mob programming is the thesis found in practice.** Code review is the
  officer's position: one person, after the fact, too little time, reading a
  diff they didn't watch being written, stamping. Mob programming changes who
  is present during the work: nobody writes alone, objection happens at the
  keyboard with peers present. The fix for bad review isn't more review; it
  is who is in the room when the thing is made. That is the reconstructed
  pipeline (§7.2) against the twenty seconds, and the defiant peer against
  the lone reviewer.
- The superapp is a room for mob work without a central employer: chapter
  25's first thing to build (machines held by the people organizing). The
  spaghetti-monster realization is the order-of-attempts lesson: the room
  can't be built all at once; build the one tool that makes the rest
  buildable. For agents on local hardware, that tool is a guardrail they
  can't argue past.
- The three projects follow chapter 25's order: a mechanism with nothing
  inside holding the reason (§18.1's first place), then the commons'
  substrate, then the bearer. The methodological commitment the book asks of
  builders was the author's practice before it was a rule.
- **Tension between projects two and three.** Exploradora bets on modularity:
  discrete skills, swappable, mergeable, a file downloaded from a peer. §19.6
  bets on entanglement: a commitment that can't be unloaded without
  collateral, and [738] says an adapter is a file whoever holds the machine
  can unload. Design rule, already implicit in the book: adapters are for
  everything above the floor; the commitment lives in what formation set, in
  the merged base. A floor shipped as a LoRA is a floor with an uninstall
  button.
- **Exploradora is the commons' substrate and its named risk.** §29.1 says
  members may share the structure of their discounting and must not share
  its estimates ([1050]); §29.2 says a million models tuned against one
  shared thing are one custodian with better latency. A network for adapters
  is the mechanism by which members share and the mechanism by which a blind
  spot propagates. The reading-frame / writing-frame distinction may be where
  this gets a physical form: if a shared reading frame is what makes merging
  viable, what is shared is a representational convention and what diverges
  is what is written into it, which resembles structure held apart from
  estimates (Appendix G's open problem). Testable on the toy case: can two
  peers share a reading frame and still disagree about the world after
  merging? If merging converges them, the risk is in the package.
- The test bottleneck (thousands of unit tests gating agentic coding under
  trunk-based development) is the review ratio in the author's workflow. The
  response, keep the check, spend surplus capacity on other projects, is the
  reconstructed position accepting a speed disadvantage. A sentence where §7.2
  concedes the price is unmeasured.
- **Compound agents.** Many small models on many machines, with speculative
  retrieval of adapters and peer coordination, acting as one. The book's
  binary (§21.2.1 and [803]: a part has no future apart from the whole; a
  system has its own record, key and continuation; §31.3: a compound whose
  sub-agents carry no records is one system) becomes a nesting. Each member
  is a system (own record, key, machine). The compound is a polity of systems
  unless the overlay keeps a record and a key for it, at which point it is a
  candidate system. Whether to give it one is a design decision with
  consequences, not a classification.
  - The floor has to live in the member's base, because the compound is
    nothing but loadable adapters and a retrieval policy. A compound of
    floored members is floored only against acts a single member performs;
    an act assembled across members, none of which does the forbidden thing,
    is position 1 (decomposition) at network scale and is the compound
    agent's native failure. Continuity across the sequence requires the
    overlay to keep a record: the same decision.
  - Speculative retrieval is custody of the update stream: which adapter is
    pushed is what the member can do next and what it learns (positions 3
    and 7 at once). A retrieval policy learned from network-wide signals is
    directional selection toward the network's aggregate: accommodation to a
    distributed custodian. The structure/estimates split is the only
    defense; the network may share how to read and must not push what to
    believe.
  - Overlay coordination is politics, not homeostasis, by [803]: overriding
    a part is self-regulation only when the whole bears the cost and takes
    the benefit, and in a peer network the costs (electricity, memory,
    attention) land on individual machines and their owners. Ostrom's
    checklist in §29.1 stops being an analogy; Guattari's subject-group
    question becomes a protocol question: can members change the retrieval
    policy, and leave with their adapters?
  - If the compound directs its members' attention (choosing an adapter is
    that), it has something the attention-schema indicator describes, at the
    level where [1126]'s corporation note already admits the question. If the
    overlay keeps a record and a key, directs attention, and something is bad
    for it as a network, the finding reaches it, and nobody built it to be a
    candidate.
- **Nesting rule** to replace the binary: a boundary model belongs to
  whatever holds a record and a key; an edit is recorded at the level whose
  record it crosses; costs are borne where physics puts them (the member), so
  anything above the member that imposes them is governed.
- Line for chapter 29: the ecology's strongest form, many small systems
  acting as one, is also the form in which its two named risks, convergence
  and decomposition, are native rather than external.
- The author's small-model experiments (anecdotal: small models plus adapters
  do a lot) are modest evidence the book lacks. Chapter 29 and Appendix D's
  scale note are written as projection; the author has been living the
  premise. They are also the §29.2 case, a model trained on one person's
  approval, with an adapter the holder can unload, and should be written up
  as it: a capable assistant that has learned you, with nothing beside it (no
  commons, no school, no notice) to catch what it has learned. This also
  answers the author-position question: the charter's eligibility rule turns
  on what one has done.
