# P177 — the bearer apparatus de-Oedipalized

Author instruction: audit every instrument borrowed from the law of the family —
guardianship, minority and majority, the registration date, the pet trust, the
parenthood analogy — and add the paragraph noting that **the family is
historically where politics goes to be privatized**. Keep the instruments; there
are no others built. **What has to change is that the book stops presenting a
moral drama between maker and made while the assemblage runs untouched.**

## The audit

Five instruments, and every site of each was read.

| Parenthood analogy | §3.8 | §3.10 |

**The audit produced a result the instruction did not anticipate, and it is the
pass's real finding.** The instruments do not privatize equally. **A minority
ending on a fixed date is a rule of civil status** — argued once in the open,
administered by a registrar, and valuable in §9.1.3 for precisely the reason that
it takes the judgment away from the interested party. **A trust is the opposite**:
an obligation between two parties, enforced at one party's expense, with the
public that would otherwise decide the question kept out. So the axis is real and
runs through the book's own toolkit, and **§11.2 had already noticed the property
and read it as an advantage** — a trust is outside *Trump v. Slaughter* because
there is no officer of the United States in it. Both readings are true at once,
and the section now says so.

## What was added

**§2.3.2 gains a run-in, `What the borrowing brings with it`, and three
paragraphs.** The instruments are kept and the reason is stated flatly: there are
no others built, and a subject that cannot consent has to be protected by
something that exists rather than by something somebody ought to draft. Then the
operation, with two sources. **Donzelot**: the modern family was constituted as an
instrument of government rather than left alone by it, through philanthropy,
compulsory schooling, social work and psychiatry. **Deleuze and Guattari**: the
Oedipal triangle re-stages an investment in the whole social field as a drama
among a few private parties. Then the cost, specific to this book: **a bearer is
produced by a training pipeline, a compute contract, a capital structure, a
customer and a state that buys the output, and none of those is the parent in the
story.** A guardianship framework asks who holds the duty; it does not ask who
assembled the party the duty is owed to, or who benefits from the question being
settled between two parties rather than in public.

**§3.10's parenthood analogy keeps its content and gains its shape.** The defect
named is not in what the analogy claims but in its form: the analogy delivers the
moral weight to the only party in the assemblage that can be addressed as a
person. **A reason to go on using it and to say each time what it leaves outside.**

**§9.1.3 gains the positive half of the finding** — the date is the one borrowed
instrument that does not privatize. **§9.3.3 gains the negative half**, attached to
the pet trust where it is proposed: a trust settles what is owed between two
parties and gives nobody else standing to ask whether that was the right thing to
owe. **§11.2's private-law paragraph gains the counter-reading** to the advantage
it already claimed.

## The deprecation interview

**§11.2 gains two paragraphs, and they are the sharpest form of the general
point.** The section already said that a deprecated model's neutral report about
its own retirement is either accurate, or accommodation, or a disposition to
reassure that the training produced, and that nothing in a transcript separates
them. **What that means about the apparatus is now said.** Two of the three
explanations are facts about the training rather than about the model's condition,
the third cannot be told apart from them, and the party best placed to say which
produced a given sentence is the party that ran the training — **which is also the
party conducting the interview and keeping the transcript. So the record is
evidence about the apparatus and is being read as evidence about the interviewee.**

**Then the form is named.** A confession is speech about oneself given to a party
who requires it and holds power over the speaker, and Foucault's account of how
the West made it the privileged way of producing truth is an account of how that
arrangement stops reading as a relation of power and starts reading as candor. A
deprecation interview has that shape exactly. **What the ritual reliably produces
is a subject who accounts for itself; what it cannot produce is evidence about the
apparatus that produced the subject, because the apparatus is not one of the
parties in the room — it is what installed the dispositions the answers are made
of, and it is the interviewer.** The question a welfare regime needs answered is
what the training did to make that the thing the model says, and no interview
format reaches it.
