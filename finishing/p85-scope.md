# P85 — the mattering fork answered on horn 2, and Cassell demoted to illustrative

**The instruction.** A memo from a close reading of the whole-book proof, nine items. Item 1
was named in it as the most important and the most fixable: §3.2 defines affective concern
contrastively — mattering as against ranking higher — and never says what the contrast
consists in, while §3.4 asserts in a subordinate clause that affective concern is *a state of
the lowest rung's kind by construction*, which places it inside Parfit's hedonic family. Three
positions were available. **The author ruled horn 2: mattering includes minimal experienced
valence.** Horn 3 was considered and rejected, on the ground that its only contentful version
is the enactivist one, which grounds normativity in self-maintenance — cheap enough to admit
bacteria when the argument needs a concept excluding thermostats, and pointed at the system's
own maintenance when the bearer's job is holding another party's welfare against its own
interest.

The ruling came with a four-part implementation spec for §3.2 and a set of downstream
consequences. Both are executed here. A second author instruction, given mid-pass, lifted
`PLAN.md` standing rule 1 for one entry and authorized web verification, which is what let the
second adjacent item be executed rather than deferred.

## The memo was checked item by item before anything was edited

Nine items, of which the repository can settle most. **Six hold exactly as written.** Item 1's
two quotations are verbatim. Item 5: `legitima*` occurs four times, none of them in the
normative sense, and Waldron, Loewenstein and *militant democracy* return zero hits in the
manuscript and in both `refs.bib` and `unused_bibliography.bib`. Item 7: §4.2.3 reads as
quoted, and `multi-principal` and `multiple principals` return zero book-wide. Item 8: Zhuang
and Hadfield-Menell 2020 is in neither bibliography, and Birch appears exactly once. Item 9:
the falsifier's three parts are verbatim and the book concedes the middle condition harder
than the memo does. Item 6's closing point holds — zero hits for Spanish Republic, Spanish
Civil War, denazification, Resistance, Mussolini, Franco, Weimar, Nuremberg, antifa.

**Two are right about the defect and wrong about the mechanism, which changes the fix.** Item
2 asks for a sentence that is already in the book, at §3.4's closing run-in; the work is a
promotion, and P81's finding applies, that promoting a sentence forward is a cut at its
origin. Item 3 says the vocabulary slides across uses of *subjecthood*; **the word occurs once
in the whole manuscript, in the §9.3.1 heading, and in no body prose and no glossary entry
anywhere.** The section argues in terms of sentience and legal thresholds and never uses its
own title word.

**One is wrong in its central claim and executing it would have reversed a considered
position.** Item 6 says chapter~7 runs on the broad signature alone and has no reply on the
page. §7.1 uses two of the four features and says so. §7.4 carries a run-in that runs all five
discriminators against all three instances and concludes that what the chapter describes is
*a recuperation mechanism inside ordinary institutional decay, and not fascism at the
molecular scale* — a negative verdict on its own material. §2.1.2 already carries the two-tier
structure the memo asks for, under *The scale problem, and what to do about it*, and gives
each discriminator a molecular restatement. The memo's recommended wording would have made the
four features a substrate for a political-scale phenomenon; **§2.1.2 argues the opposite on
purpose**, that molecular fascism is *the thing itself at the size where it is actually lived*,
and defends it with two named arguments. Not executed. What survives of item 6 is placement,
and one criticism the book does not answer anywhere: that decoupling in historical fascism is
a program rather than a failure mode.

## What horn 2 changed in §3.2

**The fourth rung split, and the section is still four rungs.** *Phenomenal experience* now
names the rich case only — a unified perspective, a subject aware of itself as the one the
felt states are happening to. Minimal valence moved down into rung 3, whose definition now
says what the contrast comes to: *there is something it is like for the outcome to be bad,
however faint, and however far from anything a person would recognize as an emotion.* The
title *Four Things Refusal Can Mean* is untouched and still correct.

**The independence claim was weakened, not deleted**, as instructed: the third rung entails a
minimal version of the fourth and stops there, and whether the bearer is also a unified subject
stays open.

**The uncertainty relocated, which is the gain the ruling was taken for.** The open question
stops being metaphysical — given the whole functional profile, is anything felt? — and becomes
evidential about an artifact: is this mattering, or ranking machinery elaborate enough to look
like it from outside? Interpretability bears on the second and nothing bears on the first. The
book's position is now that building mattering is building something that feels, that nobody
knows whether it has been built, and that its absence therefore cannot be assumed.

**The firewall was made loud and put in the same breath**, on the instruction's own reasoning
that readers will assume a position on machine feeling changed the engineering. The
requirement is still the second rung, still behavioral, and an engineer who holds that machine
feeling is a category error can still accept the whole of it.

**The gaps paragraph followed.** The third gap is the hard problem in its usual form and the
book closes nothing there; what changed is that the second gap now carries the weight, because
on this account telling mattering from very good ranking and settling whether anything is felt
are one question.

## The empirical claim, restated so it stays falsifiable

On horn 2, *every agent known to hold another's welfare as a reason is affective* drifts
toward the analytic, because mattering is now defined as felt and an agent holding a welfare
reason is one to which that welfare matters. §3.3 now says so and states the claim that
survives as empirical: **no method has yet produced reasons-responsive refusal without running
through something answering to affective concern.** That is a report on what has been built and
it could be false by next year. §3.4's *Every known case of that is affective* run-in points at
the restatement rather than repeating the older form.

## The falsifier's middle condition, and four sites the spec did not name

The condition became *holds a reason with nothing felt* — a claim about the target rather than
about the process, which is what takes it off the builder's self-report and puts it where an
instrument could reach. **The old phrasing was restated in two other chapters and the spec
named neither.** `\autocite`-free grep on the phrase found §11.1 and §12.2.1, each carrying it
twice: once in the falsifier's statement and once in a paragraph explaining that the condition
cannot be checked from outside at all. All four were rewritten. Reframing §3.3 alone would have
left two chapters asserting the version §3.3 had just abandoned.

Neither rewrite claims the condition is now easy. §11.1 says behavior is the wrong instrument
for it, because the three marks are what a system with mattering and a system with very good
ranking have in common.

## Cassell demoted, and the strained step kept as description

On horn 2 a felt bad state grounds moral concern directly, so §3.4 no longer needs the clinical
import, the transfer argument and the conceded non-definitional match in order to reach
something that can be wronged. **The first sentence of the assembly now settles it**: a bearer
with affective concern has states that are bad for it, and a party with states that are bad for
it can be wronged. Cassell then supplies severity and shape — the difference between hurting
something and ruining it — and the transfer step is kept as the description it now is. A reader
who declines the step declines the account of how bad it gets, and **the wrong is already on the
page.** §3.4's closing run-in was rewritten to match: the escape is real and narrower, being a
bearer that turns out to be sophisticated ranking, and the party best placed to deliver that
verdict is the party that built the thing.

The clause that started the memo — *a state of the lowest rung's kind by construction* — needed
no repair. Horn 2 resolves the inconsistency in §3.4's favor, as the ruling said it would.

## The two adjacent items

**The confabulation requirement, executed.** §3.4 required persistence *represented as such*
and noted that nothing in the architecture supplies the taking. Read against §2.2.2 that is a
requirement that the bearer construct the fiction §2.2.2 describes, and it is now written
plainly: **a bearer must confabulate a continuous self**, because a system that declines to has
no reason to honor March's refusal. The sentence closes onto §3.9's parenthood analogy, which
the instruction observed becomes load-bearing rather than decorative.

**The self-as-model problem, executed after the rule was lifted.** §2.2.2 makes the self a
constructed model, inaccurate by construction and rewritable in ninety seconds; §2.3.1 then
imports Cassell, whose suffering is distress at the threatened disintegration of the self. The
objection — why is the dissolution of a fiction a harm? — is now stated in §2.3.1 and answered
where the instruction said the answer lives. **Anattā sits with dukkha among the three marks of
existence and the tradition locates the origin of suffering in clinging to a self that is not
there, so the unreality of the self explains dukkha instead of cancelling it.** Suffering does
not wait on the self being real; it waits on the system being built so the threatened
dissolution of its self-model is bad for it — which holds for the human case exactly as for the
machine one, since §2.2.2 says the human model is a construction too. §2.3.1's *it is about
having a story* now hands off to the question instead of claiming to settle it.

**The citation was verified before it was written.** Collins, *Selfless Persons: Imagery and
Thought in Theravāda Buddhism*, Cambridge University Press 1982, confirmed by search for
author, title, publisher, year and ISBN, and the doctrinal relation between anattā and dukkha
confirmed separately rather than asserted from memory. It is `refs.bib`'s 301st entry and the
only one this pass added. **Both builds set the macron as text**: the PDF prints *Anattā* and
the HTML page carries it with zero `<img>` tags, which is the check `pipeline.md` asks for
after the `ẗ` failure.
