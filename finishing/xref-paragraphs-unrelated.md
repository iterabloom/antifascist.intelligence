# Cross-references unrelated to the point of their paragraph

**The test applied, stated so it can be argued with.** A reference is *unrelated*
when the sentence carrying it introduces a subject other than what the paragraph
establishes, and deleting that sentence or clause leaves the paragraph's argument
intact and complete. *Unrelated is not the same as unhelpful* — see §6's entry,
which is topically unrelated and earns its place.

### `manuscript/sections/ch02/02_02.tex:26` — §2.2

**References in this paragraph:** §3.4, §4, §5
**At issue:** §4, §5

The paragraph establishes that a rule-following system cannot hold the rule against the party that writes its rules. The clause carrying these two — *the distinction is set out here because chapters 4 and 5 are given over to methods that deliver performance and competence* — explains the book's organization, not the claim. Section 3.4's reference in the same sentence IS the paragraph's conclusion and is in the related file.

> A system can have the first two without the third, and the obvious objection is that the first two
> are all anyone needs: a rule-following system could behave impeccably without feeling anything. It
> could. What it could not do is hold the rule against the party that writes its rules. Behavior that
> satisfies a standard is produced by whatever machinery is producing it, and when the authority
> controlling reward and instruction wants a different behavior, machinery of that kind supplies it.
> Section [3.4] is where that becomes a design constraint rather than an observation; the distinction
> is set out here because chapters [4] and [5] are given over to methods that deliver performance and
> competence, and it should be legible in each case which of the three a method actually reaches.

### `manuscript/sections/ch06/06.tex:27` — §6

**References in this paragraph:** §10.2
**At issue:** §10.2

The paragraph establishes that the accounting — benefit realized where the model is sold, cost borne within a few miles of the turbines — is what makes datacenter siting the same pattern rather than a separate environmental complaint. The closing sentence introduces a government's leverage, which is a different subject. **It is reciprocated**: §10.2 closes its own paragraph with *Chapter 6's opening takes up who bears the local cost of those decisions*. Topically unrelated and useful anyway.

> What makes this the same pattern and not a separate environmental complaint is the accounting. The
> benefit of the computation is realized wherever the model is sold. The cost is borne within a few
> miles of the turbines, by residents who are not parties to the transaction and whose consent was not
> among the inputs to the siting decision. A system can be calibrated across every demographic group
> its outputs touch and still have been assembled this way, which is why the fairness literature
> rarely reaches it: nothing in the model's behavior records it. Section [10.2] takes up the leverage
> this gives a government.
