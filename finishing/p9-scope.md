# P9 scope: the fourth editorial review

The fourth review arrived as unnumbered prose, 2026-08-24. Its thesis: **the
manuscript is optimized for a reader who dips in, at the cost of one reading
linearly.** That thesis is correct, and it identifies a structural signature of
how this book was made rather than a set of unrelated slips — see "Why the book
has this shape" below.

Every item was checked against the manuscript before any ruling was sought.
**One of the three did not survive checking in the form it was stated.** The
author's ruling on the checked set was "do it all" (D-037), which lifted D-007
for this pass on the terms D-029 and D-033 set for P7 and P8.

## The items

### P9-1 — Ekman appears twice, unlinked. **Linked, not merged.**

The review: "§2.3.1 and §7.2.1 both walk through Ekman's Fore study and its
collapse, with no cross-reference between them — the same argument delivered
twice as if for the first time. Merge or link those two explicitly."

**The merge premise is false.** The two sections share one clause and nothing
downstream:

| | §2.3.1 | §7.2.1 |
|---|---|---|
| Question | is emotion a natural kind? | is emotion readable from a face? |
| The Fore study | one clause, as setup | one clause, as the FER industry's foundation |
| Evidence for the collapse | Panksepp's seven systems, Barrett's imaging meta-analysis, Himba and Trobriand free-sorting | the in-group advantage, the 2019 APS panel, HireVue, EU AI Act Art. 5 |
| Citations | C0300–C0303 | C0561–C0565 |
| Conclusion | no tidy map from named emotions to brain regions | the six-emotion target may not exist as classified |

Neither section walks through the Fore study *or* its collapse; each spends a
clause on Fore and then collapses it with entirely different evidence. A merge
would drag EU regulation and HireVue into a chapter-2 section on what emotion
is, or Panksepp's electrode work into a chapter-7 section on open problems.

**What was real underneath the error:** a linear reader meets Paul Ekman twice,
roughly 40,000 words apart, introduced cold both times. The review's own
alternative — link them — is the right fix and is what was done. §2.3.1 gains a
forward pointer; §7.2.1 replaces its cold re-introduction with a back pointer
and one sentence on why its question is the narrower one.

### P9-2 — Pointer-only sections. **Openers rewritten; the demotion declined; one section declined outright.**

The review: "§4.2.2, §4.2.3, §4.6.2, §5.2.2 … Each could be a paragraph inside
its parent section."

Checked, and the diagnosis is off by one level. Each of the first three *opens*
by delegating, but each then does work that exists nowhere else in the book:

- **§4.2.2** (418 w) carries the invariance probe — hold a decision scenario fixed, vary only the emotional salience of the people affected, see whether the output moves and whether the movement tracks a better decision. That research design appears once in the manuscript.
- **§4.2.3** (371 w) carries the performed-versus-genuine moral emotion problem: a display of distress calibrated to move an evaluator is exactly what a system trained to respond to distress will reward.
- **§4.6.2** (341 w) carries the intrinsic/extrinsic mutual-check argument.

**§5.2.2 does not fit the complaint at all** (619 w; three cross-references
confined to one paragraph of nine; active learning, drift monitoring, and
adaptability-as-attack-surface are its own). Declined, with the count recorded
here rather than argued.

So the defect was real but confined to the **opening paragraph** of each of the
three, which spent itself inventorying what the section is not about before
reaching its question. All three openers now lead with the section's own
question and demote the cross-references to a trailing clause. Bodies retained.
Word change: §4.2.2 418→397, §4.2.3 371→364, §4.6.2 341→351; net −18.

### P9-3 — "Nobody is working on this" as an escape hatch. **Done, and the count was worse than stated.**

The review: "§7.1.5 and §7.1.6 both end with a version of 'as far as I can find,
nobody is working on this under that description.' That admission is valuable
once. Three or four times it starts reading as an escape hatch. One statement of
the general point plus a table of specific gaps would be stronger."

Confirmed, and the review undercounted. **§7.1.5 alone had three instances in
527 words** ("I could not verify any research that addresses", "as far as I can
find, nobody is working on it under that description", "has not, so far as I can
verify, been extended to this one"); §7.1.6 had two; §7.1.2 and §7.1.4 one each.
Chapter 7 is 5,743 words total.

Done as the review proposed. The chapter 7 opener gains a section, "What 'open'
claims here", stating the qualification once, and a list of the **eight** gaps,
each with the section that runs into it and the nearest existing work that stops
short of it. §7.1.5's three hedges are removed and §7.1.6's one is neutralized;
the gap claims themselves are unchanged and now inherit the qualification stated
once. §7.1.6's closing line — that only one of its two problems has an active
research program behind it — is kept as written, because it is the section's
argument and not a hedge.

**The one thing that had to survive the conversion, and did.** These hedges were
doing real epistemic work under D-009 and D-030: "I searched and found nothing
under this description" is a weaker and more honest claim than "this is an open
gap," and a table format invites the stronger reading. The opener therefore says
so in the text — a search is not a survey, a program can be underway under a
name I did not think to search, and the closer a gap sits to this book's own
framing the less likely anyone has posed it in these words.

**No new verification was performed for this pass.** Every row of the gap list
restates a gap the chapter already claimed; no live search was run. The list is
a reorganization of existing claims, not a fresh literature review, and a later
pass wanting to strengthen it would have to actually search.

### P9-4 — The persistence through-line (rides along under D-035)

The review said the persistence argument "is made twice." It is at §2.4.1
(established) and §2.4.4 (applied), and since P8 yesterday also in chapter 1's

§2.4.1. The through-line is now followable in both directions.

The reviewer should be told which reading was theirs, since only one of them
leaves anything further to do.

## Why the book has this shape

Worth recording, because it predicts where the next reviewer will find the same
thing. P1 folded 282 sections into 156, and D-013 gave each repeated argument
one home and reduced the rest to cross-references. Both were correct decisions
and both leave the same residue: a pointer where an argument used to be. The
review has detected that residue. Anywhere P1 folded heavily is a candidate for
the same complaint, and chapter 4 — which absorbed the most — is where two of
the three pointer-openers were.

## What was declined

- **Merging §2.3.1 and §7.2.1.** Premise false; see P9-1. The link was done instead.
- **Demoting §4.2.2, §4.2.3 and §4.6.2 to paragraphs inside their parents.** Each carries an argument that exists nowhere else; the openers were the defect.
- **§5.2.2 as a pointer section.** Does not fit the complaint on any measure.
