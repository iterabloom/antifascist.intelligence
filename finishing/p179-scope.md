# P179 — the recuperation test repaired

Author instruction: *has the channel ever changed an outcome* is **passed by
capital every day**. Capital does not refuse a demand; it decodes the flow and
adds an axiom. Replace with **what did the demand come back as** — market
segment, metric, certification, job title — and then yes it changed an outcome,
and yes it was recuperated, **and these are the same event**. Chapter~7 documents
the axiom being added and measures it with the wrong ruler.

## The defect

The old test was the book's first structural feature's only artifact, and it was
the one an institution of that kind passes without effort. **Every version of it
in the book failed the same way**, including the two that had been sharpened:
§7.3's *changed something the party collecting it did not want changed* and
§7.1's *has a guideline ever been changed because an annotator contested it*.
Adding an axiom is exactly a change the collecting party did not want, made in a
form it can live with. The refinement did not help.

## Six sites, all repaired

**§2.1.2, feature 1** gains the correction where the feature is defined: the tell
as previously stated invites the wrong question, an institution of this kind is
not in the business of refusing demands, and Deleuze and Guattari's account of the
capitalist axiomatic is what it is doing instead — the rule set is modified so the
demand can be met and nothing that necessitated the demand is settled. Then the
replacement, with its four observable returns.

**§2.1.2's artifact list**: *whether the dissent channel has ever changed an
outcome* becomes *what the demand came back as*.

**§2.1.2's Korematsu example** was the clearest casualty and is now the clearest
case. It used to end *ask the artifact question — has the channel ever changed an
outcome — and increasingly for the Roberts Court, the answer is no.* **That was
false on its face**: the 1944 decision was repudiated, which is an outcome
changing. What actually happened is the corrected question's textbook instance —
the demand was that this never be done again, **it came back as a formula of
repudiation, quotable, and the Court quoted it while permitting the thing again.**
The objection had become citable, and being citable was the outcome that changed.

**§7.1** stops asking whether a guideline was ever changed, since guidelines are
revised constantly, and asks what the contest came back as: a revision the
annotator can point to and argue with again, or a new category in somebody else's
taxonomy, a quality score they are now measured against, and a reviewer tier they
are not in.

**§7.3** carries the correction and the chapter's self-indictment. Asked the right
way, **the chapter's own two instances answer immediately**: annotators objecting
that a category was wrong got category revisions, guideline updates,
inter-annotator agreement statistics and an occupation that did not exist twenty
years ago; raters objecting that a comparison was malformed got rubric items, a
harmlessness axis, red-teaming as a function with a budget line, and a conference
track. Every one is an outcome that changed and every one is the demand returning
as an axiom the pipeline now runs on. **The chapter had been documenting the
axiom being added and measuring it with a ruler that reads the addition as
evidence of health.**

**§7.3's unsolved problem is narrowed, not closed.** It said a recuperated
pipeline and a merely mediocre one produce the same paperwork. Revision logs,
escalations and sustained-objection rates are indeed the same; **what an objection
came back as need not be**, because a mediocre pipeline returns a revision the
objector can argue with again and a recuperating one returns a metric the objector
is measured by. That is a difference in kind, read off documents the organization
wrote for its own reasons. It is not always legible, and the section says so.

**§7.4** keeps its list of the book's inconvenient arguments and demotes it: that
list answers the question a recuperating institution passes. **The corrected
question gets the harder answer.** The demand came back as a book — a manuscript
in an established genre, written with the industry's own model, addressed to a
readership the industry helped bring into being. Alignment is the job title,
safety is the market segment, the benchmark is the metric, the voluntary
commitment is the certification, **and every one of those was somebody's objection
first**. The old ending said the author is not positioned to judge whether his
objections are real; the new one keeps that and observes that the corrected
question does not need him to, since it asks what became of the demand, which is a
fact about the field rather than about his motives.

**Chapter~7's opener** and **the glossary's `Recuperation` entry** both carried the
old tell and now carry the new one.
