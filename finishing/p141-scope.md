# P141 — the third construction marked as objected to on different grounds, and kept on the list

An author's note: the third construction is the item most likely to be lost, and
the cause is structural. Four of the five fail to a custody objection; this one
fails to two of a different kind. Mark the difference where the objections are
given, and at the close remind the reader why this one survives to the untried
list.

## The claim checks out, with one refinement that mattered to the drafting

**The recurring objection is one party in four different roles**, which is why
*custody* is the right umbrella and why the count of four holds:

| construction | where | what the party does |
|---|---|---|
| 1, the maintained justification | `:12`, `:14` | trains the stability setting and writes the ordering |
| 2, precommitment | `:39` | selects the parties to the threshold scheme — named as *the custody objection* |
| 4, a duty on the operator | `:55` | is the enforcer, on the occasion it has been compelled |
| 5, uncertainty about the affected party | `:66` | chooses the principals, *the threshold scheme's difficulty again* |

**The refinement: construction 1 meets that objection too and is on the try-first
list anyway.** My first draft of the closing clause called custody *the failure
that disqualifies the rest*, and it would have been false — `:73` puts the
maintained justification first on the same list, `:12` and `:14` notwithstanding.
**The custody objection bounds a construction; it does not remove one.** The
sentence that went in says what these two objections are not, and claims nothing
about what the custody objection does to anybody else.

## The two edits

**At `:48`, where the objections are given.**

> Neither of these is the custody objection the other constructions meet. Both
> are limits on what the arrangement secures, and neither hands it to the party
> it was built against.

**At `:73`, where the untried constructions are named.**

> The second is on that list although this section bounded it twice: an
> arrangement that produces no bearer is what Replacement asks be tried before
> one is built, and the independence it has not secured is untested, which is the
> condition both constructions are in.

## The point the second edit turns on

**`:48`'s second objection is the reason the construction qualifies, not a reason
against it.** *Composing operational refusers produces an arrangement of
operational refusers … That is defense in depth against evasion, and it is not a
bearer.* Read as a verdict, that sinks it. Read against `:85` — Replacement asks
*whether something that cannot be wronged would do the work* — **producing no
bearer is the qualification.** The closing clause says so, twelve lines before
`:85` develops it.

The first objection is left as what it is: an assumption the arrangement does not
secure, which `:73` already describes for both constructions as untried. **No
claim is made that a design could secure it.** `:39`'s *adverse in interest*
design answer is stated about the threshold scheme, and transferring it to this
construction would be my inference and not the section's.
