# P118 — the packaging caught up with the concession: §12.3's last sentence and the README blurb

**The instruction.** A critique put to the agent for investigation: the book leads with its most
contestable claim rather than its most defensible one, §3.10 having conceded that the specification
survives if the affect induction fails and that what goes is *the price, the patienthood conclusion*.
Investigation first, then the author ruled all three proposed changes.

## What the investigation found

**The critique's general characterization does not hold, and the reason is on the record.** D-196
(P98) is this same objection, put by the author in his own words — *a prior about where to CONSIDER
spending engineering effort AFTER YOU HAVE TRIED AND FAILED THE NON-AFFECT ROUTE* — and four rewrites
executed it. D-196 names five sites that already carried the ordering: §3.3, §3.10, §9.3.2, §12.2.1
and §12.3. The leading positions carry it too. Chapter 1 hedges in the same sentence as the claim
(*Hardly a proof, but that argument forms my prior belief about where to build, with the cheaper
routes owed their attempt*). The chapter 3 opener calls affect *the question to investigate*. §3.4's
patienthood passage opens on *Suppose the two constructions have been tried and have failed*. §3.10
ends by naming its own weak point: *The middle clause is the one to attack*. And the title describes
the specification, which the critique concedes.

**One of its quotations is doing work it was not written for.** *Close to worthless as evidence*
modifies **the absence of a machine instance** — the negative half of the induction, the author
saying that his evidence machines *cannot* do it without affect is worthless. It is not a verdict on
the premise the patienthood conclusion runs through. The book's own verdict is *an induction over one
species* with three named cuts, which is harsher in substance and different in shape.

**Where it lands is two sentences, and they are the first and last a reader meets.** That is what was
executed.

## §12.3's closing sentence — the book's last words of argument

**The paragraph two above it hedges and the closing sentence did not.** §12.3 already said *Section
3.3 puts two untried constructions ahead of it, and a deployment that goes straight to the bearer
owes an account of what it tried first.* Then it closed:

> the only constraint that reaches the compelled occasion is one held by a party the compellers
> cannot instruct, and building such a party means building something that can be wronged

Two universal claims, unhedged, in the position a reader remembers. **P98 repaired §12.3's body and
left its ending**, which is why D-196 could name §12.3 among the five sites that carried the ordering
and the ending could still contradict it.

**P117 sharpened the conflict rather than creating it.** The §3.4 repair established that a substitute
built without mattering is not a patient, which is exactly what *means* here denies.

**The repair keeps the ending and restores the order inside the sentence.** The first half stands —
the book does hold that reaching the compelled occasion needs a party inside the system the
compellers cannot instruct, and §3.3's operator-duty construction fails on precisely that occasion.
What was wrong was the second half. The sentence now runs in three parts: the constraint has to be
held by such a party; two constructions that would supply one with no subject in it are untried
rather than failed; and the route that has an instance behind it means building something that can be
wronged. The last clause — *and then deciding what is owed to it before anybody can tell you* — is
unchanged, because it was the good half of the ending.

## The README's blurb — two defects, one of them factual

**It said three ways where the book says four**, in at least twelve places including chapter 1, §3.1,
§3.5, §3.9, §3.10, §9.1.5 and §9.3.2. The blurb paragraph was last edited by the author on
2026-08-31 at 21:49; P81 rebuilt the introduction 38 minutes later at 22:27. **The blurb is stale
against the book it describes**, whatever it was when written — the archaeology was not pushed
further than that, and no attempt was made to establish whether three was correct at the time.

**It stated the affect claim as *requires*, with chapter 1's hedge absent.** It now carries chapter
1's own formulation — *runs through* rather than *requires*, followed by *Hardly a proof — it is the
prior I work from about where to build, and the cheaper routes are owed their attempt first.*

## What was not done

- **No other site was changed on the emphasis question.** The sweep for unhedged statements of the
  affect claim returned seven, and six of them are hedged in place or conditioned by their context:
  §3.4's *only demonstrated route* sits downstream of the *Suppose* conditional, §3.10's *only way to
  have it* carries *that anybody has an instance of* and is followed by *The middle clause is the one
  to attack*. §12.3's was the one that stood alone.
- **Nobody has read §12.3 end to end since the change**, and it is the section the book ends on.
- **The critique's misattribution was not answered in the book.** Nothing was added to §3.3 to guard
  the *close to worthless* sentence against being quoted as a verdict on the whole induction; whether
  it needs guarding is a question for the author, not something this pass settled.
