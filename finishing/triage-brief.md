# Triage brief — how to rule a row

Read this before ruling. It encodes the author's decisions and the pattern of
their choices so far, so that rulings made by different hands come out
consistent.

## The author's disposition, inferred from eleven decisions

- **Revise, not rewrite (D-007).** The substance stands. A section is not cut for being ordinary. Fates are Keep / Revise / Merge / Cut / Fold / Move / Fill.
- **They prefer filling to cutting.** Given empty headings §9.1 and §9.2 and the explicit option to cut, they chose *fill* for both. Default to `fill` for a heading with no body, not `cut`.
- **They are intellectually unafraid.** Offered the empathy critique framed constructively or unframed, they took it unframed and let their own chapter thesis absorb the hit. Do not protect the book from its own arguments.
- **They prefer directness to defensive vagueness.** They relaxed the named-persons rule rather than accept an anonymised citation, and they judged the quoted lyrics fair use rather than seek permissions.
- **They like concrete specificity, cultural allusion and voice.** They added a *Westworld* line to close a technical section. Sections that are pure abstraction with no example are the weakest in the book and the best candidates for compression.
- **Their edits all cut scaffolding**: the sentence announcing what the next sentence will do, the "not X, it is Y" frame, the superlative that cannot be defended. A section that is mostly scaffolding is a `merge` or a heavy `revise`, not a `keep`.
- **Anti-authoritarianism is the book's spine**, not a theme among others. Sections carrying that argument earn their place; sections restating it for the ninth time do not.

## A correction to an earlier claim

An earlier report of mine said the book has no AI-safety vocabulary — no
alignment problem, no boxing, no takeoff. **That was wrong**, and the chapter 4-5
ruling caught it. Measured over the manuscript: "value alignment" 48 times
across eight chapters, "AI safety" 31, "the alignment problem" 4 (and §5.3.1 is
titled for it), "existential risk" 6, "paperclip" 3, "kill switch" and
"interruptibility" once each. Only "boxing", "takeoff" and "recursive
self-improvement" are genuinely absent.

The practical consequence: do not treat the safety vocabulary as a gap to be
filled, because it is mostly already there. §5.3 is the chapter that carries it.

## What to write

For each row: the `fate`, a `target` where the fate needs one (a section number,
or a word count for a section being compressed), and a one-line `why` in plain
language. Rule every row assigned to you. Where you are genuinely unsure, rule
`revise` — it is the reversible option — and say so in the `why`.
