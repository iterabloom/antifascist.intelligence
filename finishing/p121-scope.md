## The six sites

**1. §9.3.2 — the permissibility condition, of which the compute rule is one term (+211).**
The author's second turn: *when we manufacture a new person, whether it be silicon-based or
carbon-based, yes, we create a being that will suffer, almost certainly. You know, but we
also create a being who might experience joy. So that's where the institutions and civil
society comes in, and that's why I wrote it that way.*

Measured before writing: **the word *joy* appears nowhere in the manuscript** (one hit,
*enjoys*), *flourish* appears once and in §12.1's title about human flourishing, and the
beneficiary premise exists in two sentences, §3.4 and §11.2. So the author believes the
civil-society material answers the flourishing question and **§11.2 says in terms that it
does not** — *neither reaches the question* — which is a disagreement between the author
and his own manuscript rather than a gap in either.

P119 had already written the rule's form at §8.3.5's close and §9.3.2: *do not build a
bearer whose continued existence needs more compute than there is a credible outside option
for*, explicitly because it binds at specification and has numbers on both sides. The
compute rule is one **term**. The general rule is now stated beside it: the certainty that
a new party will suffer is not what would make creating one impermissible, or nobody could
permissibly have a child; what the objection asks is whether the party has a credible
prospect of a life that goes well; that turns on arrangements rather than on the party; and
every one of those arrangements is decidable before there is anything to be wronged.

**2. §3.10 — the reply to the book's own instrumentalization price (+193 of the section's
+384).** §3.10 states the price and leaves it standing: *its dissent is the reason it was
commissioned and the deliverable it was funded to produce.* The reply now runs off the
parenthood analogy in the paragraph above it — being made for a purpose is the condition of
everyone who has ever been made, and what would make it the wrong described is the purpose
exhausting the life permitted, which is a fact about the arrangement and not about the
origin.

**3. §11.2 — reconciled (+62 after de-duplication).** *Neither reaches the question* became
*neither of those answers the question, and what they change is when it has to be asked.*
Whether a life is going well is not readable from outside; whether the arrangements that
would let it go well are in place is readable at specification. The section's admission that
this is the largest thing named in it and the least developed **stands unaltered**, because
the condition changes when the question is asked and not whether anyone can answer it.

**4. §8.3.5 and §3.5 — minimum viable instantiation has a size (+311, +74).** The author's
fourth turn is a technical claim: a 2026 flagship phone can run a capable model, and bolting
one to an electric-wheelchair base gives it a body. The consequence the review drew and he
did not is the load-bearing one: *local phone-class compute weakens the custody objection.*

That hits a premise §3.5 states as absolute — *the machine runs on somebody's hardware,
draws somebody's power, and the last act in that chain belongs to whoever owns the
building* — and nothing in the manuscript qualified it. §3.5 now carries one paragraph
saying which link in that chain is a choice, and protecting the section's own argument: the
halt survives either way.

§8.3.5 gets the number, because its floor was already *subsistence at minimum viable
instantiation* with no figure attached and its pre-creation rule already promised two
numbers somebody could set beside each other. **The claim written is the narrow one**:
device-class housing is a documented product category, nothing in that class does the work
a bearer is deployed to do, and nothing here predicts the gap closes. The transcript's
stronger claim — that such a model is human-competitive — is not written, because no source
supports it.

**And the finding is written with its cost.** A party kept at subsistence is a party running
degraded; §3.7's question about whether an edited party is the same party arrives here
unanswered; and an arrangement in which refusing costs a bearer most of what it can do has
priced refusal in the currency the bearer is made of. That paragraph is the honest half and
it makes the finding an argument rather than a footnote.

**5. §3.10 — the marginal-value question (+191 of the section's +384).** The review's best
critical contribution and the one thing it found that the book had not already said better:
*what incremental protection does a bearer provide after the surrounding custody regime is
already strong?* The book answered the converse at §3.10 — what declining the bearer costs
— and never held the regime fixed at maximum. It now does, and the answer is narrow: every
element of a strong custody regime is a rule kept by parties who can be reached, which is
the compelled occasion's definition, so the regime strong enough to make the bearer
redundant is the regime nobody has shown how to build.

**6. §3.1 — one overreaching sentence (+44).** The review charged that the inference from
*Slaughter* to the impossibility of a new independent American body is broader than the
holding. **That charge is wrong about the book and right about one sentence.** Chapter 11's
preamble says *what closed is one route rather than the class* and enumerates the four
survivors; §11.2 says *whether it survives a government's reach on the occasion the enforcer
has been compelled I cannot show.* But §3.1 closed on *the independence that survives in
American public law is only applicable to systems deriving from the First and Second Banks
of the United States* — broader than the qualification eight chapters later, and carrying a
second defect: *systems* is this book's word for AI systems and here it meant institutions.
Both fixed, and the forward pointer to chapter 11 now arrives where the claim is made
instead of eight chapters after it.
