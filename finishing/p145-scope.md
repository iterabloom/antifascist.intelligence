# P145 — the routes that bear on the middle conjunct, named where the conjunct is

Author note: three passages bear on the conjunct no behavioral instrument
reaches, they are far apart, and §3.3 should list them where it first singles the
conjunct out.

## §3.3 was not the blank the note describes, and this changes the edit

The note says the section *says nothing reaches it and leaves the reader to
discover eight chapters later that two things partly do*. **`03_03.tex:79`
already carried a two-item list**, and has since D-130:

> The tamper-resistance apparatus is where that would be attacked, and a further
> question is whether formation can be read off the artifact where provenance
> cannot be established. Neither is close to settled. Both are questions somebody
> can work on.

**The first item is contradicted in three other places.** `11_01.tex:22`:
*Behavior is the wrong instrument for the middle condition, because the three
marks are what a system with mattering and a system with very good ranking have
in common.* `12_02_01.tex:13`: a refusal that survives attack *has passed a
tamper-resistance test … and leaves this one open.* `03_02.tex:19`: *A system that
holds a reason and a system that has learned what holding a reason sounds like
emit the same sentences, so no inspection settles the question.* The
tamper-resistance apparatus is behavioral, so §3.3 was offering as a route to the
middle conjunct the one instrument the book says cannot reach it.

**So this pass is a replacement and not an addition**, which is the departure from
the instruction worth stating first. The list the note asked for went in where the
wrong list was.

## The three passages are two routes

- **§3.2's relocation** is its own route: defining mattering as minimally felt
  makes the open question a fact about a particular artifact, which
  interpretability can bear on. Stated twice in §3.2, at `:19` and `:31`.
- **§11.1 and §11.2 are one route at two levels.** `11_01.tex:22` puts the
  question — whether formation can be read off an artifact. `11_02.tex:60` is the
  experiment: *take models whose training is documented, and ask whether a reader
  who does not know their provenance can recover it from them.* **The book already
  pairs them that way**, at `12_02_01.tex:15`: *the remaining question is whether
  formation can be read off the artifact instead, and section~\ref{sec:11.2}
  states the one experiment that would answer it.*

**Written as three, the passage would tell a reader three things are being done
about the middle conjunct when two are.** It is written as two, with the second
carrying both addresses.

## The edit

`:79`'s two sentences replaced by three, in the same position:

> Two routes bear on that. The first is what the previous section bought by
> defining mattering as minimally felt: the open question becomes a fact about one
> system's machinery, which is the kind of thing interpretability reads and output
> never shows. The second is whether formation can be read off the artifact where
> provenance cannot be established — section~\ref{sec:11.1} is where that question
> is put, and section~\ref{sec:11.2} where it becomes an experiment somebody could
> run.

*Neither is close to settled. Both are questions somebody can work on* survive
unchanged and are now true of the two routes that are actually routes.

**§3.2 is named and not numbered**, being the section immediately before this one;
*the previous section* is the parallel of §3.3's own opening *the next section*.

## Why `:83` did not have to change

`:83` says *The middle conjunct is the one nothing reaches*, which now sits four
lines after a sentence naming two routes that bear on it. **It glosses itself in
its own next clause** — *the conjunct no instrument settles* — and that is
compatible with two questions somebody can work on, neither close to settled.
`:81`'s *Two of them are measurable now* is about the other two conjuncts and is
unaffected. **`:79` was the only site carrying the defect.**
