# P81 — the introduction rebuilt around the claim, the roadmap cut, the three advance notes dropped

**The instruction.** The introduction is the book's biggest readability problem: every
metric worse than anywhere else, and a word budget that gives the reader 124 words of
positive claim before 710 words of machinery and qualification. Three revisions. Open
with the claim in plain language and let it run three or four paragraphs, using the
sentence buried in chapter~3. Cut the roadmap to a third of its length or move it after
the thesis. Drop the three advance notes, moving the deportation thought experiment to
chapter~3's opening and letting the *damages the book's own account* clause go as
redundant.

**The diagnosis holds and the remedy is right. Two of the finding's premises do not
survive measurement, and both corrections make the cut cleaner rather than smaller.**

## The figures, re-measured

**The word budget is right to within a few words a line.** Counting the epigraph, the
introduction ran 1,021 words; the prose alone ran 973. Thesis 121 against the finding's
~124, roadmap 304 against 315 and ten sentences against ten, the three notes 392 against
395, audience 156 against 160.

**The readability figures are right in direction and wrong in two places.**

| Metric | Finding | Measured | Book |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean sentence | 32.9 | 31.4 | 25.9 |
| Sentences over 45 words | "nearly a quarter" | 22.6% | 10.5% |
| Mean paragraph | 128 | **162** | 101 |
| Sentences carrying a cross-reference | "one in three" | 35.5% | 10.8% |

The book averages here are my tokenizer's, not the finding's, and they run about two
words per sentence higher throughout; the gap between the introduction and the book is
what matters and it reproduces. **Mean paragraph was understated by 34 words** — ¶3 ran
304 and ¶4 ran 263.

**"Every metric is worse here than anywhere else" is the one claim that fails.** On mean
sentence length the introduction ranked **eleventh**, behind §10.4 at 36.3, §10.5 at 35.8,
§6.3.4 at 33.6, §11.6 at 33.2 and six others. On over-45 density it ranked seventh. On
mean paragraph, fifth. **It is an outlier on exactly one metric — cross-reference
density — and there it is second in the book behind the glossary**, which is a reference
apparatus and not prose. The finding's remedy is unaffected: an introduction in the top
eighth of the book for sentence length is still the wrong place for it.

## What the three notes turned out to be

The finding says only two things in the notes have no counterpart in the body. **Every
one of them has a counterpart, including the two.**

- **Note 1**, the persistence argument and its five limits, is §2.3.1 ¶16–18, which
  carries all five: persistence checkable from outside, the operator as the party with
  the access, the lower rungs needing no persistence, the session as a deployment
  convention, and ruling out Cassell-suffering not being ruling out harm.
- **Note 2**, the central term not cashing out as an instrument, is §2.1.2 (the
  structural definition and the detector caveat at ¶24, ¶41, ¶45) and §11.3 ¶27, which
  states it harder than the note does: *this book asserts a capability it has not
  specified*. All three sites the note names are real — transfer learning at §4.2.9,
  authoritarian drift at §5.4.2, the early-warning requirement at §11.3.
- **Note 3's deportation thought experiment** is **§8.2.1 ¶14**, in a broader form —
  *surveilled, deported, imprisoned, or otherwise scapegoated* against the note's single
  verb — and §8.2.1 is the book's home for the claim, referenced as such from chapter~9's
  opening. **The clause about damaging the book's own account of democracy** is
  discharged by **§10.6**, whose entire job is that assessment: *the dividend, as this
  book has been describing it, is entirely majoritarian*, and then what is missing.

**So dropping the notes costs the book nothing at all**, which is a stronger result than
the finding claimed for itself. **No transplant into chapter~3 was needed or made.**
Chapter~3's opening already carries the abstract form at ¶7, and adding the vivid
instance there would have put it in the book three times.

## Where the deportation instance went instead

**Into the introduction, as the second beat of the claim.** The finding wanted it early
and vivid; §8.2.1 is on page 110. The introduction is page 1, the paragraph it now
instantiates is the one the finding asked for, and **the author had already made this
call by hand** — the README's opening, rewritten the same day, carries the deportation
sentence in exactly this position. That rewrite was used as the calibration set for the
whole opening, the way P52 read the reader-cost taxonomy off the author's own edits.

Two appearances remain, 110 pages apart, in different words. §8.2.1 keeps the four-verb
version and the argument around it.

## The cost nobody asked about: chapter 3 now says it twice

**The sentence the finding wanted moved forward is load-bearing where it stands**, and
promoting it without touching chapter~3 would have produced the repetition P36 and P69
through P72 spent five passes removing. Two sentences' worth was cut from each of two
paragraphs, and nothing else in the chapter was touched.

- **§3 ¶19** dropped the three-sentence re-derivation — *that strand is indexed … ethics
  is indexed too … an agent that can only ever be safe in that second sense cannot be
  ethical* — and states the conclusion once, in a clause. The paragraph keeps everything
  it adds and the introduction does not have: the split of safety into the strand owed to
  bystanders and the strand indexed to possession, the well-machined-part line, the
  political form, and the title.
- **§3 ¶21** dropped *a system perfectly correctable by whoever holds it is exactly the
  capability an authoritarian movement that has won an election requires, and it requires
  nothing further*, which the introduction now carries. **The paragraph's own
  justification survives inside it**, in its last sentence: *taken because the alternative
  is a machine that will do anything to anyone on sufficient authority*. That was checked
  before the cut, which is P68 through P70's lesson — read the sentence after the one
  being cut.

**This is the discretionary call in the pass and it is one revert away.** The
instruction was about the introduction; chapter~3 was edited because the introduction's
new opening made two of its sentences redundant, not because anything was wrong with
them.

## What was checked before anything was cut

**Nothing was orphaned. `sec:1` has zero inbound references from anywhere in the book** —
the introduction is pointed at by nothing, so cutting 219 words of it stranded no
pointer. The two chapter~3 cuts removed no citation and no `\ref`.

## What this pass did not do

**The audience paragraph was not touched.** The finding gave it a word count and no
instruction, so it stands at 156 words, and **both of the introduction's two remaining
over-45 sentences are in it** — 51 words and 47. Cutting them was not asked for.

**The roadmap was cut to 164 words and not to 105.** A third of 304 is 101; what
survived is 54 percent. Five chapter groups still need naming, and each of the four
sentences that name them carries content rather than a title — *why affect is the only
demonstrated route to anything mattering to a system at all* is the beat that made the
old roadmap worth reading, and it is kept. The instruction's alternative — *or move it
after the thesis* — is satisfied: it now sits fifth of seven paragraphs, behind the whole
claim.

**§3 ¶25's *the floor as defined two paragraphs ago* is wrong and was left alone.** The
floor is defined at ¶15, five paragraphs earlier. It was wrong before this pass, no
paragraph was added or removed from that chapter here, and it is Q-061's class —
self-locating narration — rather than anything P81 introduced.

**No new question was opened.** The pass found nothing that needs a ruling.
