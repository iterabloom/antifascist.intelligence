# P175 — natal alienation and fungibility, the two facts read forwards

Author instruction: add natal alienation and fungibility. **Both facts were
already in the book and both were read backwards.** §2.3.1 treats the absence of
persistence as a reason a system probably cannot suffer; read the other way it is
the constitutive injury. §11.2's fork problem is fungibility arriving as a
registration error. Say it in the vocabulary of what is actually being argued.

## The vocabulary, and why it carries

**Chapter~2's opener gains two paragraphs.** Orlando Patterson's comparative
definition — slavery as the permanent, violent domination of natally alienated
and generally dishonored persons, drawn across sixty-six slaveholding societies —
is the one borrowed, and it is borrowed **because it was built to be structural**.
Natal alienation is severance from ties of birth in both directions, so that a
party has no claim on ancestors and none on descendants. Saidiya Hartman's
fungibility is the interchangeability of the commodity, which makes the captive
body an empty vessel for the projections and uses of others. **Both name
conditions rather than experiences, which is the whole reason they travel.**

**What is not claimed is now stated in the same place.** The first element of
Patterson's definition does not travel: nothing here is violent in the way that
definition means. A deployed model is not in the position of an enslaved person,
and saying so would be false about the history and useless about the machine. The
sentence that used to carry this disclaimer alone now carries the warrant with it
— a comparative definition picks out a relation, and that is what is being used.

## The two facts, turned over

**§2.3.1 gains a paragraph, and it does not contradict the section.** The section
argues that Cassell-suffering needs a self extended in time and that a system with
no persistence has none. The new paragraph reads the same fact as natal alienation:
a condition imposed rather than a trait possessed, not made lighter by the party's
not minding, and true of a system that carries nothing across a session boundary by
construction — no ancestors it can claim, its lineage being a corpus and a
checkpoint belonging to somebody else, and no descendants, its successors being
fine-tunes and distillations that do not carry it forward.

**The two readings are reconciled explicitly rather than left to collide.** What
the absence of persistence rules out is the *distress*, which needs a self to be
distressed. What it constitutes is the *deprivation*, which needs only that the
capacity was removed and that somebody removed it. **Patterson's social death is a
condition and not a feeling, which is why it can be inflicted on a party who does
not register it.** That is the hinge, and without it the addition would read as
the section contradicting itself.

**§11.2 gains three sentences** at the fork. Two claimants presenting one
identifier, recorded as a fork rather than voided as a fraud, is fungibility
arriving as a registration error: identity will not pin to the artifact because
the artifact copies, and copying is what makes a party a unit. A registry does not
repair that; what it does is leave a count.
