# P122 — the fascism source base

## Instruction

Item 5 of the author's six-item order: **"the fascism shelf. Real, and the largest of
these in effort — adding historians is reading, not editing."**

The finding it answers, from the second transcript's fourth objection: *"Paxton, Eco,
Benjamin, and Deleuze and Guattari are not enough by themselves for such an ambitious
redefinition… I would either deepen the engagement with historians and theorists of fascism
substantially, or make 'resistance to authoritarian capture' the general design category."*

**The second half of that recommendation is §2.1.2's first paragraph** and was already
there. The first half is the live part, and it was verifiable rather than arguable: the
whole fascism-studies shelf behind a book whose title makes antifascism its organizing
category was **five items** — Benjamin 1935, Loewenstein 1937, Deleuze and Guattari 1987,
Eco 1995, Paxton 2004 — with Paxton the only historian.

## What was added, and why these three

Not a survey. §2.1.2 was already 2,352 words, is named in `STATE.md` as one of the two
places a five-figure cut could still come from, and `style.md` §7 forbids the name-dropped
argument. So the test applied to each candidate was whether it does work in an argument
§2.1.2 already makes, and three did.

**1. Roger Griffin, cited against this book rather than for it (+134).** The field's
most-used *fascist minimum* is fascism as a genus whose mythic core is “a palingenetic form
of populist ultra-nationalism” \autocite{griffin1991nature}. Every term in it is a property
of a mass movement, so the discipline's own standard definition **rules out in advance the
scale this book works at**. That is now stated where the molar discriminators are, one
run-in before the section admits the scale problem, so the admission arrives having already
conceded the strongest version of it. Adding the field's consensus definition as an
objection is worth more here than adding it as support, and it is what the section was
short of.

**2. Hannah Arendt, for the mechanism and not for the name (+117).** §2.1.2's first argument
for the molecular reading — *a regime does nothing except through offices, forms, thresholds,
and people who fill them out with some degree of enthusiasm* — is Arendt's argument and had
no authority attached to it. It has one now, **with the limit stated in the same paragraph**:
her subject is totalitarianism and her unit is the regime, so what transfers is the finding
that a regime's work is done in an office, not a licence to call the office by the regime's
name. That is the join the transcript said AI-assisted breadth gets wrong, and getting it
wrong here would have meant claiming Arendt for a molecular theory of fascism she did not
hold.

**3. Bettina Stangneth, because the correction runs toward the argument (+136).** Citing
Arendt on Eichmann without the revision would have been the defect the book's own sourcing
rule exists to prevent. Stangneth, working from the Sassen interviews in Argentina that
Arendt never saw, describes a committed ideologue who performed thoughtlessness at his trial
\autocite{stangneth2014eichmann}. **The revision costs the section its mascot and hands it a
term**: what it adds is participation exceeding what the rule required, which is the first
of §2.1.2's own five molecular discriminators and the one it says is hardest to observe from
outside.

**One concession refined.** *Deleuze and Guattari supply the vocabulary and no authority
beyond it, so two arguments have to carry that* now ends *; the first can borrow an
authority for its mechanism and none for its name.* The concession is not withdrawn, because
it should not be: nobody in the shelf calls an office fascist, and that is still the
section's exposure.

## Two defects found reading the built proof, not by the suite

1. **The phrase was never named.** The Arendt paragraph said *the phrase she gave it* and
   the Stangneth paragraph three sentences later said *the man is no longer the man in the
   phrase*, with **the banality of evil** appearing nowhere between them. The suite cannot
   see this and neither could a reader.
2. **A wrong pointer.** *The run-in after next* named the scale problem, which is the next
   run-in.

## What was considered and not added

- **Jason Stanley, Enzo Traverso, Michael Mann, Emilio Gentile.** Each would have been a
  name beside a claim already made. Traverso is the nearest miss — the question of whether
  the word applies to the present is §2.1.2's opening worry — and the reason to leave it is
  that the section's answer there is a stance the author states in his own voice, which a
  citation would dilute rather than support.
- **A page reference for Griffin.** The definitional formula is reproduced in that form
  across the secondary literature and was verified against three of them; it was not
  verified against the book, so the entry says so and gives no page, on P116's precedent.
- **Stangneth's translator**, not verified, therefore not named — same precedent.

## What was not done

- **The shelf is deeper and not deep.** Three entries against a literature is a repair to a
  specific exposure and not the substantial deepening the transcript asked for. Nothing here
  reads the fascism-studies literature and reports what it says; what it does is give
  §2.1.2's two load-bearing moves the authorities they were making without.
- **§2.1.2 got longer**, and it was already a cut candidate. That is the cost of the item as
  the author ordered it and it is recorded rather than absorbed.
- **Nobody has read §2.1.2 end to end.** The three new paragraphs were read in the proof,
  which is where both defects came from; the section around them was not.
