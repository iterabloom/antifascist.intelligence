## What this instruction is answering

P29 (D-090) ran an earlier version of the same instruction and closed by leaving
three things with the author, in its own words:

- **"Moving chapter 3 earlier was not taken** — it would reverse D-043's
  promotion — and is left with the author."
- **"Sections, not removed.** Chapter 2 has three subsections thin enough after
  the cut to consider folding, and chapters 4 and 5 have four more. … Not taken,
  on the ground that the instruction asked for a cut and not a restructure.
  Available on request."
- **"Why 13.5 and not 20.** … Reaching 20 percent means cutting evidence … **Left
  with the author, not taken.**"

This instruction picks up all three. So the pass does what P29 declined: it folds
the thin subsections (the merges), and it goes past P29's cut — while stopping
where the next cut would be a cut to evidence, which is stated below rather than
made silently.

## How "earlier" was read, and why

Chapter 3 is **not relocated**. Two things settle that. Its own opening stages
the floor as a gap chapter 2 left — *"section 2.1.1 has already dealt with the
family of ethical theories that puts unconditional constraints first, and dealt
with it briefly"* — and sections 3.2, 3.3 and 3.4 lean on section 2.3's
vocabulary and section 2.4.1's ladder as already established. And the
instruction's own second clause, *distill Chapter 2 to the premises Chapter 3
actually needs*, presupposes that chapter 2 still runs first: a chapter supplies
premises to the argument that follows it.

So "earlier" is delivered in pages. **Chapter 3 begins on page 23 of 182, where
it began on page 27 of 189** — four pages earlier, and 12.6 percent into the book
against 14.3. Everything after it moves seven pages earlier.

## What was done

### Chapter 2 — 13,121 → 10,675 words (−18.6%), 23 → 15 sections

Eight subsections merged away:

- **2.1.2 and 2.1.3 into 2.1.1**, retitled *Six Ethical Frameworks, and Where
  Each One Runs Out*. The six-framework survey is compressed to the defeat in
  each case; what survives of 2.1.2 is the paragraph that matters — the ethics
  moves into the stipulation when a principle is compiled into a metric — and
  what survives of 2.1.3 is the UDHR paragraph, which is a floor argument
  arriving 20 pages before the floor chapter.
- **2.2.3 into 2.2.2**, retitled *Theory of Mind, and Whether Empathy Is the
  Target*. Bloom and rational compassion are the payload and are kept; the
  applications roll and the theory-of-mind opener are compressed.
- **2.3.2 into 2.3.1**, retitled *What Emotion Is, and What a System Reads*. The
  four-component emotional-intelligence inventory is gone; the Ekman/Barrett
  dispute is kept whole because section 11.8 is built on it by name.
- **2.4.2, 2.4.3, 2.4.5, 2.4.6 and 2.4.7 into one new 2.4.3**, *What Oversight
  Would Have to Cover*, 1,692 words to 926. This is the largest single change
  in the chapter and the most defensible: none of the five is a premise chapter 3
  needs, all five are research-ethics apparatus, and six inbound references from
  chapters 6, 9 and 11 name specific claims that the merged section still makes —
  each was read and checked, not assumed.

Section 2.3.4 (now 2.3.3) is **untouched**. It is the evidence chapter 3 rests
on, and section 3.4 says so in one sentence: *"Section 2.3.3 is the evidence."*

### Chapter 4 — 8,091 → 7,301 words (−9.8%), 18 → 17 sections

**Section 4.2 was not compressed, and the reason is a finding.** P33 rebuilt it
eight days ago as the production pipeline in the order its stages run; all nine
subsections argue directly at chapter 3's question and none of it is survey. It
is 3,943 of the chapter's 7,301 words — 54 percent — so more than half of chapter
4 was outside what the instruction names.

What was cut is section 4.1: 4.1.2's eight run-in heads compressed to six,
2,049 words to 1,592, with the plasticity, emotion and integration heads folded
into one; 4.1.3's developmental-trajectory run-in reduced to a sentence; and
**4.3.2 merged into 4.3.1**, retitled *Play, Curiosity, and a Disposition Nobody
Wrote Down*.

### Chapter 5 — 11,198 → 9,790 words (−12.6%), 29 → 23 sections

Six merges, each a pair that split one subject across two headings: 5.1.3 into
5.1.2 (apprenticeship is where social learning goes), 5.2.3 into 5.2.2, 5.4.2
into 5.4.1 (the ring apparatus described twice, once abstractly and once
concretely), 5.5.2 into 5.5.1, 5.6.2 into 5.6.1, and 5.7.2 into 5.7.1, whose
Minecraft and Among Us proposals — which the section itself called a proposal

Seven section openers were cut back. Compressions elsewhere took the
three-sources roll out of 5.3.3, the applications roll out of 5.1.4, and the
deployed-tools pair out of 5.4.3, which cost the one citation this pass orphans.

### What was declined

- **Merging section 5.7 with section 4.3.** D-020 ruled the two keep their play
  material separately, cross-referenced rather than merged, against the 2023
  reviews' advice. Not revisited.
- **Cutting section 5.4's ecological-systems apparatus.** D-020 confirmed it
  "earns its place and stands whole." Compressed, not cut.
- **Cutting the evidence.** The inattentional-blindness studies, the
  predictive-coding account, the Kohlberg box, the trolley literature, the
  Ekman/Barrett dispute, Singer and Klimecki's training study, and section
  2.3.4 entire are what make the claims in these chapters believable rather than
  asserted. P29 named this limit and it still holds. **Chapters 4 and 5 come to
  −11.4 percent together, against the −20 the merges plus a survey cut might
  suggest, and the gap is evidence.**

## Defects

**Two this pass caused, both found and repaired before the build.** Cutting

**Four pre-existing, found because a renumber forces you to read every citing
sentence.** All four are the D-050 class — a reference that resolves and names a
claim its target does not make:

1. Section 9.2.1 attributed Apple's iOS differential-privacy deployment to
   section 5.6.3, which never covered it; **no section of the book does.** The
   cross-reference is dropped and the citation carries the claim.
2. Section 8.3.5 opened by attributing "AI literacy … as a safeguard against
   authoritarian misuse" to section 5.6.3, which does not say it, and **no
   section does.** The sentence now makes the claim itself.
3. Section 8.3.5's cross-training reference was one section off — the claim is
   section 5.4.1's, not 5.4.2's. Repointed.
4. The glossary's **BERT** entry pointed at two chapter 5 sections, neither of
   which mentions BERT; the book cites it once, at section 4.2.9. Repointed
   there. **Federated learning** carried the same stale chapter 5 locator, now
   dropped, keeping the two that are correct.

Not found by any tool. `check_xrefs.py` resolves references and cannot read them;
`xref_content.py` catches only cases where the citing sentence names a proper
noun, acronym or year, which none of these six does.
