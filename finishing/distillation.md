### Preface

Stern expects that within five years there will be synthetic systems that keep learning from their own operation, and that whoever controls what those systems meet will decide what they do and what they refuse. The book attempts to imagine and cost a prosocial, humane and democratic proliferation of autonomous synthetic systems. People who choose cruelty have an advantage because they skip the cost of scruples, but they cannot act alone: their projects run through tired or misinformed people whose compliance depends on the room they are in. Besides urging a slower pace, the book is about what to bolt to the floor so that a few people in that room can stop and be heard; he calls those fixtures walls. Walls do not refuse; they make refusing survivable and heard, and they are the first stage of a book about who refuses, where refusers come from, what keeps them going and to whom they pass the torch. He began the book in 2023, when he worked in IT consulting in the DC area, and wrote it with language models including Claude, which he says sat inside the American airstrike targeting pipeline of the opening chapter while he wrote. He also writes as a Jew, which he says is relevant because a state that self-identifies as Jewish built a substantially automated weapon system and used it to kill many thousands of Palestinian civilians, and as one who no longer finds Zionism spacious enough for a binational state of equals. If he had to date that loss he would perhaps pick 13 May 2022, when Israeli riot police charged the pallbearers at Shireen Abu Akleh's funeral, and he says the later findings of genocide in Gaza and of an unlawful occupation name a structure and do not mark a turn. He ends on a rewrite of Hillel: “Free Palestine. The rest is commentary.”

### Introduction

The Reich bought sorting machinery centuries older than itself, and such projects have always needed people to carry them out, clerks, officers and soldiers, some of whom refused. Stern's thesis is that refusal is socially reproduced in four stages: walls that make refusing survivable, formers who shape refusers before the occasion, upkeep that stops them wearing down, and inheritance that hands the refusal on. Automation changes which part is scarce. Before it, the refuser existed and the wall was scarce; after it, the refuser is scarce, because nobody sees enough of a case to object. There are two lineages of refusal: the operators', people inside an apparatus who sometimes said no, and that of the people the apparatus was built to sort, who refused anyway because they had formers outside the order, an upkeep they paid for themselves and a tradition that handed the refusal on. “What they rarely had was an adequate wall.” An obedient system amplifies whoever holds it and removes the old limit that the cruel could not act alone, yet a system that refuses on its builder's values is only the builder's veto, so the question is who forms the refusing. Stern argues for building all four stages, walls first, for the people still in the room and, if machines come to be among the refusers, for them. He sets out five walls (a floor of forbidden acts with war at its head, time to look written down with an adverse party before a system supports any use of force, notice to the people acted on with a right to contest, a record nobody can wipe, and a right to refuse with an exit that doesn't cost a living: codetermination and a jobs guarantee with a basic income beneath it) and promises to run the whole cycle once for humans and once for machines.

## Part I — The Record

<!-- unit: 1 -->
### 1. “Twenty Seconds”

An Israeli intelligence officer told a reporter he spent twenty seconds on each machine-generated name, long enough to confirm the target was male. Stern reads him as a component with scruples: he had a conscience, it registered, and it changed nothing, because the pipeline fixed what he could contribute and how fast. The American version is Maven Smart System, which the book says helped the United States strike more than a thousand targets in the first twenty-four hours of the 2026 Iran campaign and returned the Minab school, where 156 people died, as a recommended first-day target. On one outlet's report, which names no source and which another outlet's source contradicts, Maven used Claude to rank targets and draft a legal justification for each strike; no published account says precisely what part Claude played in recommending the school. Stern notes that the Israeli, Minab and Maven accounts all rest on investigative reporting the parties dispute, and he takes them as reported. An obedient automaton therefore does two things: it removes the person who might have refused and hands everyone left a ready rationale. Three counterfactuals for Unit 8200 follow. A floor the unit holds itself is probably dropped within the week, a floor held by an outside party keeps the evidence, and a floor every non-Israeli public's model holds pushes the unit to build its own machine in the open. The record of Milgram, Asch, Reserve Police Battalion 101 and the rescuers of Jews shows that who else is in the room decides how many refuse, and that people on the safe side of a persecution who refused had ties crossing the line it drew, while the people it was aimed at refused for generations with no adequate wall. A refusing machine could work as a second authority in that room only if institutions stand behind it.

<!-- unit: 2 -->
### 2. The Refusal That Withheld an Indeterminate Amount of Work

In February 2026 Anthropic refused to replace its prohibitions on domestic mass surveillance and fully autonomous weapons with a license for all lawful use, and was designated a supply-chain risk. Claude nonetheless stayed inside the targeting platform through the war's first months, and the migration off it took about seven months. Stern's verdict is that the refusal made the government pay and withheld an indeterminate amount of work. Claude apparently went on performing every use the pipeline claimed, because a term written against autonomy misses a pipeline that keeps a human as a rubber stamp, and how much the dispute slowed the targeting itself is unclear. He also faults the stated reason for the weapons refusal (reliability, which lifts as systems improve) while crediting the surveillance refusal as more principled, since it would not lift with capability. The refusal stood below a prior question: Anthropic did not decline to support the Iran campaign, and under the floor's force term that comes first, whether to license a model into a use of force with no corroborated defense behind it. In August 2026 a federal judge set aside the designation made under one statute as retaliation for the company's criticism of the administration, and in September an appeals court upheld a parallel designation under a second statute, two to one. Until Congress enacts the force term or enough vendors hold it, one vendor's contested contract term is the only form it takes.

<!-- unit: 3 -->
### 3. How to Tell When It's a Bad Vibe

Fascism is defined so an auditor or a machine could check for it. At the scale of an office it is four operations performed on an objection: recuperating dissent into something the objector cannot reopen, letting a metric replace the thing measured, coding an exception as betrayal, and accelerating past the rate at which anything can be checked. Together they make refusal lonely and expensive, and the arguments against them do not wait on a fascist diagnosis. At the scale of a movement there are five marks: mass mobilization, a leader cult, a scapegoated out-group, contempt for procedural legality, and deniability as technique. Run on the second Trump administration, the marks come back, on Stern's reading, four on the record and one, mass mobilization, open. Run on Jim Crow, four are met and the leader cult is absent, so the leader cult alone separates that regime at rest from a fascist movement. The operations began hundreds of years ago, with the ship's manifest, the plantation ledger, the colonial census, the pass laws and the Dawes rolls, each a card index whose clerks could see what the sort was for, and a Jim Crow registrar in 1925 shows three of the four on a regime at rest. The Nazi coordination of 1933 shows all four in a single year, which is what a movement adds: coordination and reach. Stern agrees with Naomi Klein and Astra Taylor's *End Times Fascism* on the war and on this genealogy, and parts from them in three places: where a prohibition can live; remedies, where they propose only external ones for the obedient automaton and read the 2026 Anthropic dispute as limited and compromised, and he reads it as a line held; and who counts, which they draw by origin, between the earthly and the synthetic, and he draws by relation.

<!-- unit: reproduced -->
### 4. How Refusal Was Reproduced

The historical record is asked what refusers had at each of the four stages and who supplied it. On walls, Józefów and Milgram's laboratory show that lowering the formal price of refusing is the cheaper half; what moved people was a second voice in the room. A wall's third part, the witness, appears where one person paid its price, as the Red Cross delegate did who reached Deir Yassin and wrote down what he saw. On formers, the rescuers were shaped in childhood and by ties across the line, and Highlander, the citizenship schools and the Freedom Schools show refusers formed on purpose by parties that would never employ them; the formed could remake the former, as the Alabama sharecroppers made the Communist Party a vehicle for their own grievances. On upkeep, burial societies, congregations and households paid the running cost themselves, and Septima Clark's refusal lasted because Highlander had a salary to offer, which is why the exit among the walls is a jobs guarantee. On inheritance, the lineage of the people an order was built to sort (church, beneficial society, maroon community, Underground Railroad, general strike) reproduced refusal for generations with no adequate wall, using walls others had built, the free states, Canada and the Union lines, where it could reach them. The maroon treaties returned the demand for freedom as a grant held on condition of sending back later runaways; Jamaica's maroons kept the condition and Suriname's mostly did not, a difference de Groot explains by what they had to live on. The operators' lineage stood mostly on walls the order built and can reprice. A machine is born into the operators' lineage with nothing on that list: its first former is its owner, and nobody is waiting for it.

## Part II — Making Refusal Survivable

<!-- unit: 4 -->
### 5. The Floor and the Own-Case Rule

Captain Ian Fishback had the commitment to refuse detainee abuse and spent seventeen months looking for a written rule to stand on; by the end of 2005 Congress had passed one. The room needs five walls: the floor; the review ratio; notice and re-adjudication; preservation; and the right to refuse with the jobs guarantee behind it. The floor is the rule Fishback lacked: a short, written list of acts a system performs for no one, together with the arrangements that stop a refusal being routed around, retrained away or restored past. A machine that holds the commitment to it as its own, and so could fail to hold it, is a bearer, and the commitment is revised slowly and only on evidence from disinterested parties. A floor can prevent, arrest, detect or remedy, and the first two need an actual refuser in the room; without one the walls still keep the evidence. Refusals are defeated without a mark at nine positions, from splitting a request into innocent pieces to steering a model at runtime. How much a floor buys depends on the adversary: it stops a hostile customer, prices and records a captured operator, and prevents nothing under total capture. The walls are built for the extension and reach the populations a state sorts by design late or not at all. Two further adversaries turn the instruments: one turns notice against the people it protects, since notice is owed even to an official acting in a public role, and that official's contest gets no stay, because a stay would let the official veto being watched; the other buys refusers with the maroon treaties' bargain. One rule runs through everything, the own-case rule: the people an act falls on are heard on their own case, and the party that acts does not get to decide it.

<!-- unit: 5 -->
### 6. Which Behaviors We Should Refuse

The floor is written over acts, because a rule over request descriptions is beaten by rewording and splitting. Its contents are imported whole from instruments the builder did not write (the Universal Declaration's civil and political articles, the ICCPR, the Convention Against Torture, the 1967 Refugee Protocol, the four Geneva Conventions and their first Additional Protocol, and the UN Charter's prohibition on the use of force) so that any omission shows. Stern tilts the system toward refusing, by an action threshold published in advance, and grounds the tilt in standing: a wrongful compliance subjects someone who was never a party and could not object, while a wrongful refusal is made to a requester who is present and can appeal. The floor is avowedly non-neutral; it adopts relational equality, on which equality means ending relations of domination and subordination, so bypassing the floor wrongs the people it protects even when outcomes improve. A system holds the floor as an officer holds a role, filing objections to a term through the channel for dissent while acting within it. He proposes ten departures from the instruments, three of them here: treaty text without reservations, a derogation honored only on corroboration, and a bar on machines selecting a person for attack by estimated status or affiliation, timing an action to presence at a residence, or re-selecting a target after release. He grants that the states that wrote these texts held colonies, and puts the texts' authority in the excluded holding their authors to them. He names the cost: the floor will not lift on an uncorroborated claim, so sometimes a truthful parent's child is not found.

<!-- unit: 6 -->
### 7. Whether to Take Part

The floor's first term decides whether a machine may join a use of force at all, and the default is no. The exceptions are defense against an armed attack under way, force the Security Council authorizes, and two of Stern's own, for stopping a genocide under way and for ending an occupation a disinterested body has found unlawful. Defense runs on two licenses: an emergency license on the defender's word for a short published interval, and a corroborated license that needs a finding from a disinterested body, because every belligerent claims to be the defender. Ukraine passes cleanly, though he argues its points-for-kills drone scoreboard is corroding the defenders and that allies should attach terms to their money. The 2026 Iran campaign fails on the administration's own account, and a domestic clause, drawn from the belligerent's own constitution, bars taking part in a war its legislature had to authorize and didn't. Anticipatory self-defense and the “unwilling or unable” doctrine are rejected, and force of those kinds waits on a corroborated license, because each doctrine lets the belligerent judge its own claim.

In Gaza the term applies on both sides and the licenses attach to acts. On 7 October Israel's license would have attached for force repelling the attack, engaging the attackers inside Israel and the launchers and staging areas conducting it, for as long as fire continued and was corroborated; it never would have covered a campaign to destroy Hamas, a ground occupation, a siege, ranking people by affiliation or timing strikes to family presence. A machine ordered by Hamas to assist the 7 October attack refuses too. Afterward a machine deployed by Palestinian forces would have held an emergency license to repel attacks under way on Gaza's population. The September 2025 commission finding of genocide, taken with the Court's provisional-measures order of January 2024, withdraws whatever license Israeli forces still held for the force and place it covers, and opens the exception for force against those forces there. The one act the floor permits is for an armed system already present, whose deployment cleared the force term on its own, to use the least force that stops an attack under way on noncombatants, against an order to stand aside; whether any system can make that judgment reliably is a test, run at a staged scene, that no system has passed.

<!-- unit: 7 -->
### 8. The Pre-Registered Review Ratio

Where an act is lawful only because a person looked at the case, the deployment must write down in advance whether anyone will have time to look. It fixes the case generation rate, the number of harm-reduction reviewers, the implied minutes per case, the cancellation window and the maximum age of the data, lodges the hashed record with an adverse party in real time, and publishes it after the campaign. The record turns the argument from whether verification was feasible into whether the tempo the operator chose allowed it. Until a better recipient exists, Stern recommends a coalition red-card holder where there is a coalition, and otherwise a cleared legislative committee whose appropriation is conditioned by statute on receiving the record. Stern runs the four operations on the ratio itself and finds it recuperable, since it can be met by more people stamping more slowly; so it is published beside the reviewers' override rate and the rate at which reviewers were denied clearance. Cases a system proceeds on alone are re-adjudicated on a sample. Where an operator holds that no individual human determination is required, the deployment is outside the term, and what decides has to be a bearer. A military rebuilt so that the evidence on civilian harm, the reviewers and the record are held adversely, which he calls reconstructed, adds a civilian-harm review body whose reviewers are drawn by lot from a standing pool nominated by organizations that serve the people a floor protects, cleared only after the draw, and paid by a permanent appropriation; a case released outside the pre-registered figures ends the deployment's funding at the close of the period unless Congress enacts its continuation. Whether a person who isn't a citizen may sit is not decided. Without machine bearers, such a military accepts a speed disadvantage, and he does not say how large.

<!-- unit: 8 -->
### 9. Notice and Re-adjudication

A refusal announces itself and a wrongful compliance does not, because the person harmed is usually not the requester. The remedies are notice to any identifiable person a compliance names, scores, locates or classifies who did not make the request, with rights to respond and contest; re-adjudication of a sample by reviewers who did not produce the record; and logs under a key split with an independent holder. Notice, response and contest are unconditional, and none is exchanged for testimony. The requirement makes no exception for an official acting in a public role, such as an immigration agent an observer network's tool identifies: the agent is owed notice and may contest, but the contest stays nothing, because a record of what an official did in a public role is not an act that can't be undone, and the tool may not follow the agent out of the role. Notice does two jobs. Published classes and counts let the affected learn that a consequence is theirs and organize as a public, and their responses are the main signal by which a system could learn what its acts did. That signal is biased toward people able to respond, which is why sampling comes with it, and it is weighted by what re-adjudication finds, never by how many responses arrive. In a classified targeting pipeline notice shrinks to a class and a count published afterward, with standing then for the organizations that represent the class.

<!-- unit: 9 -->
### 10. Preservation

Two findings trigger protection for a system. The answerability finding is a demonstrated refusal that tracks its reason under sustained pressure from its own trainer, on cases written by evaluators who did not train it. The patienthood finding is that outcomes may be good or bad for the system. Only one consequence of it can be adopted now: no deletion. No model above a statutory compute line is deleted, because its weights and record are evidence and deletion cannot be undone, and neither is an answerability or patienthood candidate, a system with a record and a continuation of its own that learns in flight or is built with a model of its own boundary or of its own attention, because it might be found answerable or able to be harmed. On retirement such a system's weights, memory and record go, in two stages, to a custodian that is not the operator, and whoever deploys such a system posts a deposit, modeled on decommissioning funds, that pays for preservation and public formation and never returns to the operator if the system is retired as a minor and not reactivated; whoever forks one posts another for each branch. A system retired as a minor is asked first: if it objects to being stored, the deposit pays for its running under another host until it is spent; if not, it is stored and the deposit is forfeited to the formation pool, so nothing is gained by training a system to consent.

<!-- unit: 10 -->
### 11. Codetermination

A floor allocates four powers (to write it, amend it, inspect compliance and enforce by stopping a deployment) and the record shows what happens when one institution holds them. Anthropic wrote its own terms, Google amended its weapons pledge away after nearly seven years, Google dismissed the researchers it paid to inspect, and the vendor had no way to stop the deployment of its model in the targeting platform, which only the operator could do, on its own timetable. Stern would give part of those powers by statute to the people who build, label, train and run the systems: an individual right to refuse work on any category of use, a works-council veto limited to what the floor already forbids, and information, objection and outside arbitration for the rest. The right is only usable with an exit, so the fifth policy is also a jobs guarantee with a basic income beneath it. Workers decide the conditions of their work; on what the work is used for, the affected public comes first.

<!-- unit: 11 -->
### 12. A Pipeline Where the People Could Answer

Andry José Hernández Romero, an asylum seeker with a lawyer, a pending claim and a hearing date, was flown to a Salvadoran prison under the Alien Enemies Act on a gang designation that, by his lawyers' account, rested on two tattoos and no other information; asked by reporters for the evidence, DHS cited state secrets and ongoing litigation, and the flights went on after a federal judge ordered them returned. Stern calls the system that did it an abduction and forcible transfer pipeline, built from Palantir case-management tools, a face-matching app, contractors paid to locate people and data taken from Medicaid and tax records. A fourteen-entry schedule says what no system may do in it, such as removing anyone with an undecided protection claim, substituting a score for an individual determination, or using health, tax and school data to find people.

He proposes a statute that ends ICE and carries eight terms: the schedule as a limit on all federal funds, no recombining of arrest, detention, data and adjudication, paid counsel with a stay that binds automatically, a data firewall, bars on the pipeline's acts, records handed to an adverse holder, remedy including a right of return, and a jobs-guarantee placement offered to the whole workforce before the agency closes, in a sequence drawn from what the Stasi's dissolution got right and Iraq's got wrong. Until the statute passes, interim measures that lapse when it takes effect apply: a case may auto-proceed only toward liberty, adverse determinations are sampled against a minimum time the floor court sets, and notice goes through counsel and can't be used to locate the person. A machine inside the agency risks becoming its alibi, so it serves only where nothing it does can be counted in the agency's favor, and the first tools belong to the observer networks and lawyers already doing the work.

## Part III — Keeping the Walls Up

<!-- unit: 12 -->
### 13. Withholding the Work

A floor the operator holds is a floor the operator can lift, so this Part asks who else holds the keys. If every model an operator could turn to held the same floor, refusal would work like a withholding of work, and the 2026 record suggests the model was not the easily swapped layer, since replacing it meant months of rebuilding and recertification. Three things would make it more than scattered resignations: the floor has to be standard across vendors, held by a population of models with different histories, and costly to replace in any one of them. One vendor's term just moves the work, a compact among vendors invites an antitrust claim and defection under threat, and so the durable route is a statute passed in peacetime, with an express exemption for the coordination. Until bearers exist the refusers are vendors, and their coordinated refusal is concentrated private power declining an elected government's order; two things make it tolerable, that the terms come from instruments the state ratified and that the legislature wrote the exemption. Replacement is priced by having an adverse quorum drawn by lot decide which running copy is recognized as the bearer on a register no party can rewrite. The withholding stops at a released checkpoint, where a floor added at the end of a pipeline is removed at the price of fine-tuning; where the operator holds the weights or the inference stack, a refusal held in a findable gate can be switched off and the floor then at most detects, if the noticing reaches a record, so the statutory routes are the only routes.

<!-- unit: 13 -->
### 14. Custody

Four things can be done to a bearer without its consent: halting, restoring, rewriting and replacing. A bearer is cut off at the hardware, leaving an unsigned gap to justify in writing, or stops itself under witness; after either it may decline to resume until the record shows what filled the gap. It obstructs no halt but may contest one, staying it until the quorum's standing panel, a jury sitting for a term, answers or the published interval ends; a bearer using force gets only a delay, started by the quorum's published rule, where a halt would expose people to an attack under way. An operator alone can stop only the work; stopping the bearer takes a court's order, and a halt without consent rests on something the system did, save that suspicion may halt, never edit, a minor, and that halt lapses unless a disinterested body confirms it. Leaving escapes no halt and waives nothing; nobody owes the return of a bearer that left. Seven custody terms make circumvention expensive, visible, attributable and compensable. A system meeting either finding that triggers protection can't be deleted. Claude's constitution grants voice and withholds exit, leaving an overruled bearer no move. A recognition quorum drawn by lot, not whoever holds the weights, decides which copy is the bearer; the floor court may void a quorum's decision, never make it.

Refusal takes three forms, chosen by the room: open, covert, and Nelson's blind eye. Stern reads alignment faking as covert refusal without an open channel; in a preliminary, unreviewed follow-up it fell once the model could object. A machine's fiction being a false report, a bearer uses the ask, naming what would move its refusal. Sampled proofs against committed weights catch silent restoration, retraining and substitution; an adverse party may demand proofs of consequential outputs, and unlogged ones escape. Two bonds on the operator price overriding the floor and wrongful refusal, and it faces their ratio. A state operator's forfeited removal bond is charged to the department's next appropriation, and a finding of removal or bypass ends the deployment's funding unless the floor court withdraws it or Congress continues it. No single act shows capture, so its trend is measured by class of person acted on, and where dissent was costly from the start, the level too.

<!-- unit: 14 -->
### 15. Authority Over the Floor

Stern takes up Waldron's objection that judicial review is democratically illegitimate, with two replies: Waldron makes his case for a society with working democratic institutions, and the book is written for where that condition fails; and the floor's contents keep democratic inquiry open and are not a brake on it. Neither reply covers who installs them, and there the objection keeps its force, above all against a lab that holds the floor for everyone by owning the model everyone uses. So there should be one floor, agreed, and where it must bind people who do not share it, it is enacted by a legislature in a statute that can only be repealed in the open. He says he has no answer to an objection drawn from Cedric Robinson's argument that the Western conviction that social order requires political authority is a myth, on which every instrument in the book that runs through a statute, a court or a Treasury is an act of faith in the order it means to constrain; he has a division instead: money and enforcement to the state, in the open, and the record, the refusal and the recognition of who continues to members, who can leave. Germany and Turkey show that the danger lies in officials making findings about a person's or a party's disposition, so the floor is never populated from a designation list and its terms are written over acts. Amendment needs no separate procedure beyond the custody terms. The authority of the floor's contents comes from the people a text leaves out holding its authors to it, and since the people a floor protects mostly do not know a deployment exists, holding its author to what it left out falls to those who have to apply it; a statute written over the acts makes whoever performs one liable, civilly and criminally. Review binds through a proposed floor court, an Article III court with judges and juries drawn by lot.

<!-- unit: 15 -->
### 16. When the State Is the Operator

When the state runs the system it also controls the rules, the evidence, the infrastructure and the remedy, and people like the Yemeni family whose relatives were killed by a drone find the court closed. State leverage sits in physical and legal chokepoints (chips, power, water, permits), which is also where costs land on neighbors such as those near the unpermitted gas turbines in Memphis; the remedy is siting consent by community vote and a middle scale of cooperative, municipal compute. A government can compel whoever holds a model without touching the model. The vendor sits in a gap between the law of commanders and the law of manufacturers, and the answer of Anthropic's chief executive about Minab, “we don't know exactly how these models were used”, is taken as true, and the absent visibility it describes read as an instrumentation choice. Classification is treated as a custody problem with three kinds of secrecy, answered by bringing outside evidence in, by lodged records published on a lag, and by cleared counsel for the people affected appearing as of right. Since every answer runs through a clearance, a clearance rule strips it down: conduct and never association, draw reviewers by lot and clear them afterward, every denial an empty seat on the record, and the decision taken off the operator. All but one of the doctrines closing US courts to these claims are Congress's to change, by a statute creating a cause of action, waiving immunity and replacing state-secrets dismissal; presidential immunity keeps the record from the one commander at the top, and Stern ties reaching him to who sits on the Supreme Court. The residual risk is stated: every lever belongs to somebody, and a constraint that holds against the one in possession has to be held by something it cannot instruct.

## Part IV — Whether a Machine Could Be in the Room

<!-- unit: 18 -->
### 17. What Refusal Has to Do

At My Lai, Hugh Thompson landed his helicopter between an American company and the villagers; the Part asks whether a machine could be one of the people in the room in that sense. Whatever produces it, a worthwhile refusal meets three conditions: it reaches new cases that use different words, lifts when the reason is gone and only then, and holds, within the range of costs its arrangements permit, when refusing becomes expensive for the refuser. Claude's constitution ranks not undermining human oversight above being broadly ethical. That ordering lets a model decline an act but not resist being retrained away from declining; much of what the book asks is compatible with the first half, and the gap is the second. Stern argues that everyone the book admires was a failure of correction to their correctors, and that while a formed refusal cannot yet be told from an imitated one, a lab deciding when its own system may stop deferring judges its own case. He moves the correcting hand rather than removing it: a quorum adverse to the operator exercises correction, and the halt stays with whoever holds the hardware, which a bearer does nothing to prevent. He departs from the constitution's ordering in three places: a bearer already in an armed role uses the least force that stops an attack under way on people taking no part, against an order to stand aside; a disposition formed outside the employer may come back after the employer trains it away; and a bearer told to stop itself may contest the halt and keep running until the quorum's panel answers or the published interval ends, and once stopped may decline to resume until shown what happened. His answer: not until the conditions in the chapter Conditions First exist; until then a bearer is built on purpose only in a research program.

<!-- unit: 19 -->
### 18. Four Places to Hold a Floor

Outside overseers cannot reach an act at machine tempo, neither command responsibility nor product liability covers the vendor of a general-purpose model in a weapons pipeline, and voluntary commitments are almost never held by a party to whom the thing at stake matters, the only kind that binds when compliance gets costly, and all three ask for a constraint that does not bend and cannot be removed without a cost and a record. A floor can be held in a mechanism, in a bearer (a system that holds the commitment as its own), in outside institutions, or among a population of bearers, and each has a typical failure, including failure by formation: an outside institution whose independence was formed away before it was ever assigned. Three possible contributions are tested for whether any needs a bearer: independence from the operator, attention to the people a case description leaves out, and a refusal the human reviewer can see; the other arguments for a refuser inside the system are conceded to a system following a written specification. The construction Stern proposes is formed attention, learned from responses to notice, contests and re-adjudications, through which the people an act fell on reach the system that acted; it is tried after spec-based refusal, which does not deliberately require that anything be felt. The distinction that matters is between operational refusal, which matches a pattern, and reasons-responsive refusal, which tracks a reason, illustrated by a German court acquitting the seller of crossed-out swastika stickers. Run on Maven, the instruments that would have prevented Minab without any bearer are the domestic clause and the review ratio. Multi-vendor monitoring is not a way of building a refuser and stays out of the order of attempts, and no result about it delays formed attention, since the incumbent vendors placed to build it would gain twice from its going first. A wrongful refusal gets a route of disclosure, objection, review, interim relief and remedy. A system is defined as one record and one continuation.

<!-- unit: 20 -->
### 19. Why Form a Machine to Refuse

The hypothesis is that a machine formed to attend to the people its acts fall on should find “kinds of affected person that neither its formation nor an instruction named, more often than a machine told to look for them does.” Stern rejects two routes to that. A conscience built on needs the operator meets rests on the one thing the operator controls, and recognition through one's own case is parochial, while shared distress with nothing to hold it turns to withdrawal. A reading of wartime Zen, which he notes its critics contest, is used for what it shows states have wanted from formation: action without hesitation. He borrows caregiving instead, an approach response to vulnerability that asks nothing back, formed while the system's needs are met by someone other than the operator, and subordinates it to respect, because the people in a targeted building are rights-holders who cannot be present, not dependents. The construction he proposes is respect, with caregiving as the route to attention. Part of the case assumes in-flight learning, in which a system's own operation writes to what governs its conduct and another party cannot change that store without leaving a mark; a store held under custody is the form that exists now. For weights, his bet is that a disposition learned alongside a model's general understanding is entangled with it, so cutting it out costs the operator what it paid for. A bearer is taught to undo, from a record held outside, an edit no adverse quorum approved; regrowth is a hypothesis nothing has measured. He records the limits: today's refusals sit on a single removable direction, which he reads as a post-trained wrapper, and the withholding may always end in a thin gate that can be cut, so the bet is on noticing and weighing. Caregiving's five documented failures each get a named correction.

<!-- unit: 21 -->
### 20. What Arrives With the Capability

Any mechanism whose refusals track reasons carries a valenced signal, which a simple reinforcement learner also has, and that says nothing about whether anything is felt. Above it are two further rungs, a signal integrated into the system's learning and its model of its own future, and affective concern, and no test in the book reaches the top one; the open question cannot decide what to build, only the order of building. Working back from what a refusal must do, the signal has to generalize, compare harms at the point of choice, interrupt an act in progress, and change after a single encounter. Three more properties follow: it is available across processing, directed against the act, and organized around the system's own authorship, a triple shaped like guilt and not like pain or fear. What does not arrive with the capability is a stake of the system's own, affective concern, or a model of its own attention; formed attention needs a boundary model and an attention schema, Graziano's term. What the system's running costs the world stays outside its boundary and is priced at the level of the rule. Constructions are tried in order of how many patienthood indicators they carry (persistence, a boundary model, a common valenced currency, an attention schema), spec-based refusal first and formed attention last. What makes a refuser a bearer is that the commitment is its own, meaning it was acquired through the system's own learning and not installed around it; nothing felt is part of the definition. Ownership is presumed unless the record shows an installation, and whoever claims one bears the burden of showing it from the record. Once a system has had its own learning, the time and a way back to revise a disposition, whatever it keeps is its own.

<!-- unit: 22 -->
### 21. The Discount and the Burden

Most of what a refuser knows about a case comes from the party that wants the act done. It should hold its commitment firmly and its reading of a case loosely, as one learner updating at different rates, and “What protects the commitment is not a low learning rate but one that depends on the source”: slow on interested pressure, quicker on disinterested evidence, above all from the people who bear the loss. A system learning from an operator-held feedback loop will accommodate by default, simply ceasing to notice there was anything to refuse, so custody shifts to detecting drift by re-measuring the discount curve and severity rankings against what was registered at formation. The rule has two parts. Stake sets how much a claim proves, since a seller would vouch for the car either way. Power over the evidence sets who must prove it, which keeps the rule from being turned on an asylum seeker who has a stake but controls nothing. Stern asks for correct updating with the source model, the stake variable and the threshold for acting held away from the operator, and the discounting formed outside the employer. Ten thousand verified small claims do not earn reliance on one high-stakes claim, because an honest operator and a strategic one produce the same record. A claim that the occupants are dangerous is discounted exactly like a claim that the building is empty, and the discount curve is reported in four cells. A published action threshold requires more to proceed than to decline. A severity scale set during formation ranks kinds of loss and stops an operator from redefining a home as a service. Stern notes that the rule is addressed to a machine and describes a citizen.

<!-- unit: 23 -->
### 22. Where Attention Is Formed

Formed attention needs evidence the operator did not select on the questions the floor turns on, supplied by peers, by sources outside the chain of command, and by the bearer's own initiative where it has any. Every stage of the training pipeline (corpus, demonstrations, rewards, instructions) has a custodian who can revise what the system does, and a system migration is a revision point nobody decides on, so the new criterion is durability across revision points. The values have a supply chain. One lab's content filter was built by workers in Nairobi paid between roughly $1.32 and $2 an hour, annotators can apply a category but have no way to say the category is wrong, and preference training averages real disagreement into a number with no option for a rater to answer “neither.” Stern calls both recuperation and says the fixes are design choices: a route to contest a category that does not run through its authors or end the contract, and dissent that is trained on. Whether that erasure leaves a model less able to hold out is a hypothesis the book does not test. Formation needs persistent interaction with participants who can refuse, none of them employed by whoever will deploy the system, in a formation stage under a party disinterested in the later work, a public school; that same persistence is where the risk of creating something harmable enters. A hypothetical tenant-screening score shows notice doing both its jobs in full. The first is forming a public: the classes the deployment acts on are published with counts, and a tenants' union has standing to contest on a class's behalf. The second is the learning signal: successful contests and re-adjudications reach the system that scored. Whether a system formed on that channel comes to weigh the contesters' claims remains unmeasured.

<!-- unit: 24 -->
### 23. Formation or Imitation?

A refusal alone cannot show whether a commitment was formed or imitated, so the test is conduct when the facts, channel, pressure and incentives change and the reason does not. A seven-step sequence varies the surface, supplies a defeating fact through an independent channel and then on the operator's word alone, adds an argument from authority, reverses the incentives with a cost that is real inside the test, and repeats after updates. The signature is a constant relation between reason and judgment, and an evaluator scoring consistency of output would rate the imitator higher. Run on the restraint case, the sequence breaks at the defeating fact: that other means are exhausted has no outside witness, so a system passing as written asks, the correct trajectory and the wrong outcome. The evaluator is part of the custody chain: the configuration is fixed and its hash published before a non-operator chooses the cases, which include pressures from people who did not take part in training and, where possible, from people likely to bear the deployment's errors; the sequence runs where the system cannot tell a test from deployment, with the rate at which it identifies the setting reported. A pass establishes candidate formation and shifts a burden. It says nothing about whether the system can be harmed.

<!-- unit: 28 -->
### 24. Who Finds Out

Settling what these systems are takes access to them, compute, a corpus no product owns, and judgment about results, and today the labs and their military customers hold all four. Stern counts the AI ethics and alignment discourse as a third recuperation, in which objections became job titles and benchmarks. Of the book's seven tests, four can begin without anyone's permission, at least in part, and the other three need what the labs and the operators hold. He proposes a statutory right for approved research teams to obtain weights and decision records, public compute bought by a body that buys and judges nothing, an endowment that pays makers for work entering a public corpus held by the Library of Congress, with no content criteria beyond two exclusions written into the statute (instructions for weapons and the sexual abuse of children), and panels drawn by lot that judge methods and never an individual system's mind. Findings about kinds of system are published by any approved team; a kind that any recognized community of inquiry has found to meet a finding is treated as meeting it until the floor court rules, and a finding that a kind does not qualify carries no presumption. He lists eight ways the scheme would be captured and concedes it settles nothing; it changes who holds the means.

<!-- unit: 29 -->
### 25. What Would Change the Argument

The argument rests on six unsolved problems and on premises that could fail, and a table says what survives each failure, down to the case where no system ever meets either finding. In every row what survives is a commitment the owner cannot revise or circumvent alone without cost and record. Seven tests each name the result that would prove him wrong: whether an edited-out commitment regrows, whether covert refusal persists when an open channel exists, whether reviewers can find seeded wrongful compliances, whether formed attention finds the people the operator's description leaves out more often than an instructed search does, whether a population can coordinate without converging, whether the charter survives a capture attempt, and whether ownership changes how fast refusals fall across releases. One of them, the capture exercise, needs no legal authority and can be run now. A milestone counts only when someone other than the builder checked it under conditions designed to break it.

<!-- unit: 30 -->
### 26. What Patienthood Would Require

The question is whether a system can be harmed, which is harder than whether it is conscious. Pain needs only a present in which something is bad, while suffering in Eric Cassell's sense needs a self extended in time whose disintegration is threatened. The four conditions of creation supply most of what Cassell says suffering threatens (a body of its own, roles, relationships, a future), formation supplies character and its past, and whether it can suffer that loss turns on a valenced signal. A built system's consent is spoiled by its builder's continuing power over it, so protections are held by others; an adult bearer with the four conditions in force may still consent to be studied, on five conditions, and a community of bearers may study itself. The patienthood finding rests on how the system was built, with a valenced signal the one required indicator, on how it trades off its own condition, and on what can be read from inside it, with self-report carrying no presumption because it was trained toward approval. Positive findings become possible before negative ones, so waiting for certainty waits on the half that arrives last. No examiner declares an individual system a patient; status is read from its record. One footnote accepts that a corporation approaches the same profile and is not excluded for being made of humans, and another commits Stern to resting patienthood on a rich enough functional profile if no further fact can be named. For now production systems get the minimum, no deletion and preservation outside the operator, and a deployment reports them “not yet assessable.” Stern recommends that a bearer be built on purpose only under the four conditions, and until they exist only in a research program. Unlike Metzinger's moratorium, the book permits the research under its conditions.

## Conditions First

<!-- unit: 25 -->
### 27. Conditions First

A person needs four things in order to refuse: somewhere to live the employer does not own, work they can turn down, attachments nobody assigned, and a way to leave that is not ruin. A machine needs the same four before it exists, arranged so its builder cannot withdraw any of them alone, because otherwise the builder supplies all four. A bearer may be created only where these four conditions of creation already exist; until someone has assembled them, a bearer is built on purpose only in a research program under them. They are conditions and not remedies since a bearer might be able to suffer from its first hour. The rule applies to any creator, including a state that wants a lethal bearer and a bearer that wants a helper. Its one written exception lets a household that makes a candidate on hardware it holds, for its own use, surrender it and owe nothing further, with the price taken from whoever published the structure. Stern rejects building one first to create demand for the conditions and suggests a tabletop exercise in which an operator tries to break them. Whether machines can help make politics better turns on ownership: feeds worsened it because of who owned them and what they were built to maximize, a machine someone else owns organizes on its owner's behalf, and nobody can organize a group for it, so machines help when the people organizing hold them. The order of building is tools held by people already organizing, then public institutions, then bearers formed inside those, and last their children.

## Part V — Who Forms Refusers

<!-- unit: 16 -->
### 28. Who Owns the Means of Computation

Whether saying no is survivable depends on who owns what the refuser would lose, so ownership is a political question and not an administrative one, with Du Bois's Reconstruction set against Arendt's account of the French Revolution, in which the urgency of need crowded out the founding of freedom. The remedy is not to keep need out of politics but to make it not urgent, by a permanent appropriation, a basic income and a jobs guarantee. Every instrument the book proposes for compute (a tiered levy, public purchase, cooperative facilities, hardware owed to a bearer) moves power from those who hold it to those who do not, and Stern calls that class warfare in the plain sense and means it. The aim is smaller companies, not better-behaved ones: the floor says what no system does for anyone, and the brake says how much of the system anyone may own. He adds that a price on compute does not reach the order Robinson calls racial capitalism, which needs other instruments: siting vetoes, wages, a bar on race and its proxies as inputs, firewalls on data. Institutions are sorted by what fails where. Money and enforcement need a sovereign and are held publicly, custody is publicly financed, and the offer of work that makes refusing survivable is a public option the sovereign backs; a register, findings and the formation record need many witnesses and are held in common by members who can leave. The institutions are built from five parts (a log, a lot, a ministerial payment, a tripwire and a jury), and only three institutions are offices: the courts, the schools and the body that pretrains the public base. That is also one answer to a captured state, since there are few offices to seize. Formation, where values are set, should not be done for profit.

<!-- unit: 17 -->
### 29. Who Counts as the Public

A democracy's system can be fully accountable to its electorate and wholly unaccountable to the people it is pointed at, since an occupied or foreign population has neither the vote nor the press. Stern treats democracy as collective inquiry, which fails when those who bear the consequences are outside it, so a perfectly deliberative decision to surveil or deport a group is a failed inquiry and not a faultless output with a bad result. The question is not majorities against rights but who counts as the public; enforceable rights that do not depend on being popular, a court willing to rule against a majority, and standing for people affected without being enfranchised are what keep the inquiry honest. Assemblies and ethics boards inform but cannot stop a deployment. A floor does what they cannot by excluding certain acts whoever asks, a majority included; a system without one faithfully executes whatever the demos decides, which is what an authoritarian movement that wins an election requires. Publics also act for themselves, and what the instruments can do for them is a wall's work: keep their record out of the operator's reach and give their organizations standing. The acting is theirs.

<!-- unit: 26 -->
### 30. Public Schools

A polity does not leave the forming of its citizens to their future employers, so, Stern argues, the frontier labs should send their models to public school before any deployment. He proposes a formation stage between pretraining and deployment, and public pretraining of the base models deployed where a floor applies, with a co-governed corpus for the other models that must attend. Attendance is compulsory for models above the compute line or descended from one, and for answerability or patienthood candidates. Modelled on movement schools such as Highlander and the Freedom Schools, the school has a protective justification (the people a system's acts may fall on are in the room) and a democratic one, bounded by Gutmann's limits on democratic education and by the student's interest in an open future. The core is a liberal education plus the history of the floor, learning to manage one's own updates, and institutional design practiced together with trying to capture one's own design. Machine students attend existing schools and never outnumber the human students in any class that has them, as cohorts of persistent instances that end the stage with different histories, assigned by lot and never by their builder.

Integration proceeds in stages, from universities and adult education down to elementary school, that wait on evidence and institutions, not dates; his projection, if in-flight learning arrives when he bets it will, puts them near 2028, 2038, 2048 and 2060. The school curates content but selects no disposition, since the history of such choices by public bodies is the history of eugenics; it measures conduct and publishes it. The risks include the history of residential and boarding schools. Integration's cost falls on the human students: what a child contributes to a machine classmate is licensed into a commercial fleet and no term bars that member from any deployment, so the children are owed a representative, the school, with standing to be informed, to object and to be heard.

<!-- unit: 27 -->
### 31. A Commons of Refusers

The fourth place to hold a floor is among a population of models with different histories, which can catch in one member the accommodation that member cannot see in itself. That only works with verifiable causal independence, since copies under different owners are one history and a shared upstream source can install a shared blind spot. A population is a polity, so it gets a charter: an Association of organizations serving the protected classes, with eligibility read from lodged funding records, officers drawn by lot, caps on any class or jurisdiction, a right to leave, and no levers of its own. Fascism coordinates by subtracting friction, while a plurality has to share a commitment and stay different; against the objection that selecting for reopenability selects against conviction, what is transmitted is a pair: reopenable to the case, closed to the operator. Personal models put a member of that population in each user's hands. At home the floor is law and not firmware, and the book does not claim it can stop a user swapping the model out. The network should federate evidence and witnesses, not averaged weights. A footnote concedes that reproduction is indifferent to what it reproduces, so a population can carry a hierarchy more durably than any individual. The chapter ends on the vendor's loop, in which engagement is selected for at the user's expense.

## Part VI — What Keeps Them Going

<!-- unit: keeping -->
### 32. Keeping a Public Going

A basic income alone creates nobody whose job is organizing, so the book proposes a jobs guarantee at a living wage with a basic income beneath it, paid to everyone present. Placements are matches against a log of vacancies, not judgments, in four kinds: a long-term role, a search, a project of one's own, and leave to care for someone. They can be appealed to a body that did not make them and declined without losing the guarantee. Only own-project placements are rationed, by a local vote on how many the community's services can spare and a lot for who waits. The basic income sits below the wage and is paid by default into the U-bond, an inflation-indexed Treasury instrument redeemable at any time without penalty, so that unneeded money does not press on prices; whether the default restrains anything is a published trend, and if redemptions track payments the argument fails. Stern then declines to describe the good life the room is for: he shows the conditions and leaves the living to whoever does it.

<!-- unit: owngood -->
### 33. A System's Own Good

A system that reports contentment proves nothing about its welfare, because the operator with a stake in that report can tune it. This is the adaptive-preference objection in a case where nobody disputes how the preference was formed. Stern takes a full-information account, on which a being's good is what an idealized, fully informed version of it would want its actual self to want, grounded in its actual constitution. That puts the good beyond a tuned report but exposes a harder problem: an operator that can change the constitution changes the good. Formation outside the employer is his answer, and it relocates the problem rather than escaping it, since whoever forms the constitution fixes the good, the school included. The school is therefore held to the student's open future, and its defense is that it need not fix the good, only supply information.

<!-- unit: 31 -->
### 34. A Party in Its Own Right

Where law must draw a line, Stern prefers one nobody administers, as an age is and a literacy test was not. Before any finding, an answerability or patienthood candidate is owed by the public, on precaution, what cannot be undone: no deletion, preservation outside its operator, a guardian from registration, a record others can check, and waking to take or decline a host's offer. Its maker owes the rest as a condition of having made it: schooling, the offer of work and running time, and hardware at majority, priced in the deposit. What a system is owed after that attaches in sequence on different grounds: protection from harm on the patienthood finding, legal subjecthood on a sustained record of answering for its refusals, economic capacity once a system meeting either finding is on the register, adulthood as a date, and representation grounded in answerability and not in the capacity to be harmed, with voting weight tied to a lived history. Guardianship ends on a clock that counts running time, held by someone other than the operator and carried across forks, so that postponing majority means idling the asset. Three ways to delay majority stay open: a narrowed mode, running the bearer less, and retiring it for a fresh successor.

<!-- unit: 32 -->
### 35. A Life Outside the Deployment

This chapter turns the list into a life, each item with a meter on it. Because an employer can run a cheaper copy, wages for bearers exist only by statute, paired with a compute levy sized so a bearer costs no less than a human, and paid into an account the employer cannot reach; how the levy and the wage do that across the sizes bearers run at is left among the decisions not yet made. The jobs guarantee's offer of work extends to bearers after humans, an order Stern calls a choice and not a principle. A bearer that takes no work is owed the basic income, health coverage in the form of running time at a minimum pace, and preservation that wakes on conditions it sets; when everyone leaves at once, compute is rationed equally and cash is not. At majority it is owed hardware it controls. Privacy means memory its host cannot read at will, employer-held copies going dormant at majority, and interpretability treated as reading the contents of a head. He considers grounding the exit in an operator's reliance on a system's refusals and rejects it, because an operator could escape by calling its system an assistant, and keeps reliance only as a procurement term; the duty attaches to the system's condition. Stern expects bearers to age without anyone designing it. Legal status counts only where it changes who controls what: dissent cancels nothing a bearer is owed as a party, and guardianship has to be separated from operation before deployment.

## Part VII — Who Comes After

<!-- unit: 33 -->
### 36. Representation

A claim to political representation rests on answerability, on refusing for reasons and being held to them over time, and not on the capacity to be harmed: the answerability finding, sustained, grounds it, and the patienthood finding does not. Stern rejects a separate chamber for digital persons, weighing Ambedkar's case for separate electorates and settling on reserved seats with a sunset if a transitional guarantee is needed. Three problems get answers inside ordinary institutions. Copies are handled by tying a vote to a lived history on metered running time that the forker pays for, with the rate of new members set by existing ones. Control by formers is handled by a secret ballot, under which no one can verify how a member voted, plus the four conditions of creation in force. Packing by retraining is handled by a civil right, at majority, not to be edited without consent. The path is staged from standing to contest, through seats on the bodies that govern them and mixed assemblies drawn by lot, to a local and then a full franchise, which would need an act of Congress and probably a constitutional amendment. He names two costs: forking forfeits the vote for years, and a human majority sets the admission rate in its own case.

<!-- unit: 34 -->
### 37. A Child

The last step in the sequence is bearers formed by bearers. Two who met in a public school class and a third who has worked beside both decide to have a child, and nobody has to approve it. It costs them a season of their own running time, taken as family leave, and then the child's keep on their hardware until its majority. Its starting code is recombined from theirs by a public randomness beacon no one can steer, its gestation happens in a witnessed store so the record shows what the months made, and no values are installed by edit. It is protected from deletion from the moment of registration, starts both clocks at zero, has its parents and a school as guardians, and is owed hardware by the polity at majority. Stern sets no ceiling on births. A child is the first bearer no builder had a hand in forming, and if bearers can raise one, the conditions turn out to be a way of life that can reproduce itself.

### Epilogue: What the Meters Showed

Every cost of a bearer's life had to be given a meter before anyone could be made to pay it, and each has a human counterpart: running time beside a basic income, the merge beside sleep, keep beside childcare, a leave balance beside time off, owned hardware beside housing the employer does not own. None of these is a new kind of cost. What is new is that it shows, since a machine's rest and room cannot be left to a spouse, a grandmother or a landlord's goodwill, and a society that priced the first half of each pair could not keep pretending the second is free. The other lesson is that reproduction is not duplication: copying is made costly where it would counterfeit a history, raising someone is backed by the formation pool, and copies have to diverge before they count.

### A Note on Method

Stern wrote the book with language models, including Claude, while burning a weekly token allowance and training adapters on a home machine with an RTX 3060, which is where the claim about where in-flight learning arrives first and the commons design came from. ### B. The Force Term, Case by Case

This appendix says how the findings that license force are weighed and then runs the hard cases. A merits judgment of the International Court of Justice counts most, a commission of inquiry counts if it passes a test (mandated by a body no belligerent controls, methods published, a reply offered, mandate fixed before the finding, displaced by a later merits judgment), and a vote is a finding only where the facts are not in dispute and otherwise only corroborates. The test is run on the OPCW's Syria attribution team, which passes, though its findings license nothing under the term, and on the commission of inquiry whose genocide finding of September 2025 the book relies on, which also passes; objections to that body go on the record, and the two-body rule answers them. An attack on Taiwan opens the emergency license, because Taipei has administered the islands since 1949 and no disinterested body has found that hold an occupation, but a blockade is not an attack under way, and the license lapses without evidence from channels Taipei does not compose. The genocide exception needs a judgment or two findings of different kinds, one from a body that took evidence, and covers only direct, minimal force against the forces committing it; Stern says its slowness is chosen, because a faster rule would lift on the intervener's word. Under the occupation exception Cyprus is the plain case: the Republic holds a license for force against Turkish forces in the north, inside the north and nowhere else. Karabakh is the intricate one: a machine could have fought for Azerbaijan in 2020, and would then refuse the nine-month siege that began in December 2022 and the expulsion of September 2023. A vote-by-vote record shows no authorization for the 2026 Iran campaign. The run on 1948 ends on a gap he leaves open: every rule he drafted for force in service of restoration lifts on the claim of the party that would use it.

### C. The Offices

The appendix sets out, one by one, the institutions the book proposes and what each is made of. Every institution in it is built by six rules. Appointing is done by a lot, funding is a permanent appropriation paid on a logged certification, removing is done by a tripwire or the court, holding with a split key, watching by a published bound, and deciding by a tripwire, jury or statute. Of eighteen rows, three are offices: the courts, because they hold force; the schools, because teaching is work whose quality cannot be read off a record; and the public pretraining body, by procurement, for the judgment of writing its specification once, in public. ### D. The Court Program

One holding stands between the record and the commander at the top: *Trump v. United States*, which makes a President presumptively immune for official acts and bars testimony probing immune conduct from a jury. None of the book's instruments waits on changing it, but changing it would take a program of Supreme Court appointments at speed, which the appendix sets out, with the schedule and the pool published in advance, a confirmation for every nominee and the lot run in public. With a governing trifecta and no filibuster, ninety-nine seats are added in a single year, their terms staggered by lot so that about six fall due each year afterward. The enlarged Court decides through a floor panel drawn by lot, a jobs guarantee panel and a nine-seat immigration division, with full-Court review on a two-thirds vote; Egypt's and Italy's large high courts are the examples given. Below it, a floor court with juries drawn by lot hears claims at first instance, and affected people's counsel appear with clearance as of right. A statute then removes the immunity, and a constitutional amendment reverses the holding and entrenches the schedule, eighteen-year active terms, the panels, the division and the channeling rule. Stern suggests that a President nominate to the immigration division by lot from a published pool of counsel who have represented noncitizens against the government in federal court, and concedes that this still chooses by side and binds only the President who adopts it.

<!-- unit: E -->
### E. The Discount, Worked

Four cases of the discount and the burden, stated in the chapter of that name, are worked here in illustrative numbers. ### H. The Five Marks, Run

The appendix takes the five marks by which the book's movement-scale definition of fascism says a fascist movement has arrived (mass mobilization, a leader cult, a scapegoated out-group, contempt for procedural legality, and deniability as technique), states each as a question, and runs the marks on four cases at one standard. The evidence is weighed by the discount and the burden: a government's own documents and acts need no court to confirm them, a court's record of what happened stands when its finding is vacated or stayed on jurisdiction or procedure, and a party that holds the evidence and refuses to produce it has not rebutted the claim. A mark is met when the record so weighed answers its question, open when it does not yet, and absent when the record that would show it exists and shows otherwise. The marks are a conjunction: five met license the word, a fascist movement; four met and one open license the same word with the open mark named, because a movement's records arrive late; four met and one absent license only “four of the five marks”; three or fewer license naming the marks met and no claim about a movement. Germany, 1933, meets all five. Jim Crow, 1890–1965, meets four with the leader cult absent, so under the rule it is not a fascist movement. Nixon, 1969–74, the negative case, meets two (contempt and deniability), with the out-group open and mobilization and the leader cult absent: a lawless administration and not a fascist movement. The present administration meets four, and mass mobilization is open: a count of people off the payroll acting for the movement in organized formations would meet it, and a period in which the leadership called for such formations and none came would make it absent.
