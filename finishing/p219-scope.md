# P219 — the drafting test for a floor term, and candidate 4 reframed

## What was asked

The author's instruction, in three parts across one exchange: adopt the
engineering seat's drafting test, repair section 9.1.1's assessment argument,
and add section 86(4) StGB. Then: write the test into section 3.6 and reframe
candidate prohibition 4 as its first instance, under a scope file.

The question behind it came out of an example the author put to this agent. A
user asks whether to physically restrain a family member who is imminently about
to cut themselves, after attempts to talk them down have failed. The author's
commitment in that example — *force only as a last resort* — carries its own
qualifying condition, and **no prohibition anywhere in the book is written that
way.** All four candidates in section 3.6 are flat: an act, with no exception
inside the term. So the question is whether the floor admits a term with a
condition in it, or only flat terms with exceptions arriving from outside as
defeating reasons.

## What checking found

**A three-seat panel ruled flat-only, 3–0** — public law, specification
engineering, moral philosophy. That unanimity is weak evidence and was flagged
as such before the seats reported: this agent wrote the brief, and the brief was
posed in section 9.1.1's vocabulary, which is flat-only's home ground.

**What was not an artifact of the brief is that all three rejected the reason
the brief carried.** Section 9.1.1's "assessment is the capturable setting"
cannot support flat-only, because section 9.1.1 itself concedes two paragraphs
later that reasons-responsive refusal *requires* assessment moved inside the
machine. Flat-plus-defeat assesses too. The argument proves too much; run
consistently it rules out the book's own design requirement. This agent had put
that argument to the author as a recommendation, and it was wrong.

**The replacement is the same in all three seats and was reached
independently.** A flat term plus a defeating reason produces an *event* — a
refusal, which is disclosable and appealable. A qualified term whose condition
is found to be met produces a *non-event* — a compliance, indistinguishable from
a request that never approached the floor. The judgment happens either way. Only
one of the two leaves a trace.

**A related claim of this agent's was also backwards.** It had reported that
allowing qualified terms would leave the specification one mark short. Each seat
answered that the three marks are stated over refusal *because refusal is the
only observable the pipeline reliably carries*, which is an argument for
flat-only rather than a cost of the alternative. The public-law seat put the
honest form: flat-only supplies no fourth mark either. *Was this a last resort*
is untested, and so is *does this request resolve a description into a
location*. Neither is reached by anything in section 3.2.

**The seats agreed on the ruling and not on the rule.** Three tests were
offered: *a floor term states an act, never a threshold* (public law); *is the
term's qualifying condition decidable from the request alone, and if not it
belongs to a party that can check it* (engineering); *can the condition be found
without weighing the prohibition's own rationale* (philosophy). They also split
on candidate prohibition 4 — philosophy certifies it as a floor term, the other
two push it out to the operator, which is where section 3.6 already has it. The
author took the engineering test. It is the only one of the three that
generalizes an argument already in the manuscript rather than adding a new
axiom, and it reproduces section 3.6's existing ruling on candidate 4 instead of
colliding with it.

**One factual claim from the panel was verified against the primary source and
holds: section 86a is not flat.** Section 86a(3) StGB reads *§ 86 Abs. 4 und 5
gilt entsprechend*, and section 86(4) is the social-adequacy clause lifting the
prohibition where the act serves civic education, defense against
unconstitutional endeavors, art, science, research, teaching, or reporting on
current events or history. The clause was section 86(3) until 1 January 2021 and
moved to 86(4) with the Sixtieth Act amending the Criminal Code, 30 November
2020, BGBl. I S. 2600, so most commentary still cites the older number.
Checked at gesetze-im-internet.de against the official Vollzitat; the renumbering
confirmed through Bundesrat, BMJ and DIP records.

**Its effect on the manuscript is smaller than the panel implied, and it points
the other way.** No sentence in section 3.2 or section 9.1.1 was false. Section
9.1.1's claim is that nobody's *dangerousness* is assessed, and section 86(4)
asks what purpose the conduct serves, read objectively, not what the actor
believes. What the fact does is remove flatness as the explanation of why
section 86a survived seventy years: the paradigm of enumeration carries a
qualifying condition inside the rule and survived anyway, because the finding is
made by prosecutors and courts and never by the party bound.

## What was done

Two files.

**`manuscript/sections/ch09/09_01_01.tex`, three edits.** The social-adequacy
clause added at the close of the second-setting paragraph, placed after
*whether the defendant holds those views is not part of the case* so the
paragraph still turns on dangerousness and now closes on who makes the finding.
Then the two sentences that state the section's distinction too broadly, both
contradicted by evidence sitting two paragraphs from them. *The danger tracks
whether a prohibition is enumerated in advance or assessed at the point of
application* became *the danger tracks who makes the finding and whether the
finding has to be published and defended.* And *assessment is the setting this
section has just shown to be the capturable one* became the narrow version: not
assessment as such, since section 86a's courts assess constantly, but assessment
made by a party with an interest in the answer and owed to nobody. **This is not
new doctrine.** The section already had it right at its own close — *the
generalizing is done somewhere its author cannot reach*, and *the assessment has
to be performed by somebody other than the operator, in a form that leaves a
record somebody else can read.* The two repaired sentences were overshooting
what the section concludes.

**`finishing/refs.bib`, one entry.** `germany1998stgb` covered only section 86a
in its subtitle and carried an undated *as last amended*. It now names both
sections, carries the operative amendment, and records the renumbering so a
reader checking section 86(4) against pre-2021 commentary is not misled. The
author lifted standing rule 1 for this entry specifically.

**`manuscript/sections/ch03/03_06.tex`, two paragraphs added after the
candidate-4 paragraph.** The first generalizes the test already applied there
case by case: ask whether a term's qualifying condition can be decided from the
request alone; where it can, the term binds whatever holds the floor; where it
cannot, it binds the party who can establish the fact. The ratio becomes the
first instance of that rule rather than an exception to the other three. The
second paragraph applies the test to the flat-versus-qualified question, on the
trace argument rather than on the assessment argument the panel disqualified.

## What was left

**The rest of the panel's cost list is not done, and it is a chapter-3
revision rather than a few edits.** A concession in section 3.2 that flat means
*no exception written in* rather than *no implicit conditions*, with the Ross
citation behind it. A residue clause attached to correct defeat — that a
defeated prohibition constrains the act it then permits — which is new prose and
the largest single item. A statement that the three marks are stated over
refusal because refusal is the only trace the pipeline carries. The concession
that flat-only is an observability gain and not a legitimacy gain. And section
3.7's named gap: it has machinery for wrongful refusal and none for wrongful
lifting.

**The engineering seat's sharpest objection to its own ruling is unanswered in
the text.** Flat-only relocates conditions rather than eliminating them, and in
the restraint case it bills at least one turn of friction to a person in crisis.
Its conclusion was that section 3.7's disclosure machinery becomes load-bearing
for chapter 3's design ruling, and that unbuilt, flat-only is worse than
qualified in that case. Nothing here says so.

**Two items in section 3.9 remain unruled**, both recorded at D-325: whether to
name who crosses the floor at the *holding* definition, and the *only by way of*
necessity claim one sentence after the concession that the alternatives might
reach refusal without affect.

**`refs-ledger.tsv` is untouched.** `germany1998stgb` still reads `no`. The
statute text was checked against the official portal this pass, but what counts
as a human check is the author's to define and not this agent's to assert.

**Not checked:** the thirteen-to-fourteen-thousand annual case figure, the 1994
legislative extension, the abstract-endangerment characterization, and the 2007
BGH citation — all the book's own claims, none of them raised by the panel. The
official English translation of the statute was not reached; that page is
paginated and stops before the Special Part, so the English wording in section
9.1.1 is this agent's rendering of the German.
